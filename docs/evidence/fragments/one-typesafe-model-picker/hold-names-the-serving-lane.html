Two recorded picks held a node without a fact Jev needed to weigh the hold. At 2026-10-08T02:31:54Z Jev held review-of-member-friction-asserts-its-own-writes at a hold probability of 0.83 (confidence 0.66); at 2026-10-07T07:46:40Z it held review-of-subscription-lanes-record-no-spend at 0.84 (confidence 0.68). Both records, in docs/state/reckon/picker-holds.jsonl, carry only the deterministic exclusion reasons review-excluded-backend; in-harness-backend. The rendered state listed the exclusion of every other lane as a bare absence, so a hold read as "wait for any lane to free".

The lane figures that decide it: a review node's role withdraws 7 of the 8 declared backends from review routing (claude, claude-opus, claude-haiku, codex, codex-astra, codex-luna, native), and native is also the in-harness backend, which a routed dispatch cannot execute. One lane is left — the local lane — so exactly one lane can serve the node; a hold waits for the local lane alone, and waiting for a metered lane to free changes nothing because none of them may run a review. That is the fact the state lacked.

Verdict: the holds were wrong — not because the local lane was congested, but because Jev lacked the fact that holding meant waiting for the one lane that could serve the node. The coordinator overrode the first hold with --local (run r-20261008T023220940800-review-of-member-friction-asserts-its-own-writes) one minute later, and the picker's own record shows a route, not a hold, on the next pick at 2026-10-08T02:33:41Z: the lane the hold waited on was serving (~/.config/reckon/crew/pick-timings.jsonl).
