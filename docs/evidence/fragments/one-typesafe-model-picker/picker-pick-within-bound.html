picker-pick-within-bound — a pick measures each phase once and reads no foreign ledger

What changed. Two phases inside pick() were re-done work. snapshot._fit measured every candidate's context window twice: routing._competence_verdict already measures the window and returns it under context, and _fit then called routing._context_fit_verdict a second time. The measurement reads the backend's instruction chain, whose repository lookup shells out to git rev-parse, so each candidate paid that subprocess twice on the pick's critical path. _fit now consumes the measurement the competence verdict already returned and only measures again when that verdict skipped its context check. Separately, lane_context._expected_wait profiled every live local worker by reading its project's whole ledger through run_time_profile; when the pick already holds one project's records, a worker from another project now contributes nothing rather than putting a second ledger's size on the pick's critical path — matching the rule that an unmeasured shape reports no invented duration.

Result against the bound. The median total fell from about 3.34 s to about 1.37 s, inside the 1.5 s target and well inside the 5 s dispatch bound. The two ledger-scaling phases that were on the pick's critical path — the duplicate context measurement (whose cost is the instruction-chain read, not the ledger) and the foreign-project wait profile — are both removed; no phase that scales with ledger size remains inside pick(). The observed distribution supports keeping the bound at 5 s: the worst of three runs is 1.52 s, roughly a third of the bound.

Cut phase outside this node's fence — reported, not fixed. The dispatch path pre-builds three inputs before pick() runs and outside its 5 s bound: ledger.runs (one ledger.load over 1567 per-run files, ~3.4–5.4 s), routing.shared_verdict_inputs together with capabilities.project_cache_status (~3.1–6.0 s), and budget.preflight (~2.7–6.5 s). These sit in ledger.py, capabilities.py and budget.py, outside this node's write scope, and they scale with ledger size. They do not touch the pick's bound, but they make a cold dispatch slow overall; the measured per-phase figures are recorded here and the follow-on node carries the repair. They are not a reason to raise the 5 s pick bound — they are a separate dispatch-latency defect.

Slow-Jev fallback. A pick whose Jev client runs longer than the bound must yield a recorded fallback, never a refusal. The bound is enforced in dispatch.dispatch_picker_selection, which runs pick() in a thread with PICKER_DISPATCH_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 5.0. The new test test_slow_client_still_times_out_as_a_recorded_fallback lowers the bound to 0.05 s against a client that sleeps 0.4 s and asserts the result is {"action": "fallback", "fallback_reason": "timeout"} — the recorded fallback, returned before the client finished.

Negative control. Reverting the _fit change — restoring the second _context_fit_verdict call — makes test_fit_reuses_the_competence_context_measurement fail: the test monkeypatches the second measurement to raise AssertionError("context fit measured twice in one _fit"). The control ran 1 failed / 3 passed with exit 1, the failing id being exactly that test (negative-control.log).

Gate. Base arm and head arm run the picker test files with the ledger present; each is judged by delta against the base revision, not by an absolute count. Results are recorded in the manifest's baseline_suite and after_suite keys.
