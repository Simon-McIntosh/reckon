A deferred section held by a decision or a gate is the plan's continuation

A plan whose declared work is resolved but which keeps one section declared deferred is waiting on someone else, and the wait is already recorded: an open decision names the section it holds, or an open gate names it. That record states what the section waits on and who must act, so it stands as the plan's continuation and a landing on such a plan owes no followup. Before the change the guard accepted only an open followup or a chain-closing outcome, so the coordinator had no legal landing at all: a followup restating the wait was refused as hidden work, and no followup was refused as no continuation — the placeholder form the close op retired.

continuation_present in reckon/_store.py gains one clause: after an open followup and a chain-closing outcome it consults _deferred_section_is_held. A decision is open until it records a choice, which is the attribute the lock op writes and the roadmap reads a decision's readiness from. A gate is open until it records a verdict, which the pass and fail ops write together with the closed status. Either holds the deferred section when the sections it names include it. Both write paths — the op writer and the HTTP patch writer — reach the rule through the one continuation_present entry point, so they cannot disagree. skills/reckon-edit/SKILL.md states the rule for coordinators: no placeholder followup for a plan waiting on a decision or gate, and locking the decision or settling the gate ends the wait so the next landing owes a real continuation.
