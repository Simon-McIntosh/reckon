Every read surface lists the open decisions holding work

A plan waiting on the lead carries no followup, so its open decision is the only record that it waits and on whom. Both surfaces that a reader consults now list that record from one derivation. _decision_rows in reckon/roadmap.py gained a waiting_context option which carries the owning project, the plan's last-modified date and the recorded recommended/recommended_by onto each decision row. The option is opt-in, because a caller that consumes rows for readiness compares them whole: a row that gained a field would read as a different decision rather than the same one with more to say. build_roadmap therefore emits both lists — the readiness rows it always did, unchanged, and a new top-level waiting_decisions list carrying the context — from the one _decision_rows call, so no surface re-derives a wait.

waiting_decision_rows(report) is the single reader of that list. The roadmap's default summary view, mcp_views._roadmap_project_summary, attaches it as open_decisions with its open_decision_count, so the view a coordinator reads by default no longer drops the decisions the raw report already derived.

The served /crew route carries the same rows for every mounted project under open_decisions, and reaches them through unsettled_decisions(..., waiting_context=True) — the roadmap's own derivation, applied to the plan state the poll already parses and memoises (serve._crew_plan_state, one whole-document read per plan per file identity, shared with the crew row's own sprint/effort read than the crew row's own read under the existing crew-plan-details memo entry). Serving the report's list directly from this route was built and then refused by two contracts the route already carries: a report build reads every plan a second time through _plan_html.read_state, so a poll that reads one plan twice reported two reads where the pinned case requires one, and the first poll pays the whole build, which the route's one-second budget over forty live pointers does not allow. The report's own list is published instead on the discovery payload (serve._attach_ready_set sets waiting_decisions), where a caller that wants the report itself reads it; the parity case is what keeps the two surfaces from drifting apart.

A wait that names no section holds the whole plan, and a reader of a row whose scope key was simply missing could not tell that from a decision that named nothing. Every waiting row therefore states its scope: sections is a list, empty when the wait is the whole plan, and holds_whole_plan carries the answer in a field of its own. The scope keys are added under waiting_context only, because a row consumed for readiness is compared whole and the readiness rows must not move. Both surfaces carry the field, and a case asserts it on each.

tests/test_every_surface_lists_open_decisions.py mounts two projects holding three plans, with every environment-resolved directory pointed at temporary paths. The first project's first plan carries an open decision scoped to a section, a gated decision held by a pending decision-lockable transition gate, and a locked decision; its second plan carries an open decision that names no section, so the whole-plan scope has a plan of its own to be read from; the second project's plan carries its own open and locked decisions, so a row from one project cannot pass for the other's. Five cases: the summary view lists exactly the open and gated decisions, with their sections, their recommendation and who made it, and the plan's last-modified date, and excludes the locked one and the other project's rows; the /crew payload lists the same rows for every mounted project; the two surfaces agree on both projects, compared in order, so a difference in membership or ordering fails; the whole-plan wait states its scope on both surfaces; and locking the open decision removes it from both. The plans' date is written into them rather than taken from today, so the cases cannot pass on the day they are written and expire.
