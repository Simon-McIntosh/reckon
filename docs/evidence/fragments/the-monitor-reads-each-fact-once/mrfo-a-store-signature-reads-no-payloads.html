A store signature reads no payloads

The defect. The signature a stored-history reader compares was taken from the rows themselves: LENGTH(payload) for every run in the project, and, at the base, a second pass over the attached archive. A signature read on the live store therefore read every payload, and under a working fleet it is taken again whenever the store's stamp moves. A cold read of the live store cost 1,165,905,463 bytes, and the picker's read beside it 2,319,775,486 bytes.

Why a mirror rather than a trigger. A payload-free mirror of the signature columns has to be written by the store's own write path because SQLite refuses a trigger that writes another attached database, and the retired history lives in a separate file. The mirror carries each row's encoded payload and detail lengths, every indexed column the signature compares, the revision a backfill stamps, the activity seconds, a present flag and a monotonic sequence number. It is maintained by append, sync_run, retire, stamp_revisions, delete_run and the rebuild pass that brings an older store's declaration up to date.

The fallback. Rows written by a process running an older declaration would not be in the mirror, so a full read compares COUNT(*) between the mirror and the runs table in both the main and the attached archive store, and answers the payload-length query when they disagree. In that transitional case every row stays visible at the base cost rather than disappearing from the signature.

The measure

tests/test_a_store_signature_reads_no_payloads.py prepares a store whose payloads total 210,000,000 bytes and measures each read with the process's own read counter (/proc/self/io rchar). After one row changes, the signature read costs less than 5 MB, and the picker read costs less than 5 MB; the store's own statement counters show the incremental signature returning one row and the picker's read decoding one row. Doubling every payload leaves the signature bytes, the signature rows, the repeat picker bytes and the decoded rows unchanged, and the read that takes the picker's change costs about one row at either size. The signature moves when a row is rewritten in place, archived or deleted, and not on an untouched store; and over that sequence of states both reads answer the base query and ledger.load(picker_only=True) value for value.

The arms. The scoped gate at c22bc47ad372 reports 62 passed in 60.94s (0:01:00) and ends EXIT=0. The suite's baseline arm at 5ac9f1d1df7a27012084bffb48099375a5c8a23a reports 58 passed in 37.94s, and its after arm at c22bc47ad372 62 passed in 67.18s (0:01:07); both end EXIT=0.

Negative control. At 5ac9f1d1df7a27012084bffb48099375a5c8a23a the first case fails on the byte bound with the signature read cost 210,452,555 bytes against a payload total of 210,000,000, and the log ends EXIT=1.

The live store

A probe read the live reckon store on a copy of its two files taken to node-local disk, on all_debug, once through each of the twelve projects it holds. Bringing the older copy's declaration up to date costs 571,030,342 bytes once (the mirror build, not a read). After that the signature read costs 8,549,419 bytes in total across the twelve projects, against the base's 1,165,905,463, and the cold picker read costs 584,839,141 bytes against the base's 2,319,775,486. The second picker read, over a store that has not moved, costs 4,501,608 bytes in total — 271 KB to 656 KB per project.
