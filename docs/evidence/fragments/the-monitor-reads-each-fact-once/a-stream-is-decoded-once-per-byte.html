A stream is decoded once per byte

The defect. A terminal run's stream cannot change, yet every producer pass asked promotion_checks._terminal_stream_data for a completion stamp and a budget timing, and recovery_stream._observed_stream for the observation the classifier reads. Each of those decoded the whole stream again, because the only cache of a stream's records — the shared events cache — is bounded by bytes (32 MB), so a fleet whose streams exceed that bound evicted its own entries and re-parsed every stream on the next pass. On this node's fixture of forty terminal runs at five thousand records each, a pass made 200,040 json.loads calls from reckon/_backends.py at the base revision, every one of them over bytes the process had already read.

What landed. reckon/_backends.py keeps a per-stream summary under the file's stat identity: the observation each settings key folded and the earliest and latest zoned stamp the stream states. The summary records the byte offset the last read reached and the digest of the stream's opening. A stream whose identity is unchanged is answered from the summary with no decode at all; a grown stream is read only from that offset and extended through the dialect's own fold; a truncated, replaced or in-place-rewritten stream fails the opening check and is read again whole. The records are not retained, so the cache is bounded by the number of streams the process is watching (2,048 entries) rather than by their bytes, and it survives the events cache evicting a stream's records.

The gate

tests/test_a_stream_is_decoded_once_per_byte.py builds a temporary crew home of forty terminal runs whose streams hold five thousand records each, and reads every run through _terminal_stream_data, _stream_completion_stamp and _stream_budget twice in one process. A recording stand-in for the json module counts the decodes made from reckon._backends. At head the first pass makes 200,040 decodes — one per record — and the second pass makes 0, returning the same measures, stamps and budgets. Appending ten records to one stream makes the next pass decode exactly 10; truncating a stream makes it decode that stream's whole content, 1,000 records. Gate: 3 passed, EXIT=0 (gate-head-bd7f02bc7b50.log).

One accounting repair this exposed. recovery_watch.watch_ticker took and reset the parsed-bytes counter at the top of its tick, while the transition pass that runs after the registration reads streams too. Those reads were dropped by the next reset, and the poll reported less than it parsed. The producer now takes the counter at the registration only, so a poll reports every byte its tick parsed; tests/test_producer_reads_only_what_changed.py passes in full — 7 passed.

The declared negative control restores whole-stream decoding — the summary read returns nothing and the bounds read falls back to the events cache — and the same file at the same revision then reports the first case's second pass at 200,040 decodes, the append case at 200,050 and the truncation case at 196,039, failing all three (negative-control-bd7f02bc7b50.log, EXIT=1). The counter therefore sees an absence at head and a presence with the summary disabled, rather than an instrument that cannot see either.
