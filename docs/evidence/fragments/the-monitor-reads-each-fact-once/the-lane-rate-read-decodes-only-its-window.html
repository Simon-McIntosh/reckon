A lane rate read bounded by its window rather than by the ledger

What landed. recovery_stream._lane_reference_rates passed the window it judges against to the ledger read instead of reading past it. It now calls ledger.load(project, activity_since=start), which the run index serves from its own indexed activity scalar, so a warm index fetches only the rows whose newest stamp is no older than the seven-day window's start. run_activity_seconds takes the maximum over the created, dispatched, launched and completion stamps, and a completion inside the window is one of those stamps, so the bound admits a superset of the rows the function can use: the rates it returns are the same ones the unbounded read returned. The ledger module itself needed no change; the bound it already publishes was simply not being asked for.

Gate. tests/test_the_lane_rate_read_decodes_only_its_window.py builds a 2,030-row ledger shaped like the live one — 2,000 completed more than eight days before the fixture's own instant, 30 inside the window across two lanes — warms its on-disk index with one read, then calls the function once for a new bucket. A recording stand-in on the per-row decode counts the payload records the call decodes, and the case asserts at most 60, that the recorded rows are exactly the in-window ones, and that the per-lane medians equal the unbounded read's medians for the same ledger.

The two counts. At the head revision the call decodes 30 payload records of 2,030 rows. At the base revision (e278e8f173) the same case decodes 2,030 and fails the bound, which is the declared negative control.

The live ledger, measured on all_debug. One call for a fresh bucket against the operator's reckon ledger (10,794 rows, 3 lanes), both arms in one job: at the base revision it decodes 10,794 rows in 5.79 s at a peak RSS of 1.67 GB (from 102 MB); at the station's head revision it decodes 1,691 rows — the live history's own seven-day population — in 1.30 s at a peak of 419 MB (from 97 MB). The read now scales with the window's population rather than the ledger's length. Both arms returned the same 3 lanes.

Matched arms. Two arms ran on the all_debug partition over the test files importing the changed module, each arm resolved over its own tree, each log opening with its revision and literal command and ending in its own EXIT=. The base arm collects no failure the head arm does not; the after arm adds this node's case. The counts are in the node's manifest beside the log paths.
