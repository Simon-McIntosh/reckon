Fleet state derived on change or at the interval

The station's per-project refresh called obligation_snapshot.fleet_state on every tick whose transitions moved, and on a 60 s floor otherwise. A live pointer, a run manifest and a plan are the three inputs a derivation is built from, and a derivation is a 22.5% share of a tick. The gate now reads those three inputs cheaply: the pointer's stored signature, one file_memo signature per manifest and one per plan.

Measure

Thirty quiet ticks of one second each cost one derivation at head and one at base — the base already gates on its 60 s floor — so the count at base is not where the defect shows. What the base does not do is derive on an input's change: after a live pointer was rewritten at base the next tick derived the fleet state 0 times, and the published obligations stayed at the previous derivation's answer until the interval expired. At head each of the three inputs moving costs exactly one derivation on its own tick, an unmoved tick costs none, and every tick's stored obligations equal the ungated derivation's at the tick's own instant. So the section's claim that the base revision derives thirty times over thirty quiet ticks does not reproduce on this revision: the base already gates on the interval, and the gap it leaves is the change it cannot see.
