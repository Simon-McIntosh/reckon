A landed record is written only once the run's row and its place on the primary branch exist

At revision 398a0d09708dda09bec869b7a512404cf9eb2902 a landing writes its close comment, its section declaration and the removal of the run's live pointer whether or not anything recorded what had landed; at head af80e1240ff1a5750e309c950dbd1397b98374fc each of those writes is refused until the run's head is on the primary branch and its ledger row is written.

What changed. Two incidents had the same shape. In r-20261122T215439012216 a promotion was settled without a promotion: the merged-head replay exited 3 and wrote no log, and a minute later a close comment was written, the section was set to done, the pointer was removed, and the run's head was not on the primary branch until a hand merge forty-two minutes later. No ledger row was written. In r-20261121T205405910971-mast-known-state-carriers-rebuilt a hand-merged head went through the landing pass as a delivery the branch already carried; the landed comment was written and the pointer removed, again with no row, so a later crew complete found no record at all.

One precondition now sits in front of every write. landed_record_refusal requires the run's ledger row, a head that is an ancestor of the primary revision, and answers with the reason when either is missing. finish_landing asks it before publishing and accepts the row its caller already read, so a release path does not re-read the store. The integrated release asks it before releasing a workspace, and the record pass of the landing pass asks it before any merge that will publish, then publishes before it retires the pointer. A refusal records itself on the run (landing-row-refused, declaration-refused) and leaves the plan file, the declaration and the pointer where it found them.

A carried delivery with no row is recorded rather than settled into no record: record_a_carried_delivery writes a passed row naming the primary revision that carries the run's head, commits it under the landing write lease, and hands the row to the release that follows.

What does not change. A landing whose merge and row both succeed writes the same close comment, the same declaration and the same release as before, and a section still closes only through the one writer that emits c-close-<section>. A row that is present and names a head the branch carries passes every guard without a write.
