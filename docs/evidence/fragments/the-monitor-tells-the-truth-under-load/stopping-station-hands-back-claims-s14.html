A stopping station exits within a bound and hands its claims back

A station gave SIGTERM a flag that only the loop between ticks ever read. A tick held in a derivation doing storage IO, or the shutdown's join of every derivation in turn, could hold the process open for as long as that work took. Measured on 15 November: two of three restarts were still running 120 s after SIGTERM, were answered with SIGKILL, and every claim the killed station held was orphaned — seven at 13:00Z, six of them other sessions'.

Two promises, each kept where it landed

The bound is enforced from a timer thread armed by the signal handler itself, because the paths the shutdown waits on are exactly the ones that never read the stop flag. The ordered unwinding keeps whatever time it has left up to the bound and cancels the timer when it finishes, so a clean stop is never cut short; at the bound the process hands back its claims and exits where it stands.

Handing claims back is keyed on the launch step rather than on the phase, and on the holder rather than on the run. dispatch_claims.release_unlaunched_claims walks the live pointers, keeps those whose claim.json names this process as holder, and gives back each whose step has not reached worker-spawned:

  • the run's launch refusal is recorded first, naming the step the span reached, because releasing the claim removes the run directory that would hold it;
  • a claim the admission pass took from the queue goes back to the queue, with the brief and steering the launch had already stored, since removing the run directory would take the only copy of a request nothing else would dispatch;
  • every other unlaunched claim is released outright, pointer first and run directory after.

A claim at worker-spawned or identity-published, and any claim whose pointer already names a pid, is left exactly as it stands: a worker is running behind it, and handing it back would let the queue launch a second worker over the first. A claim another process published is not this station's to give back.

The same handback runs on the ordered path as well as at the bound. Whatever the shutdown did, a dispatcher thread caught between its claim and its worker dies with the process, and nothing else in that process is left to release what it published.

Where the change lives

  • reckon/crew/station.py — STATION_STOP_BOUND_SECONDS states the bound; StopBound arms it from the signal handler and hands the claims back at it, and is cancelled when the ordered shutdown finishes; run_station arms it in the stop handler and calls dispatch_claims.release_unlaunched_claims() in its own finally; DutyBeat.drain now spends one bound across every worker it joins rather than one per worker.
  • reckon/crew/dispatch_claims.py — LAUNCHED_STEPS names the steps a worker stands behind, and release_unlaunched_claims is the one handback: refusal record first, then the queued request back to the queue or the claim released outright.
  • tests/test_a_stopping_station_hands_back_its_claims.py — the step-against-phase handback, the queued request that goes back to the queue with its brief, and a real station process signalled while its tick and its derivation are both blocked.
