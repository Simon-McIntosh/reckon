A declared wait is judged against itself, not against a log's clock

A parked run's overdue flag was age_seconds > expected_horizon_seconds, and a declaration that named no horizon took the reader's default: the 900 s log-staleness bound. That number is a limit on a log file, not a limit the wait declared, and it was held against a scheduler job that was still running. Reproduced at the base revision with a live probe three hours in — ["python", "…/probe.py"] printing RUNNING, no wait_expected field — the row read wait_overdue=True and recovery_classification='wait-aged', which is the reading that sent a multi-hour compute wait to "investigate" at r-20261114T211231918544. The wait now carries expected_horizon_source, and while its own probe reports a live state a default horizon is not held against it: overdue is false and the row stays waiting. A declaration that names its own horizon is still judged by that horizon, and a wait_file condition — whose reader derives pending from absent paths rather than taking an answer from the awaited work — still ages into the alarm.

The producer-gone verdict rested on three second-hand facts: the run's own process being gone, a probe value that had stood still, and a scheduler query asked on this host. The third is meaningless for work submitted elsewhere, and the second is what a healthy wait looks like. An answering probe was the one first-hand fact in the case and was not consulted. Reproduced at the base revision with a script probe answering PENDING on every sweep: the row read producer-gone with the detail "the run's own process is gone — no process of the run is alive and the probe 'PENDING' has not changed for 1200s — and the wait names no scheduler job". A declared probe that answered is now the wait's own evidence and the verdict returns before its second-hand legs are read, whatever its argv names, so a parked worker with such a probe is never read abandoned.

A job the scheduler cancels before it starts reports CANCELLED with an elapsed time of zero, and CANCELLED is a way a job ends, so a declaration that lists it matched its own terminal state and the row offered a resume against work that never ran (r-20261114T210059239602). Reproduced at the base revision: the row read ready to resume: the compute job writes its final rows reported 'CANCELLED 00:00:00', a declared terminal state. A probe naming a job with a cancelled state and no elapsed time is now carried on the row as external_wait.suspect_probe, the condition is reported unknown rather than met, and the offered action is the declaration's repair. The same state with an elapsed time behind it is still terminal, so a job that ran before it was cancelled ends the wait as before.
