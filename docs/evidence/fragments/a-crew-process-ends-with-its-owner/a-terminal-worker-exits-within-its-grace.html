A worker whose manifest turns complete exits within its grace

What changed

The supervisor no longer waits indefinitely for a worker that has already delivered its verdict and then lingers, holding its run's slot. It watches the run's manifest; once the manifest reads a done status that belongs to this attempt it gives the worker the grace period to exit on its own, then ends its process. The signal is attributable because the sender record is written first, through the shared signal home.

  • reckon/crew/dispatch.py: _run_supervisor collects the worker's exit through _reap_worker_on_its_terminal_manifest instead of a blocking os.waitpid. The helper polls (WNOHANG) on a coarse interval and reads the manifest's status only when its mtime has advanced past the attempt's baseline and changed since the last read, so an unchanged manifest is never reparsed and shared storage is not read across the whole life of every worker. A done manifest's deadline is its own write time plus TERMINAL_MANIFEST_GRACE_ENV (RECKON_WORKER_TERMINAL_GRACE_SECONDS, default 300 s, shortenable by a test); once it passes the worker is signalled through signal_worker (sender record first) with a SIGKILL escalation if ignored. The done set is complete and failed; blocked is terminal but kept for resume, and in-progress is non-terminal.
  • reckon/crew/dispatch.py: _supervisor_manifest_baseline_ns is the generation this attempt may claim — the pointer's manifest_baseline_mtime_ns, or the attempt's own start when the pointer carries none. A resumed run keeps its manifest path, so without this the previous attempt's complete manifest reads as this attempt's delivery and the new worker is killed on the first poll.
  • tests/test_a_terminal_worker_exits_within_its_grace.py: a stub worker writes its manifest and then sleeps far past the grace, so its exit can only be the supervisor's act. Cases: a complete manifest ends the worker within the grace and leaves a sender record; a blocked and an in-progress manifest keep the worker running; a manifest left by a previous attempt (a resumed run) does not end the worker until the worker rewrites it complete; and the done-status set excludes blocked and non-terminal spellings.
