A worker whose manifest turns complete exits within its grace

What changed

The supervisor no longer waits indefinitely for a worker that has already delivered its verdict and then lingers, holding its run's slot. It watches the run's manifest; once the manifest reads a done status it gives the worker the grace period to exit on its own, then ends its process. The signal is attributable because the sender record is written first, through the shared signal home.

  • reckon/crew/dispatch.py: _run_supervisor collects the worker's exit through _reap_worker_on_its_terminal_manifest instead of a blocking os.waitpid. The helper polls (WNOHANG), reads _worker_manifest_done_status, and once _manifest_done_deadline (the manifest's own write time plus TERMINAL_MANIFEST_GRACE_ENV, RECKON_WORKER_TERMINAL_GRACE_SECONDS, default 300 s, shortenable by a test) has passed, signals the worker through signal_worker (sender record first) and escalates to SIGKILL if it is ignored. The done set is complete and failed; blocked is terminal but kept for resume, and in-progress is non-terminal.
  • tests/test_a_terminal_worker_exits_within_its_grace.py: a stub worker writes its manifest and then sleeps far past the grace, so its exit can only be the supervisor's act. Cases: a complete manifest ends the worker within the grace and leaves a sender record; a blocked and an in-progress manifest keep the worker running; and the done-status set excludes blocked and non-terminal spellings.
