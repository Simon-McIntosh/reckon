A producer holds a lease, not a lifetime

What changed

  • reckon.crew.runs gains the producer's lease registration: watch_registration_path, read and read-modify-written by read_watch_registration and update_watch_registration. Each write merges its fields into what is on disk under a sibling lock and lands by renaming a fresh sibling over the destination, so the producer's pid and a follower's lease_renewed_at never drop each other and a reader never sees a partial record. producer_lease_seconds resolves the interval (RECKON_PRODUCER_LEASE_SECONDS, else 600 s) and renew_producer_lease is the follower's throttled renewal. The registration is a file of its own rather than the seat record, because the seat is an advisory lock held on its file's inode for the producer's whole life: a follower could not take that lock and a rename would detach it.
  • reckon.crew.recovery.watch_ticker — the producer loop — reads the lease on every wake-up and ends the seat when the recorded instant has fallen a full interval behind. No sleep outlasts the lease's remaining time, so the exit lands within a second of the lapse however far the interval has backed off.
  • reckon.cli._follow_watch_lines renews the lease on its wait pass through the new nested _renew_producer_lease.

Evidence

tests/test_producer_holds_a_lease.py runs in a temporary RECKON_HOME with RECKON_PRODUCER_LEASE_SECONDS=3 and holds three cases: an unattended producer ends within a second of its lease lapsing (timed against the instant the producer itself recorded); a producer stays up past three lease intervals while a follower renews once per half interval, read through lease_renewed_at; and two concurrent writers of the registration — the producer's pid and the follower's lease_renewed_at — both survive.
