§5 — One of two racing dispatches survives the claim check

Two dispatches of overlapping write paths can each publish a claim before either reaches its admission check, because the claim is registered at the run id's own moment and the live claims are read earlier still. Each then reads the other as a live claim and, refusing on sight, both withdraw — measured on r-20261001T024141507018-figure-thumbnails, whose two review compositions (dispatched 04:59:26 and 04:59:31) both withdrew at 04:59:37, leaving the run with no review until a later sweep.

The claim registered first owns the paths. _peer_claim_is_a_later_racing_arrival (reckon/crew/dispatch.py) makes a dispatch disregard a peer claim that has not launched a worker and was registered after this dispatch's own, ordered by registration time and then by run id when the times are equal, so exactly one of the two proceeds and the other refuses when it checks, naming the winner. A peer that has launched its worker — its record carries a worktree or a pid — is no longer a racing arrival and refuses every newcomer exactly as before. A peer whose registration cannot be shown to follow this one (an absent timestamp, or this dispatch holding no claim of its own) is treated as established and refused. The ordering is applied at both admission checks by passing this dispatch's own run id and registration timestamp into _raise_repository_scope_conflict.
