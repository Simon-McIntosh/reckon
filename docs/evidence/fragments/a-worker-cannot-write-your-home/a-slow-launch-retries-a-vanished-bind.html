A slow launch's vanished-bind refusal is retried

The retry that recovers a launch refused over a read-only bind source a rename left missing for an instant keyed on the child exiting inside a half-second startup window. On a placed launch the child is the local scheduler client, which outlives the step's own start, so the sandbox's refusal reaches the launch's own stderr seconds after the launch and the client is still running when it does. A refusal that landed four seconds in was therefore recorded as a launch failure although the same launch a moment later would have begun normally.

The startup watch now reads the refusal from the launch's own stderr inside a window sized for a placed step, and ends early on the first of two facts: the launch's stream beginning, or the child exiting — whose stderr decides the answer. A try counts as dead only once its child has exited, because the retry collects a dead try by waiting on it: reading the refusal on its own would declare a worker that is still running a failed launch and then block in it. The window is kept well clear of the interval a healthy child of this launch stays alive, since an exit inside the window is recorded as a launch failure. The stream path is threaded through both spawn sites.
