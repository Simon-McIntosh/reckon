The resolver's review named five follow-ons. This node closes the three that live in this scope and declares the two it cannot: the one that revokes a lift by name, the one that returned a None multiple for two of the three forms, the copy of the pace default, the environment read in the resolver, and the flight slot that makes the lift's own ceilings settable by a layer.

What landed

clear() now names the lift it revokes one of two ways. It takes lift_id, which revokes that exact record whatever its scope, or it resolves the lift for the given session, where a session's own lift governs it over a global one and a bare clear with neither argument still revokes only a global lift. So a session-scoped lift is revocable by a coordinator that sees it in the listing, and a global clear never takes a session's owned lift out from under it.

effective_budget no longer returns pace_multiple: None. Only the multiple form raises the multiple and carries the lifted figure; the drain-by and uncapped forms keep the configured multiple and mark their own hold in the block's pace_hold marker, which is what a reader consults for those forms.

configured_pace_multiple reads reckon.crew.pace.policy(config) rather than keeping a second copy of the default. effective_budget takes its session only from its argument: the environment fallback is removed, so a resolver that reached for RECKON_SESSION cannot answer a different question from the one its caller asked.

budget.lift with max_multiple and max_hours is declared in reckon/schema/flight.yaml as a LiftConfig block and regenerated with scripts/regen_flight_schema.py into reckon/_flight_schema.py and docs/_shared/flight.schema.json. BudgetConfig forbids extra keys, so a layer writing the ceilings validates and a layer writing an undeclared key under the block does not. The shipped defaults (3.0 and 168) are the fallback budget_lift.ceilings applies when a configuration sets neither.
