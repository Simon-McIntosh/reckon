The control loop on the fleet, 22 November

Every measure the research record took on 16 November was taken again on the live fleet on 22 November, at revision 3a0f33d362, between 07:07 and 08:16 UTC. Three dispatch previews had a median of 13.863 s against a bound of 30 s. Ten admission receipts had a median pass of 205.4 s against a bound of 20 s. Three reads of one declaration write's readiness report took 28.9 s, 39.6 s and 99.4 s, median 39.6 s against a bound of 30 s. One plan's rows came back from the run store in 9.22 s, returning 1,055 of 12,925. Over 24 hours, 82 runs recorded a delivery and the 90th percentile from delivery to review launch was 1,421.9 s against a bound of 600 s. One bound is met, the preview, and three of the four are missed.

The admission miss is in the launch stage. A pass's own decision work — release and wait together — has a median of 61.7 s, already three times the bound, and the launch stage adds 7.2 s to 372.1 s on top: every receipt that names a slowest step names dispatch at 101 to 261 s for a single launched run, while a dry-run preview of the same plans takes 13.9 s. The service restarted mid-sample (six receipts on one code stamp, four on the next) and both stamps miss the bound. Every receipt also reports 2.33 GiB to 7.99 GiB resident against the 2 GiB threshold it sheds memory at, read after that shedding had already run.

The per-read times the receipts now carry do not account for the time. Release's named reads sum to 0.86 s to 9.05 s of a release stage that ran 15.4 s to 93.9 s, and every named read together — release, wait and the lost-session reads — sums to 1.06 s to 10.41 s of a pass that took 29.0 s to 434.1 s. So the receipt that names its reads still cannot say where its own minutes go: the time is inside stages the receipt does not break down, and for the largest passes inside the dispatch call each launch makes.

The review miss is in the request, not the launch, which is the split the record asked for so the two could be told apart. Across the 24 hours to 07:44 UTC, 419 candidate runs were read from the store, 82 recorded a delivery, 75 were requested and launched, and 7 were never requested. Delivery to request had a median of 44.9 s and a 90th percentile of 1,037.3 s; request to launch had 76.0 s and 189.2 s. The worst case was a run delivered at 21:35 on 20 November whose review was requested the next morning. All seven unrequested runs carry one obligation reason: the run has no live pointer, so no sweep reaches it to ask.

The readiness read misses its bound too, and not in the place the sections aimed at. Three reads of the same helper took 28.9 s against a snapshot of the store, then 39.6 s and 99.4 s against the live store, median 39.6 s against 30 s. The landing audit is not the cost: it found eleven plans holding runs, one declared done section among them, and zero promoted rows to compare ancestry for, so it ran against nothing and made zero git probes. The cost is discovery over live pointers — classify_pointer at 92.0 s over 176 calls inside a profiled 108.9 s call — and each classification reads the run store, where stored_run_rows took 80.8 s over 207 calls and _refuse_owed_migration 77.5 s of it over 177. The same helper against a quieter store is 28.9 s, so the miss scales with the store's load rather than being a fixed cost. Two further reads beside these — 42.9 s and 14.7 s on a clone with cold git caches, 53.3 s and 32.0 s on a docs root with no repository — are dominated by the first git object read and are not the same operation. §9 stays open on all three misses, each recorded against the stage that caused it.

The measures are the baseline's, taken the same way. The previews ran with RECKON_HOME at a temporary config home whose crew directories are symlinks to the live ones, because the worker fence makes the live crew home read-only — under the live home they failed three times at about 8.8 s with OSError: Read-only file system on a watch lock, which is the reading rather than a measurement. The receipts were accumulated from the verdict log, which trims at 1 MiB and holds about six passes, so a single read cannot supply ten. The store read ran against a node-local SQLite backup of the hot store and its archive, three times, each returning the same 1,055 rows as the exhaustive filter over the same snapshot. The census enumerated candidates from the run store rather than by walking the crew home, and counted a delivery from the delivery_recorded stage receipt. Every heavy step ran on rigel.

What could have made a reading false was checked. A census with an empty or unknown population reports null lags rather than zeros, so a quiet window cannot read as a fast one. The sampler targets ten distinct passes, so a nine-receipt set fails the section's read floor rather than being averaged. The previews ran with --local so the picker's routing cost was not inside a preview-cost measure, and created no worktree and no live pointer (two of each before, two after). The readiness read was repeated under a profile to separate the landing audit from the rest of the helper, and the audit's own ancestor probes were counted, so a cheap audit is distinguished from an audit that was asked nothing.
