A worker prompt never both asks for its landing record and forbids it

The defect, reproduced

An implement prompt carried two clauses that met as a contradiction. The landing contract asked the worker to write its evidence anchor into its scope's fragment under docs/evidence/fragments/<plan>/<node-id>.html; the artifact clause said the node wrote no figure and no evidence prose. Composed at the base revision through the shared builder, both prompts — the unnamed-artifact one and the figure-named one — carried exactly two sentences mentioning the prompt's evidence prose, and neither excepted the fragment:

A small node writes one `landing:` line in its manifest, stating what changed and its
measure's figure, and writes neither evidence prose nor a figure unless its done-when
names that artifact; naming one does not permit the other.
Your done-when names no artifact, so this node writes no figure and no evidence prose.

The base-arm log records the refusal on each prompt: a non-zero exit line and failing test ids.

The fix, stated once

The landing fragment is the node's evidence anchor and is always permitted at its scope path; a figure still needs the done-when to name it; the withheld evidence prose is the prose outside the fragment. The permission is stated, not merely implied by a qualified ban: the small-node rule says the fragment is always permitted at its scope path and then withholds the prose beyond it, and both artifact clauses say the withheld prose is the prose outside the fragment. The named clause reads:

Your done-when names an artifact, so this node may write that artifact. It permits no
other figure and no evidence prose outside the landing fragment, which is always
permitted.

Gate

tests/test_a_worker_prompt_never_forbids_its_landing_record.py composes the implement prompt for a node whose done-when names no figure and for one whose done-when names a figure, through the small-node suite's own builder helpers. It asserts three things on each: the prompt carries the landing-fragment instruction; the prompt explicitly permits the landing fragment at its scope path; and every sentence that mentions the prompt's evidence prose names the fragment and permits it — so a reworded ban that names the fragment while still forbidding it is rejected, not waved through by the word alone. A mutation case states such a ban and requires the guard to refuse it. The figure-named prompt still grants its figure.

The clause-wording assertions in tests/test_brief_prompt.py (the plan-prompt snapshot) and tests/test_small_node_prompt.py are updated to the new wording. The head arm passed: 43 passed, exit 0.
