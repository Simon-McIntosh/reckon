Every surface shows the one progress figure

A plan's progress reached the surfaces by two different routes. The plan list, the project index and the discovery row carried the effort-weighted figure the section parser derives, while a graph endpoint divided shipped members by total members and rendered that percentage into the same bar-and-percent slot. Two readers of the same closure therefore saw two numbers, and neither surface could tell which one it was showing.

The graph endpoint now carries the closure's rolled-up figure under progress, computed by derive_fleet_impl_imputing_effort — the one roll-up function — over the endpoint's own member plans. roadmap.py weights nothing of its own: it hands the member plan rows to that function in both places it reports a closure, so an endpoint and the fleet summary cannot disagree. The completion counts stay counts, and shipped_fraction stays on the endpoint for readers that want the raw ratio.

The surfaces render what they are served. The graph's endpoint row paints endpoint.progress; the endpoint detail paints the plan row's served impl; and the crew flow bar paints the impl of the inventory row its bar resolved against, so a flow item and the plan card it points at agree by construction. No surface divides shipped by total.

The schedule bar names its plan once. Layout is unchanged; the duplicate slug key is gone and plan carries the slug the surface's bar readers already resolve against the inventory in the same payload. On one fixture the bar list is less than half the size it was when each bar repeated the whole inventory row it referenced.

A row holds one figure or none, never two. The graph draws its rows from the roadmap payload when the page carries one and from the plan inventory when it does not, and those two paths differ in what they know: the inventory path has no closure roll-up, because the roll-up is computed server-side. That path used to fall back to the endpoint plan's own impl, which put one member's figure in the closure's slot where a reader could not tell it from the closure's own. It now shows an em dash and leaves the bar unfilled, and never reads the endpoint plan's figure in that slot.

The crew flow item's progress readout takes a style rule beside its siblings: .r-derived-flow-progress in docs/ui/crew.css, sharing the hours readout's flex: none and opacity and taking tabular figures so the percentages line up down a lane.

The gate is tests/test_every_surface_shows_the_one_progress_figure.py: it serves one temporary project of two sectioned plans and reads the figure back through every served surface — the index row, the discovery row, the graph endpoint, the schedule bar and the fleet summary — asserting each one equals a reference computed in the test from the section's own progress and roll-up functions. The fixture is chosen so the two candidate numbers cannot be confused: the roll-up reads 0.10 where shipped-over-total reads 0.50. The file's second case asserts the graph and crew sources state the figure and derive none — endpoint.progress in the graph, plan.impl in the crew flow, an empty slot on the inventory-only path, and neither a shipped_fraction read nor an inline shipped-over-total expression.

At the merged population of the nine affected files the suite is 58 passed, EXIT=0 against 55 passed, EXIT=0 at the base revision — the three added cases are this node's two files, and no case was deleted or loosened.
