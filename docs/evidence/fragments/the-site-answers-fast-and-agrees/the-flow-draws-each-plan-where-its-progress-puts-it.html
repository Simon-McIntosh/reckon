The flow draws each plan where its progress puts it

The derived flow figure placed a bar from its plan's status and its live runs, but only the placement was computed in Python: the surface then re-derived the bar's state from the plan row, re-packed the lanes from the bars it drew, and resolved each bar against an inventory keyed by bare slug. Three readers of the same figure could therefore disagree with the schedule report and with each other, and a plan and a research document sharing a slug resolved to whichever row the surface saw last.

The derivation now decides all four facts and the surface draws them. In reckon/schedule.py, derive_schedule gives every bar a state: recorded when the plan's status is terminal (shipped, done, superseded, historical); in flight when a run serving the plan is in a live phase (working, starting, dispatched); held when the effective status is blocked or when the plan's only pointers sit in a held phase; and predicted otherwise. A pointer in any other phase sets no state at all, so a stalled or abandoned pointer neither invents a state nor moves a bar.

Position is anchored on progress. A bar that is not recorded is anchored at the reference instant, or at the latest end of its prerequisites when that is later; its done share sits behind the anchor for p · w and its remaining share runs ahead of it for (1 − p) · w, where p is the plan's effort-weighted progress and w is its wall-clock hours. A half-done eighteen-hour plan therefore spans −9 to +9 with its split at 0, and a plan whose progress has reached 1.0 ends at the anchor rather than running its full length ahead of it. A recorded bar ends where it landed, as it always has. Every bar carries its split point.

Each bar carries its plan's typed key — the nav_key from reckon/resources.py, which is the bare slug for a live plan and a type-prefixed key otherwise, so a plan and a research document that share a slug can no longer resolve to one another — and the lane index the server's best-fit packing placed it in. The packing writes the index back onto the bar, so a reader renders the packing the server chose rather than repeating it. The schedule report in reckon/roadmap.py serves the same four fields and names the lane count.

docs/ui/crew.jsx now draws what it is served and computes none of it: the status-to-state mapping, the live-run lookup and the lane packing are gone (RECORDED_FLOW_STATUSES, ACTIVE_FLOW_STATUSES, flowPackLanes, flowPlanLiveRun). flowLanes groups the served bars by the lane index they already carry, flowDonePercent shades the done share from the served start and split, and the inventory map keys on row.nav_key || row.slug so each bar resolves to its own typed row. The rows are labelled as lanes rather than as sessions, and a lane is marked live only from a bar the payload served as in flight.

The gate is tests/test_the_flow_draws_each_plan_where_its_progress_puts_it.py, nine cases over a temporary project at a fixed reference instant. Five read the derivation directly: the half-done plan spans its progress around the reference; a plan at full progress that is not recorded ends at now. A working run puts the bar in flight at the same position, and an older held pointer beside it moves neither the state nor the start, while that held pointer alone does hold the bar; an in-progress plan whose effective status is blocked reads held without its position moving; and a draft plan with a working run reads in flight. A shared slug leaves the plan's typed key on the bar and the research document no bar at all. Every bar carries a lane index and no two bars in one lane overlap. Two cases run the shipped surface over a served payload: one asserts each drawn bar's state class, position and shaded done share come from the payload (a 50% and a 25% done share, both plans in progress, only the one with a working run drawn in flight) and that the rows read as lanes; the other asserts a bar whose plan and research document share a slug draws the plan's sprint and progress and not the research row's.

The fixture takes an impl and its run a phase, so the cases assert the served state, split and typed key rather than a status-to-state mapping; the parity case now compares the served bars against the derivation on state, typed key and lane as well as position.
