Three promotions started together: every landing is far under a minute, and one of twelve met a held index lock

What was measured. A temporary origin and a temporary checkout standing in for the shared working tree, a temporary crew home, and N completed runs whose worktrees each hold one committed change. crew complete --land was started for all of them in separate processes within five milliseconds of one another, from the revision this node measured (1d16388a31, which carries the read-only-what-moved promotion checks and the landing commit's held-lock retry). Each promotion's wall clock is whole-process time from launch to exit.

The refusal, quoted. In attempt 2 the third promotion reached its landing commit while a peer held the checkout's index, and its own report reads:

could not commit the landing writes for run
'r-20261115T01000200000000-measure-run-2' in .../repository:
fatal: Unable to create '.../repository/.git/index.lock': File exists.
...
the ledger row for run ... is already written; do not re-promote.

The run was not rolled back: its ledger row was written and its pointer was kept, so the run is left delivered with its reviewed head off the primary branch until a later promotion retries it. The landing commit's retry bound (twelve attempts half a second apart, about six seconds) is shorter than the time three concurrent landing commits take on a 2,000-file checkout.

Negative control. The same driver against the code before the read-only-what-moved checks and before the landing commit's held-lock retry (aa8ab137ba^, extracted to a scratch tree): three attempts of three promotions, nine in all, every one landed and pushed, at 3.73, 3.83, 3.89 s (three-file checkout) and 6.49 s (2,000-file checkout). No promotion took minutes and no lock collision reproduced in those nine. The collision was therefore observed once in four head attempts and never in three pre-revision attempts, so the negative-control half of the done-when is recorded as not reproduced rather than as reproduced.

Verdict. Every landing that completed did so in at most 16.47 s, well under the sixty-second bound. The done-when's second clause — none fails to land — did not hold in one of four attempts: one of twelve promotions lost the shared index to a peer at its landing commit and was left with its head off the primary branch. Both clauses were also measured on the pre-revision code, where the collision did not reproduce in nine promotions.

A separate defect seen in every attempt. Each promotion that landed and pushed still exited 1 with “the reviewed head landed and was pushed, but the worktree was withheld: its HEAD is not reachable from the integration revision”, because the workspace-outcome release step judges the worktree's head against a revision that does not carry it before the land step merges it. This reproduces at the base revision in tests/test_one_call_lands_a_run.py (assert 1 == 0), so it is not this node's change; a successful landing cannot be judged by the command's exit status until it is repaired.
