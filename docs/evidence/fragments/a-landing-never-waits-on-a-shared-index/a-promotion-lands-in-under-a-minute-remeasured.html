Re-measured with the released-lock retry in place: twelve of twelve promotions landed, the slowest in 15.31 s

What was measured. A temporary bare origin, a temporary checkout standing in for the shared working tree, a temporary crew home, and three completed runs whose worktrees each hold one committed change. Each checkout carried 2,000 bulk files so three landing commits would contend for the shared checkout's index. crew complete --land was started for all three in separate processes within a few milliseconds of one another, and each promotion's wall clock is whole-process time from launch to exit. The harness is measure_promotions_concurrent.py in the run directory; the same driver ran both arms.

Head arm — 4 attempts of three promotions, at 1b8a0d372a. That revision carries the landing commit's released-lock retry (bcc56fcc9c): a git call that failed because this checkout's index.lock existed is retried up to the 130-attempt, 0.5-second bound even when the lock has already been released. Twelve promotions, twelve landed and pushed, none refused, none rolled back.

Verdict on the head arm. Every promotion landed and its land commit is reachable from the origin's main. The slowest was 15.31 s, well under the sixty-second bound, and no promotion refused or rolled back. The done-when holds at this revision.

The collision did not reproduce on the comparison arm, and the arm is known to lack the fix. Absence needs the instrument shown to see something known present. A deterministically held lock was used as that check, since a natural collision is a race and cannot be commanded at the exact pre-lock window:

  • The comparison tree genuinely predates the retry. The landed test tests/test_a_landing_commit_retries_a_released_lock.py passes 5 cases at the head tree. Copied into the bcc56fcc9c^ tree, its released-lock case fails with Right contains one more item: 2 — the retry did not happen — while the other four pass. So the comparison arm's helper still returns the failure after one attempt when the lock has been released, exactly the defect the retry repairs.
  • The harness records a refusal when one happens. With a second process holding the checkout's index.lock for 150 s before a single promotion, the promotion was refused at 148.64 s and rolled back, with the report reading “Another writer has held the checkout's index lock for 83.2s, past this landing's 64.5s retry bound (130 attempts 0.50s apart)”. A refusal is therefore recorded by this driver, not silently counted as a landing. (An exploratory arm holding the lock for 80 s landed at 81.85 s, once the holder removed the lock as the final retry began.)

So the comparison arm's twelve landings are twelve attempts in which no peer held the shared index long enough to refuse a landing commit; the run records the collision as not reproduced in twelve attempts rather than as evidence about this node's change.

A separate defect, present in every one of the twenty-four promotions. Every promotion that landed and pushed still exited 1 — all of them, in both arms — with “the reviewed head landed and was pushed, but the worktree was withheld: its HEAD is not reachable from the integration revision”. The workspace-outcome release step judges the worktree's head against a revision that does not carry it before the land step merges it. It reproduces at the comparison revision as well, so it is not this node's change and no landing can be judged by the command's exit status until it is repaired.
