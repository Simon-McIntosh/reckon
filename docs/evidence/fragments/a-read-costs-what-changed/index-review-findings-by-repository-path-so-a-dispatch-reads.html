A findings read indexes the store instead of reading it

What changed. reckon/crew/findings.py keeps an index of finding rows per repository path, keyed on the review store's stat identity through reckon/file_memo.py. Each review directory's record identities are memoised on the directory's own signature, so an unchanged directory costs one stat and no record read; a record whose own stat identity moved is re-read and only that record is; a record that vanished leaves with its directory's rescan. Directory stats are taken concurrently, because a serial pass over two thousand directories is latency-bound on the shared filesystem. The rows, grouping, recurrence and artefact handling are unchanged.

The first call is unchanged in kind. A process that has never read the store still reads every record once; what the index removes is the repeat. Within one long-lived process — the MCP server, or a dispatch that asks once per node — the second and later reads cost a stat sweep and open no record.

The check

tests/test_findings_are_indexed_by_path.py holds three facts against a temporary store, counting record opens through the store's own reader: a second call after no change opens no record; after one record is rewritten, that record is the only one opened and its new rows are returned; a deleted record's rows are gone. Its negative control drops the memo, and all three cases then open records — the unchanged-store case opens every one.

The finding population the change touches is green on both arms: 51 passed at the base revision, 54 at the head (the three new cases included), no added failure.
