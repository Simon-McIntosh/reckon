A land refuses a head behind the run's worktree before it merges

What changed

_land_a_promoted_run in reckon/crew_run_commands.py now reads the head of the run's worktree before it calls merge.land. The worktree path comes from the record the promotion wrote, falling back to the live pointer captured before the promotion deleted it. When the tree is still a directory and its head is neither the reviewed head being merged nor an ancestor of it, the land is refused: nothing is merged, nothing is pushed, and steps.land names both revisions alongside the reckon crew merge command for the worktree's head, which is the revision a reader can land instead. The check reuses the release step's own instrument — merge._is_ancestor against HEAD — so the two read one reachability fact rather than two definitions of "behind".

The release step already read that fact, but only after the merge and its push. In the landing this section exists for, the land merged 956b6e5d while the run's worktree sat at 7ffee8b2, and the commit the worktree held reached no reader. Reading the same comparison one step earlier is what turns a post-hoc report into a refusal.

An absent worktree has no head to compare, so the guard is silent and the land proceeds unchanged; a worktree whose head cannot be resolved is likewise left to the release step's existing verdict rather than refused on a guess.

What this does not do

The check lives in the land step of crew complete. The standalone reckon crew merge verb still merges the head it is given without consulting any worktree, because it is handed a revision rather than a run. Promotion's own choice of revision is a different section's repair; this node assumes the promotion reached it with a head and stops that head from being merged ahead of work the run still holds.
