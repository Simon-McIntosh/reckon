A completed reload is not reported as a stop

The follower reported producer <project> stopped during its reload begun <stamp> whenever a poll found the seat not live while the seat record still carried reload_started_at. That marker is written by the producer just before it replaces its own image, and the replacement clears it by rewriting the record, so a poll landing inside the replacement reads the record mid-write and finds no producer. The branch now reports only a reload whose window has elapsed, or one the producer's own log records as failed.

Which liveness reading turns false

Measured, not inferred. A real crew watch producer was armed from a copied source tree and forced to reload in place while a poller read runs.producer_live and each reading it consults every 5 ms: 2000 polls across the reload, and the answer was False on none of them — the pid reading and record_process_alive stayed live, and the record kept its pid and its marker. The reading that does turn false is the transition stream's freshness (_seat_stream_fresh). A record that is momentarily unreadable or names another host is judged by exactly that reading, so producer_live answers False for the reload's whole length. A record rewrite is truncate then write, and a concurrent reader sees the empty window in 156 of 173 rewrites (156 empty reads in 199,993).

The module window clause is wider than this node

tests/test_every_module_fits_a_worker_window.py fails at both arms, and this node cannot close it: 13 modules breach the 50,000 bound at the base revision, and reckon/crew_follow_commands.py is one of them at 50,277 tokens before this change and 51,026 after it (+749 for eight helpers and the report branch). The plan expected the follower at about 42,400 after the reloader family moved out; landings since then have grown it past the bound, so the clause needs a node of its own whose scope covers those modules.
