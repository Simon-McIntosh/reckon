The follower whose arming shell is gone ends the stream, not the pipe

Two crew follow processes writing into one grep end the stream because each follower's own arming process is gone, not because the pipe broke. A follower fixes its owner once, as the pid and kernel start time of the process that armed it (follower_owner(), reckon/crew/follower_registration.py), and _check_consumer() in reckon/crew_follow_commands.py ends the arming when that owner is no longer alive — committed blob d325cb9fe1, lines 1718–1719: if owner_pid <= 1 or runs.process_alive(owner_pid) is not True: then consumer_gone = True; return. The wait loop at line 2164 (while not _stopped() and not lifetime_elapsed and not consumer_gone and not settled:) then leaves, the generator ends without the settle row, and the process returns 0. It prints nothing: that path exists so a pane whose reader has gone does not accumulate orphans.

Two followers for one project cannot be written into one pipe in the foreground, so the shell writes them as background jobs and exits at once — bash -c 'crew follow -p P -s A & crew follow -p P -s B &' | grep .. Both followers are then reparented while the pipe reader is still reading, and the owner check ends each of them on its next wait pass (0.1 s). Their exit records name the reason ended — the normal-return path, exit status 0 — and the reader then sees EOF and exits. A single project-wide follower has no such short-lived intermediate shell, its owner is the shell that is running the command, and it stays up; that is the asymmetry the report observed.

The fix, as a done-when a later section can carry: a follower whose arming process has gone but whose stdout still has a reader stays, and it ends only when writing to that stdout fails, so two followers backgrounded by a short-lived shell into one grep keep streaming for the reader's lifetime. Closing it either writes the sample row and catches EPIPE from _echo_follow_line's flush as the end condition, or asks whether stdout is still open to a reader instead of whether the arming pid is alive; the owner check keeps its present job for a follower no reader holds.

The claim is guarded by the suite, not by this record alone. tests/test_follower_checks_its_owner.py and tests/test_orphaned_follower_releases.py run the real follower command against a temporary crew home and require a follower whose owner is gone to leave and free its registration. That pair was measured three ways — at the pinned revision, at the tree carrying this fragment, and against a scratch copy of the pinned revision whose _check_consumer() returns before it reads its owner.
