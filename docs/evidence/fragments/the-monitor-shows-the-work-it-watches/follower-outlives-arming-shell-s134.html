A re-parented follower keeps streaming while a reader holds its pipe

The consumer check in reckon/crew_follow_commands.py now asks whether the follower's output still has a reader before it treats a dead owner as an ending. _stdout_has_reader(owner_pid) answers for the descriptor's kind: a socket or a terminal carries a reader by construction and answers yes; a regular file answers no, so an orphan writing into one still ends and frees its registration; a pipe first takes the cheap POLLERR probe, the same condition that makes the next write fail with EPIPE, and then follows the read end to the pids holding it through follower_registration._pipe_reader_pids. The follower keeps streaming only when one of those readers is a process other than its current parent, and re-parenting is judged by comparing the parent against the owner recorded by follower_owner(), never by the parent's liveness — under a live subreaper the parent is a running process that never armed the follower. A live parent holding an unread pipe for a dead armed owner is a takeover, and keeps the owner-only ending. The verdict is remembered once won, so the /proc walk runs at most once.

The new test arms two session followers as background jobs of a shell that exits at once, writing into one reader, and asserts that both are alive 2.5 s later with each still holding a registration naming the process the shell started. It then closes the reader and asserts both followers end and both registrations are free. The base revision ends both inside the span; the owner-only check restored as a negative control fails the first case; the two sibling guard files still pass, each arming its follower with a regular-file stdout.
