Roadmap reports section-level edges

The roadmap's critical path and blocking edges report a section-level edge when a plan is held by one section of another: the edge's ref carries the target anchor (plan#sN) rather than the plan name alone, and a plan-level depends_on keeps its plan-level edge.

What changed

reckon/roadmap.py resolves a gate's outcome source into dependency rows and graph edges. Three authored forms are read:

  • a declared outcome source — gating_plan — carrying the #section anchor;
  • a #section ref written into the gate's own measure or evidence text;
  • a section named in words beside the plan it belongs to, as in cut-cell-current-attribution section 6 or a bare sections 1 and 2 carrying the gate's own plan by context. This form is marked "source": "gate-text" and binds only when the named section exists on the named plan, so a passing mention never invents an edge.

Each resolved ref produces a dependency row carrying the waiting section, the target ref with its anchor, and the gate it came from. Those rows join the raw view's depends_on, section_depends_on and section_readiness; an unsatisfied one also enters the path graph, and every path in the raw view's open_paths — the critical one included — gains an edges list naming the section and gate on each step. A plan-level depends_on yields no entry: its edge reads with an empty section and gate.

Read at head through the CLI, with the project mounted from a copy of the live plans outside the write fence:

  python -c "from reckon.cli import main; main()" \
  roadmap --project nova --json-output

Both gates carry their dependency as prose in the authored files: no data-gating-plan, no plan-section-depends-on and no section data-links exist in the mounted corpus. The gate-text provenance marks exactly that: the section was read from the gate's own words, not from a machine ref. Authoring the ref as gating_plan is the durable form and resolves the same edge with "source": "declared".
