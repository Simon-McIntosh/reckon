A full fleet job kills a worker before its coordinator

The fleet job holds every process in one memory cgroup, and with no declared ordering the kernel killed whichever process was largest — the station at 3.0–6.7 GiB and the admit service at 2.7–6.1 GiB, each kill dropping the pass in progress. A coordination service now takes its place in the kernel's OOM ordering before it becomes the service, and every dispatched worker is raised above it.

What changed

  • reckon/crew/oom_priority.py — a service asks for -900, a worker for +500; a refused lowering falls back to the value the process already held and the caller gets the value the kernel actually holds, read back rather than echoed.
  • reckon/crew/fleet_supervisor.py — run_declared_service claims the service side before execvp, so the adjustment survives the exec, and logs the reading.
  • reckon/crew/dispatch_launch.py — both worker spawn sites raise the child's adjustment after the spawn, which an unprivileged parent may always do, and which the child keeps across the exec into its harness and passes to its own children.
  • tests/test_coordination_services_survive_a_full_job.py — reads the ordering from processes that were really started: a service through the supervisor's service mode and a worker through dispatch's detached spawn.

The two-hour memory measure: not produced here

The section's measure asks for each service's resident memory every ten minutes over two hours of live passes, every reading under 1 GiB. That window cannot be produced inside this node's sixty-minute dispatch, and the section's own wording places it after this section and §71 land. The ordering it protects is in place and guarded by the test above; the resident-memory figure is owed by the measurement node that opens the window, and this record states that rather than quoting one.
