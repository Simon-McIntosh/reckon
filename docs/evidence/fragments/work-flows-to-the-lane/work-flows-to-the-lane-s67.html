What the instrument cannot read, stated

Neither admission instrument retains a four-hour window. The crew-home verdict log is trimmed to its last mebibyte and held 7 passes when read at 07:07Z; the admit service's log is trimmed to its last four mebibytes and began at 08:15:02Z when first read. The window was therefore measured from a spool: a copy loop ran every fifteen minutes from 06:52Z to 12:10Z, and the wait probe took a copy on every poll. The spool holds 560 passes spanning 2026-11-22T06:52:42Z to 12:02:23Z, the in-window figure is read from it beside both live sources, and coverage is continuous: the largest interval between consecutive passes inside the window is 279.8 s, and every 15-minute bucket holds between 17 and 44 passes. The spool is 20 MB and lives in the run directory, not the repository.

The identity figure is a reading of a live directory at the window's close, and the census reports it as such (reads_live_state: true). A positive control ran first: with 150 synthetic exec-*.identity files placed in a temporary claims directory, the same code reported 150, so the count of 1 is an absence of others rather than a blind filter.

The third limit is the run store's pruning, described above: a mean or a median over a window older than the retention horizon is a reading of what survived, not of what ran. Any later reader comparing this record's control row to §9's own record will find different numbers, and both are correct for the population each was computed over. The population is reported beside every figure for that reason.

The fourth limit applies to the current window too, and this record met it: a run's completion and landing records are written when the run finishes its turns, so a window read two minutes after it closes can miss a run that completes inside it. The first reading here had four implement runs and the settled one has five, and the fifth moved the median from 19.7 to 31.3 minutes. A window's figures are therefore not final at its close; they settle over the minutes that follow, and the only reliable statement is the population each reading was computed over — which is why both readings are on this page and in the committed history.

The promotion median rests on a cohort of five implement runs, settled from the four the first reading saw. It is the whole cohort that completed in the window — none lacked a landing record — but five runs is a small base, and the fifth run moved the median by more than eleven minutes, which is the clearest illustration of that; the p90 beside it (96.2 min) is the honest companion figure.

Verdict

Two of the section's four figures hold. No admission pass held a request on a claim under a shared root, and the claims directory held one identity file at the window's close. The mean of 1.15 does not hold, and it cannot hold in this window: twelve dispatches over four hours bound it far below twelve. That is a reading of how much work the lane was given, not of whether the lane can carry more — the same instrument reads 23.55 over §9's window when the ledger holds that window's runs. The promotion median does not hold either on the settled cohort: 31.3 minutes over five implement runs against a bound of 20, where the first reading of four runs gave 19.7 minutes and met it. The fifth run completed at 11:17Z and was promoted at 12:07Z, so its record landed after the first pass; on the whole cohort the bound is missed. That is an improvement on the 49.1 minutes the same gate measured on 17 November and still outside the bound. Of the two figures that hold, the shared-claim clause is met while a stale claim on a peer plan's figure directory did hold two requests for the entire window; that hold is reported under outside this node's scope, where releasing it belongs. One earlier measurement of this same section, on the night of 17 November, is recorded beside this one as wftl-a-second-nights-window-meets-the-landing-targets.html; the two agree on the shape of the result, and the promotion latency has improved from 49.1 to 31.3 minutes while remaining outside the bound.
