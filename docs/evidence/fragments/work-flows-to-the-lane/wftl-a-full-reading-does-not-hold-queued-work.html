A full reading at one instant does not hold queued work

What changed. An admission pass read the router's worker allowance once, and a reading of full at that instant held every queued request until the next pass — 45 to 185 seconds on the fleet, while a full reading clears in seconds because a worker holds a router request for about a quarter of its run. A candidate held for an exhausted allowance is now deferred to the pass's second sweep. There, once per session per pass, the pass re-reads the lane, bounded by the pass's own launch deadline and by the 30 seconds the measure names, and replaces the grant it cached with the figure the lane actually published. A paused lane is never waited on: a pause is a decision, not an instant, so the pass takes its reading, holds every request and moves on.

The measure. A temporary crew home holds twelve queued requests and a stand-in router that republishes a real lane document before every read: full for the first five seconds of the first pass, then open with a slot for every request. The test reads all twelve launched, and the last launch lands well inside the 60-second bound the measure puts on the cohort. Two companions hold the guard's other edges: a paused lane reads no launch and takes no pause at all, and a lane that stays full spends only the pass budget it was given rather than the whole wait.
