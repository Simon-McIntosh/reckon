The follow loop holds a half-written record until its newline arrives

What the loop now does

A producer appends one JSON record per line to the watch stream, and may still be writing the last one when the follow loop reads it. readline returns the bytes written so far with no trailing newline and no error: admitting that fragment as a whole record delivers the record truncated, parses it through the legacy fallback, and the producer's completion then arrives as a second fragment. Snapping the opening offset to the last newline narrows the window but does not close it — the loop can still open on a record whose newline has not landed.

The loop now reads through read_whole_line, which leaves the handle at the line's start when the line has no newline, returns nothing for it, and lets the fuller line arrive on a later read. The record is delivered once and whole, and the recorded stream offset only ever lands on a line boundary. The boundary probe that gives the reader its opening offset (_follow_boundary) reads backward in chunks of at most 64 KiB rather than loading the whole stream to find one newline.
