An admission pass reads a plan's review evidence once

What changed. Every queued request an admission pass considers was judged against its own fresh reading of the same plan: its review coverage, its section digests, its parsed state and its document contract were each taken per request, and the dispatch gate was asked once per request even when two requests asked the same question of the same section. A pass with n queued requests on one plan paid for n readings of that plan. In reckon/crew/queue_admission.py, plan_review_pass() now holds one record per plan for the length of a pass, keyed by the plan file's content digest together with the identities and digests of the stored review records the judgment reads — so a review stored during the pass changes the key and the next decision recomputes, rather than reusing a reading that has been overtaken. The dispatch-gate fallback is memoized in the same record, keyed by the section the request builds and by the gate's own exemption answer.

Wait resolution has its own stage. admit_once times advance_waiting_requests and _release_plan_review_holds into waits_seconds, and _record_stage_timings subtracts that figure before computing launch_seconds. A pass that spends its time advancing waits no longer reports that time as launch cost, and the verdict record carries the wait figure beside the other three stages.

Measured

Five cases over a temporary crew home, each pinning one half of the behaviour: three requests on one plan cost one coverage, one digests and one state read; three uncovered requests cost two dispatch-gate reads (one for the two implement requests on a shared section, one for the review request's exemption); a review stored mid-pass changes the key and forces a recompute; every request's verdict still matches the reading it replaces, including the uncovered, review-removed and unanswered-finding cases; and a half-second wait stage reports waits_seconds >= 0.5 with launch_seconds < 0.1.
