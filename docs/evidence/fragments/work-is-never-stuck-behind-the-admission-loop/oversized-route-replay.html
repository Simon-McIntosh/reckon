Why an oversized node did not reach the reserved lane, and which reasons the rules now keep

The refusals reproduce; the attribution the record carries does not. replay_oversized_route.py rebuilds node an-admission-pass-reads-each-project-once from the coordinator's own command line, resolves the flight configuration from the four layers committed at the moment, runs reckon.crew.picker.snapshot.candidates unchanged over those inputs, and reads the picker's own _fallback_choice for the answer. Both attempts are refused again, and the exclusion rows are not the ones the record holds. The 02:29:07Z attempt now excludes six rows — four claude reserved-lane, one clive (the local wrapper) reserved-lane, and one native in-harness-backend — where the record holds eleven, and the 02:34:43Z attempt excludes nothing at all: its three codex candidates are offered with the reservation beside them.

The two rules that moved the reasons. The claude reservation is enforced, and it reaches the local wrapper: reckon/crew/dispatch_picker.py:reservation_family resolves a backend's policy family through its dialect and keeps a wrapper on its harness's reservation, so clive — a local button under a claude harness — is a child of the claude policy. For that family the withheld reason becomes an exclusion (enforce_reservation = policy_family == "claude" in the candidate loop), which is why clive now carries reserved-lane instead of the context-fit and competence verdicts the record holds: the reservation is judged on the policy's own terms before the scan's verdicts are ever consulted. A reservation outside that family is evidence, not an exclusion: _reserved_lane_evidence returns the policy's words as a preference beside the candidate, so the lane stays offered and the decision the policy describes is left to whoever weighs preferences — the router, or the fallback when there is none. That is why the codex lane's three recorded reserved-lane rows no longer appear, and why the lane attempt now offers all three of its models.

What each input cost the attribution. Recorded: the node definition (the dispatch command line), the plan section and its declared capability (a-read-costs-what-changed §6 declares data-capability-reasoning="deep"), the context estimate (234021 in both pick-timing rows), and the flight configuration, reconstructed from shipped and project at 9bdaea5ab6 and the host layer at dotfiles@2892816. Unrecorded, reconstructed: the budget window reading. A pick-timing row keeps each reason code and the context estimate and nothing of the budget verdict the reason was judged on, so any attribution resting on the window percentage is conditional on that reading. The replay therefore runs both branches — no reading, and a fresh reading at 72% — and both produce the same rows, so the refusal does not rest on which one it was. Unrecorded, not decisive: the lane document, which feeds worker slots and congestion and is read by no exclusion. Never reached: the serving probe. Every recorded row was withheld before the probe, and the codex candidates that are now offered are weighed by the fallback, which spends no availability evidence either, so no probe result entered any answer here and availability reads not-probed.

Why the answer is read from the fallback, and what the fallback does with an offered reservation. _fallback_choice drops every offered candidate that is reserved without a lift in force — an unlifted reservation is the orchestrators' own capacity and is never where the fallback puts a node — then prefers a local candidate with room and then the default backend. On attempt-1 the local candidate is the withheld clive, so nothing is left; on attempt-4 the only candidates are the three codex models, all reserved without a lift. Both attempts therefore return None, which the picker records as refuse with ; default-backend-ineligible appended to its reason. The replay does not ask a router: a router's answer is one model's judgment of one moment and is nowhere in the record, so asserting it would report on whichever endpoint answered. The limit that leaves: with the codex lane now offered, a live dispatch on these inputs would ask the router and could place the node there, so what is reproduced is the answer the policy gives when no router is consulted — the one place the policy's own words decide, and it decides the same way twice. What the record's rows assert about the lane — that the window term removed it before any router saw it — no longer holds, and that is the finding, not a reproduction gap.

Which recorded rows are gone, and what replaced them. Attempt-1's record carries eleven rows and the rules keep six: the six superseded rows are clive's two context-fit and one competence, and the three codex reserved-lane rows, and the row that arrived in their place is clive's own reserved-lane. Attempt-4's record carries three rows and the rules keep none: all three are the codex reserved-lane rows, and no row replaces them. The report prints each superseded row beside the rule that removed it, so a reader comparing the rules against the record is not left to infer which of the two moved.

What that changes about the node's story. The record was read as: the codex window term withheld the lane before the lane-attempt dispatch could reach it, so the dispatch had to be replaced by the separate free lane the work landed on. Under the rules now in force the window term no longer removes the lane: it travels beside three offered candidates, and the only path that still refuses is one with no router, where the fallback will not spend an unlifted reservation. So the lane is reachable by preference and not by right — a dispatch naming it gets a router question that an oversized node may still lose, and the refusal moves from "the policy withheld the lane" to "the router was not asked" or "the router preferred elsewhere, or the offers were not spendable". Any later attribution of this node's routing has to read the pick-timing row's decision_source to know which of the two happened, because the two are no longer distinguishable from the exclusion rows alone.

Measure: the replay log records two attempts replayed, six exclusion rows for the no-lane attempt and none for the lane attempt, every recorded row the rules no longer emit printed with its rule, both outcomes reproduced, and it ends on a bare EXIT=0 line. tests/test_oversized_route_replay.py runs the same script and holds the record's own rows beside the rows the rules keep, so a change that stops withholding a reserved lane, or that lets the fallback place a liftless reservation, fails there rather than being discovered by a reader comparing prose.
