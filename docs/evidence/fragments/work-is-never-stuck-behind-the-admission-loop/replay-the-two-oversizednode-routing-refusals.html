The two oversized-node routing refusals, replayed

A read-only replay of the two recorded pick refusals, and the account of what decided them. replay_oversized_route.py sits beside this record and rebuilds each attempt from the dispatch that made it: the node from the coordinator's command line, the flight configuration from the four layers committed at the moment, and the pick-timing row that recorded the refusal. It then asks reckon.crew.picker the same question in process, with no routing or competence verdict stubbed.

Both refusals reproduce exactly. The 02:29:07Z attempt replays to its eleven exclusion rows — four claude reserved-lane, clive's two context-fit and one competence, three codex reserved-lane and one native in-harness-backend — in the picker's own order; the 02:34:43Z attempt replays to its three codex reserved-lane rows alone. Both read refuse from no-eligible-candidates; default-backend-ineligible, and the run exits 0.

Three findings. The reserved-lane tier floor was met, so the term that withheld codex is the policy's window term, not its difficulty term — and the record does not carry the window reading, so that attribution is conditional on a reconstructed input, which the replay shows does not change the row set either way. The later attempt's local lane was absent because the dispatch named --backend codex, and naming a lane narrows the picker's offering to that lane's backends alone. And the oversize waiver is unreachable on any configuration declaring an in-harness backend: it requires every unreserved candidate to be excluded on context fit alone, and the in-harness candidate's reason never is.

The per-attempt account, the input-by-input provenance, the file-and-line citations for each path that withheld a lane, and what each of the waiver, the candidate scan and the dispatch reroute would have to change are in the replay report.
