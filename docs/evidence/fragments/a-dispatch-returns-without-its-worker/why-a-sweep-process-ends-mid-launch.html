Repair — proposed as a plan section

A launch span is dated and bounded. Why. A review launch that blocks between its claim and its attempt record holds the claim indefinitely: no record says who holds it, the run reads as in flight to the very dispatch that would repair it, and only a coordinator who notices can discard it by hand. Measured three times in two days on this host (01:38Z on 9 November; four instances on 8 November), each cleared only by a hand crew discard. Deliverable. The claim record carries the launching pid, host, launched_at and the step reached, so a stranded launch names its holder. The span from the claim's publication to _prepare_attempt_records is bounded by a deadline; on expiry the claim is released, the run directory removed and a refusal recorded naming the step it reached, so a blocked launch becomes a recorded refusal rather than a stranded run. The follower runs its sweep on its own thread whatever its arming deadline, so a blocked sweep cannot stall the pane's delivery. Done when a new tests/test_a_launch_span_is_dated_and_bounded.py shows, in a temporary crew home, that a launch held inside the span past the bound releases its claim, removes its run directory and records a refusal naming the step; a second case shows the follower still delivering rows while its sweep is held; and claim.json published at the base of the span names pid, host and step. Negative controls: removing the bound leaves the claim held and the run directory kept; removing the always-threaded sweep stalls delivery, quoted in the log. The test files importing reckon.crew.dispatch add no failure against the base revision, run one arm at a time with at most -n 4, with logs under the run directory. Ruff check and ruff format --check add no findings on the staged paths. Write paths: reckon/crew/dispatch.py, reckon/crew/dispatch_sessions.py, reckon/crew_follow_commands.py and the new test.
