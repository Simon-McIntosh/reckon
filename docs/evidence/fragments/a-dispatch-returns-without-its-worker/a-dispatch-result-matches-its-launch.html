A dispatch result matches its launch

A refused dispatch returned its refusal after it had already launched — leaving a live pointer, a worktree or a running worker behind, which a reader takes for a run whose process died without a manifest. Three mechanisms produced such a refusal, and all three are now regression-guarded by tests/test_dispatch_result_matches_launch.py. Separately, the contract-validation refusal returned ok: false with error: null, carrying its reason only under validation.findings, so a caller keying on error read a failed contract as a dispatch that would proceed; every ok: false the dispatch command emits now carries error and detail as non-empty strings.

What changed

  • reckon/cli.py composes the dry-run contract-validation verdict as a refusal with error: "contract-validation" and a detail that names the failed properties and their findings, rather than emitting ok: false with neither key. The findings stay structured under validation.
  • reckon/cli.py adds a non-empty detail to the three competence-refusal emits on the dispatch and shadow commands, which carried error but no detail.
  • tests/test_dispatch_result_matches_launch.py drives a stub-backed dispatch in a temporary crew home through each post-launch refusal, the survival check, an unresolvable --member, and every ok: false the dispatch command emits, live and dry-run alike.
