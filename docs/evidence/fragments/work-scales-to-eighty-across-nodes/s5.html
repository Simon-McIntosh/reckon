The station tick at 230 live runs: 20 ticks, both arms

The instrument. tests/measure_station_tick_at_230_runs.py builds a temporary crew home: four mounted projects, each a real repository, and 230 live runs spread across them, each with a 1 MiB stream. Before each of 20 consecutive ticks it appends 8 KiB to every run's stream on the all_debug arms — 32 KiB on the run's own node — as a live fleet's producers do; after each tick it records the tick's duration and the station's resident set size, read from the process's own VmRSS. A reading that reaches 30 seconds or 2 GiB makes the run exit non-zero.

Why this record is qualified and not passed. The section's done-when names this record's control: the same measure at the base revision, recording ticks over 30 seconds. What the section does have is the repair's mechanism, shown to fire, and its bound met at two populations on two nodes.

Why the time control cannot be produced from this measure. Two facts, both measured here rather than reasoned from the code. First, the measure appends to every run's stream before every tick — 8 KiB on the all_debug arms, 32 KiB on the run's own node, each arm's JSON recording its own arrival_bytes — so every stream moves on every tick and the memo this node repaired has nothing to skip; the arm where the memo's effect shows — a quiet fleet — is the one the counter measured, 874 opens before and 0 after, and not the timed arms, whose medians are 2.028 s and 0.816 s. Second, on the same population the base's cold first tick costs 9.166 s against a steady 2.027 s, so the cold tick is the dearer of the two. A population whose steady base tick reached 30 s would put the shared cold tick — run by both revisions, since both decode the backlog once — at roughly twice that, over the same bound the section sets for every tick. So no population of this measure can show the base over 30 seconds while the head stays under it: the control the section names and the bound the section sets cannot both hold for one measure of this shape.
