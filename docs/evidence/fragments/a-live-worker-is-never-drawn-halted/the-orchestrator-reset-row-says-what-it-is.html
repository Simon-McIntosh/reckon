Landing record — the orchestrator reset row says what it is

A usage-limit reset is recorded against a budget group and reaches every pane as one broadcast row. It is not a state change of a run, but the pane mapped it onto the working word like any other row, so the lead's announcement drew limit claude-orchestrators new → working. Nothing in that line said the row was a reset, and nothing said a stopped session should continue, so each reader had to work both out — three such rows cost a coordinator three checks, and two other sessions reported ignoring the row entirely. Reproduced at the base revision through the real producer path: 01:38:16 limit claude-orchestrators new → working – 0w 0h 0d 0q.

The reset row is now drawn as one clause in place of the transition: ticker.Painter.render returns to _reset_line for a limit-reset row, which prints limit reset · <group> · <clock> · <who may continue> and appends the announcer's recorded reason when the record carries one. The same event now reads 01:46:55 limit reset · claude-orchestrators · five_hour · orchestrator sessions may continue · the lead reports the usage limits have reset – 0w 0h 0d 0q.

The closing clause is chosen by the group rather than fixed, because a reset recorded against a worker lane's wallet does not free an orchestrator session and a row that says it does is the claim this row exists to make trustworthy. ticker.group_is_paced asks the resolved flight config which groups a backend draws on: a group it names is a worker lane's wallet and the row says that lane may continue; a group no backend declares, once some wallet is declared, is the orchestrator wallet and the row says orchestrator sessions may continue; a config that declares no wallet at all, or cannot be read, leaves the group unknown and the row says only that sessions stopped at the usage limit may continue. The answer never comes from a group's name, and the pane caches it per group because it costs a config resolution.

The headline, the group and the instruction come from the row's own fields: the row's to_state names the reset, its node carries the budget group, and the instruction is the pane's words rather than the producer's. Only the clock and the announcer's reason are read out of the sentence the producer composes onto the row's free-text field (lift_watch._reset_detail), which the renderer drops for every other row because no other row needs it. A sentence matching neither pattern costs the row neither fact silently — it narrows the clause to the headline, group and instruction rather than blanking the row.

The carriage, the once-per-pane claim and the reset family's pane memory are untouched: ticker.stream_carries still carries a limit-reset row by its word, the pane's shared claim still keys on the event id in lift_id, and a reset still reads and writes no lift-group memory. Only the words changed. The clause is cut to the pane like every other cell, so a long reason cannot wrap the row: at the pane's own minimum width the rendered line measures within that width and carries no newline.

tests/test_the_orchestrator_reset_row_says_what_it_is.py holds seven checks and drives the real producer path — budget_reset.mark_used into lift_watch.sweep_resets across two project streams, through the real follower_row_path claim — so a measurement counts the line a pane prints rather than a fixture's opinion of it. The checks are: the recorded orchestrator reset draws once and its line contains limit reset, the group, the clock and may continue and contains no arrow at all; the recorded reason reaches the line; a detected reset that records no announcer still carries the instruction and shows no announced by clause; a second sweep announces nothing again while a second pane still draws the event once; and the row stays on one line at ticker.MIN_WIDTH; a reset recorded under a group a configured backend draws on says that lane may continue and carries no orchestrator claim; and a config declaring no wallet whatsoever leaves the row naming no lane at all. The temporary crew home is isolated, it carries a declared host layer so the group reading is a fact of the test rather than of the machine, and the fixture asserts the real claim directory is untouched afterwards.
