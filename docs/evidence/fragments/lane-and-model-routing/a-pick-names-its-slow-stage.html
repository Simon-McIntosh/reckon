a-pick-names-its-slow-stage — a pick records each stage's time and names the stage a deadline ran out in

A dispatch-path pick runs a known sequence of stages — the ledger records, the verdict inputs, the budget snapshot, the context estimate, and the Jev call — all under one wait, PICKER_DISPATCH_TIMEOUT_SECONDS (5 s) in reckon/crew/dispatch_picker.py. When that wait expired the recorded selection read fallback_reason: "timeout" whatever had actually spent the budget, so every expiry read as a Jev timeout though most of the pick is spent before Jev: over 2026-10-12/13, 91 picks fell back as timeout while Jev itself answered in 0.3–0.6 s. dispatch_picker_selection now times every stage and carries the elapsed milliseconds of all five on the returned selection under stage_ms. When the deadline expires it names the stage still running — fallback_reason: "timeout in budget-snapshot" — and records the same stage_ms map with None for the stage that did finish and the finished stages' times beside it. The three dispatcher-scope inputs are marked while they run (through build_picker_inputs), so a slow input is attributed to itself; the context estimate and the Jev call are timed but not marked, because neither is a single dispatch stage a reader can act on and an expiry inside the opaque picker call is the one reading that legitimately stays bare. reckon/crew/picker/outcomes.py classifies a staged timeout by its stage (a staged reason summarises under the key timeout:<stage>) while a bare timeout remains its own count, so a report shows which stages spend the pick's budget rather than collapsing them into one.
