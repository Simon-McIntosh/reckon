A reserved lane reads the window its account publishes, and any lift releases it

The account surface reports one operative window and names it primary whatever its length, so both of the clocks the picker knew by name read not_published. _reserved_five_hour_utilisation read only clocks.five_hour, returned nothing, and _reserved_lane_exclusion treated nothing as no headroom, so codex was withheld on a reading that was four seconds old. Separately, only a credits lift released the window term, and a session lift governs its own session over a global one, so granting the stronger lift removed the release the weaker one gave.

What changed

  • reckon/pace_report.py: fill_window_clock is the one reader of a group's fill window. It returns the observed five-hour clock, or -- when neither named clock is observed -- the group's observed operative window from its allowance (utilisation, observed_at, resets_at, window_minutes), written in the clock shape. pace_row's inline fallback is replaced by a call to it, with unchanged output.
  • reckon/crew/picker/snapshot.py: the picker's window term (_reserved_fill_window_utilisation, formerly _reserved_five_hour_utilisation) reads through that reader and keeps the picker's own freshness rules -- the shelf life, taken from the published document for a document-sourced group, and a reset that has not already passed. A group publishing a fresh operative window therefore sends no refresh_reserved_window probe.
  • Whether any lift governs the lane's group for the pick's session is resolved for every candidate whose lane is reserved, whatever the freshness of its reading -- the no-window case is exactly where the release matters, and a bare credit_lift flag cannot carry it because _budget_exclusion_reasons returns before it resolves the block. _reserved_lane_exclusion takes lift_in_force in place of credit_lift and skips its window term under any lift, so the lift-aware budget verdict governs the lane and the tier term alone keeps an ordinary node local.

Evidence

tests/test_a_reserved_lane_reads_its_published_window.py drives nine cases through a live snapshot carrying checked_at, a budget view and a lift store in a temporary crew home, and asserts the real crew home is untouched: an operative window at 0% offers the lane to a hardest-tier node and the tier term withholds it from an ordinary one; the same window at 70% is withheld naming 70% and the 60% ceiling; past its shelf life it is withheld as unknown or stale; an observed five-hour clock at 30% beside an operative window at 90% governs and offers the lane; with no window at all an uncapped session lift over a global credits lift offers the lane to a hardest-tier node and the tier term withholds it from an ordinary one; the same with a session lift naming another session is withheld as unknown or stale; a drain-by lift leaves the budget verdict to withhold it; a fresh operative window sends no probe; and pace_row fills clocks[five_hour] with the operative window's primary clock while clocks[seven_day] stays not_published.

Both negative controls were applied alone, under pytest with the default short summary. Removing the operative-window fallback failed the first case (4 failed, 5 passed). Restricting the window-term release to credits lifts failed the uncapped session-lift case (2 failed, 7 passed). Neither mutation was left in the tree.

The importing test files (pytest -n 8, both arms on the fleet node) ran 2 failed, 539 passed at base ecd22bbd7 against 2 failed, 548 passed at head.
