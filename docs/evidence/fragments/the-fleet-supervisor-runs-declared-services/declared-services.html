Declared services start once, restart with backoff, and reload

The batch step reads <config home>/fleet/services.json (configuration home resolved through $XDG_CONFIG_HOME, otherwise ~/.config) and starts each declared service as its own-session child behind an exclusive lock in the runtime directory: the wrapper takes the lock on a descriptor that survives the exec, so a second start, a reload or a second supervisor is refused by the lock rather than by bookkeeping. A service that exits is restarted with exponential backoff — 5 s at first, doubling to a 300 s cap, reset after 600 s of steady running — driven by a supervision tick and never by a liveness timeout. stdout and stderr append to one log at <state dir>/services/<name>.log, trimmed in place to an 8 MiB bound keeping the newest 4 MiB, cut at a line boundary.

On reload the config is applied before the module re-executes: a newly declared service starts, and a service no longer declared is stopped by the pid the supervisor recorded for it (nothing is signalled unless its lock is held). The health sampler still starts through its own unchanged path, and reckon names no project.

Seven cases in tests/test_fleet_supervisor_runs_declared_services.py run against a temporary configuration home, runtime directory and state directory throughout, with an injected clock for every backoff and a synthetic service; every child is stopped by its recorded pid and none survives a case. They cover: own session and held lock; no second copy from a second start or a reload; restart on the backoff and not before; reload starts the new service and stops the removed one by its recorded pid; the health sampler still starts beside a declared service; both output streams append to the size-bounded log; and the backoff sequence doubles to its cap and resets after steady running.
