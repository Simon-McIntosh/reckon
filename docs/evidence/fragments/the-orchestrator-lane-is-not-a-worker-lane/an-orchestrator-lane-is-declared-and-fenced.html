Landing: a lane declares the orchestrator role, and dispatch reads it

A backend declares serves_orchestrators in flight config, and a dispatch whose resolved lane declares it stops before anything is written, naming the lane, why it is fenced and a discharge. The stop records rather than refuses, per §4: the locally served lane's after-repair death rate is not stated, so refusing would move a review onto a lane that does not finish it.

What landed

  • reckon/schema/flight.yaml — serves_orchestrators added to BackendConfig, declared and never inferred from a backend's name; absent means the lane serves no orchestrator.
  • reckon/_flight_schema.py, docs/_shared/flight.schema.json — both regenerated from the LinkML source.
  • reckon/crew/dispatch.py — _dispatch_orchestrator_lane_stop reads the resolved backend's declaration and returns the stop; it is composed in plan_dispatch beside the lane gate, carried as DispatchPlan.orchestrator_lane_stop into the payload, appended to the plan's warnings and written to the run record. _orchestrator_lane_discharge_candidates names the configured lanes that serve no orchestrator and can resolve the node.
  • tests/test_orchestrator_lane_fence.py — seven cases: the declaring lane's stop and its discharge, the schema default, the declaration removed on the same node and lane, a non-declaring lane launching, the stop on the launched run's record, and the ordering probe that no pointer exists when the declaration is read.
