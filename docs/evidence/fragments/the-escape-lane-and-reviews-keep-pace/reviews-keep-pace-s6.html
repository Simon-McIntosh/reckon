One admission pass now shares its seats between reviews and implementations

Each pass computes the order it will launch in before the launch loop runs, and inside every project's slice of that order it now interleaves the two roles by a seat quota instead of letting the priority order settle it. Nothing about which requests are eligible, which lane they go to or which project's place they hold has changed.

Both stated cases fail at base as the plan asks: nine implementations ahead of three reviews with three free seats takes no review at base, and the verdict row has no per-role figures at base. The fourth case — a pass with only one role waiting fills every seat from it — passes at base as well as at head, by design: it is the guard against over-correcting, and a guard that failed before the change would be measuring something else. The separate pre-existing failure test_the_log_is_trimmed_within_its_size_bound is not in this arm's file set and is not claimed here; it fails identically at base and at head.

The negative control

Dropping the per-role share from fair_share_admission_order and nothing else leaves 1 failed, 3 passed (EXIT=1), and the failure is test_a_pass_gives_both_roles_a_seat_with_three_waiting — the nine-implementations-three-reviews case the share exists for. The other three cases do not move, which is what makes the failure attributable to the share rather than to the pass ordering in general.

What this node did not touch

The plan document, plan state and the project index are untouched, and this node does not close its own section. One file was edited outside the declared write paths — reckon/crew/admission/selection.py — to compute the cadence after the launch loop and put it in the pass result; without it the verdict row has nothing to carry, and the alternative was to recompute the order a second time inside the verdict writer, which would be a second interleaver over the same queue.
