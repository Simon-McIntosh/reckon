A shadow is reviewed on its patch

What changed. tests/test_a_shadow_is_reviewed_on_its_patch.py was red on the primary branch. Three of its four cases passed; the fourth, test_a_run_that_is_not_a_shadow_keeps_todays_brief, compares the whole composed brief against a spelled-out expectation whose blocks are spelled rather than read from the composer, so that a change to what the brief states turns it red instead of moving both sides together.

The composer gained a block the expectation did not account for. Commit 9c00916421095ef186210bec2e6ff03af439811e (2026-11-17) inserts _reviewed_scope_block(record, head) between the identity lines and the review record contract of every composed brief, naming the reviewed node's declared write paths and the paths its range delivered. The file was last changed on 2026-10-24, so its expectation still expected the identity to be followed directly by the contract.

Verdict: the test was wrong, not the product. The scope block is deliberate product behaviour: a reviewer scores a node against its section's expected scope and can only do so when the brief names what the node declared and what it changed. The test's rule — that a non-shadow brief is the rubric, the identity and the record contract — is the test's rule, not the code's, and the code no longer keeps it.

The repair spells the new block as its own constant, beside the identity and record-contract constants it already spells, and places it where the composer places it. Nothing is deleted or weakened: the assertion remains whole-text equality over the composed brief, and the two absence assertions ("shadow" not in text) are unchanged.
