A widen reaches a running repair round's next attempt

A repair round is a resume of the reviewed run rather than a second node, so the round's worker is that run's live attempt and every amendment of its claim was refused while it ran. A coordinator who saw that a finding's file was missing could neither widen the run nor advise it, because the round runs its own turn: the finding's path never reached the round's next attempt.

widen_claim in reckon/crew/claims.py now records the widen when the run's live attempt is the resume a repair round opened, read through the new live_repair_round predicate in reckon/crew/resumption.py. The added paths land on the run's own claim, which is what the next attempt's fence is composed from, so they arrive among its writable binds. A live worker that is not such a round still keeps its claim fixed, exactly as at the base revision, because its sandbox was fixed when it started; nothing reaches a worker mid-turn.

Measure: at the base revision bc6d7fd9 the reproduction refuses the widen with its worker is still running; wait for that process to exit; at the head it is accepted and the next attempt's composed fence carries the widened directory as a --bind. tests/test_a_running_repair_round_takes_scope_and_advice.py asserts both behaviours, the first on the composed launch rather than on the pointer's recorded write paths. The declared negative control drops the carry of the recorded widen into the next attempt, and the bind assertion fails with the composed argv printed. Logs sit under the run directory.
