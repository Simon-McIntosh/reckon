A review run's promotion derives its own gate, total and manifest wait

All three refusals §1 records are repaired here, each at the point where a different copy of one record is read. A review promoted with crew complete --run <id> and no gate flags now promotes: the stored record is its whole deliverable and the check a reader can re-execute, so it stands in place of whatever the manifest's tests: line declared. That line may hold a list of the checks the node ran, and its own repr was what reached _require_executable_gate_command and refused the promotion. The derivation fills only what the caller left absent, so a review promoted with an explicit gate command and log path keeps them: that caller named the evidence they want re-executed. A caller that supplies one of the two and not the other is refused rather than completed, because a check pairing its command with a log the promotion found elsewhere records a run nobody performed.

A record carrying six dimension scores and no total is now complete, carrying the total those scores sum to. One helper answers that question for every reader of the record a promotion handles — the delivery verdict, the payload the promotion commits, and the ledger block that records it — so no two of them can disagree about what a given record scores. The reviewer's run-directory copy is the one file in the path that never passes through the store's own writer, which is why the committed row previously held the dimensions beside a null total. A record that states its own total keeps it, and one that scores nothing keeps its absent total rather than gaining a sum over a subset of the dimensions.

A review promoted a few seconds before its worker writes manifest.md now waits a bounded twenty seconds for that file and, when it never appears, refuses naming the path, rather than promoting a run whose own account of itself does not exist.
