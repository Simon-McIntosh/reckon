The stubborn-producer arming test gives its stop room to run

Cause. The case set ARM_BOUND=2, so _stop_watch_producer_within in reckon/crew/dispatch_watch.py handed the stop child only the remainder of that budget. The child starts a fresh interpreter and imports reckon.crew.recovery before it can signal. Timed on the fleet node at load average 54 to 71, that start alone took 1.75, 2.17, 2.32, 2.46, 2.46, 2.78, 3.35, 3.38, 4.01 s over ten runs — against a remainder of about 1.9 s, so subprocess.run killed the child before it reached unwatch. Arming then exited 3 naming its own bound, correctly, while the marker the test asserts had never been written. Product behaviour is sound: the bound did what it promises, and the test asserted a delivery that bound never guaranteed.

Change. Test only, tests/test_arm_lock_is_never_held_by_a_follower.py. The arming bound is now STOP_CHILD_ARM_BOUND_SECONDS = 12, sized so the stop child can start, signal and finish; the communicate timeout is derived from it (ARM_OUTPUT_SECONDS = STOP_CHILD_ARM_BOUND_SECONDS * 2) rather than being a separate literal; and term-seen is read through a bounded wait whose failure message is still "the stop request must reach the producer". Given that room the child reaches unwatch, which signals the producer and then reports its own seat bound, so the assertion that arming names a bound accepts whichever bound ended the attempt instead of pinning the product's two-second figure. Both claims stand: arming ends with exit 3 naming a bound, and the stop request reaches the producer.

Reproduction at the base. 7db6cd8de4, the case alone 10 times with the 19-file importer population of reckon.crew.dispatch_watch beside it at -n 6: 10 of 10 failed, every one at line 205, the stop request must reach the producer. A poller over /proc recorded the marker's first appearance against every ARM process's exit: in all ten the marker appeared after the last ARM process had gone, by 0.8 to 2.9 s, so it came from the fixture teardown's terminate and never from a stop child. No base run reached the signal.

Measure. At the head, the same 10-of-20 protocol under the same population, load average 64 to 71: 20 of 20 passed. In all 20 the poller saw the marker written before arming exited, by 2.2 to 3.0 s — the stop was delivered while arming was still waiting, which is the claim the base runs could not show. Suite arms over the node's own test file: base 7db6cd8de4 1 failed and 4 passed, EXIT=1; head 5 passed, EXIT=0. Ruff check and ruff format --check add no findings on the staged path.

Negative control. Declared mutation: restore the two-second bound and the immediate term-seen assertion, and at least one of 20 loaded runs fails. Applied on an extraction of the head and run 20 times under the same load: 20 of 20 failed at the term-seen assertion, EXIT=1. The head arm fails none of them.
