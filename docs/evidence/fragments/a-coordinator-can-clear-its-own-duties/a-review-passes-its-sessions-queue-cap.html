A review is exempt from its session's queue cap and launches before that session's implementation

What changed. Two edits. In reckon/crew/runs.py, queue_dispatch no longer refuses a review once its session holds its allowance of queued runs, and no longer counts a queued review toward that allowance: the cap counts and bounds the session's queued non-review runs, so a review is neither refused at the cap nor allowed to shrink the room left for implementation. The predicate is reckon.crew.queue_order.pointer_is_review, which reads the role from the record root and from the node — the two places the dispatch writes it. In reckon/crew/queue_order.py, admission_order selects a session's next launch with _next_to_launch: the oldest queued review if the session holds one, else the oldest row. A review of a delivered run is what lets a run land, so it takes the slot ahead of an implementation queued earlier. The session's place in the cross-session order — its starvation clock and its lane-occupancy count — is still taken from its oldest waiting row, so a review at the head of the queue does not make a long-waiting implementation read as fresh.

  • The review is queued, not refused. A fixture session at its cap of two queued implementation nodes dispatches a review and gets phase: queued with a position, not queue-full. A further implementation dispatch is then still refused QueueFull (held 2, cap 2), which is the control; a queued review does not reduce that allowance, so a session holding one review still admits its full allowance of two implementations.
  • The review launches first. With one implementation queued 30 minutes ago and another 10 minutes ago in one session, a review queued one minute ago is first in admission_order; the two implementations keep their join order behind it. Where a session's oldest row has waited past the starvation age, its review still leads — the oldest row, not the review, sets the clock, so the exemption cannot mask a starved implementation.
  • The negative control. Restoring the cap check for reviews (if held >= cap rather than if not pointer_is_review(record) and held >= cap) turns the review-queued case red: the review dispatch raises QueueFull: session coordinator already holds 2 queued runs, at its cap of 2 — exactly the refusal this node removes.
