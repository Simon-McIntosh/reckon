Attribute a promotion's time by stage (partial)

A no-code promotion (crew complete --gate not-run) was profiled from process start with py-spy record --format raw at two populations of the store it actually reads — the committed ledger, at the live 7 787 run documents and at one twentieth of that — with the node at load 70–102, and its filesystem calls counted with strace -f -c -e trace=%file. A first round at 300 and 22 304 seeded run directories showed that population is not the driver. The implement-landing verb is still unprofiled, so the plan's both-verbs measure is unmet.

Three findings carry into proposals. Reading the ledger index to append one row is the whole of the promotion's growth, and at 7 787 rows it is half the promotion. The plan-body parse is a constant that dominates a small ledger and would dominate nothing once the index read is bounded. And 40.5 % of the command's main-thread samples sit outside _complete_locked, in interpreter start-up and module import — the fixed cost a profiler attached late cannot see, and why the plan's three late samples each caught a different stage.
