The index keeps no parse cache of its own

reckon/metadata_index.py no longer carries _META_BY_DIGEST, _META_HEAD_BY_DIGEST, _digest_memo, parse_meta_shared or _parse_meta_head_shared; a grep -c for all five in that module and in reckon/resources.py returns 0. file_memo.memoized gains an opt-in by_content identity that keys an entry on the file's content digest instead of its stat identity, so byte-identical content at two paths parses once while every caller that does not ask still uses the stat signature. The resource reader in reckon/resources.py is the one reader moved onto it.

The defect, reproduced. At the base tree, clearing file_memo alone leaves the index's cache serving the head parse: two byte-identical paths parse once and stay at one parse state after the clear. At head the same sequence re-parses, because one memo now owns the parse. Counted with a wrapper on _plan_html._parse_meta_head_uncached: base 1, 1, 2 across initial read, post-file_memo.clear() read and post-metadata_index.clear() read; head 1, 2, 2, in the landing run's two-caches-reproduction.log.

Measure. tests/test_one_cache_owns_a_plan_files_parse.py holds that identical content at two paths parses once (counted with a wrapper on the parser), that distinct content is parsed in its own right, that changed bytes are parsed again, and that none of the five names survives in the index's module. All four pass at head. The gate — every test file that imports reckon.metadata_index or reckon.file_memo plus tests/test_every_module_fits_a_worker_window.py, run with -n 8 -rf — is 227 passed at the base tree and 231 passed at head, zero failures either side, so head adds no failure. The negative control replaces file_memo.memoized with a passthrough and the two-path case fails at 2 == 1 with EXIT=1.

Two findings in reckon/resources.py (an exception-name lint and a pre-existing formatting difference) are present at the base tree and are not this change's: ruff on the five changed paths adds no finding at head over that base.
