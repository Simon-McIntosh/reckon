The MCP plan summary takes its blockers from the roadmap's assembly

The plan branch of reckon.mcp now calls roadmap.plan_blocker_state with the roadmap's own arguments — the plan row it just read, the blockers the inventory reports for it, the dependency rows it already resolved, the plan's decision→section scoping and whether a cleared hold's probe released it — and puts the answer in the summary's input under blocker_state. mcp_views._summary publishes that list and its effective_status for a plan selector, falling back to its own _blocking assembly when the key is absent, so the explicit-blocker and dependency row shapes the summary already published are unchanged. Held, gate and placeholder rows join the list as row dicts carrying a kind; an open decision joins it as a kind and id reference, because the summary already publishes that decision's question, status and choice under open_decisions and names the plan in its resource block. The roadmap keeps publishing the full row.

tests/test_the_resource_summary_reads_the_roadmaps_blockers.py builds a project in a temporary home with seven held plans and an eighth stored blocked with nothing recorded, and compares the three surfaces against each other rather than against literals: they agree on the blocked state and on the blocker kinds for every held plan, the released and free plans read open everywhere, and the unrecorded plan reads blocked with a non-zero served count. The audit reports orphaned-blocked-status once, in category lifecycle, and not for the released plan. Arm A of the negative control routes the summary back through _blocking and the decision-held plan disagrees (3 failing cases); arm B restores the deleted reckon/mcp.py orphan copy and the audit reports the plan twice.

The new assembly made five pinned expectations in tests/test_mcp_views.py stale, and they are now refreshed rather than relaxed: that file's shared fixture gives every plan an unscoped open decision, which the roadmap and the served view already report as a plan-level hold, so a summary that agrees with them reads those fixtures blocked and lists the decision row. The two status pins assert blocked, the three list pins expect the decision reference beside the rows they already asserted, and the dependency cases still assert that their own row clears when the dependency ships while the decision reference remains. The published shapes the section protects are untouched: an explicit blocker is still a bare id and a dependency a resolved row dict, and the summary's compactness bound stays at 60 % of the raw read — the reference is 40 bytes where the full row was 129, so the fixture summary measures 659 against the same 1117-byte raw read and the bound holds with room to spare. The gate is judged by delta against the base arm and adds no failure (133 passed at head against 4 failed / 129 passed at base, the base failures being the new test's own cases against the unassembled summary).
