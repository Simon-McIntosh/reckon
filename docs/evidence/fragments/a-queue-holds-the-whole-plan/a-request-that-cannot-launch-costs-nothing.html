A request that cannot launch costs a pass nothing

Every admission pass attempted each queued request whose back-off had elapsed, and three refusal kinds that waiting cannot cure were therefore dispatched and restored on every pass: a live run's claim on a write path the request needs, a node larger than its lane's context window, and a node outside its dispatch contract. Each attempt cost a whole dispatch() — project and plan discovery, then two roadmap builds. The field record had four such requests refused every 14 to 20 minutes for nearly two hours with the local lane idle, one of them attempted 54 times.

admit_once now judges a request before it reaches the launch. Its declared write paths are compared, in the repository's own spelling, against the declared paths of every live run whose claim claim_disposition still finds binding, and a collision holds the request held-on-claim naming the holder, with the slot going to the next candidate. A recorded failure of kind CompetenceLimit or a node-contract refusal holds it held-until-changed: the pass compares the request's dependency guard — its brief, its plan document, the flight configuration and the code stamp — and releases it on the first pass that sees any of them move, never on a timer. Every other failure's retry_at is now stamped from the moment the attempt failed rather than from the moment the pass started, so a pass that spent its time on other requests no longer records a retry instant already past.

The gate counts dispatch attempts through a patched _launch_queued. Four tests pass at the head revision: a stopped run's binding claim holds a request with zero attempts and the same request launches on the first pass after the claim is gone; a CompetenceLimit request stays held across two passes two hours past the old back-off with zero attempts and is retried on the pass after its plan stamp moves; a ValueError refusal stamps retry_at exactly 30 s after failure_at and more than a year after the injected pass start; and a pass over four requests, three of them held on claims, attempts exactly one launch and reports it launched. The suite ran 4 passed in 10.05 s at the changed revision.

Removing the claim pre-check is the declared mutation: the four-request arm then reaches four launches and fails on its attempt count with EXIT=1. The head arm passed the same test. Receipts: logs/head-1.log and logs/negative-control.log in the run directory.

The tests that import reckon.crew.queue_admission were checked as a serial related gate over the admission neighbours — test_a_held_request_stays_queued.py, test_a_full_queue_is_a_wait_not_a_refusal.py, test_the_admission_pass_survives_a_pointer_without_its_time.py, test_overlapping_queued_requests_launch_in_turn.py and test_a_discarded_request_stays_discarded.py — with 35 passed and 2 failed at the changed revision, against 10 passed and 1 failed at the base revision extracted from 1211c30e8b. One of the two is pre-existing in this environment, failing identically at base with a git revision-range warning inside the fixture; the other is a neighbouring test that still asserts the behaviour this section replaces, and it is reported out of scope below. Receipts: logs/admission-neighbours-head.log and logs/overlapping-base.log.

Two defects the review found are repaired here. The held-until-changed classifier matched the node-contract refusal by its opening words, node is not dispatchable, and the refusal a full lane gives opens with the same words: a backend at its partition admitted-cores bound, or at its login memory-slice bound. That refusal is cured by an occupying run finishing, and a run finishing moves nothing the dependency guard fingerprints, so the hold would have outlasted the condition that caused it — the stranding this section exists to remove, reintroduced for a second refusal class. The capacity refusal carries the occupying-run remedy, so the classifier now excludes it and it keeps the bounded back-off it had before. The gate gains an arm that raises that refusal with its own wording and remedy and requires the launch to be attempted again once the back-off expires; with the fix reverted it is the only arm that fails (EXIT=1).

The second defect was a neighbouring test still asserting the behaviour this section replaces: it expected the later request of an overlapping pair to be left queued with a recorded ScopeConflict failure and a retry_at. With the claim pre-check that request never reaches the launch, so it records neither. It now asserts the held-on-claim hold, names the holder in the verdict detail, and requires the launch on the pass immediately after the earlier pointer leaves — which only a hold that recorded no back-off can satisfy. Reverting that commit fails the assertion on the absent failure (EXIT=1); the file passes at the repaired head, and its one remaining failure is the pre-existing environment one that fails identically at base.

The gate ran at the repair's base revision 509b754be2 (4 passed) and at its head 86f3f3d4ed (5 passed), both through the same command. Receipts: logs/repair-baseline-suite.log, logs/repair-head-suite.log, logs/repair-overlapping-head.log (10 passed, 1 failed — the pre-existing environment failure), logs/negative-control-capacity-refusal.log and logs/negative-control-overlapping-hold.log.

One record does not reconcile itself. The neighbour test is in the repair dispatch's write scope but not in the live pointer's declared paths, so crew check --manifest reports it as the one remaining finding, and a worker cannot record the grant from inside the run: crew widen takes the pointer lock under ~/.config/reckon/crew/locks/, which the run's write fence mounts read-only. The repair needs a caller outside the fence, or adjudication against the dispatch.
