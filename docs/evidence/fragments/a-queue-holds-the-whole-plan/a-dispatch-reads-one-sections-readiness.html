A dispatch reads one section's readiness, not the project's

A dispatch's own-section readiness check now reads its plan document and the plan documents its declared waits name, and derives the verdict through the roadmap's own section_readiness_verdict. It no longer builds the whole project: reckon/crew/plan_review.py gains section_readiness_rows, reckon/roadmap.py gains section_readiness_rows and section_verdicts_by_plan, and reckon/crew/queue_admission.py's _readiness_rows takes the plan and refs it is asked about instead of reading the project.

Measured on the 60-plan fixture: the bounded single-section read takes 0.045 s against 0.662 s for the whole-project build, a ratio of 0.067, and both runs start from cleared index and discovery caches. On the held-by-gate, held-by-decision, waiting and ready fixtures the bounded verdicts equal the whole-project verdicts section for section, project_section_readiness builds the roadmap once, and the dispatch read resolves no plan beyond the two it names.

The plan-review gate's store read is cached for the length of one admission pass: list_plan_reviews inside plan_review.review_read_pass() loads each project's stored records once, so three queued requests on one plan cost one read.

Negative control: routing the single-section check back through the whole-project build fails the read-count arm, and its log records a failing test id and a non-zero exit.
