A queued launch keeps the run's full record

An admitted launch publishes the launched run's record: the same keys a direct dispatch writes, with the request's queued-life facts moved under admission_history instead of sitting at the top level where a reader of a live run takes them for a launch that failed.

Measure

PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python -m pytest -p no:cacheprovider -q tests/test_a_queued_launch_keeps_the_run_record.py — 7 passed, EXIT=0, at revision 31e53ae308.

  • A request refused once and then launched by one admission pass carries argv, worktree, lane and every key a direct dispatch writes (key-set delta against a direct dispatch is empty), and carries none of failure, failure_at, failure_attempts, retry_at at the top level.
  • The same facts are present under admission_history, one entry per admitted attempt, holding the refusal's text, its time, its attempt count and the retry instant alongside the request's queued_at.
  • The launched record reads as a promotable run: its recorded argv resolves the lane settings promotion reads its stream with, its worktree is a real directory, and a promotion dry run reports no refusal about a missing argv. The queued-shaped record the defect produced is refused with records no argv and its backend is not in the supplied config, so the arm can fail.
  • Both restore paths a failed admitted launch takes — annotating the live pointer and writing the snapshot back — keep waits and wait_projects, and a later re-derivation of the refs from the plan drops neither.
  • A wait whose request ref names no project records an issue on the request (the wait names no project, so its landing cannot be read) and raises it as an obligation, instead of sitting PENDING with no reason a reader can reach.
