The time fence follows the attempt through dispatch, a lane change, and a blocker

A fence names the attempt's launch instant and the deadline its budget sets from it. Three places could leave that statement attached to the wrong attempt: the dispatch prompt composer, a lane change relaunching from a retained prompt, and the manifest audit accepting a blocker's claim that the fence expired.

What landed

  • reckon/crew/reports.py: audit_manifest now judges a blocker that cites an expired time fence against the attempt's own record. It derives the deadline from the fence statement the manifest quotes, or from attempt.json beside the manifest plus the node's time_budget, and reports the claim as a defect when the manifest was recorded before that deadline. An unresolvable deadline stays silent rather than guessing, so the check adds no finding it cannot date.
  • tests/test_time_fence_follows_the_attempt.py (new, 4 tests) drives the real doors: crew.dispatch() with a stub launcher and the composed prompt read back; change_lane against a stopped pointer holding a first attempt's fence; and audit_manifest against a blocked manifest with an attempt record beside it, in both directions (early claim, genuine claim).

Items (a) and (b) of the section were already wired in the base revision: dispatch() reads the clock once and passes the instant to the composer, and change_lane already restated the fence. Their tests therefore pin existing wiring against regression rather than introducing it. Item (c) is new behaviour: its test fails at the base revision and passes at this head.
