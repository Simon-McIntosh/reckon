Why one phrase read as an outage

The two causes are different facts about the lane. In the first, the lane's document is present, fresh and measured, and its throughput block has not been published yet — a figure that is late, which a reader can wait out. In the second, the backend names no lane document at all (the codex-shaped row), so there is no lane to read and nothing the lane could publish. One phrase covered both, and so also covered a document that was present and healthy; a reader holding a stalled row attributed the stall to the lane rather than to the worker the row describes. The repair is that the cell's text carries the cause, so a reader distinguishes a late figure from an unread lane without opening the document.

The cell resolves the backend's declared document through the same four-layer flight configuration a dispatch reads and takes it through reckon/crew/lane_document.py's own readers, so the freshness judgment and the throughput keys stay the module's rather than a second spelling kept in the ticker. A boolean mean and a document that is absent, unparsable or not an object all take the unpublished clause, because none of them is a measured figure.

Two properties are load-bearing. The clause holds one width in every state, so the run's own figure stays on its column as rows move down a pane: the longest clause (lane mean unpublished) fixes the cell's width, unchanged from the width the single phrase occupied. And the run's own rate is rendered in every case, including the one where no lane clause follows it, because it is the row's own measurement and withholding it would cost the reader that fact as well. A row that names no backend still adds no cell at all — the document is declared per backend.
