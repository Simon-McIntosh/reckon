A dispatch prints one row: the arrival collapses into the run's first transition

Landed. RowPolicy in reckon/crew/ticker.py gains an arrival hold: the kind (None, "dispatched") is withheld for a 90 s window and discharged by any transition out of dispatched. Unlike a noise pair, the resolving row is kept — it already reads dispatched → <state> — so the launch collapses to one row. An arrival that has not moved by the window's end prints late, because a run still sitting in dispatched is a duty. When the first move out of dispatched is itself a held flicker (dispatched → abandoned), the hold passes to it, and on abandoned → working the single released row carries from_state dispatched rather than abandoned; a flicker that does not complete prints its opener late as before and the arrival is dropped. A second arrival from a re-arm keeps the standing hold rather than printing an extra row.

Focused gate. tests/test_a_dispatch_is_one_row.py (six cases, the five named in the section plus the repeated-arrival case) together with tests/test_follower_prints_only_policy_rows.py and tests/test_ticker_row_is_one_transition.py reads 36 passed at EXIT=0. Neither pre-existing suite rewrote an expectation: the base arm of those two files reads 30 passed, 0 failed at EXIT=0, so the change adds zero failures against the base revision.

Negative control. The declared mutation removes the (None, "dispatched") hold from the arrival table, so the arrival row prints at once; the arrival-then-working case then sees two rows. Applied to a scratch copy of the head, tests/test_a_dispatch_is_one_row.py runs EXIT=1 with five failing ids including test_arrival_then_working_prints_one_row.
