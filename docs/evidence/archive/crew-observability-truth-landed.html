§18 — Four endpoint drops in one day, and the reason string that covers two causes

Recorded 2026-09-14 across three coordinators sharing one locally served lane. The endpoints document went to zero four times: a cluster of eight terminals between 06:52:19 and 07:00:20Z across four sessions, one near 09:11Z, one at 13:39:05 with the document rewritten one second later that ended four of five of a peer's workers, and a deliberate deployment swap at 14:11:41Z that this plan's own fleet was inside for.

The improvement is measured, not asserted. The morning cluster classified all eight runs abandoned individually and cost a peer an hour of hand reconstruction. The afternoon event held all three affected runs instead: each kept a session id resolved from its own stream, a resume command, and an in-progress manifest recording what had already been delivered. All three resumed with every turn intact. Recovery from a lane event is resume, never redispatch — a peer's run in the 13:39 event held 87 lines of uncommitted work a redispatch would have destroyed.

One reason string, two causes, both measured 22 minutes apart. At 14:11:41Z three runs were held reading the backend refused the turn on a rate-limit (consumer queue backpressure) while the endpoints document held zero endpoints and the serve was being replaced — the reason was wrong. At 14:33:48Z a review was held with the identical string while the document listed one endpoint and the lane served normally, and the reason was right: 11 records carrying error: rate_limit, 10 api_retry system records, against 20 live pointers and 7 working. The string cannot discriminate; the endpoints document can, and the routing pre-flight already reads it while the classifier does not consult it. In both cases the terminal record's own subtype read success.

A resume crosses models and no surface says so. Settled by probing the cheapest of three held runs rather than by reasoning. The resumed run's stream carries the replacement model on all thirteen records, so it genuinely ran on it; the live pointer's agent block still read the old model, alias and window, all captured at dispatch, and the ticker rendered the old alias on the very transition reporting the resume. A promotion would commit the dispatch-time model onto the ledger row, attributing a run's whole wall time, token total, generation rate and imputed cost to a deployment that produced only part of it. The model stamp exists, it is written once, and a resume is precisely the event that invalidates it. Today's three resumed rows are excluded from lane calibration for that reason.

Why the overruns were not node size. All three maps overran their 40-minute budgets by 150–160 percent. The serve was running at a median 8.6 tokens per second against 17.5–101.4 on the ten prior days, from 1,353 of 1,453 prefill batches carrying zero cached tokens — a serving-build defect rather than contention or brief size. Reading three consecutive overruns as evidence the briefs were too large would have shrunk future nodes for a reason that disappeared when the deployment was replaced.
