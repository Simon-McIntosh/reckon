a-pick-names-its-slow-stage — a pick records each stage's time and names the stage a deadline ran out in

A dispatch-path pick runs a known sequence of stages — the ledger records, the verdict inputs, the budget snapshot, the context estimate, and the Jev call — all under one wait, PICKER_DISPATCH_TIMEOUT_SECONDS (5 s) in reckon/crew/dispatch_picker.py. When that wait expired the recorded selection read fallback_reason: "timeout" whatever had actually spent the budget, so every expiry read as a Jev timeout though most of the pick is spent before Jev: over 2026-10-12/13, 91 picks fell back as timeout while Jev itself answered in 0.3–0.6 s.

Production path. In the dispatcher the three dispatcher-scope inputs are built once, outside the pick's own bound, by build_picker_inputs, and handed to dispatch_picker_selection as pre-built figures — all three production call sites (reckon/crew/dispatch.py and reckon/crew_dispatch_commands.py on the dispatch path, _record_shadow_picker_selection on the shadow path) read that way. So the stages a production pick is actually inside when the deadline expires are the context estimate and the Jev call, and the naming fix has to reach them rather than only a caller that rebuilds the inputs itself. build_picker_inputs therefore records each input's elapsed milliseconds into a thread-local sink when its caller hands no stage_ms, and dispatch_picker_selection reads that sink, carries the three input times onto the selection's stage_ms and clears it, so a production pick records all five stages without the caller threading the timings through its own call. The context estimate is now marked as well as timed, because in production it is the stage that spends the pick's budget before the picker call is reached: a caller that hands pre-built inputs and gets no answer before the deadline now reads fallback_reason: "timeout in context-estimate" with the three input stages' milliseconds beside it. The Jev call remains timed but not marked: its own snapshot, render and Jev stages are inside it, so an expiry during the opaque call is attributed to no single dispatch stage a reader can act on and keeps the bare timeout, its duration recorded beside it.

reckon/crew/picker/outcomes.py classifies a staged timeout by its stage (a staged reason summarises under the key timeout:<stage>) while a bare timeout remains its own count, so a report shows which stages spend the pick's budget rather than collapsing them into one.
