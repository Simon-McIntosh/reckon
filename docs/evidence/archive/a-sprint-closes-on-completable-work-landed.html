A sprint closes on completable work — landed evidence

The §2 and §3 done-whens pass. A plan carrying a held blocker is reported by roadmap as held rather than merely blocked, the row carrying kind=held with explicit-blocker behaviour unchanged. And a sprint is closable only when every incomplete item is held: closing one carrying a single un-held incomplete item is refused, naming that item. The record below is the part worth reading — the two sections counted and refused separately, the explicit plan unchanged in every reported field, and the existing endpoint-row held key left alone because it counts a different quantity.

Record scope

This record covers §2 and §3, the two implementable sections of the plan. They land as separate commits: §2 established the held vocabulary, and §3, the closure rule that reads that vocabulary, is the follow-on this record now closes. §4 is a deferral and is recorded as f-ascocw-003 rather than implemented here.

Held reads held beside an unchanged explicit plan

Commit d6715087. The blocker's own resource kind now survives end to end. _derive_lifecycle in reckon/serve.py accepts the composed blocker rows and maps each sprint item's blocked_by id to its resource kind, defaulting to explicit only when the resource says nothing; the blocking rows it builds therefore carry the authored kind instead of hardcoding explicit for every id. build_roadmap in reckon/roadmap.py reports held rows on a pending plan under a new held_blockers key placed beside the unchanged explicit_blockers, and a held blocker still holds the plan open: it keeps the plan out of the synthetic persisted-ready fallback, out of is_ready, into is_blocked, and into the effective-status derivation. The graph resolver treats explicit and held rows as blockers and ignores every other kind. The plan-resource view in reckon/mcp.py exposes the held ids as held_by alongside the unchanged blocked_by.

The fixture and the two digits. A fixture carries one held blocker and one explicit blocker on two plans. The held plan reports len(blocked) == 2 and held_blockers carrying one row of kind held with explicit_blockers empty; the explicit plan reports explicit_blockers carrying one row of kind explicit with held_blockers empty. Each count is stated as a digit in the test. The case that fails if held rows are treated as explicit was written first and observed failing against the base revision, before any implementation: at base the fixture yields a single blocked plan and the two-row assertion fails, because every blocked_by read as explicit. A second test drives the lifecycle derivation directly with a blocker resource of kind held and asserts the derived blocking rows carry kind=held.

The quantity that was not overloaded. roadmap's existing endpoint-row held key is a count of blocked members per execution-path endpoint — schedule-deferred members whose path still holds them open. It is a different quantity from a blocker kind and is left untouched; the new reporting rides only on the per-plan blocking rows.

Proven at the entry point, not at a helper. The failing-then-passing case exercises build_roadmap itself over a composed project state, which is the surface an agent, a CLI caller and the MCP read. The end-to-end chain through discover_plans over a git-backed fixture was verified separately and reports the held plan as held and the explicit plan unchanged, both blocked.

A closing sprint is refused while any incomplete item is not held

Commit e3b160c7. Closing a sprint is now a gated operation on the sprint resource itself: setting its status to a completed status runs a closability check in the close path. The check reads every item whose plan is not lifecycle-terminal and refuses the close, naming the offending plan, unless each such item is held by a blocker resource of kind held. The refusal leaves the closing sprint byte-identical.

The carry-forward mirrors an item move. A sprint whose incomplete items are all held closes normally; the held items move to the next open sprint in composed order, retaining their blocker references, under the same two-resource version check, move journal, and compensating recovery as move_sprint_item, so an interrupted close restores both resources. Completed items stay on the closing sprint. A held-incomplete sprint with no open advancing sprint to receive its work is also refused.

Both measures are the refusals the plan demands. The refusal case is demonstrated as its own test rather than only the success, and the carry-forward is proven by set membership: after the close, the moved item is asserted present in roadmap's pending_work over the returned set, never as a count. The failing case was observed at the base revision before any implementation, exactly as the §2 record describes for its own first-written test.

Reading held, not re-deriving it. Held is read from the same composed blocker state the §2 work defined — the resource's blocker kind == held — not re-implemented in this section. The measure drives apply_resource_ops, the operation a caller performs to close a sprint, so the surface exercised is the one an agent, a CLI caller, and the MCP reach.

Scope and follow-ons

reckon/project_state.py gained the closability check and carry-forward (plus a life-cycle import it already had precedent for); reckon/lifecycle.py and reckon/serve.py needed no change this time, because held is read from the same composed blocker rows the §2 work defined. The endpoint-row held count in roadmap and the schedule it derives from remain untouched by design — a per-execution-path count, not a blocker kind. The close path rejects a plan whose status is missing entirely, treating it as incomplete and un-held, which is the conservative direction.

What remains is the §4 deferral: the executable probe that validates and clears a held blocker's subject. It is a separate plan by the locked probe-execution-boundary decision, unblocked now that the closure rule reads a held blocker's kind, and it is recorded as f-ascocw-003 on the plan rather than folded into this record.
