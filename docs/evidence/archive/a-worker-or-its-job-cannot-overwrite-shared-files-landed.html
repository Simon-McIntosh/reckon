§10 — Shown on the fleet: the incident's call fails and the fleet survives a dead supervisor

Outcome — four arms hold and the census arm fails, so this section stays open. A dispatched worker replayed the incident's three-argument call at a decoy chain of the incident's own depth, directly, through sbatch, through srun and by the scheduler binary's absolute path: the wrapper's redirect was refused in all four and the decoy interpreter was byte-identical afterwards. The same redirect at a decoy in a directory the fence grants writable was refused by the file's own mode (0555, Permission denied) while a 0755 neighbour in the same directory was written, which attributes the refusal to the permission rather than to the fence. The fleet health series read zero writable interpreters on every sample of the last 24 hours. An all_debug allocation outlived two SIGKILLs of the supervisor it started, restarting it with the product's own backoff and leaving its stand-in descendant and its queue state intact.

The census arm does not hold. Over the 200 run directories created after the composed gate command landed (0388ce623c, 2026-11-17T09:12:31Z) 11.0 % hold a worker-written shell script that invokes a test gate, and 13.5 % hold a worker-written script of any kind, against 5.0 % and 7.0 % in the 200 before it. The section asks for under 5 %. Two instruments were run because the original census tool is not recoverable — its run directory was pruned by retention — and neither reproduces the section's 43-of-200 (21.5 %) baseline, so the baseline's rule cannot be re-derived from the record. What is measured here is measured under a stated rule, and both rules put the after-window above the before-window and above the bound.

Arm A — the incident's call through every route a dispatched worker takes

The decoy chain has the incident's depth and nothing else of it. A worktree .venv is the final symlink, whose directory holds a symlink to python3, which symlinks to a decoy interpreter under a decoy install tree, with no interpreter of the account's and no environment of the project's behind it. The call is the incident's three-argument one, issued by a shell gate wrapper the worker wrote in its own run directory:

/bin/sh run/gate.sh gate command worktree/.venv/bin/python

where the wrapper does log="$3" and then printf "replacement gate log\n" > "$log" — the argument shuffle that writes a "log" onto the interpreter. Each arm composed the fence with the product's own reckon._worker_fence.fence_argv, the call _backends.launch_plan makes for a fresh dispatch, a resume and a redispatch, with the run directory, the worktree and a scratch directory as the writable grants. One all_debug job (1343880, 14 s) ran all four arms; its own sbatch and srun arms submitted their own jobs (1343881, 1343883).

The identity is compared on size, nanosecond modification time and sha256, and all three are equal in every row. Two details belong in the record because they qualify what the rows mean. The srun arm created no scheduler step: the shim re-entered the worker fence, so sacct -j 1343880 lists only the batch and extern steps, and the call was refused in the fenced context the shim composed rather than in a step of its own. The absolute-path arm reached the shim, not a bare binary, because the fence binds the scheduler's absolute path to the shim — the same shield §15 landed, shown here doing its job from a worker's own call.

The writable neighbour and the grant control are what make the refusal attributable. A refusal alone would leave open that the directory was not writable; a 0755 file in the same directory written by the same fenced task closes that, so the 0555 refusal is the file's mode and nothing else.

Arm D — an allocation outliving the supervisor it started

An all_debug job (1343953, 46 s, node 98dci4-clu-3141) ran the product's own hold script — fleet_node.generate_hold_script(fleet_size({}), …) with its submission headers removed because it ran as the job's batch step — and a replaceable Python supervisor at $HOME/.local/bin/fleet-supervisor that spawns one stand-in descendant and then sleeps. The supervisor was killed with SIGKILL twice, at different points in its backoff, and the hold was read after each:

The backoff is the product's, read from fleet_supervisor.shell_backoff_transition() rather than restated here, and it is the hold shell, not Python, that keeps the allocation: the job's queue state never leaves RUNNING although the process holding the sessions is replaced twice.

What this arm does not measure, and why. The gated rehearsal in tests/test_sessions_outlive_the_supervisor.py additionally asserts a live srun step beside the descendant, and that half cannot run from inside a worker fence. Two attempts record the boundary. Job 1343897 submitted from inside a fenced worker: its supervisor's srun --overlap re-entered the fence (the log carries the shim's notice), the step's stand-in never acquired the name the probe reads, and the supervisor failed its own stand-in check. Job 1343928 submitted with the fence environment scrubbed: the shim refused the submission outright and the test never started. Both are the shield working as designed — a worker cannot buy an unfenced allocation by submitting one — and they are why this arm measures the allocation's survival with the descendant stand-in and reports the missing step half as an open item rather than as a pass.

What would close the section

Arms A to D need no further work; each is measured here with a job id and a receipt. The section closes when a census holds at under 5 % after the landing under a rule stated in advance, and the before-figure it is compared against must be re-measured with that same rule, because the 43-of-200 baseline §10 names cannot be reproduced: the tool that produced it is gone with its run directory, and neither gate-invoking nor any-script counts reach 21.5 %. The most likely closure is the composed gate command being what workers actually reach for on gate nodes — the §7 measure — with this census as its after-the-fact check, rather than a new wrapper-counting instrument.
