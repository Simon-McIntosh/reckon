Nothing leaves the window — landed evidence

The guard's first verdict is clean, and that is the less interesting half. The interesting half is that this guard had never produced a verdict at all, and everyone who looked at it had a ready explanation that was wrong.

Record scope

A capture found a dropdown clipped off-screen at both widths while five gates stayed green, and none of the five could have been strengthened into the catch — a box can render with every correct field and still sit outside the viewport. This plan built the guard for that class.

§2 — The guard walks, for the first time

At the base revision the guard expired twelve seconds into its run, at the first routable surface, and wrote no verdict. It waited on .r-list .r-row for the plans surface; a commit two hours after the harness was authored replaced the mounted component with one rendering .r-artifact-index and .r-artifact-row. Eleven days under the wrong explanation

This failure had been filed by three separate sessions under the host's documented browser-navigation fault. It is not that: the surface renders healthily, and a direct probe at the same revision shows #plans drawing 27 rows with zero JavaScript errors. That probe is the positive control, and it is the only thing that separates a guard which cannot navigate from one that navigates fine and waits on a name that moved.

The general shape, which is worth more than the fix: a documented fault is a ready-made explanation, so anything failing near it gets filed under it and stops being investigated. The louder cause absorbs the quieter one. Checking the positive control before filing a failure as environmental is what breaks that.

§3 — Whatever it finds

It found nothing. The first verdict is recorded and carries zero violations across all 22 surfaces, so this section's deliverable is the recorded negative result rather than a list of repairs. That is a real outcome and not an empty one: the guard is now the thing that would report the next clipped control, and the clipped dropdown that motivated the plan has since been fixed by the surface work it prompted.

The guard that guards the guard, and why it needed a second commit

A stale selector is exactly what disabled this guard, so the repair added a case that fails loudly when a declared readiness selector matches nothing rendered. That case was first written into tests/spa_containment_harness.py — a filename pytest collects under neither of its patterns. A default collection over tests/ found it zero times. A guard written to make the next rename loud did not itself run.

The corrective moved it into tests/test_spa_viewport_containment.py, which already imported the harness, rather than renaming a module other files import by name. The measure was the collection count, not the case's existence, because the test exists was precisely the claim that had already failed once: 0 before, 1 after, counted in both trees and re-counted on the integrated branch.
