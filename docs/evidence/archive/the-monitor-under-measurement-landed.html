§2 — Why followers go stale: a cause census

Deliverable. docs/research/data/monitor-staleness-census.json, one row per cause, each carrying its count, the pattern searched, the population searched and quoted examples with their transcript paths. Scope was the reckon, nova, imas-efit and imas-ambix checkouts including their worktrees: 1384 transcript directories, 1583 transcript files, 1292 Monitor calls (all time) of which 700 were armed inside the window.

The positive control holds. The plan set the control that the imas-efit transcripts for 2026-09-22 yield at least nine expiry notices, because that coordinator reported nine. The matcher, Monitor expired after (\d+)m with (no|\d+) events? delivered, returns 17 for that session and day. The instrument was additionally shown to see prose that merely quotes the string: two such records were found and excluded from the count, because a notice is delivered as a <task-notification> event, never as text a reader is quoting.

700 = 600 + 100 + 0. Every armed follower in the window falls into exactly one outcome, so the census is a partition rather than three overlapping searches. Zero armings were still running when the window closed.

The host's parameter set. 453 Monitor calls passed persistent; the harness answers such a call with "runs until TaskStop or session end", and answers a call without it with "expires in 30m unless the source ends first". None of the 453 persistent calls met the thirty-minute expiry; 730 non-persistent calls did. The finding that matters for the repair plans is the timing: every one of the 453 persistent calls predates 2026-09-16, so persistent_passed_in_window = 0 and not one of the 700 in-window armings carried the flag. The re-arm storm the coordinators reported is a re-arm storm in a window where nobody was passing persistent. The median interval between successive armings in one session is 30.23 minutes over 64 sessions, which is the thirty-minute expiry read back out of the transcripts.

Sweep-launched workers survive the follower. Yes as to parentage, no as to the kill. _sweep_lapsed_holds (reckon/cli.py:1633) calls sweep (reckon/crew/resumption.py:964), which takes launch = _spawn (reckon/crew/resumption.py:993) and calls it at reckon/crew/resumption.py:677. _spawn (reckon/crew/dispatch.py:4560) starts the worker with subprocess.Popen at :4585, which makes it the follower's child as its docstring states, but passes start_new_session=True at :4592, which places the worker in its own session and process group. A host expiry that kills the follower therefore does not take the worker down.

What the census does not establish
  • Code reload is an opportunity, not an observation. The in-place re-exec leaves no banner a transcript distinguishes from ordinary prose, so the row counts the 198 commits that advance follower_code_stamp, not reloads observed.
  • The streams could not carry the producer-gap count. The .events files stamp time-of-day only, and the imas-efit stream holds no ticker rows at all, so producer gaps are derived from the transcripts. Two baseline bursts are visible where the streams do carry rows.
  • The resumed-stream coincidence does not discriminate. 1846 run streams end inside the window; 463 end within a minute of a notice, against a uniform null of 221 and a notice coverage of 12% of the window. Both run ends and notices cluster strongly, so the uniform null is the wrong reference and the coincidence is reported as uninformative rather than as evidence that a worker died at an expiry.
