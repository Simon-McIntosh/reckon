The positive control

dsv4.1-flash is twelve characters against a ten-column cell. It appears among the overflowing model cells in all four projects, as the table's second overflow row shows. The matcher is therefore reading the model cell it claims to read: an alias this wide drawn from the live local lane cannot be absent from an overflow count unless the cell is not being measured. The other over-wide aliases the census sees are gpt-5.6-sol (eleven), deepseek-v4-flash (seventeen) and claude-sonnet-5 (fifteen).

The clip point

One real blocked row (n-resolve-cross-module-refs, detail 66 characters) is rendered at every width from 100 to 220. The reason is clipped across a single contiguous window, widths 145 to 198, and fits whole again at 199. Below 145 the reason does not render at all: the margin is below the twelve-column floor, so the cell is suppressed rather than truncated. The operating width of 160 lies inside the clip window, so this row's clause is cut in ordinary use — and 199 columns is what it would take to carry a 66-character reason whole. That is why no row blanks a reason for want of margin at width 160: the margin question only arises below the width at which the clause stops appearing entirely, and at 160 the clause is present but shortened.

What the counts settle

The from-and-to pair is present on every transition in every project, so a destination-only layout shortens exactly the transition rows — 2889 / 3887 / 748 / 844 — and no transition would lose information a destination-only reader needs. The generation-rate cell is unmeasured far more often than the wall cell (92–99 % of rows against 26–46 %), so the absence marker a reader sees is overwhelmingly the rate's. The zero-minute age form renders in all four projects, which is the lead's undecodable-cell report reproduced as a count.

Caveats

Each project's stream carries pre-JSON plain-text lines (444 / 1164 / 0 / 262) written before the structured record existed. They hold no fields to render, so they are counted separately and excluded from every denominator rather than guessed at. The streams are append-only and live: denominators drifted by one or two records between the first and last pass of this census, and the committed file is the single frozen snapshot.

How often a stall or an unreadable row misleads — landed evidence

The §5 done-when produces monitor-stall-census.json with denominators for both counts. The figure worth acting on is the one the monitor cannot draw: 97 of 118 stalls were a process that had already stopped writing, and another 9 have no attempt stream alive at the stall instant, so the row a reader acts on is reporting on nothing in 106 of 118 cases. 41 of the 118 later completed with no resume at all, which is the row that reads as a live stall and is not one. Two positive controls were set by the plan and neither reproduces as written: the named run completed after a resume rather than without one, and the nova invented status word is not on disk. Both are recorded below rather than reconciled away, because a control that is edited to agree with the plan stops being a control.

The instrument, and the field that does not exist

A stall transition in a watch stream carries no process-liveness field. The records under ~/.config/reckon/crew/watch/*.events say a run's stream went quiet for more than the stall window; nothing in them says whether the writer was alive at that moment. The census therefore derives liveness from a second, independent record: the attempt stream that was active at the stall instant — the latest of stream.jsonl and resume-N.jsonl in that run's directory whose first content timestamp precedes the transition. A record in that same file after the stall instant means the writer was alive; no record after it means it was gone. Nine stalls fall before any attempt's first record and are reported as unknown rather than guessed.

The stall window is set once: DEFAULT_WATCH_STALL_WINDOW in reckon/crew/node.py is "15m", 900 seconds, emitted in the transition's detail as stream quiet for <N>s. The --stall-window flag on reckon crew follow places the same value from reckon/cli.py, so a follower armed with a shorter window writes more stalls over the same fleet.

Timestamps are read from content, never from mtimes. On this workstation a manifest may be hand-edited hours after its run ended, so a file's mtime orders the repair, not the run.

The unreadable status count

Population: 4,378 manifest files carrying a status: line across 4,326 run directories, every project on the workstation. The reader's terminal vocabulary is complete, blocked, failed (reckon/crew/reports.py); 194 files declare something else, in 41 distinct spellings.

Two of those 194 are not defects and are counted separately: none are the a | b template placeholders the reader already detects. Thirty-two are words outside the reader's extended vocabulary at all — not in-progress, running, waiting, pending, derived, but done, delivered, partial, working, reported, shipped, implemented-pending-review, handover-pending, ready-for-review, delivered-awaiting-coordinator-adjudication. The rest are a recognised word with prose appended on the same line — complete (provisional — being updated; see checkpoint) — which the line-oriented reader sees as one unrecognised token.

The 53 files kept as manifest.md.asdelivered and its siblings are the ones that matter for attribution, because the live manifest.md has usually been hand-repaired to a recognised value by the time anyone looks. The two imas-efit words this plan's memory attributes to a worker inventing a status — delivered-awaiting-coordinator-adjudication and ready-for-review — survive only in their as-delivered copies, which is exactly why a scan of live manifests alone cannot find them.

For every group the census also records whether the run ever emitted a terminal transition. The answer is yes for all but the two blocked (phase-2 finding …) and blocked — the measure is met … rows, whose runs never reached a terminal state at all. So the invented word is a signal of a hand-edited manifest; it is not a reliable signal that the run failed to finish.

The two positive controls, and why neither holds as written

Control 1 — the named run. The plan requires r-20260922T140247463532-rail-env-remaining-eighteen to appear as a stall that later completed without a resume. It appears, at 2026-09-22T14:32:30Z, and it did complete under its own run id with no redispatched duplicate. It completed after a resume: resume-1.jsonl carries the completing attempt (content 15:14:15Z to 15:21:08Z), the ledger records attempt 2 with attempt_kind: "resume" and completed_at 15:21:08Z, and the stream emits completed_unpromoted at 15:21:12Z. Three independent durable records agree, so the half of the control that holds is "stalled then completed"; the "without a resume" clause is refuted by this instrument. Reported here rather than reconciled: the census marks the run completed_after_resume and carries the conflict in positive_controls.stall_later_completed.claim_conflict.

Control 2 — the nova word. The plan requires at least one nova manifest from 2026-09-22 whose status word is outside the recognised set. The literal reading holds: 17 of the 98 nova manifests for that day are outside complete/blocked/failed, namely in-progress and waiting, twelve of them kept as as-delivered copies. The stricter reading the plan's own memory states — a word no reader recognises, ready-for-review or delivered-awaiting-coordinator-adjudication — is not reproduced for nova on that day; those two words are imas-efit's. The most likely reason is that the six manifests nova-a9 repaired by hand were overwritten in place, leaving no as-delivered copy. The census records this as held: false with the literal count beside it, so a reader can see which reading failed.

Scope and follow-ons

This node changed no source code and no test; it wrote one data file, one figure and this record. The measurement is a snapshot at 2026-09-22T20:25:02Z and a fleet that keeps running moves every figure in it — re-run the census rather than quoting these counts.

The liveness split is a derived instrument, not a recorded fact. Making the instrument unnecessary is the repair a follow-on plan wants: a transition that carries whether its writer was alive at emission. Until then, the four stalls that never reached a terminal state are the population to act on, and the 106 that did are the population a stall row currently misreports.

Failures outside this node's fence are not repaired here; the two positive-control discrepancies are the substance: the plan's §5 wording should be corrected to "later completed, after a resume" and its control 2 restated against the project that actually holds the word.
