§1 — Why: review time is spent on edits nobody needed read again

Over 5 and 6 October the local lane ran 456 plan reviews (48.7 run-hours) and 130 code reviews (21.5 run-hours) beside 100 build runs (83.4 run-hours), counted from the committed ledgers of reckon, imas-ambix, imas-codex and nova. Plan reviews outnumbered builds 4.5 to 1, and reviews of both kinds took about 46% of the lane's run-hours. The imas-ambix log shows the shape: an edit such as "call the 268 JT-60SA coil entries filaments" is followed by a plan review, its promotion and its release, and then by the next edit.

The cause is measured in plan-review cycles over 308 stored reviews of 32 plans. Only 18 reviews carried no finding. Of 313 answers, 259 were "acted", and acting means editing the plan, which moves its fingerprint, which earns the next whole-plan review. 49 of 280 re-reviews were triggered by the document unit alone, 42 of them by authored comments: landing records and rulings that answer a finding. The commonest finding type, duplicate_owner, is 270 of 502 findings, and 83% of them follow a review that already raised the same type, so a plan with ten missed instances of one class takes about ten rounds to clear.

The code also disagrees with the rule the skills already state. reckon-edit hard rule 9 defines a material revision as a change to a decision, a done-when, a section declaration or a gate, and says a landing comment or an impl move needs no review. The fingerprint in reckon/crew/plan_review.py instead digests every word of section prose and every authored comment and followup, and it normalises section declarations out. The reflex therefore reviews what the rule says needs no review and is blind to one of the four things the rule says does.

Reviews are also not durable. A promoted plan-review run's ledger row carries review: null and an outcome sentence. The findings, answers, fingerprints and unit digests exist only in the host's configuration directory, ~/.config/reckon/crew/reviews/<project>/, which is not versioned and not shared with any other clone. Its record timestamp is the time a record was stored, not reviewed: 92 records of one plan carry the same stamp from a batch import. Answering a finding rewrites the record in place. The nova directory holds 2.5 GB that is not review records at all: copied worktrees, a 627 MB tree.tar, a .git and a .codex left by one September session that used the store as a working directory.

The imas-ambix coordinator is writing an RCA of review time on the local lane. Its findings are added to this section when it lands, and any it raises that this design does not answer become sections.

§2 — One definition of a material revision

Deliverable. One Python owner of the question "does this change need a reviewer?": classify_revision(before, after), taking two plan documents and returning a class (material, correction or bookkeeping), the authored units that changed, and one reason per unit. The classes are these.

  • Material, which is the rule 9 set widened by the structural changes it implies: a decision's question, options or choice changed; a section's done-when changed; a section was added or removed, or its declaration moved to implementable or deferred; a gate was added, changed or removed; a relationship changed (depends-on, blocks, after, section-depends-on); or a section's prose changed by more than the correction bound.
  • Correction: section prose changed within the bound, with none of the material changes above.
  • Bookkeeping: authored comments, followups, landed cards, a declaration moving to done, and the server-managed scalars the fingerprint already normalises out.

The correction bound is a flight key, review.plan_correction, with a word count and a fraction of the section's words, declared in reckon/schema/flight-defaults.yaml beside review.plan_settle_seconds. It is measured against the last fully reviewed baseline, not against the previous edit, so a run of small corrections that together exceed the bound is material. The done-when is read by one reader in reckon/_plan_html.py beside structured_section_spans; _done_when_plan_overlap_warning in reckon/crew/dispatch.py moves to it rather than keeping its own. Rule 9 in skills/reckon-edit/SKILL.md names this function as the definition.

Calibration. The bound is chosen from the stored history, reusing the pair machinery in docs/figures/plan-review-cycles/measure_review_cycles.py. For every consecutive pair of reviews, record the class the change between them would receive and whether the later review's acted findings landed in a unit the change classed as a correction. Report that miss rate against the bound and choose the default where no more than 5% of acted findings followed a correction-only change. The table goes into this plan's evidence fragment.

Done when fixture plans drive each material trigger, a correction, an accumulated correction crossing the bound, and each bookkeeping change to its class in a test; the calibration table is recorded; and the default bound is declared in flight defaults with its miss rate beside it.

§3 — Only a material revision earns a full review, and an answer earns a check

Deliverable. The sweep in reckon/crew/recovery.py, the crew review-plan verb and the dispatch gate require_plan_reviewed in reckon/crew/routing.py all read §2's class, measured from the last full review's baseline.

  • Material earns a full review, as now.
  • Correction or bookkeeping earns no review. The sweep stores a classification record (§5) naming the class and reasons, so a skipped review is auditable, and dispatches nothing.
  • An answer earns a check. When the change since review N is confined to the units that N's acted findings name, plus corrections and bookkeeping elsewhere, the sweep dispatches a check review at the light tier (reckon/review_tiers.py). Its brief carries N's findings, the answers and the diff. It reports, for each acted finding, whether the edit resolved it, and it reports new defects only inside the changed units. A finding a check raises on an unchanged unit is recorded as follow-on and never blocks.
  • A round cap. After two consecutive rounds on one baseline whose findings were all acted on, later findings on that baseline are recorded as follow-on. This writes into the record the practice coordinators adopted by hand, deferring further findings to a later pass.
  • The gate passes when the last full review's baseline reaches the current content through corrections, bookkeeping and answered checks, and when it refuses it names the class and the reason. crew review-plan applies the same test and answers "nothing material since review X" rather than dispatching; --force dispatches anyway and records why.

The landing-beat comment in skills/reckon-build/SKILL.md and its "Review the design" step point at the classifier, so the skill and the code state one rule.

Done when a test drives the sweep through each class and through a response round and observes a full review, nothing, and a light check respectively; the gate passes on a corrected plan and refuses on a done-when edit, naming the class; the research script, replayed over the stored history under this rule, reports the reviews that would have run beside the 308 that did; and one live plan on this host receives a correction that earns no review and a done-when edit that earns one.

§4 — A reviewer reports every instance of a finding type

Deliverable. The design-review prompt composed for a plan review tells the reviewer that a finding of one type is a census: when it finds one instance (an unplanned importer, a duplicate owner, a missed reuse), it searches for every instance of that type in the same pass and lists them all under one finding. The prompt is composed in one place today; this changes that text and nothing else.

Done when the composed design-review prompt carries the instruction in a test, and the recurrence of a finding type after an acted same-type finding is re-measured one week after landing against the 41.9% baseline for duplicate_owner, with the figure recorded on this plan.

§5 — Every review is a committed record, apart from the plan it reviews

Deliverable. A review store committed in each project's repository, beside the ledger, holding plan reviews, run reviews and §3's classification records under one schema.

BodySubject (kind, project, plan or run, section); what was read (blob or head sha, base sha, fingerprint, unit digests); the §2 class that earned the review; tier and rubric; the reviewer's model, effort, backend and lane; dispatch and completion times from the run record, never the store time; findings verbatim (id, type, severity, anchor, text, reason); scores for a run review; and the sha256 of the raw report.

Done when a review run promoted in a test lands its record under docs/state/<project>/reviews/ inside the promotion commit; the gate still reads it after the staging file is deleted; an answer appends an event and the body's bytes are unchanged; the server and MCP return a record by id; and the ledger row names the id.

§6 — Reviews are kept after their work lands

Deliverable. A lifecycle derived in Python from the record and the plan, never stored on the record: open (a blocking finding is unanswered), answered, superseded (a later full review of the same subject exists), and landed (the reviewed section has collapsed, the plan has shipped, or the reviewed run is promoted). The gate, the sweep, the obligations view and the SPA's default view read only the hot set, open and answered but not landed. The look-back views read the archive: finding-type recurrence, declined recurrence across plans (declined_recurrence), §2's calibration, and how often a later review contradicts an earlier one.

What is discarded is what the record already distils: the raw report text and a review run's scratch, after review.raw_report_retention_days (default 30) on the host.

Done when each lifecycle state is derived in a test from fixture records; a plan shipping moves its reviews to landed without any record being rewritten; declined_recurrence and §2's calibration read the committed archive; and the raw-report retention runs on the host with its removals printed.

§7 — Import the host store, and set aside what is not a record

Deliverable. A migration command, dry run by default and --write to act. For each project, it parses every file under the host store. Each one matching the plan-review or run-review shape becomes a committed record, timed from its run id and deduplicated by subject, reviewed sha and review run id. Everything else is listed by path, size and modification time, then moved, not deleted, to a quarantine directory outside the store. From then on a guard on the store refuses to write anything that is not a record. The 2.5 GB in nova is the known case. Deleting quarantined content is a lead decision taken on the printed inventory.

Done when the dry run's inventory is recorded with counts and bytes per project; the imported count per project equals the recognised records; the store holds records only; and a test shows the guard refusing a non-record write.

§8 — A review of a brief-carried run names its brief

Deliverable. _review_dispatch_fields in reckon/crew/recovery.py copies the reviewed node's plan and section and never its brief, so the review of a run dispatched with --brief composes a node with no authority, and dispatch refuses it as not-dispatchable. This was measured on 2026-10-06 for plan-review-cycles. The composed review carries the reviewed run's stored brief as its authority when the reviewed node names no plan, and the review-missing obligation's command is then dispatchable as printed.

Done when a test composes the review of a brief-carried run and the dispatch dry run validates it.

§9 — An author can hold a plan out of review while writing it

Deliverable. Two edit_plan ops, hold_review (with a reason and an optional expiry) and release_review. They write a plan-review-hold meta carrying the holding session, start, expiry and reason, normalised out of the fingerprint like the other server scalars. While a plan is held, the sweep skips it, crew review-plan refuses and names the holder, and the gate refuses a build of it. Release classifies the change since the last review at once, with no settle wait, and dispatches what §3 says. A hold lapses at its expiry, two hours by default, and the holder's own edits renew it.

Done when a test drives the hold through the sweep, the verb, the gate, release and expiry.

§ Decisions

Where do review records live?

Committed. A review is evidence about a plan, and the plan, its ledger and its evidence are already versioned and travel with the repository. The host store is invisible to other clones, has no history, timestamps records with the store time and rewrites them on every answer. A host database would carry the same faults. Promotion already commits every review run, so the record travels in an existing commit.

Is a review discarded once the work it reviewed lands?

Keep. Every measurement that led to this plan read history: the 83% same-type recurrence, the 18% bookkeeping trigger rate, the store-time defect. §2's correction bound is calibrated on history and must be re-calibrated on it. The lead's rubric rule counts a finding type declined across three or more plans, which spans plans and time. Plans cite the review behind a ruling. A record is about 4 KB. What gets discarded is the raw report text and run scratch, which the record already distils.

Does a correction earn any review?

None. A correction is by definition below the size at which a second reader changes the outcome, and §2 calibrates that size against the history. Accumulating corrections against the last full review stops a material change being made in small slices.

What does an edit that answers a finding earn?

A check. A full re-review of the whole plan after each answer is the loop measured in §1. Nothing at all would leave an answer unread, and an answer can introduce a defect of its own. A check confirms the answer and reads only what changed.
