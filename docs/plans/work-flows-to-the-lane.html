§1 — Why: the lane waits on coordinator bookkeeping, not on capacity

On 11 November at 14:50 UTC the local lane (~/public/imas-ambix/lane.json) published 38 worker slots, a router gate of width 12 with nothing in flight and nothing waiting, and an engine able to hold 30 requests. A worker holds a model request for 23 % of its life (requests_per_run 0.232), so the display's 2 of 12 meant about eight workers. The crew's 52 live run pointers, across all projects, read:

PhaseRuns
working or starting9
complete, waiting for a coordinator to review, dispose or land it35
awaiting an upstream7
blocked1
queued0

The work existed: the reckon roadmap listed 138 sections ready with no run, about 150 estimated hours across 55 plans. Four things stood between them and the lane.

  • A session's own dispatch waits on its landing backlog. dispatch refuses every new node for a session holding a complete run unpromoted past the 15-minute unreconciled_run_grace. In November the median implement run took 65 minutes to work and then waited a median of 59 minutes, p90 190 minutes, between completing and being promoted. Most sessions therefore spend most of their time unable to dispatch.
  • Every finding holds landing. 1,134 of the 1,441 reckon runs dispatched in November were reviews. 92 % of reviews raise a finding and 9 % raise a blocking one; that rate is 9 % at a run's second review, 12 % at its third and 9 % thereafter, so re-review is not low-yield on what blocks. The lander's _undisposed_finding_guard (reckon/crew/queue_landing.py) refuses a run while any finding of any severity is undisposed: in the hour to 14:50 the admit pass logged 864 such landing obligations.
  • A shared-root claim still held a queued request. From 13:54 to 14:50, 92 admit passes held one request on a claim on reckon/crew/picker/lane_context.py, a file under a declared shared root, naming no exclusive pattern; the holder recorded no worker process. concurrent-edits-meet-at-the-merge §2 landed on 8 November to admit exactly that overlap.
  • Every Python process leaves a file in the claims directory. reckon/crew/dispatch_claims.py publishes claims/exec-<pid>.identity when the module is imported. Since 6 November that left 116,359 files that nothing removes; a bounded find over them did not finish in 60 seconds on GPFS. In the week to 11 November, 31 of 38 launch refusals were launch-span-exceeded at the step that publishes a claim; whether the directory causes them is not yet shown.

Steering is already in place: crew tell reaches a running DeepSeek or codex worker at its next tool call (a-live-worker-can-be-reached §8, §9). What a coordinator lacks is the moment to use it: it is not told when the admit service submits one of its queued requests to a worker. The incoming 8×B300 cluster widens the lane several-fold; none of that is usable while each running worker needs about two coordinator-hours of bookkeeping around it.

§2 — Only a process that takes a claim publishes an execution identity, and the identity goes with it

Deliverable. Importing reckon.crew.dispatch_claims writes nothing to the crew home. A process publishes its exec-<pid>.identity file only before it takes a node dispatch claim, and the file is gone once no claim of that process remains or the process has exited. Identity files left by dead processes are removed by a sweep that removes only files whose pid is not running, or is running under a different execution identity, and prints each removal. The reclaim decision keeps its present meaning: a later dispatch still tells a live holder from a re-executed one by the identity file.

Done when a new tests/test_an_execution_identity_is_published_with_its_claim.py shows, with a temporary crew home: a subprocess that only imports the module leaves no exec-*.identity file; a subprocess that takes and releases a claim leaves none after it exits; a claim held by a live subprocess is still refused to a second dispatcher, and one whose holder re-executed is still reclaimed; and the sweep removes a planted identity file for a dead pid while keeping a live process's file. Negative control: restoring the import-time publish makes the first assertion fail. The test files importing reckon.crew.dispatch_claims add no failure against the base revision, measured on all_debug, one arm at a time with at most -n 4. Ruff check and ruff format --check add no findings on the staged paths. After landing, the coordinator runs the sweep on the live crew home and records the count of identity files before and after in this plan's evidence record. Write paths: reckon/crew/dispatch_claims.py, the module that hosts the sweep (named in the manifest) and the new test.

§3 — A coordinator's own dispatch is not refused on its landing backlog

Why. a-gate-halts-only-on-substance §7 lets a queued launch through its session's backlog, and keeps the refusal on a direct dispatch and on the queue-time check. The lead ruled on 11 November that dispatch no longer waits on the session's landing backlog at all: the backlog is shown as duties, not enforced by refusing new work. This section supersedes §7's clause keeping the direct-dispatch refusal, and waits on §7 so the two land in order.

Deliverable. A direct dispatch and a queue-time check succeed for a session holding overdue unreconciled runs. The run's pointer and ledger row record the backlog it was dispatched over, naming those runs, so a later reader sees the dispatch was made over unreconciled work. The overdue runs still appear as the session's obligations. --allow-unreconciled-runs keeps parsing and changes nothing. The project-wide queue bound queue.max_requests still refuses a dispatch past it.

§4 — A finding that does not block is deferred at landing, not held

Why. On 8 November the lander stopped writing exemptions for undisposed findings, because an exemption the landing pass wrote was a judgement nobody made. That reason stands. A deferral is not an exemption: it records that the finding was carried forward unjudged, and leaves it open where the backlog is read.

Deliverable. A run whose review carries no undisposed blocking finding lands, whatever its non-blocking and follow-on findings say. At landing, each such finding still open gets a disposition of a new kind, deferred, naming the landed run and the rule that deferred it, and it stays listed as open work in crew view findings and the MCP findings view until someone acts on it or exempts it. A blocking finding, a finding with no severity, and a review with added failures hold landing exactly as now. Recording a deferred disposition by hand is refused, so the kind always means the rule acted.

Done when a new tests/test_a_non_blocking_finding_is_deferred_at_landing.py shows, with a temporary repository and crew home: a reviewed run with two open non-blocking findings and one open follow-on finding passes the landing guard, and all three read deferred naming the run, and are listed by the findings view as open; a run with one open blocking finding is refused naming it; a finding with no stated severity is refused; and a hand-written deferred disposition is refused. Negative control: treating every severity as blocking makes the first case refuse. The test files importing reckon.crew.queue_landing or reckon.crew.findings add no failure against the base revision, measured on all_debug, one arm at a time with at most -n 4. Ruff check and ruff format --check add no findings on the staged paths. Write paths: reckon/crew/queue_landing.py, reckon/crew/findings.py, the disposition CLI module (named in the manifest) and the new test.

§5 — A session is told when its queued work is submitted to a worker

Why. A coordinator queues many sections in one beat and the admit service submits them as slots free, possibly hours later. The coordinator can steer a running worker with crew tell, but it is not told the moment one of its requests starts, so the steering it learned since queueing reaches the worker late or never.

Deliverable. When the admit service submits a queued request to a worker, the coordinating session's watch stream gains one row of its own kind, within the admit pass that launched it. The row names the run, the plan and section, the lane and model, the advice the worker was launched with, and the crew tell command that reaches it. Launch never waits on the session: a session that is away still has its work submitted, and its live and obligations views list every run submitted since it last read them.

Done when a new tests/test_a_session_is_told_when_its_work_is_submitted.py shows, with a temporary crew home and a stubbed launch: an admit pass that launches two queued requests of one session writes exactly two such rows to that session's watch stream, each naming its run, section, lane and advice; a request of another session writes none to this one; a launch with no reachable session still launches; a pass that launches nothing writes no row; and the session's live and obligations views, read after the pass, list both submitted runs as submitted since that session's previous read, and list neither on the read after that. Negative control: removing the row write from the launch path makes the two-row assertion fail. The test files importing reckon.crew.queue_admission add no failure against the base revision, measured on all_debug, one arm at a time with at most -n 4. Ruff check and ruff format --check add no findings on the staged paths. Write paths: reckon/crew/queue_admission.py, the watch-row writer module (named in the manifest) and the new test.

§8 — Jev's call on whether a re-review is needed is recorded beside its outcome

Why. A re-review finds a blocking defect as often as a first review, so skipping them on a rule would lose real catches. A predictor might still find the re-reviews that will be clean; whether it can is measured before anything is skipped.

Deliverable. Before each second or later code review is dispatched, a Jev judgement of the probability that it finds a blocking defect is recorded on the review run's ledger row, with the inputs it was given. When the review is stored, its outcome (blocking finding or not) is recorded beside the prediction. A view reports, over the recorded rows, how many blocking re-reviews and how many clean ones each threshold would have skipped. Every re-review is still dispatched: this section skips nothing. When Jev is unavailable the row records the prediction as unavailable and the review is dispatched as usual.

Done when a new tests/test_a_re_review_need_is_predicted_in_shadow.py shows, with a temporary crew home and a stubbed Jev: a second review's ledger row carries the prediction and its inputs, a first review's row carries none, the stored outcome is recorded beside the prediction, the view's counts at two thresholds match the planted rows, and an unavailable Jev still dispatches the review and records the prediction unavailable. Negative control: a prediction recorded after the review was stored is refused by the view. The test files importing the modified modules add no failure against the base revision, measured on all_debug, one arm at a time with at most -n 4. Ruff check and ruff format --check add no findings on the staged paths. Write paths: the review dispatch module, the picker module that calls Jev and the view module (each named in the manifest) and the new test.

§9 — The lane is fed while coordinators land

Deliverable. A record, in docs/evidence/archive/work-flows-to-the-lane-landed.html under a s9 anchor, of one four-hour window. After §2 to §6 land, the coordinator queues at least 20 ready sections and names the window's start; the node measures that window after it has closed. The same figures are reported for 7 to 11 November as the baseline.

Done when the record shows, against that baseline, a mean of at least 12 working workers in the window, a median completion-to-promotion time for implement runs of at most 20 minutes, no admit pass holding a request on a claim under a shared root, and fewer than 100 identity files.
