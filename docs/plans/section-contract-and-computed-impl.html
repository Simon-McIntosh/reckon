Why this plan exists

The lead's directive of 2026-09-19, given while re-planning nova's S19: "the sections should always contain the effort, depth, risk and number of retries (a strong signal for difficult work: if this is the third attempt, try a better model). The implementation is then the sum of all the complete implementation times divided by the sum of the complete implementation time and the predicted time for all remaining items, so plan metrics do not go stale or need reassessing, they are calculated. Positive control ensures sections are always written with schema-controlled metadata and links to other complete plans or plan subsections. Our DAG could support both."

Measured on nova the same day: the roadmap sees plans, so the four items that decided the sprint (a solver-route regression, an X-point production wiring, a field-response production builder and a topology rebank) sat as followups on plans at impl 0.6, 0.88, 0.92 and 0.99 with no edge, no effort and no readiness; three plans at 0.99, 0.995 and 0.88 carried 14, 7 and 4 open followups of real work while reading as finished; a 46-hour plan had its second half unsized; and routing difficult nodes to a stronger lane was the coordinator's per-dispatch judgment because nothing recorded how many times a section had already been attempted. The nova coordinator re-authored that work as sections with a hand-written contract table, which is exactly the thing this plan makes structural. Section-level dependency refs already exist (slug#section on depends_on, resolved by the roadmap, and gates with gated_sections), so the DAG half of the directive is documentation, not implementation; the contract, the computed figure, the attempt counter and the refusal are new.

One caution the halted reckon coordinator recorded and this plan inherits: a computed figure is only as good as its inputs, and reckon already carries a field written, read as authoritative and wrong on a third of rows (wall_seconds, disagreeing with elapsed time on 328 of 1,074 ledger rows). So the computed impl must state where section effort comes from and how a wrong figure is detectable, or it will become a number nobody questions because it is calculated.

§3 — impl is computed from section effort, never authored

impl becomes the sum of effort over sections whose status is done, divided by that sum plus the predicted effort of every remaining implementable section; deferred sections are outside the denominator, as the declarations map already intends. The field stays on the plan as a read-only projection: edit_plan refuses a set impl on a plan that carries section contracts, and reckon crew complete moves impl only by moving a section to done. The impl-must-move promotion rule then reads as a section transition rather than a number the coordinator remembers to bump, which is where it failed three times on nova on 2026-09-19.

Where the effort comes from, and how a wrong one shows. The predicted effort is the section's authored figure; the completed effort is the same authored figure, not the measured worker time, so a plan's impl is a ratio of estimates and says so in the SPA. Beside it the roadmap reports, per plan, the measured worker-hours of the promoted runs that landed each done section against its authored effort, so an estimate that was wrong by more than a factor of two is visible on the plan and the calibration table is fed by every landing. The measured figure comes from the ledger's dispatch-to-completion elapsed time, not from wall_seconds, until that field is repaired.

Done when. impl on a contract-carrying plan equals the computed ratio on read, a plan with a new section added shows impl fall by the predicted amount without any other write, the SPA labels the figure as computed, and the calibration column shows authored against measured effort per done section.

§4 — attempts is a tool-owned counter

reckon crew dispatch increments the target section's attempts when it launches a node on it (a resume does not increment; a redispatch under a new node id does), and reckon crew complete records the attempt's outcome (promoted, failed with its classification, superseded) on the section. Nothing else writes the counter. The count is displayed on the section heading and in the roadmap's ready set, so a coordinator opening the sprint sees which sections have already consumed two attempts.

Done when. Dispatching to a section twice and promoting once leaves attempts at 2 with outcomes recorded, a resume leaves it unchanged, and the counter survives the plan's round trip through edit and merge.

§5 — attempts steers the capability class, never a named model

Reckon's routing rule is that source, plans and skills may not encode a model hierarchy, so the lever is the capability request. Flight config gains a rule that raises the effective capability class of a node whose section attempts is at or above a threshold (default 3): general becomes orchestrator, reasoning becomes deep, verification becomes strict; the backend then resolves from the raised class as it does today. The dispatch summary names the raise and the count that caused it, so the coordinator reads "third attempt, raised" rather than inferring it.

Done when. A dispatch on a section at attempts 3 resolves to the backend the raised class maps to in the test flight config, the summary states the raise, and a section at attempts 2 resolves unchanged.

§6 — Section-level dependency refs are documented, audited and used

parse_plan_ref already parses [project:]slug[#stage] and the roadmap already resolves a stage against the target's sections with a wiring finding when the stage is missing; gates carry gated_sections. What is missing is that the create and edit skills say so in one place, that audit-doc warns when a plan-level depends_on could have been a section ref (the consumer's first implementable section does not need the whole target), and that the roadmap's critical path reports section-level edges so a plan blocked on one section of another shows the section, not the plan.

§7 — Wiring is asserted at creation: a plan with no wires is refused unless it says it stands alone

The lead's point of 2026-09-19: if the DAG half needs no build, the thing to build is the assurance that the wires exist, because the roadmap can only follow wires that were drawn. Measured the same day: of sixteen live nova S19 plans, nine declare no depends_on, no blocks, no informs and no gate, including four authored that morning by a coordinator who knew the rule; of thirteen live reckon S18 plans, ten declare none. The critical path the roadmap reports is therefore the path through the plans that happened to be wired, not the path through the work.

Positive control at the two moments a plan is born or grows. edit_plan(create=True) and audit-doc refuse an implementable plan that declares no depends_on, no blocks, no informs and no gate unless it carries an explicit plan-standalone declaration with a one-sentence reason (a plan that genuinely feeds and needs nothing is rare, and saying so is the assertion). A new section appended through §2's contract must name its links or declare none with the same explicit word. The reckon-create and reckon-edit skills carry the litmus already written in reckon-create Step 1.5 as a checklist the author fills in the plan (for each plan in the sprint: does my first section wait on it, does it consume my evidence, does one section of mine wait on one section of it), so the wiring is authored from the sprint's plan list rather than recalled. The roadmap gains a wiring finding, unwired-plan, at warn for existing plans and at error for plans created after this section lands.
