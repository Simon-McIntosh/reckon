§1 — Two lanes, two exhaustion modes, one of them silent

Every figure an orchestrator needs to choose a lane already exists, and none of it reaches the orchestrator at the moment it chooses. That is the whole of this plan. The surrounding work makes the numbers readable; this makes them arrive.

The two lanes in use do not run out in the same way, and the difference decides what an advisory has to say about each:

Scarce quantityquota in a rolling windowthroughput

Measured on the metered side, 2026-09-08. The weekly window opened at 02:28Z and resets at 02:28Z on 2026-09-15. Two live probes: 30% utilisation at 10:34Z, 46% at 15:57Z — sixteen points in five hours and twenty-two minutes, a rate of about three points an hour against a sustainable 0.35. The engine reported a burn multiple of 5.7× and projected exhaustion for the following evening, six days before the reset. Every one of those figures was available from crew preflight, and none of it was said to any of the four coordinators spending the window.

Measured on the local side, and the measurement is the point. A paired run of one mechanical node took 131 seconds on a loaded lane and 133 on a free one, so at three concurrent workers there is no queueing component to recover. Reported from the night of 2026-09-07 and not yet reproduced here: 32 concurrent workers degraded the lane. Between three and thirty-two the curve is unknown, which is precisely the interval any advice to move work onto this lane would push us into.

So the naive response to a spent metered lane — send the work to the local one — trades a loud failure for a quiet one. A quota outage names itself and its reset time; a contended local lane simply makes everyone slower, with no refusal and no way for any session to tell whose fleet is responsible.

§2 — The advisory arrives where the choice is made

A coordinator learns its lane's trajectory only by asking, and the one moment it is certainly not asking is while it dispatches. The advisory therefore rides on the dispatch itself, unrequested, and says the three things that change a decision: what the lane's projected exhaustion is, whether that precedes the horizon of the work in hand, and which lane would serve this node's role more cheaply on measured evidence.

The shape is not new and should not be invented. Gate enforcement already has an advisory mode that records a warning where a strict mode would refuse, and a named backend that cannot be honoured is already reported rather than silently substituted. This is the same contract applied to a resource whose scarcity is shared.

Done when a dispatch onto a lane whose projected exhaustion precedes the work horizon emits an advisory naming the utilisation, the burn multiple, the projected exhaustion and the reset; when that advisory names the lane that measured rework says would serve the node's role more cheaply, or states that none would; when it is emitted without being asked for and without adding a call; and when nothing about it refuses, holds, or alters the resolved routing. Asserted through the dispatch payload over a lane fixed at a spending trajectory, requiring the advisory present and the resolved backend unchanged.

§3 — The choice is recorded, so advice becomes evidence

An advisory that is only printed is a preference. What makes it a mechanism is that the run records both halves: the advice that was shown and the lane that was chosen. The record already carries the requested backend beside the resolved one for exactly this reason, so the field has a precedent and a place.

This is also the only way the guidance can ever be judged. With the pair on the ledger, a later reader can ask whether advice was taken, and whether the runs that took it cost less per durable node than the runs that did not — which is the return-on-experience loop closing on itself rather than accumulating as opinion.

Done when a run whose dispatch carried an advisory records the advisory and the chosen lane on its live pointer and on its promoted ledger row; when a run that took the advice is distinguishable from one that declined it; and when both are readable from the committed record without reading a stream. Asserted over a promoted run of each kind.

§4 — One window, four coordinators

The metered window is an account-wide resource and every reading of it is session-local. Four coordinators were live on this workstation while it went from 30% to 46%, in three repositories, and none of them could see the others' draw. A coordinator that halves its own spending has no way to know whether that changed the trajectory or was absorbed by a peer.

The join already exists. The coordinator directory names every live session, its project and repository; the ledger records what each run spent. What is missing is the sentence that says a lane's remaining capacity is being drawn by n sessions rather than by this one, so the figure a coordinator reads is the figure it actually faces.

Done when a coordinator reading a metered lane's headroom is told how many live sessions share that window and what they are shipping; when the advisory of §2 carries that count so a trajectory is attributed to a fleet rather than to a session; and when a session that is the sole drawer is told that too, because sole responsibility changes the decision as much as shared responsibility does. Asserted over a directory holding coordinators in more than one repository against one window.

§5 — The local lane's ceiling is a curve nobody has measured

Advising work onto the local lane without knowing where it degrades is how a quota problem becomes a latency problem. Two points exist and they are far apart: no measurable queueing at three concurrent workers, and reported degradation at thirty-two. Worker count is known to be a poor proxy for the pressure that matters, since admission is governed by instantaneous in-flight requests and eight workers mid test-run generate less of it than four mid-reasoning — so the measurement has to record what it can defend rather than a worker count alone.

Done when the lane's throughput is measured at several concurrencies spanning the reported degradation, with node size held constant so the curve is not confounded by what the workers were doing; when the measurement records in-flight requests beside worker count and states which one tracks the degradation; when the reported thirty-two-worker result is either reproduced or contradicted on the record; and when the resulting figure is expressed as something an advisory can carry. Asserted from the committed runs of the measurement itself.

§6 — Return on experience reaches the decision

The ledger already answers which configuration costs least per durable node, charged for rework, per model, effort, specification level and role. That answer currently reaches a coordinator only if it goes looking, and the one conclusion drawn from it today — that read-and-report work is zero-rework on both lanes and therefore belongs on the unmetered one, while implement work on the local lane costs about twice the metered lane's runs per durable node — lives in a plan comment rather than anywhere a dispatch would surface it.

Prose guidance and ledger evidence drift apart by construction: one is written once and the other updates on every promotion. The remedy is that the advisory quotes the ledger rather than a document, so the recommendation ages with the evidence.

Done when the cheaper-lane clause of §2 is derived from the routing figures at the time of dispatch rather than from any written table; when a role with too few samples to discriminate says so instead of recommending; and when the derivation is one call that spends nothing. Asserted by moving a role's measured figures and requiring the recommendation to follow them.

§7 — What this plan does not do

It does not schedule, rank lanes into a hierarchy, or choose on the orchestrator's behalf; the choice stays where it is and the advisory is deleted from the decision the moment the orchestrator has read it. It does not replace the existing hard ceiling, which remains the only thing that refuses. It adds no new command and no new tool: the advisory rides on a call that is already being made, and the shared-window count comes from a directory that already exists.

§ Decisions

Where is the advisory emitted, given that it must arrive unasked?

The dispatch payload alone is disproven rather than untested. Every dispatch on 2026-09-14 returned lane_declaration.utilisation_pct, and a coordinator opened three waves on the codex lane without acting on any of them, because position is not rate: the burn multiple of 2.87x existed only on the budget and pre-flight surfaces. The pre-flight cannot satisfy the requirement that the advisory arrive unasked, because it has to be invoked, and that is exactly how it was missed - it was run once before the backend was chosen, where role defaults resolved it to the local lane and it never evaluated codex at all. The follower is the only channel that reaches the coordinator and the lead without being asked for, and reckon already measures its delivery from the descriptor its lines are written to. So the payload carries the figure for the record and the follower carries it to a reader.

What does the local lane publish as its scarcity signal, once measured?

Unchanged as a choice; its source and its comparison are both rewritten, because the figure the original rationale named was deleted rather than revised on 2026-09-14. THE UNIT. Worker count remains a bad proxy: a worker reading files or running a suite is not making a request, so the same worker count saturates or does not depending on phase mix. That argument never depended on the relay and survives its removal. THE SOURCE, corrected. The original rationale compared in-flight requests against the router's configured admission allowance. That allowance is gone - commit 76a6a23 removed AdmissionLimits, the controller, consumer identity and the refusal paths outright, because the filter had refused 402 of 1,075 requests in an afternoon while the engine sat at 0.0% KV occupancy, so it was never protecting anything. The engine publishes the same quantity directly and better: Running, Waiting, KV usage and prefix-cache hit rate, once per interval in the serve log. That is an observation of the lane rather than a configured allowance, and it counts every fleet sharing the serve, which a per-consumer figure structurally could not. THE COMPARISON, corrected. There is no constant to compare against and none is needed: Waiting above zero is the scarcity signal, because the engine degrades by queueing and never by refusing, so a request waiting is the observable equivalent of the refusal the relay used to emit. Measured on the four-card V4-Flash serve after the memory split was fixed from 0.92 to 0.85: 128, 512 and 1536 concurrent all served in full, 4,789 tok/s at 1536, engine peak 1023 running with 439 waiting at 53.0% KV and zero preemptions. At 0.92 the same serve died at 256, so any figure taken earlier that day is void. TWO CAVEATS THAT BIND HARDER THAN THE CEILING. First, a request ceiling is not a fleet size: reckon dispatches runs and the engine counts requests, and the measured band is 0.5 to 1.0 requests per live run, rising toward 1.0 as a fleet grows because overlapping turns become likelier. Convert at 1.0, never at the low end, since the conversion shrinks exactly when it is being relied on. Second, capacity is usually not what binds: a closure partitioned by exclusive file ownership yielded four independent nodes against a proven-good 128, and a pressure test that told three fleets to run hot never exceeded five concurrent. The binding constraint is the width of the dependency graph, which is a property of the plan, and no lifted ceiling moves it.

How does a coordinator learn that peers are drawing on its window?

The directory for who, the ledgers for how much, because each already answers exactly half and neither answers the other. crew(view=directory) named five live coordinators across five repositories on 2026-09-14, live and costing nothing, which is the who. The ledgers carry promoted runs with their recorded token figures, which is the how much, and they lag by exactly the promotion beat. The gap this closes was measured the same day: a session moved the codex weekly window from 4 to 7 percent while two peer coordinators were live, and could not attribute any of that movement to itself, so it had to report the account-level rate and explicitly decline to claim the session's share. A single source would have left that gap open in one direction or the other.

§ Followups

Start with the advisory and the local measurement together

§2 and §5 are independent and should run in the same wave. §2 is buildable now, because the projected exhaustion it reports is already computed by the pre-flight and needs no new probe, and the metered window is where quota is being spent today. §5 gates any advice that points work at the local lane, and until its curve exists an advisory can say a lane is cheap but not whether it is free.

§3 follows §2, since there is nothing to record until an advisory exists. §4 needs §2's payload to carry the count. §6 goes last: it replaces the advisory's recommendation clause with a ledger-derived one, which is only worth doing once the clause has a shape. Settle the delivery point before §2 is dispatched; the other two decisions can be settled by the nodes that implement them.

THE RECOMMENDATION HAS THREE AXES, NOT TWO, AND ONLY TWO ARE CURRENTLY MEASURABLE. Quality is answered by rework charged per durable node, which the ledger already derives and which discriminates where pass rate cannot: measured 2026-09-08, the local lane runs 0.51 rework on implement work against the metered lane's 0.23, while both sit at zero on read-and-report work. Burn is answered by utilisation against a window, with a rate and a projected exhaustion the pre-flight already computes. Speed is the axis with no honest instrument yet: wall clock on a worker is dominated by local suite execution rather than by inference, so a median that mixes them ranks the machine rather than the model. The paired measurement taken today makes that concrete — one node ran 131 seconds on a loaded lane and 133 on a free one, so at small node sizes there is nothing to recover and a speed recommendation derived from wall clock would have been noise. Until tool time is recorded separately from generation, speed should be reported and not recommended on, which is what the speed-enters-routing decision has to settle.

A CONFIG DEFAULT OUTRANKS AN EXPLICIT FLAG, AND THIS PLAN IS WHERE THAT BELONGS, BECAUSE ITS WHOLE PREMISE IS ADVISING RATHER THAN OVERRIDING. Measured by causing it on 2026-09-14. A backend named in roles.implement.by_spec_level wins over --local on the same dispatch, so an operator asking for the local lane on an implement node silently received the metered one: --local with no member resolved codex-terra rather than clive, while the review role, which carries no overlay backend, resolved clive correctly from the same invocation. The lock was withdrawn the same day it was added and the lane is now chosen per dispatch instead. The measurement behind it stands - terra is 59% and 40% cheaper per durable node at exact and guided on the worker-plus-coordinator column - so what is missing is a mechanism for expressing a preferred lane that LOSES to an explicit flag, not a reason to stop preferring terra. The verified matrix after withdrawal, all five from dry runs of one node: --local with no member resolves clive; --local with a clive-harness member resolves clive; --local with a codex-harness member is refused naming both the backend the flag resolved and the harness the member declares; no flag and no member resolves clive; and --backend codex-terra resolves terra. So specifying the local lane now either lands on it or refuses, and never silently goes metered. The general rule for this plan's advisory surface: precedence must run explicit flag, then member declaration, then role or specification overlay, then host default, and a resolution that discards a higher-precedence input has to refuse rather than proceed. An advisory that can be silently overridden by configuration is the same defect as a lane that reports the wrong position - the caller acts on something other than what they asked for.
THE PREMISE OF THE LOCKED SCARCITY-SIGNAL DECISION CHANGED THIS AFTERNOON AND THE LOCK IS NOT REOPENED HERE, DELIBERATELY. Recording it so the next node to touch section 5 does not build against a figure that no longer exists. What was locked at 15:00Z: local-lane-scarcity-signal = in-flight-requests, on the reasoning that router admission is instantaneous in-flight requests rather than workers, with the admission figure to be measured later. At the time the local relay enforced AdmissionLimits with max_in_flight 2 and max_queued 4, keyed per consumer, and the consumer id fell back to client host joined with user-agent - so every Claude Code process on the login node shared one bucket of two. Measured at 16:37Z: seven workers across three coordinators, 48% of router requests refused, and the engine at 0.0% KV occupancy against max_num_seqs 1024. Nothing was saturated except a filter in front of it. What changed: the operator removed the bound entirely rather than fixing its keying. Verified independently at the source rather than taken on report - commit 76a6a23 'let the engine schedule; remove the router's request bound', zero occurrences of AdmissionLimits, _AdmissionController, max_in_flight or _consumer_id remaining in the agent modules, and an explicit TCPConnector limit replacing aiohttp's invisible default of 100 so that deleting one bound did not leave a lower undocumented one behind it. The reckon-09 session measured 8, 16 and 32 concurrent through the relay, served 32 of 32 with zero errors, against 435 of 1202 refused an hour earlier. WHAT THAT DOES TO THE DECISION. The choice of unit survives: worker count is still a bad proxy, because a worker reading files or running a suite is not making a request, and that argument never depended on the filter. What does not survive is the comparison. There is no relay-side admission constant any more; the only control left is the engine's own scheduler, which degrades by QUEUEING and never by refusing. So there is no rejection rate to count and no fixed number to count against, and an in-flight figure on its own advises nothing - it can say how many requests are outstanding and not whether that is many. The option this now favours is latency-curve, which section 5's own decision offered and which I argued against at 15:00Z on the grounds that it measures the consequence and needs a long observation before it can advise. That objection was sound while a crisp refusal signal existed to prefer instead. It does not survive the refusal signal being deleted: where degradation is graceful, the consequence IS the observable. WHY THIS IS A COMMENT AND NOT A REOPEN. An open non-deferred decision inside a closure makes its graph target refuse, and ship-lane-aware-20260914 is shipping this closure now with two members in flight. Reopening would block a peer's live wave to correct a decision whose implementing section is the deepest member and not yet being built. The staleness is recorded where the implementing node will read it; the reopen is the lead's call and costs a peer nothing if taken after their wave drains.
CORRECTING THE COMMENT WRITTEN FIFTEEN MINUTES AGO. It concluded that with the relay's refusal deleted there was no crisp scarcity signal left, so the latency-curve option now had the better case. That was an over-correction and it was wrong for a specific reason worth naming: I established that the RELAY no longer refuses and inferred from that that NOTHING refuses, without checking whether the engine publishes a signal of its own. It does. Running and Waiting are reported once per interval, and Waiting above zero is the queue forming - which is the observable equivalent of the refusal that was removed, sourced from the engine rather than configured in front of it. So the locked choice was right and only its rationale was stale. The decision is relocked in place with the source and the comparison rewritten rather than reopened, because the choice did not change and reopening would have refused a peer's live graph ship for a correction that does not alter what a node would build. The shape of my error is the one this session has been cataloguing all day, and it is the first time I have made it about an absence rather than a presence: I read the disappearance of one signal as the absence of any signal, because I checked the surface I had been looking at and not the one behind it. An unaimed instrument returns a confident zero; an instrument aimed at a layer that no longer owns the answer returns a confident nothing.
