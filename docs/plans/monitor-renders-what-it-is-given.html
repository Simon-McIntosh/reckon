§1 — A row that cannot tell an unrun probe from a pending one

An imas-ambix incident idled four workers for 24–47 minutes each. The reporting session spent the incident investigating a serving lane whose request queue had been 0.0 all night, because the monitor row read as a node waiting on a slow dependency. The information that would have collapsed the diagnosis in seconds is computed, and never reaches the reader.

recovery.py:1890-1897 computes wait_condition_state and wait_observed, which separate a probe that was never executed from one that ran and has not gone terminal. Those are different situations with different remedies, and the pane renders them identically.

This plan is scoped to that defect and the two sites it lives at. It does not inherit the incident's own diagnosis of why those runs stalled — see §6.

§5 — One letter, two quantities

ticker.py:230 maps "waiting": "q", so the compact counter 5q means five runs waiting. It is read as engine queue depth, on a screen that sits beside a model serve with a genuine request queue. Two quantities, one letter, adjacent on the display. This is the cheapest of the three repairs and independent of the other two.

§5 — Landed: the counter spells its own word

The waiting counter renders 3queued instead of 3q. The decision waiting-counter-letter locked spell-out-queued, and STAT_LETTER now spells that one bucket's name while the other three keep their initial. The compact rule is intact — a counter is its count followed by a short form of its bucket's name — and what q failed at is visible in the table itself: w, b and u are the initial of the word the state column already prints for that bucket — working, blocked, unpromoted — so a reader learns the mapping off any other row in the pane. q was the initial of queued, a word no column printed, so nothing on the surface could teach it and it read as request-queue depth beside a model serve that has a genuine one. The comment above the table claimed the pane taught every entry in the state column; that was true of three and false of the fourth, and it now says so and names the waiting bucket as the exception.

Width is the cost, and the reason pays it. The spelled cell is five columns wider, so the counter block goes from 15 columns to 20 and its floor, MIN_WIDTH, from 139 to 144. A row is still exactly the width it was asked for at 180 and at 208 columns, because the reason cell absorbs what remains; the block holds one width as counts change — zero, nine, ten and ninety-nine occupy the same columns — so the row's right edge never moves; and a zero is still rendered rather than dropped and dimmed, which is what lets a reader watch a drain reach it.

The falsifier. The substring queued does not occur anywhere in the row the base revision renders, and the base revision maps the bucket to q, so all three assertions added here fail against it and pass with the change. Measured over the three files this section's work touches, run with the same invocation at both revisions: 98 passed at base 7dfae503, 101 at head, the delta being exactly the three added assertions and zero added failures. The three failures at base are recorded in the §5 counter evidence anchor.

§6 — A dry run that returns before the thing it predicts

The same shape at a third site, in resumption.py's sweep():

if dry_run:
    resumed.append({**entry, "would_resume": True, ...})
    continue
try:
    launched = _resume(...)

The dry-run branch returns before the launcher is ever called, so every guard the launcher applies is unreachable in a dry run. would_resume: True means the sweep would call the launcher, not the launcher would resume. Two different claims sharing one name, and the weaker one is reported.

Measured consequence from the same incident: a hand crew resume-ready --dry-run reported checked 4, would_resume 4, skipped 0 for four runs a real sweep refuses. Acting on that output would have attempted to resume four actively-working sessions. A dry run whose purpose is to predict the real action over-reports systematically, in the direction that causes harm.

Two repairs are available and either suffices: consult the launcher's guards before the dry-run return, or have the dry run report what the launcher would say rather than what it would be asked. The second is the smaller change and the more honest name.

Verification limit, carried deliberately. The branch structure is confirmed in source. The launcher's live-session guard is reported, not verified — it comes from another coordinator's CLI output reading resume-refused because the process is live, which is their reading of their own run. The finding does not rest on it: a dry run that returns before the thing it predicts cannot predict anything that thing does, whatever its guards turn out to be.

§6 — Landed

The first repair, guard consultation, was implemented. The rename was the smaller change and the plan names it as the more honest one, but it fixes the wording and not the harm: the damage was an operator acting on a list of runs a real sweep refuses, and only consulting the guards empties that list. A dry run that cannot answer the question the operator is asking, under a more honest name, is still unable to answer it.

_launcher_refusal() in resumption.py mirrors the guards resume_plan() applies — the run must be a CLI-spawned run, its process must have stopped, and its lane's budget verdict must not hold it — and is consulted before the dry-run return. A refusal is reported through the same formatter the real sweep uses, so a prediction and the action it predicts name a refusal identically. The consult writes nothing: the launcher's ledger hold-check and its context_fit pointer stamp are the traces of an attempt, and a prediction must not leave them.

Parity is held by a test that asks the launcher and the prediction about the same record and requires the same exception and message, rather than by asserting each side separately — two surfaces compared, not two copies asserted about.

One failure is newly visible in an adjacent test, and it is not re-swallowed. test_a_met_dead_wait_is_walked_by_the_resume_sweep builds a pointer with no launch field, which resume_plan() refuses as not a spawned run — so a real sweep never resumes it either, and the test's would_resume is True was true only of the sweep's intention. That fixture rests on the same fail-open this section closes; it needs launch: "cli" added, and that file lies outside this node's write scope, so it is reported rather than edited. Weakening the guard to keep it green would restore the defect.

§8 — Order, and the falsifier that enforces it

§3 lands before §4. A renderer-only repair delivers next_action and lifting_condition, leaves an unrun probe and a pending one rendering identically, and would be reported as addressing the incident's cause. That is worse than not shipping: it closes the visible half and reports the diagnostic half closed with it.

The measure that enforces the order is a paired assertion:

  • Falsifier: a run whose wait_condition_state is never-executed renders a different row from one that is pending. This fails if only §4 lands, because the field never arrives.
  • Positive control: the same test asserts next_action reaches the row. Without it the falsifier can pass vacuously on a build where nothing renders at all.

Neither half is sufficient alone, which is the property this plan's measures are built around.

§9 — What the word abandoned measured

The lead's review of two live panes asked whether an abandoned row was a false positive: the worker may have finished and failed only to leave a manifest the classifier accepts. The census is recorded at What Abandoned Means in Practice, with its per-run evidence at the abandoned census anchor.

The measurement does not establish that the name is wrong. Over 270 abandoned transitions and 224 distinct runs across five projects: every run directory resolves (224/224), but only 55 worktrees still do, so 169 runs (75.4%) are neither for nor against — the work is neither present nor absent. Of the 48 whose base resolves, 33 hold commits beyond base and 24 hold commits dated inside the run's own window. A substantial minority demonstrably finished: 21 runs wrote a status: complete manifest inside their own window before the label landed.

The sample pools two classifier generations. The arm refusing abandoned when a run holds commits beyond its base (recovery.py:1660) landed in e662009c at 2026-09-14T21:57Z; 217 of the 270 transitions predate it. On the 38 post-guard runs, two still hold a commit inside their own window, so the guard is neither shown broken nor shown to hold universally — its source reads record['worktree'] and record['base_sha'] from the live pointer, so a pointer missing either field returns zero.

So abandoned has been an accurate statement about the process and an unreliable one about the work, most sharply before the guard landed. Whether it remains unreliable now cannot be settled from this population, which is contaminated and half-unresolvable; it needs a prospective sample taken while worktrees still exist. No repair and no rename is proposed — the question was only whether the work was finished. This is a measurement, not a section of the renderer, and it changes no row.

§10 — The critical-path coverage cell, and why it waits

The counter block answers how much work is in flight and says nothing about which work. A fleet can read 7w all day while the plan on the critical path has nobody on it — measured on nova, 2026-09-18: the roadmap ranked the critical path first for a whole sprint while twelve runs closed plans already between 85 and 99 percent complete, and the path itself carried zero workers. A coverage cell on the row is what makes that visible where a coordinator already looks, rather than in a report nobody opens mid-wave.

This section owns the cell and not the figure, and the split is the project's rule rather than a preference here. The coverage figure is a derived fact a reader without a pane would also want, so it is computed once in Python and consumed by the surface. Deriving it a second time inside the renderer would be two implementations of one truth — the same defect with a longer fuse, because nothing fails when the copies drift.

The figure belongs to work-scheduling-surface §3 — roadmap coverage per open path, computed from live pointers whose process is alive rather than from transition events. That section is queued behind its own plan's push-status node because the two share reckon/roadmap.py. Its coordinator offered to let this cell read live pointers directly and to have §3 adopt the same helper afterwards. That offer is declined: it places the first implementation of a derived fact inside a renderer and then asks the owning module to follow it. Waiting costs a queue position and buys one derivation.

Done when the row carries a coverage cell for the critical path whose value comes from the roadmap's own derivation rather than from any figure computed in ticker.py, asserted against a fixture in which coverage and the working count disagree — they must differ, or the assertion passes on a renderer showing the wrong number.

Measured cost, in this session, to another fleet. A peer coordinator running a concurrency measurement asked for ascent and retirement stamps in UTC. Ten were sent: five read from plan records and correct, the rest transcribed off monitor rows with a Z appended by hand and wrong by two hours. The same message quoted the rule a stamp from a record is safe, a stamp a coordinator typed is only as good as the coordinator, and then broke it.

Why the mixed set is the dangerous shape. A uniformly shifted set is detectable from any single row and corrected with one subtraction — another fleet sent exactly that earlier the same day and it was caught immediately. A mixed set is not detectable that way: the correct rows sit beside the wrong ones and vouch for them, so nothing inside the set contradicts anything else, and a reader who spot-checks one row clears the whole thing. The peer caught it only because one reported retirement was in the future, which is the one thing a clock cannot do.

That falsifier is worth keeping wherever stamps are published: is any stamp in the future? It costs one comparison and it catches the case that internal consistency cannot.

Where I would push back on the lead

promoted should not become merged. The reasoning offered was that merging is what the orchestrator is doing — but promotion and merging are two distinct steps in a fixed order, and the state tracks the second. A coordinator merges the worker's commits into the primary branch, then runs crew complete, which records the ledger row. That happened thirteen times today, always in that order, and the merge is frequently hours before the promotion. Renaming promoted to merged would name the state after the step that already happened rather than the one it records, and would make a genuinely accurate name wrong.

If the concern is that promote reads as ceremony rather than work, the honest alternative is recorded, which is literally what the step does.

A state rename must fence every asserting file in one scope: a previous attempt here took four nodes and three resumes because the fence was drawn narrower than the vocabulary's reach, including one file that read emitted names out of source by regex. Measured on another project, and it is the mechanism behind “the monitor shows nothing”. A peer session probed its own 1.9 MB event stream after losing a node, and the result refutes the obvious explanation while producing a better one. Counting only transitions whose observed_at is later than a run's own resume log mtime, 9 resumed runs emitted 16 post-resume transitions, every one of them non-zero. So resumption does not silence a follower, which was the standing hypothesis and is now retired.

The silence is a conjunction of four behaviours, each defensible alone. A worker writes a placeholder wait stanza at orientation and never rewrites it — four distinct wordings were logged in one night, including the self-satisfying wait_probe: ["true"] with wait_terminal: exit:0. The recovery sweep treats waiting as eligible and relaunches it, which is reasonable. State change is the only event trigger, so a run whose manifest is frozen emits nothing for its entire working life. And when the follower is torn down the watch reports no events delivered, which is true and reads as nothing was happening.

The timeline is exact. Dispatched 03:44:51. Placeholder manifest written 03:45:37, which is the last state change the run ever produced. Its coordinator died at 05:46:38. A sweep relaunched it at 09:50:44 and it worked for thirty minutes. At 10:20:43 a monitor expiry took the follower and the worker together, mid-edit, with a modified source file, an untracked test and zero commits. Manifest mtime at the end: 05:45:36. Status was waiting at 03:45 and still waiting at 10:20. Events for that run across its whole life: 2. Events project-wide during its thirty-minute working window: 0.

The follower was correct throughout. It emits on state change and the state never changed. That is what makes this a surface defect rather than a bug: every component did what it was specified to do, and the composition rendered a working run indistinguishable from an idle fleet.

What it asks of this plan. A liveness signal keyed on newest-stream mtime rather than on state change covers the whole class, including the case with no teardown at all — a frozen manifest plus a long node is silence on its own. It is the same correction that retired process_alive as a liveness test: the pointer's pid is the launching process and exits while the worker runs on, so stream freshness is the fact and every proxy for it has now failed at least once.

The lead's finding, and it names what the counter block is for. A row ending 2w· 2b· 6u· 1q carries two quantities that are the orchestrator's inbox and two that are not, in one visual grammar that cannot tell them apart. b and u are work waiting on the coordinator: a blocked run needs a decision, an unpromoted one needs merging, reviewing or recording. w and q are work in progress and need nothing. All four render identically, so the two that demand action do not stand out from the two that forbid it.

Nothing re-raises the inbox, which is what turns it into wallpaper. The follower emits on state change, so a run that became unpromoted at 03:00 produced one transition and is silent from then on. Its count persists in the block on every subsequent row — always present, never new — and a figure that is always there stops being read. This is the same mechanism recorded at c-a-frozen-manifest-is-invisible-for-its-whole-working-life seen from the other end: there a run was invisible because its state never changed, here a backlog is invisible because its count never changes either.

Measured on this session rather than argued. Nine runs accumulated unreconciled across one working day while the coordinator read every row the follower emitted. Four sat in scoring with their code already merged into the main branch and their ledger not knowing it, the oldest for fourteen hours. Two were review runs that had died writing no record. The coordinator's own account when asked was two are being handled, seven are not — and the counters had been on screen throughout.

What this asks for. The actionable count needs to read differently from the in-flight count, and it needs a way to escalate on age rather than only on change, because the transition that created it is the only event it will ever produce. The same argument applies to the cross-project case: a coverage cell on the row is subject to this exactly as the inbox is, so it should be designed against this finding rather than beside it.

A stronger form of the finding above, caught by the lead while the drain was running. The coordinator reported that the blocked count had moved from two to one. It had — on exactly one row, for a few seconds. By the next row it read two again, and the lead's screen showed two throughout. Both statements were true of the same display.

What happened between those rows is the point: one blocked item was recorded and cleared, and a different run entered the same bucket moments later. The count was stable while its membership turned over completely. A total can express how many and never which, so it cannot distinguish a backlog being worked from a backlog standing still — and those are the only two states a reader of that cell is trying to tell apart.

The consequence misleads in both directions. A coordinator who clears an item sees no movement and concludes the work did not land. A reader watching a steady 2b for an hour cannot tell whether it is the same two runs untouched or twenty runs passing through.

And the two were not comparable in kind. Measured at that moment, one was a dead review correctly recorded, and its replacement was a healthy worker whose stream had been written 162 seconds earlier and whose manifest carried a placeholder waiting stanza from orientation — the defect recorded at c-a-frozen-manifest-is-invisible-for-its-whole-working-life. The cell counted a real item and a false one identically, and the false one arrived because of a defect this plan already owns.

What this asks for, beyond the previous comment. The actionable cell needs identity or age, not only a total: the oldest item's age, or a count of items unchanged since the reader last saw them. A number that reads the same for “two items, untouched, fourteen hours” and “two items, both new since your last row” is not reporting the quantity a coordinator acts on.
