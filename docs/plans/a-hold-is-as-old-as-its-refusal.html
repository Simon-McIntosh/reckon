§1 — The fix that landed can be undone by reading it

A spend hold used to be immortal. A refusal that named no reset time was stored with no expiry, so it held its lane for as long as the process lived — measured on 2026-09-03 at five hours and forty-nine minutes past its own observation, against a direct probe of the same account that returned a served turn with real token accounting. An ageing rule landed the same afternoon: a judgement carrying no reset time stops being read as current once its observation is older than a declared shelf life, and the lane returns to unknown headroom rather than exhausted.

That rule has a defect, found by a peer session within the hour and verified here. The age is measured from the pointer's observed_at, and observe rewrites that stamp whenever it re-reads a run's stream. The stream still carries the original rejection, so re-observing a refused run re-stamps an old refusal as a fresh one. A coordinator investigating why its lane is held extends the hold by looking. Their sentence for it is better than mine: a stale hold can be renewed by probing the wrong instrument.

Two further properties were measured alongside it and both belong here. The hold is per backend, and so is its cure: one lane went from held at 100.0% exhausted to clear at 1.21% the moment a single resume was served and observed on it, while its sibling stayed pinned at the stale figure because nothing had been served there. And the fence has no lever at all: budget.policy() honours evidence_shelf_life_minutes, the schema does not declare it, so the key is refused as an unknown input — while the ceiling is capped at 100 and utilisation reads exactly 100.0, so no ceiling override lifts it either. The only exit is waiting out a hardcoded hour.

§2 — The age is taken from the refusal, not from the last read

The age now comes from the newest rejected rate_limit_event in the run's stream and its closest immutable surrounding timestamp, published as age_source; the mutable pointer observed_at survives only to pick the freshest reading. 62 passed against a 61-passed pre-feature gate, asserted through budget.preflight — the same decide path dispatch uses — with the observe-then-decide sequence as one case: 100 repeated observations leave both the timestamp and the stale verdict unchanged, a stale refusal releases immediately after an observe rather than being renewed by it, a fresh refusal still holds despite a run created three hours earlier, and an unparseable stream reports created_at-lower-bound explicitly. §3 — The shelf life is declared, so the fence has a lever

Declared in reckon/schema/flight.yaml with its meaning rather than only its type — the description states that the field bounds how long a refusal naming no reset keeps describing the present, that a value at or below zero restores the indefinite hold, and that it has no effect on a refusal that named a reset. Verified at the entry point an operator holding an incident actually uses: reckon crew preflight --project reckon --set budget.evidence_shelf_life_minutes=1 is accepted and reports evidence_shelf_life_minutes: 1.0 in its resolved policy, where it previously failed as an extra input. Landed under commit 30a4460, which wrote no comment against this section — found by the currency audit against the tree rather than the page.

§ Decisions

Which immutable stamp dates a refusal?

Locked 2026-09-04 by the lead. The rate-limit event's own position in the run's stream, read with the surrounding records' timestamps, is the only candidate that is the refusal itself rather than a bound on it, and it is immutable under re-reading - the sole property the renewal defect turns on. The run's created_at was rejected because it dates the run: a long run refused near its end reads far older than its refusal, which ages a hold out early and so under-holds a lane that is genuinely spent, the wrong direction to be wrong in. A first write of the budget record was rejected because it dates when we noticed rather than when we were refused, and every record already written carries no such stamp, so existing holds would have no age until something rewrote them. The accepted cost is that the decide path reads the stream, and a stream carrying no parseable rate-limit event needs a stated fallback rather than a silent zero - name created_at as that fallback explicitly, so an unparseable stream reads as a lower bound and never as a fresh refusal.

§ Followups

Declare the key first, because it is the lever an operator needs tonight

§3 goes first even though §2 is the deeper defect, and the ordering is deliberate: declaring the configuration key is the only change here that gives an operator holding a live incident a way out, and it is a schema declaration plus its regeneration rather than a behaviour change. Until it lands, a stale hold is escapable only by waiting out a hardcoded hour.

§2 follows and is the load-bearing correction, since without it the ageing rule can be reset by the act of investigating why a lane is held. Both touch reckon/budget.py, so they are one write scope and must be sequenced rather than run together; §3 also reaches the LinkML source and its generated schema, which are regenerated by script and never edited by hand. §4 is independent of both and touches the dispatch refusal message.

Every node must assert through the call preflight and dispatch actually make rather than through a helper, and §2's measure has to include the observe-then-decide sequence as a single case — the defect is invisible to a test that decides once, which is why the ageing rule shipped with it.

So a stranded pointer nobody owns fenced every resume in the project. FIRST WRONG THING: THE TWO PURPOSES DISAGREE ABOUT ONE READING. Dispatch and resume differ only by a reserve - the ceiling less a reserve for dispatch, the full ceiling for resume - which was meant to protect a stuck worker's ability to be answered by withholding headroom from new work. At a recorded 100.0 that inverts: dispatch opens on the reserve while resume, the cheaper and more valuable call, is the one refused. The purpose that exists to keep a worker answerable is the purpose that cannot answer it. SECOND WRONG THING, AND IT IS THE ONE TO FIX: A PER-RUN REFUSAL IS BEING READ AS A PER-PROJECT FACT.
