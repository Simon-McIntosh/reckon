§1 — Why: the control loop is slower than the work it controls

The local lane is kept full by coordinators dispatching, the admission service launching, and landings releasing the sections that wait on them. On 16 November each of those steps took minutes: coordinators reported single dispatches of three to ten minutes, a three-plan declaration of 26 minutes, and a roadmap read that did not answer in seven. A lane cannot be filled faster than its control loop turns.

Most of that ground is owned. the-monitor-reads-each-fact-once holds the station's tick, the repeated decode of the live pointers and the landing check; the-monitor-tells-the-truth-under-load holds the station's beat and its per-stage timings; history-persists-as-detail-washes-out holds the activity-bounded store read; delivered-work-lands-without-a-coordinator holds the promotion queue's order, its rate and the reload cadence. This plan takes only the costs none of them owns, as measured in where dispatch, admission and landing spend their minutes.

§2 — A dispatch checks the claims its own paths can meet

Why. Every dispatch pays for a census of every claim in every repository before it asks whether any of them touches its own paths. At 86 s a call, queueing a twenty-section plan one call at a time is half an hour, and a coordinator's dispatch timeout is routinely reached.

Deliverable. The launch paths already narrow their scope check to the candidate's paths and repositories; the preview and plan-validation path still asks for a report over every claim. Every scope check a dispatch makes, on every path, costs what the candidate node's paths can meet, not what the fleet holds, through the narrowing that already exists. Its verdicts are the same as today's for every candidate, and an exclusive-path conflict is refused exactly as it is now.

Done when. A test builds a temporary fleet of 240 pointers across three repositories. For a candidate whose paths can meet claims in one repository only, the scope check completes in at most 6 s and at most 20 git subprocess calls, and its verdicts equal a fresh exhaustive reference. A candidate claiming a figure directory another live run holds is refused with the same refusal as at the base. Negative control: at the base revision the same candidate exceeds the subprocess bound; removing the conflicting claim removes the refusal. Three previews on the fleet node have a median under 30 s and create no worktree and no queued request. The fleet-node previews are the only step that runs there; the test runs on all_debug with at most -n 4, and importers add no failure against the base. Write paths: reckon/crew/dispatch_plan.py, reckon/crew/dispatch_claims.py and tests/test_dispatch_preview_cost.py.

§3 — Promotion ordering lists the live pointers once

Why. Each pass over the promotion queue re-lists the live pointers once for every review request that names its source by node. The cost grows with the queue, and the queue is longest exactly when landings matter most.

Deliverable. The resolver already accepts a shared pointer population that its callers do not supply. One pass over the queue lists the live pointers at most once, a pass with no review request lists none, and the other callers that resolve a reviewed run inside a loop share one listing the same way. The order and the reviewed-run identities are unchanged.

Done when. A test orders 100 queued requests over a temporary population of 300 pointers: 20 reviews naming a source node, reviews naming a complete run id, non-reviews and one ambiguous source. The pass lists the pointers at most once and returns the reference order and identities; a pass with only non-reviews lists none. Negative control: the base makes at least 20 listings for the source-node cohort. On three matched trials against a copy of the store, the read plus ordering takes at most a quarter of the base. This section waits on the order change in delivered-work-lands-without-a-coordinator §2, which rewrites the same function. Runs on all_debug; importers add no failure against the base. Write paths: reckon/crew/promotion_requests.py, reckon/crew/recovery_review_delivery.py and tests/test_promotion_order_reads_one_population.py.

§4 — One slow project does not hold the others' refresh

Why. The refresh stage has no time bound, and it is where a four to seven minute tick sits. What each project's refresh reads is owned elsewhere. What is not owned is the schedule: eighteen projects are refreshed in turn, so a slow read in one delays the obligations of all, and a project's index trailed its head by over three hours on 16 November.

Deliverable. The refresh stage returns within a stated budget. A project whose refresh has finished is published; a project still reading is answered from its last result, marked stale with its age by the marking the roadmap snapshot already uses, never as a fresh empty answer and never through a new answer state. What an unchanged project reads is owned by the-monitor-reads-each-fact-once and is not part of this section.

Done when. A test holds one temporary project's input read open indefinitely while a second project's result is ready. The stage returns within 10 s, publishes the second project's result equal to an independent cold derivation, and answers the first from its last result marked stale with its age. Negative control: at the base the held read keeps the finished project unreported past 10 s. Runs on all_debug; importers add no failure against the base. Write paths: reckon/crew/station.py, reckon/crew/station_obligations.py and tests/test_project_refresh_returns_within_its_budget.py.

§5 — A plan write returns readiness without rebuilding the roadmap twice

Why. A write that names a section declaration answers with two fields, readiness and closure blockers, and builds the whole roadmap twice to get them. Recording a wave of landings is a run of such writes, each holding its coordinator for minutes.

Deliverable. The write's two returned fields are read through the roadmap snapshot that already keys an answer on plan state, the branch head, the store's write counter and the live runs, in place of composing a roadmap ahead of it. A metadata or text write, and a declaration write that changes nothing, build no roadmap. A declaration write that changes readiness builds at most one. The two returned fields equal a fresh roadmap read taken after the write, and an answer is never reused across a change to the written plan or to anything its readiness depends on.

Done when. A test writes to a temporary project of several plans and counts roadmap builds: none for a metadata write and for an unchanged declaration, at most one for a declaration that changes readiness. The returned fields equal an independent roadmap after the write, in cases that include an upstream plan changing and a promoted run not yet landed. Negative control: at the base the unchanged declaration builds a roadmap, and a deliberately stale reused report fails the equality check. On the recorded project fixture the work outside the landing check takes at most 5 s. Runs on rigel for the fixture and all_debug for the test; importers add no failure against the base. Write paths: reckon/mcp_edit_plan.py, reckon/mcp_deadlines.py and tests/test_plan_write_returns_current_readiness.py.

§6 — Reading one plan's runs costs that plan's runs

Why. A read of one plan's runs visits the store's whole history. During one roadmap build the store was read 145 times for 179 s. The history only grows, so every plan-scoped read gets slower with each landing.

Deliverable. The store already indexes its query keys in the hot store and the archive; the plan-selected read is brought under that one query contract, with no second index beside it. A read selected by plan visits a number of stored payloads proportional to that plan's runs, and returns exactly what the exhaustive read returns, from the hot store and its archive alike. A read of every record is unchanged, and an unreadable store is still reported as a failure, never as an empty plan.

Done when. A test reads one plan from a temporary hot-and-archive store of 20,000 rows of which 50 match. It visits at most a constant multiple of 50 payloads and returns the exhaustive reference, including rows whose plan identity comes from a node definition or a moved implementation. A changed plan identity, a replaced store and a newly archived row are all seen. Negative control: at the base the read visits the historical population; a reference with one matching archived row removed fails the equality check. The selected read takes under half the matched base time. Runs on all_debug; importers of the store add no failure against the base, measured on rigel. Write paths: reckon/run_store.py and tests/test_plan_selected_store_read_is_bounded.py.

§7 — An admission receipt names the reads inside each stage

Why. A freed slot is refilled only when a pass reaches it. One pass took 81.6 s, of which 79.9 s was releasing upstream waits and resolving holds before a single launch decision, and nine earlier passes ran from 72 to 472 s. Each receipt already records a time for its release, wait, launch and landing stages. What it does not record is which reads those stages made, so the cause of 80 s is not known. work-flows-to-the-lane §77 set the target of a pass filling the free slots within a minute and is still open; this section waits on it and measures what its receipts leave.

Deliverable. The existing per-pass receipt carries, inside its release and wait stages, each read with its count and its time. No second receipt is added.

Done when. A test runs one pass over a temporary crew home of 240 pointers and 80 requests and finds one receipt whose per-read times sum to each stage's time within 5 %, each read named. Negative control: a receipt with one read omitted fails the sum check. Ten consecutive receipts from the live service after landing are recorded in the evidence with their largest read named. The temporary-home run is on rigel; importers add no failure against the base, on all_debug. Write paths: reckon/crew/admission/service.py and tests/test_an_admission_receipt_names_its_reads.py.

§8 — The review of a delivered run is requested inside its window

Why. A delivered run cannot land until it is reviewed, and the review is requested by the crew. the-monitor-reads-each-fact-once §28 gave that request a window of 120 s that a slow refresh must not hold, and is landed. Over the last 50 delivered runs of 16 November the launch still followed delivery by a median of 17.5 minutes and a 90th percentile of 96.9, and 9 had no review launched. The cause is not established: the request may be late, or made on time and then waiting for a lane slot or a worker. Coordinators dispatched reviews by hand all day.

Deliverable. For every delivered run, the review is requested within 120 s of the delivery being recorded, and the time from request to launch is recorded apart from it, so a late request and a slow launch are told apart. A delivered run with no review requested after ten minutes is an obligation naming the reason. A review request takes the priority of the run it reviews.

Done when. The stage receipts of delivered-work-lands-without-a-coordinator §13 give, for each of the last 50 delivered runs on the live fleet, the delivery, request and launch instants: the request follows delivery within 120 s at the 90th percentile, and every run without a request carries an obligation with its reason. A test delivers a run in a temporary crew home while a stand-in tick is held for ten minutes and finds the review requested within 120 s on a controlled clock, with the run's priority; a second case withholds the review's precondition and finds the obligation at ten minutes. Negative control: with the request made to wait for the tick, the first case fails. If the census shows requests on time and launches late, the section records that split and the launch delay becomes a section of its own. The census runs on rigel; the test on all_debug; importers add no failure against the base. Write paths: reckon/crew/recovery_review_dispatch.py, reckon/crew/resumption.py and tests/test_a_delivered_run_is_reviewed_within_minutes.py.

§9 — Shown on the fleet: a coordinator's hour

Why. Each section is proved in a temporary home. The lane fills only if the live operations are fast.

Deliverable. The measurements of the research record repeated on the live fleet after §2 to §8 and §10 have landed, beside its baseline.

Done when. The evidence record holds, measured the same way as the baseline: three dispatch previews, ten admission receipts, one declaration write's readiness read, one plan-selected store read, and the review-launch census for 24 hours. The dispatch median is under 30 s, the admission median under 20 s, the readiness read under 30 s in total, and the review launch 90th percentile under 10 minutes. The figure of §1 is redrawn with both sets on one axis. The section closes only when every bound is met; a measure that misses its bound leaves the section open, recorded with the stage that caused it. A measurement that read fewer than three previews or ten receipts fails as unread. Heavy steps run on rigel. Write paths: docs/evidence/archive/dispatch-admission-and-landing-take-seconds-landed.html and docs/figures/control-loop-latency-after.

§10 — An admission pass decides its launches within 20 seconds

Why. §7 names the reads that make an 80 s pass. This section removes them.

Deliverable. A pass at this workstation's population, about 240 live pointers and 80 queued or waiting requests, decides its launches within 20 s of starting, with the same launches and releases as today.

Done when. A test runs one pass over a temporary crew home of 240 pointers and 80 requests: it completes within 20 s and its launch and release decisions equal those of the base pass on the same home. Negative control: the base pass on that home exceeds 20 s. Ten consecutive receipts from the live service after landing have a median pass time under 20 s; the ten receipts before 22:05 UTC on 16 November are the baseline. The temporary-home runs are on rigel; importers add no failure against the base, on all_debug. Write paths: reckon/crew/admission/service.py, reckon/crew/admission/selection.py and tests/test_an_admission_pass_decides_within_its_budget.py.
