§1 — The rows are scoped and the numbers are not

reckon crew follow --session S selects which runs it reports, and every line it prints ends with three figures partitioning the whole project's fleet. A reader who asked for one session's runs is shown a count that includes every other session's workers, with nothing on the line saying so.

Measured on this workstation at 07:30 on 2026-09-15: a session with exactly one run in flight was reading 6w · 1b · 0u. The six were four sessions' work; the one blocked row belonged to a session that had stopped eighteen hours earlier and had already been dispositioned by its owner. Neither figure was wrong about the fleet and neither was about the reader.

This matters more than a cosmetic mismatch because of what a coordinator does with those numbers. The closure discipline says a session does not end while work remains, and the posture line is what a reader checks before believing the fleet is quiet. A scoped follower showing other sessions' workers reads as work still running when the reader has none, and — the dangerous direction — a reader whose own last worker has stopped sees a non-zero working count and waits for a transition that will never come, because the workers it is counting are not the ones it is following.

§2 — Count the population the reader asked for

The session is already threaded through the follow path: the command passes it to the generator, and the renderer already uses it to decide whether a row carries an owner glyph. Only the counts ignore it. When a session is given, the partition is computed over that session's live pointers; with no session the behaviour is unchanged, because an unscoped follower is watching the fleet and its figures are about the fleet.

The counted population and the reported rows then come from one selection rather than two, which is what stops them disagreeing again when either changes.

Done when a scoped follower's trailing figures count only the runs of the session it was given, asserted against a fixture holding runs from two sessions where the two populations differ — the assertion fails if the counts are taken over the fleet, and both the scoped and unscoped totals are stated as digits. An unscoped follower's figures are unchanged over the same fixture, asserted rather than assumed, since the unscoped path is the one every peer coordinator is reading right now. The fixture includes a foreign run that is blocked, because the observed defect was a blocked count a reader could not act on.

§3 — Out of scope

The grid, the column widths and the row text are not touched; this changes which runs are counted, not how a line is drawn. The renderer is held by another session's node at the time of writing and needs no change for this.

Whether the figures should carry their own age is a separate question. They are a snapshot taken when a transition printed, and a fleet that goes quiet leaves the last line's numbers on screen indefinitely — correct when printed, silently stale afterwards. That is a real defect and it is not this one; fixing the population first means the number that goes stale is at least the reader's own.

§ Decisions

When a follower observes additional sessions, do their runs join the counts?

A scoped follower counts its own session and nothing else, and the unscoped reading stays available for the fleet-wide question, so neither question loses its answer — they stop sharing one. Measured on this workstation on 2026-09-17: one lane carried twenty-one live worker processes across five coordinator sessions, of which four belonged to the reader. A scoped follower counting everything it observed would have reported a figure that was eighty-one percent other people's work, including blocked runs the reader had no authority to touch, which is the defect this plan names — a blocked figure the reader could do nothing about. The fleet-wide count remains the right instrument for cross-session contention and was the one actually needed when a peer asked how much of the lane was ours, but that is a different question asked by a different reader.
