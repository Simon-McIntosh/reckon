§1 — Why: a codex review lost every race for a seat, and the queue drained at one launch a minute

On 14 November the lead ruled that nova's inverse-equilibrium-baseline §1 is reviewed on codex, the fast lane kept as an escape hatch for work the local lane should not carry. The review was refused seven times between 20:51 and 21:21 UTC: four times at the placement roster's cap (25 workers already occupy the placement reservation's roster, whose cap is 25) and twice at the reservation's cores bound (28 of 28 cores admitted). One reservation (reckon/crew/placement.py, RESERVATION_ROSTER_LIMIT) seats every lane's placed workers. The admit service launches a queued local request the moment a seat frees, while a dispatch named to a metered lane cannot queue at all, because admission launches only the local lane. So every freed seat went to local work first, and the codex request could win one only if a retry happened to land in the gap.

The queue itself drained slowly. The admission pass that ended at 21:14:17Z spent 1,046 s launching, its oldest admissible request had waited 22,959 s, and 15 requests were marked stalled. A one-minute profile of the admit service sampled nothing but _adjacent_live_peers: every launch parsed the Python files of every live peer to decide adjacency, again for each peer pair and each launch. Seats free only as fast as workers finish, and a request reaches a freed seat only as fast as a pass launches, so a slow pass costs the whole fleet.

Reviews and implementations also compete in one queue with nothing to keep their cadence matched. A delivered run waits on its review before it can land, so a review that queues behind a wave of implementations holds finished work. And the same evening the sweep filed a review-self promotion request on a review its coordinator had held, moving a request that never launched out of the queue for good.

§5 — A request for a named lane queues, and is admitted into the reserve ahead of the local queue

Why. §2 makes room, but a codex request still gets a seat only by being dispatched at the moment one is free. A coordinator should state the request once and have it run when room and the lane allow, as local work already does.

Deliverable. A dispatch naming a lane other than the local one, refused only by the roster or cores bound, is stored as a queued request exactly as a held local dispatch is. The admit service launches such a request into the reserved seats, and launches a request carrying an explicit priority override (crew queue reprioritise) before ordinary work of its lane. A named lane under a budget hold keeps its request queued and says so on the request, and nothing is rebound to another lane without the coordinator. The ordering reads the priority that a-session-spends-a-priority-budget defines; this section adds no budget of its own.

Done when. A second case shows a local request with a priority override launches ahead of an earlier local request without one. A third shows a codex request under a budget hold stays queued with the hold named. This section also carries queued-work-needs-no-workaround §17, moved here on 14 November, and its cases: a codex dispatch at the cores bound or the roster cap is queued; a local dispatch that falls back to codex on context fit and meets the cap is queued, and its output names the context-fit shortfall, which the roster message hid from nova-09; a refused dispatch never prints a created run id without a pointer; and a local dispatch that passed the queueing pre-check but meets the bound at the later check in preparation is queued, not refused with nothing queued (D179). Once named-lane requests queue, the reserve is held only while one waits or a named dispatch met the bounds within the last pass, so local work fills every seat when no escape-lane work is waiting: the router reads about 0.43 requests per worker, so a standing three-seat reserve leaves local cards idle. §6 — Reviews and implementations are admitted at a matched cadence

Why. A delivered run lands only after its review, so a review queued behind a wave of implementations holds finished work, while a wave of reviews holds back new work. Both are wanted, at a similar cadence.

Deliverable. When reviews and implementations both wait, each admission pass shares the seats it fills between the two roles in proportion to how many of each are waiting, with no role taking fewer than one seat in three, and among reviews the review of a delivered run goes first. Fair use between sessions still applies inside each role. The share is a named constant, and the pass records, per role, how many it launched and the age of the oldest it left waiting, so the cadence can be read.

Done when. A test queues nine implementations and three reviews with three free seats and shows one pass launches at least one review and at least one implementation, the delivered run's review first. A second case with only one role waiting fills every seat from it. A measurement over one evening of live passes, read from admission-verdicts.jsonl, reports launches and oldest waiting age per role before and after. Write paths: reckon/crew/admission/, reckon/crew/queue_order.py and the new test.

§7 — An admission pass is bounded, and says what each launch cost

Why. One pass ran 1,046 s launching. A pass that runs long holds every freed seat until it ends, and its stalls hide which step was slow.

Deliverable. A pass launches until a stated time budget (60 s by default, from flight) is spent, then ends and lets the next pass continue, so freed seats are filled within a pass's budget. Each launch records its own duration and its slowest step on the verdict row. With §3 landed, profile the admit service again and repair the next largest per-launch cost the profile names.

Done when. A test with launches stubbed to take 20 s each and a 60 s budget shows a pass launches three and the next pass takes up the rest. A 120 s py-spy record --idle of the live admit service before and after names the per-launch cost removed, and the median launch duration over one evening falls below 15 s. Write paths: reckon/crew/admission/, the step the profile names, and the new test.
