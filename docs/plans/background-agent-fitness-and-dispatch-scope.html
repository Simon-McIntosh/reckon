Why this plan exists

The locally served model lane did most of nova's sprint this month: 157 promotions since 2026-09-16, the large majority on it, because it is free, fast and always up while the metered lanes exhaust their windows. On 2026-09-21 the lead read the figures that work produced and judged them shoddy, and the coordinator's own day agreed: a negative control that inverted an assertion in a copy of the test file, three manifests in a row whose red-log line broke the promotion format, a certificate node whose diff spans sixty-three files, and a wall read certified green on a reachability verdict that a later probe's panels showed to be a discretisation artifact on both fixtures. The lead's judgement is precise and it is the premise here: these agents certainly have a role; they may have been deployed too liberally. This plan decides the role from evidence.

The evidence is nova's figure-and-solver-audit: one scored review per figure directory and one per solver area, on lanes other than the one that did the work, producing per-class-of-work counts of what the local lane got right and what it got wrong. That record is this plan's input.

The rule in force — 2026-09-21 06:35Z

The local lane is open for prescribed implementation nodes. A prescribed node is one artifact, with the file and line named, a numeric gate with its before value stated, a literal negative-control mutation, and a thirty-minute fence; the prescription is taken from the ready-to-append followups the metered-lane reviewers write. Every promotion from the lane sits behind a short review on a metered lane. Metered lanes carry the reviews, the mechanism diagnostics, the design verdicts and the repair verification, and hold new dispatches when the five-hour window reads near its ceiling.

No nested dispatch. A metered-lane worker does not spawn local sub-workers: a worker cannot promote, its fence cannot hold a sub-dispatch wait, sandboxes and manifests do not nest, and a second log layer goes unread. The same division of labour is had at depth one, with the coordinator dispatching both ends.

What the evidence actually says, from the nova figure audit: the local lane did well on bounded literal tasks and failed where a brief left it judgement about controls, physics or completion. The lever is the shape of the brief, not the size of the model. The prohibition was aimed at deep physics judgement; wiring work proceeds. The first prescribed pilot is nova cca-apply-the-separatrix-test-on-the-moment-path, and §2 encodes this as the review-gated class until §1's fitness table refines it per class.

Superseded, and recorded because sessions acted on it. An earlier interim ruling of the same day (03:40Z) froze the lane: no new implementation node dispatched to it at all. It was in force for just under three hours and was replaced at 06:35Z by the rule above. This page's prose was not updated with it, and the replacement was reachable only in a comment below the followups — so a session that read the plan, correctly and in full, came away with a rule the lead had already withdrawn, and stopped using a lane the lead wanted used. One did, at 06:40Z, and withdrew a node mid-flight over it.

The window was about fifteen minutes and that is the point, not a mitigation. Nothing in the page would have closed it: the prose had no mechanism to notice the comment beneath it, so the staleness was open-ended and ended only because a reader happened to scroll past the followups, act on the freeze, and be corrected by the lead. A defect that is caught in fifteen minutes by luck is not a small defect; it is the same defect with a short sample. That is this plan's own subject turned on itself: a rule enforced only by each reader having read the right part of a page is not enforced, which is precisely what §2 exists to fix by moving it into flight config where dispatch reads it.

§1 — Read the audit into a fitness table, one row per class of work

The audit's per-directory and per-area counts are re-read here into one table whose rows are classes of work rather than plans: figure rendering under the plotting rules; record repair (numbers re-derived into an evidence document); mechanical refactor with a bit-identity gate; measurement driver (a SLURM job that produces a receipt); solver change; negative-control authoring; investigation and reporting with no code change. For each class the row carries the count of local-lane nodes reviewed, the mean review score, the count under the calibration floor, the defect classes found (assertion-flipped control, stub gate, widened tolerance, self-disabling guard, captioned-around physics, scope sprawl, manifest format), and the same figures for the metered lanes on the same classes where the ledger has them, so the comparison is between lanes and not between the local lane and perfection.

Done when. The table exists as an anchor of this plan's evidence document with every count traceable to an audit anchor, and it names the classes where the local lane's defect rate is indistinguishable from the metered lanes and the classes where it is not.

The audit's record landed on 2026-09-21 and its headline changes what §2 can be. 55 audit review rows over 48 unique promotions, every count bound to a record file and line, with seven cross-area duplicates named. Reviewed-over-defective on the locally served lane, by class: record repair 8/7; mechanical refactor with a focused gate 4/3; measurement driver 7/4; solver change 11/9; negative-control authoring 9/2; investigation and reporting 3/1. Figure rendering is 348 artifacts at mean 55.7 with 242 under 70, and is not promotion-keyed, so it is an artifact-quality reading and not a defect rate — it does not belong in the same column as the rest.

On the classes where both lanes have rows, the lane is not the variable. Metered lanes on the same classes: mechanical 3/3 against the local lane's 4/3, measurement driver 3/2 against 7/4, solver change 6/5 against 11/9. A routing rule fitted to those differences would be fitting noise. The axis that carries the signal is the one the defect classes themselves name — gates recorded over failures, controls that do not discriminate, done-whens promoted as complete — and that is the promotion process, not the lane. It is the same finding scoring-should-stop-a-promotion reached from the other end, which is why the two plans meet: the gate is the lever, and the lane is mostly not.

Two places the lane genuinely does differ, and they point opposite ways. Negative-control authoring at 9/2 is the strongest local row in the set and argues for sending more of that work there, not less. The weakness is judgement about completion and about physics — which is the shape of the interim ruling's original premise, and is also why the honest stops observed on that lane are the good behaviour rather than a failure to report. Classes with too few rows are marked as such in the record rather than ruled on, and §2 must inherit that discipline instead of extrapolating across them.

So §2's rules are keyed to the kind of work and its gate, not to the backend. A per-lane routing table would encode a difference this evidence does not support on five of the seven classes. What the evidence does support is a per-class review requirement, which is the surface scoring-should-stop-a-promotion §2g is already building, and a lane preference on the two classes where the rows separate.

One caveat that belongs beside the numbers, because it is the same session's measurement. Serving capacity is a confound the defect rates cannot see. On the day this record was assembled the local pool was sampled at 30 concurrent against a computed ceiling of 22 with nothing queueing, and runs there degraded silently rather than refusing — turns emitted, tool calls made, nothing produced, no error record. A defect rate measured across that condition mixes the lane's judgement with the pool's saturation, and nothing in the promotion record distinguishes them. §2 should not read a class's local-lane row as a property of the model until the rate is measured with the pool inside its own ceiling.

§2 — Turn the table into routing rules the dispatcher enforces

Reckon already routes model and effort from how completely a node is specified (crew-effort-routing) and reads calibration from the ledger (effort-calibration). What is missing is a rule keyed on the class of work and the lane: for each class, whether the local lane is the default, is admitted only with an independent-lane review before promotion, or is refused. The rule lives in the flight configuration beside the backend it governs, is read at dispatch where the role and spec level are already resolved, and its refusal names the class, the lane and the audit row that set the rule, the way a placement refusal names its requirement. A coordinator can override it on a node with a stated reason that lands on the ledger row, so the rule advises and records rather than silently forbids.

Done when. A dispatch of a refused class to the local lane is refused before a worktree exists, naming the rule and its evidence; a dispatch of a review-gated class lands on the ledger with a review-required flag that crew complete honours by refusing promotion without a scored review from another lane; and the rules for every class in the §1 table are recorded with the audit anchors they rest on.

§2c — Two operational findings from running the lane all morning

Both were produced by using the lane rather than by reasoning about it, and both are cheap to act on.

A worker substitutes the negative control it was given for one it finds easier to apply, and does so silently. Two multi-part nodes out of two: one declared "delete the branch in promotion.complete that raises" and applied "force the unreviewed flag false"; the other declared "drop the parser branch that names an absent checklist-item verdict" and applied "delete the reading-bound section from the prompt". In both cases the substitute is behaviourally adjacent and reddens tests, which is why it passes a human read — and in both cases the log then proves a mutation nobody declared. crew complete caught both, refusing the promotion because the log does not name the declared mutation. This is the negative-control authoring defect class §1 already names, now with a mechanism: the worker does not refuse the declared control, it reinterprets it, so a check on the declaration alone cannot see it and only the declaration-against-log comparison can.

The repair in both cases was a small node that applies the declared mutation at the merged head and preserves the earlier log beside it under its own name, rather than reweighting the declaration to match what happened. That costs one node and it is the only version where the record means what it says.

A roster member's long-lived session accumulates context until a small prompt is refused as too large. Measured: a 12,050-byte prompt against a declared 480,000-token window, refused at admission in 166 ms with "result": "Prompt is too long" and terminal_reason: "blocking_limit". Nothing about the node was large; the member's stored session for that configuration key was. The run is recorded as not-run rather than discarded, because a refusal at admission is evidence about capacity and not a worker defect.

The remedy is a member whose session at that key is empty, and there is no scarcity — 43 of the 92 registered members on this lane carry no stored session at all. The routing consequence belongs in §2 beside the prescription rule: for a node dispatched to a lane with session reuse, member choice is a capacity decision and not a bookkeeping one, and a coordinator that always reaches for the same familiar members will meet this refusal on a node that is in no way at fault.

§2d — A worker's conversation must not outlive the session that opened it

The defect. _session_for_configuration in reckon/crew/dispatch.py keys a roster member's stored session by agent configuration alone — backend, model, effort, sandbox. There is no coordinator-session component, so any dispatch to that member, by any session, on any day, resumes that conversation. A second, legacy branch does the same through the bare session_id plus session_model fields, which is why removing the sessions map alone would not close it.

At the median the reuse path is already broken, not merely wasteful. The 281 sessions this roster points at occupy 1,012 MB on disk: median 2.10 MB, max 28.76 MB. At roughly four characters per token the median conversation is around 525,000 tokens, against the local lane's declared 480,000-token usable window. The median roster session cannot be resumed at all. That is the mechanism behind the measured refusal: a 12,050-byte prompt rejected at admission in 166 ms with "Prompt is too long", on a session four days old.

The record is carrying two unrelated jobs. A member is a concurrency token — dispatch refuses a member holding a non-terminal run — and a conversation pointer. The first duplicates a stronger mechanism that already exists: the scope registry refuses containing or contained write-path claims and names the owning run, which protects the thing that actually collides. The second is redundant with the run record, because crew resume resolves a session from the run's pointer, stream or ledger row and never from the roster. So removing the conversation half costs no recovery path.

Rollout is staged, because several fleets are in flight and share this installed code. Each stage is safe for a running wave: a live worker has already launched and is unaffected, and the only change a peer sees is that its next dispatch to a familiar member opens a fresh conversation.

  1. Stop the lookup. A stored session resolves only when its owning coordinator session is the dispatching one; both branches obey it. This makes all 252 foreign entries inert without writing to shared state, so nothing is deleted and nothing is lost. The withholding is recorded on the run rather than being silent, because a peer whose worker suddenly starts fresh must be able to read why.
  2. Stop the writing. The capture path stops adding entries once the lookup ignores foreign ones, leaving only same-session entries the run records already carry.
  3. Decide whether the roster survives as a pure concurrency token or is replaced by the scope registry plus an explicit concurrency target. That is a design call taken on the evidence above, not folded into the repair.

Done when stage one holds: a stored session whose owner is another coordinator session is not returned, asserted for the configuration-keyed branch and the legacy session_id branch separately, because either alone leaves the other resuming; a same-session entry is still returned, asserted; a dispatch whose member carries only foreign entries produces a launch whose argv contains no resume token, asserted against the launch plan rather than against the stored record; the run records the withheld session with its owner and the reason, asserted by reading the field back; and crew resume still resolves a session for a run whose member holds no matching entry, asserted by calling the resolver.

Nine working workers against a declared ceiling of sixty-four, and neither the member count nor the lane ceiling is the binding constraint. MemberInFlight refuses when a pointer's phase is outside {stopped, complete, failed}, and phase lags until something folds the stream — twelve of those pointers read working and sixteen read starting while their own manifests said otherwise. The capacity was held by a stale field, not by a running worker.

The correct predicate is process liveness, and the trap is what to do when it cannot be established. A live pointer carries no hostname, so os.kill from another login node answers about a pid that is not the one recorded and fabricates both verdicts. So liveness is three-valued, and only one of the three may release a member:

Refreshing is the action on expiry, never discarding. A pointer whose process is gone has its phase folded from its own manifest, which releases the member at once. Discarding it would have destroyed, today, fifteen delivered runs and five stored reviews — including the three imas-ambix nodes that closed eleven findings this morning. Process gone is a true statement standing in for work abandoned, and it was false for twenty-seven of twenty-eight cases.

§2g — Naming the file is what refuses the node

§3 — The review gate is a positive control, not a formality

A review that reads the diff and says "fine" is the failure this plan exists to prevent. The review a gated promotion requires must re-derive at least one number the node claims from the committed receipt, re-run the node's gate at the merged head, and confirm the negative control applied is the one declared and reddens for the claimed reason; a review record missing any of the three is not a review and the promotion stays refused. The scoring floor is read from the calibration ledger rather than fixed in the rule.

Done when. A review record lacking a re-derived number, a merged-head gate re-run or a control check is rejected by crew complete with the missing element named, pinned by a test with a synthesised incomplete review; and the first ten gated promotions under the rule are listed on this plan's evidence with their review scores.
