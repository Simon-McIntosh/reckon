§1 — Why: a row is a turn, and most rows ask for nothing

Each line the follower prints reaches the coordinating session as a notification, and each notification can open a turn. The session host from a-follower-lives-as-long-as-its-session keeps that stream open for the whole session, so the stream's density is now the main cost of watching a fleet. The lead reads the same lines in the same transcript, so a row that wastes the coordinator's turn also costs the lead attention.

On 2026-10-08 the lead asked for the stream to carry important transitions and to drop start-up flap and rapid transitions: a dispatch should not produce dispatched → working, a bare working and an exit row in quick succession. The measurement below is the case for doing that, read from every Claude Code transcript under ~/.claude/projects with a Monitor event notification since 2026-10-07 (14 sessions, 2,676 notifications, 3,128 rows) and from the producer streams under ~/.config/reckon/crew/watch/*.events since 2026-10-05 (6,825 transitions over 2,027 runs).

About three rows in five ask for nothing. The producer stream also shows the rapid transitions directly. A run reaches a settled state through a chain of rows written seconds apart: exited-unfinished → blocked within 2 s, blocked → wait-aged → blocked within 3 to 8 s, working → stopped → discarded within a median 18 s. The stream also carries rows whose state does not change at all (withdrawn → withdrawn, recorded → recorded, working → working). Session 9cf43820 received dispatched → working at 02:50:15 and then a bare working for the same run at 02:53:00. That was the replay of a follower its host had restarted at 02:52:42. Session 184d1501's first plugin batch at 02:34:36 replayed rows stamped 11:55 and 12:07 the day before.

The producer already says which rows ask for something. Every transition carries a recovery verb: observe on 2,125 of 2,125 rows into working and 268 of 275 into withdrawn, an empty verb on every terminal record, and promote, review, decide, resume, wait, investigate, repair or redispatch on every row that needs the coordinator.

The follower already filters. RowPolicy in reckon/crew/ticker.py holds the three noise pairs NOISE_PAIRS names (launch flicker, first-commit exit phantom, stall flap), folds same-state rewrites into the counter block, and collapses a launch's arrival into its first transition. PaneRowPath wraps it for the follower through follower_row_path, and replay_row_policy drives the same class for a measurement. transition_class sorts each kind into coordinator duty, observer context or counter, after the 2026-09-25 follower signal audit. That audit decided observer context should still print, as the rows a reader needs to follow progress, and crew-observability-truth reduced a launch to one dispatched → working row on 2026-10-04. The rows above are what that policy lets through. Starts, terminal echoes and attach re-announcements are observer context, and the gap replay (_follow_gap_rows) still let 446 bare rows through. This plan changes one decision in the existing policy: on the coordinator's pane, observer context is held back. It adds no second policy.

crew_follow's docstring in reckon/crew_follow_commands.py says every transition is delivered and that no option narrows to action states, because hiding a recovery hides the news a reader is waiting for. This plan keeps that guarantee in a different form. Every change that asks for action or reverses an action row the reader was shown is delivered, and only rows that ask for nothing and reverse nothing are held back. The producer's log keeps the complete stream for a human.

The active plan a-follower-keeps-your-work-in-view proposes a needs-you line on a ten-minute cadence. Under host delivery that is a turn every ten minutes per session whether or not anything changed. Its owner should weigh this plan's measurement before building that cadence. Nothing here edits that plan.

§ Decisions

Does a dispatch produce a row when its worker starts?

The lead's direction on 2026-10-08: dispatched → working rows are not needed. The dispatch call already returned the launch. A launch that fails after it was admitted reaches stalled or launch-failed, which the policy delivers. A launch withdrawn at once is the dispatch's own refusal, which the dispatch call already reported.
