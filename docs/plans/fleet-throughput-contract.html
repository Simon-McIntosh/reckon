§1 — Why

Measured across 259 runs the fleet sustains 2.26 concurrent workers against a 13-wide peak, and roughly 45% of purchased worker-time idles at the contract's wave barrier (fleet review §2/§5/§6). None of the serialization is in code: it comes from the reckon-ship contract's wave-and-wait shape, the over-broad landing freeze, a uniform 25m time ceiling, and per-run polling that makes wide fleets expensive in orchestrator context. This plan raises throughput by removing artificial serialization only — the DAG and file-scope disjointness still decide what may run together, so no parallelism is forced that plans do not expose. Depends on crew-state-integrity and dispatch-lifecycle-safety: a wider fleet is exactly what trips the ledger races and the member-collision loss.

§6 — The test role cannot execute tests

Found by using the role §4 had just added: the shipped test role resolves to a read-only sandbox, and the worker protocol states that a node executing builds or test runs belongs in worktree-full because caches outside the delivery directory stay read-only. So the one role whose entire purpose is running a suite is configured in the tier that forbids the writes a suite needs — uv run pytest must create .venv/ and .pytest_cache/ inside the worktree. Adding a role name the CLI accepts is not the same as adding a role that can do its job, and the parity test added in §5 checks that a role resolves, not that its sandbox suits its work.

Deliverable. Give test the sandbox tier its work requires and pin the property by role semantics rather than by name, so a future role that executes cannot be shipped read-only. Done when a dispatched test-role node runs the full suite and writes both its cache and its manifest, and a test asserts that every execution-capable role resolves to a sandbox permitting worktree writes.

§ Decisions

Wire backends.*.concurrency as a dispatch admission check, or delete it from the schema?

Wiring gives a real per-backend cap tunable per host/project; deleting keeps admission purely roster-driven and the config surface smaller. The status quo — declared and ignored — misleads every reader.

Lead decision 2026-08-17: admission stays roster-driven — the member roster already bounds real concurrency, so a per-backend numeric cap duplicates it; removing the declared-but-dead key is cheaper and more truthful than wiring an admission check the current fleet scale does not need.
