§1 — Why: a contract nobody reads end to end

The reckon-build skill and its references grew from 11,475 words on 12 August to 34,714 on 26 September, counting every file in the skill directory; with the user-global guardrails, a coordinator carries about 49,000 words of binding instruction before it reads a plan (crew pattern review §9). Most of those rules record a measured incident and are individually right. Many are now enforced by the tool — the eight-property contract, the budget fence, the gate fence, the closure fence, the follower requirement, the review requirement — so the prose restates a refusal the coordinator will meet anyway, and the survey's complaints are about rules colliding rather than rules missing (§3).

§2 — Classify every rule

Deliverable. A table, written as its own anchored section inside this plan's cumulative evidence record, with one row per binding sentence in skills/reckon-build/SKILL.md and the eight files under skills/reckon-build/references/ (the harness files under references/orchestrator-harness/ included). A binding sentence is one that states a rule a coordinator or worker must follow — it contains or implies must, never, always, refuse or an imperative — not every sentence of those files. Each row carries the rule, whether code enforces it (naming the refusal or check that does), whether it is still wanted, and one of four dispositions: delete as enforced, keep, merge into a named surviving row, or delete as obsolete. The worker proposes the "still wanted" column; the lead confirms it before §3 starts.

Done when every binding sentence has a row; each row marked enforced names a test that drives the guarded action far enough to make the refusal or check fire, and the test has been run with its log cited; and the row count is stated alongside the files' total sentence count, so the scope of "binding" is visible.

§3 — Rewrite the core

Deliverable. skills/reckon-build/SKILL.md rewritten to at most 4,000 words, stating the coordinator's loop, what it owns and what it must never do. The references keep the rows §2 marked keep and the rows merged into them, and serve only a coordinator composing a delegation by hand because reckon crew dispatch was not used for it. Before any rewrite, the 26 September token baseline below is measured and recorded in the evidence record, so it cannot move.

Done when the skill and its references together are at most 12,000 words; the existing skill-contract parity tests (tests/test_skill_contract_parity.py) pass; and, over at least five coordinator transcripts on each side, the input tokens a coordinator spends loading the skill and its references before its first dispatch are at most half of the baseline. The user-global guardrails and plan reading are outside this plan and are not part of the measure.
