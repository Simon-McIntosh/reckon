§1 — Why: a contract nobody reads end to end

The reckon-build skill and its references grew from 11,475 words on 12 August to 34,714 on 26 September; with the user-global guardrails, a coordinator carries about 49,000 words of binding instruction before it reads a plan (crew pattern review §9). Most of those rules record a measured incident and are individually right. Many are now enforced by the tool — the eight-property contract, the budget, gate and closure fences, the follower requirement, the review requirement — so the prose restates a refusal the coordinator will meet anyway, and the survey's complaints are about rules colliding rather than rules missing (§3).

§2 — Classify every rule

Deliverable. A table, as an anchor of this plan's evidence record, with one row per binding sentence in skills/reckon-build/SKILL.md and its references: the rule, whether code enforces it (with the refusal or check that does), whether it is still wanted, and its disposition — delete as enforced, keep, merge, or delete as obsolete.

Done when every binding sentence has a row, and each "enforced" row names a test that exercises the enforcement.

§3 — Rewrite the core

Deliverable. A SKILL.md of at most 4,000 words that states the coordinator's loop, what it owns and what it must never do, and points to references only for hand-composing a delegation the tool did not prepare; the references keep only what §2 marked keep.

Done when the skill and references together are at most 12,000 words, the skill-contract parity tests pass, and a coordinator transcript sample shows input tokens before the first dispatch reduced by at least half against the 26 September baseline.
