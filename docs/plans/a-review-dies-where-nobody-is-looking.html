A Review Dies Where Nobody Is Looking

Two coordinator sessions instrumented the same failure independently on 2026-09-22. The shape is now precisely characterised and the cause is not known. Across 192 streams on the locally served lane, 57 carry no result record and 36 end mid-thinking with the process gone, empty stderr, zero error records and zero rate-limit events. 34 of the 36 are review-role runs in the read-only sandbox. Zero implement-role runs died this way. Deaths arrive 15 to 35 minutes in, at record counts from 220 to 12,105, and thinking length does not discriminate.

A memory kill was the leading hypothesis and its own instrument refuted it. The user slice reports oom_kill 529 with memory.events.local at 0 and no surviving child cgroup owning a kill, while sitting at 31.9 of 64 GiB — a compelling story for a limit nobody watches. Then a review died at 21:21:31 in exactly this shape and the counter read 529 at 21:28:23, unchanged. The one Exit code 137 that seemed to confirm it belongs to a different failure: the stray sweep review that died sixteen seconds after dispatch on the uncommitted capture of §5, where the dispatch tore its sandbox down and the tool result recorded the kill. So the SIGKILL had a nearer explanation and the counter never moved. The hypothesis survives only if the sandboxed client runs outside that slice, which is a launcher question and is checkable.

This is recorded rather than quietly replaced because the retraction is the useful part. Two sessions and this coordinator found a cause, cross-confirmed it, and it was refuted within the hour by the counter proposed as its acceptance test. Every surviving discriminator is a property of the shape, not of a mechanism: review role in the read-only sandbox, no implement-role deaths, no error text, thinking length irrelevant.

Every surface then reports it as something else — a stalled run, an abandoned run, an unreliable lane, a model that cannot be trusted at high effort. Sessions responded rationally to those reports by moving reviews onto the lane that runs the orchestrators, which is a second failure caused entirely by the first being misattributed.

§1 — Make the death say what killed it, before theorising again

Every account of this failure so far has been inferred from what the run stopped doing, because the runs carry no record of how they ended. That is the defect to close first, and it is the reason a plausible cause survived an afternoon: with no terminal record, any hypothesis fits.

The terminal record is not this plan's to build — it is worker-death-is-recorded, which already carries it with its own measured inventory. That plan was written before these deaths were investigated, sat as a draft outside every sprint, and states the general case: a worker ended by a signal writes nothing on the way out, so its pointer keeps the phase it held, the seat stays held, and the correct recovery becomes unreachable because nothing says the run needs resuming. The oldest such pointer measured here had read working for three days. This section depends on it and adds one requirement of its own: the record must distinguish a signal the worker did not choose from a clean exit, because that is the single distinction every account of these deaths has been unable to make.

The memory candidate is closed for the instrumented slice, on three readings. oom_kill held at 529 at 21:22, 21:28:23 and 21:52:08 on 2026-09-22 while memory.current sat near 32 GiB of a 64 GiB cap — and across that window two local-lane reviews ran continuously, one died in the thinking shape at 21:21:31, and five more local nodes were in flight on another session's lane. A counter flat across a wave that contains the failure is the strongest negative available, and it retires the hypothesis this plan carried for one afternoon.

The last sub-case was read rather than assumed, and it finishes the hypothesis. A live worker on the lane reads its own cgroup as 0::/user.slice/user-39486.slice/session-143284.scope — inside the slice the counter belongs to, so the counter was aimed correctly. That scope's memory.max is max: there is no per-scope limit for a worker to hit, its memory.current is 6.1 GiB, and its own oom_kill is 0. Memory is done.

The same read settles the plan's ordering, which is the more valuable half. The launcher runs workers in the login session's scope rather than a systemd-run scope of their own, so no cgroup event can ever attribute a worker's death — there is no per-worker scope to carry one. The terminal record this section asks for first is not merely the cheapest instrument, it is the only place the cause can come from.

The candidate that survives: whether the read-only sandbox imposes a wall-clock or idle ceiling that a long review turn crosses. Deaths arriving at 15 to 35 minutes fit a time bound; record counts at death spanning 220 to 12,105 — two orders of magnitude — fit neither a context bound nor a memory one, which is the only asymmetry between the candidates that needed no new instrumentation.

One fact constrains every candidate and has now held twice: resume continues a dead review in the same session and it streams again. Both reviews resumed after mid-turn deaths on 2026-09-22 completed and stored their records, scoring 91 and 88. So the work is sound when it finishes, whatever kills the process does not poison the conversation, and the cause is bounded to the run.

§2 — The experiment neither session could run

Both sessions report the same confound and both say so rather than guessing around it: every review ran at xhigh and every implement ran at high, so effort and role cannot be separated in the observed data. The role split is real; the claim that effort causes the deaths is not established by anything measured.

Measure. Re-dispatch a review on the same lane at effort high, against the same class of node, and compare death rate and the OOM counter against the xhigh population. One of three results follows: effort discriminates and the fix is partly routing; role discriminates and the fix is wholly provisioning; neither does and the cause is elsewhere. Until this runs, no fix may be justified by effort.

§3 — A deliverable that exists is not an abandoned run

13 of the 36 deaths had already written a complete manifest and their review record. The process ended on the final turn, after the work. The sweep reads those as abandoned and the reviews are lost, so a third of the losses are recoverable without touching a memory limit at all.

Measure. Classification reads the deliverable before it reads the process: a run whose manifest is complete and whose review record parses is terminal-with-deliverable whatever its exit looked like. Falsify by presenting a run with a complete record and a killed process and requiring it not to be classified abandoned; and by presenting one with a killed process and no record and requiring that it still is.

The deliverable is read before the process, and that is now locked

So the defect was a silence rather than a misfire: the ordering that produces the reading was unstated and untested, and nothing stopped the next edit from reordering the arms and losing the deaths to abandonment again. This change locks the property and records the falsifier that catches a reversion.

§4 — The sweep's composition is deterministic, attributable and printed

The two sessions disagree about what the sweep does, and that disagreement is itself the finding. One measured the review taking the sweeping session's runtime default. The other tabulated six compositions and found it matching neither the sweeper's rationing nor the owning run's lane consistently — a local run reviewed on the orchestrator lane, the same node re-composed locally after a discard, another session's run composed on the orchestrator lane under that session's id with an auto-registered member. Two competent readers watching one surface cannot agree what it does, which means the composition is not observable.

Measure. The review lane is composed from the owning run's recorded backend and the owning coordinator's recorded rationing, never from whichever runtime happens to be sweeping, and the composed argv is printed on the scoring row. The sweep bookends only runs whose pointer session matches the follower's own session. The copyable next-action carries the owning lane rather than --local, which it did in every observation from both sessions.

This is where the orchestrator-lane reviews came from, and it matters for sequencing: a fence at the dispatch boundary would not have refused them, because the party choosing the lane was a project-wide sweep attributing its own default to someone else's run.

Landed. The lane is composed from the owning run's recorded backend, the locally served lane and the configured rest in that order, and the new flight key review_excluded_backends removes a named backend before any ordering, so a fallback cannot step around it. A sweep bookends only runs whose pointer session matches the follower registration its process holds, and the printed next-action carries the owning lane. Gate: 19 tests across the new module and the two reflex modules, the 13 pre-existing ones unchanged against base 75b39d4d with no added failures; the declared negative control fails the two exclusion tests. Evidence: §4 record.

§5 — A rollback must not replace the cause it was rolling back

Verbatim, reproduced three times: a dispatch printed refusing to remove worktree … claimed by live runs r-…-review-of-cca-one-certificate-row-per-process — naming the run that same dispatch had just created. The real cause was found only by running the registration by hand: cannot commit roster registration: uncommitted member registration(s); commit or discard that roster write before retrying, against a session capture that crew resume had written into the ledger and nobody had committed.

Mechanism: the pointer is published, _register_session_member raises, the except block signals the worker and calls _remove_worktree, which refuses on the pointer the same call just published, and its error replaces the original. The worker reads dispatched → abandoned after six seconds with only an init record.

Measure. The original exception survives its own rollback — chained, not replaced — and the removal skips the claim check for a pointer published by the same call. Separately, a capture-only dirty ledger does not block a registration. Falsify by forcing the registration to raise and requiring the raised error to name the registration, not the worktree.

§6 — The validator does not check the state it validates against

Measured here rather than accepted: reckon's parser is last-wins on a duplicated plan-* scalar — a file carrying plan-impl twice, 0.9 then 0.1, parses as 0.1 — and audit-doc returns OK on a file carrying six duplicated pairs. Four evidence records in this repository carry duplicates today.

Why it bites: a union-resolved merge of two landing records leaves two plan-version lines; the writer updates one and the reader takes the other, so two successful set impl calls coexist with a promotion refusing that impl did not move. One project measured an audit plan reading impl 0.0 to the roadmap while its writer kept updating the first line.

The same gap has a second, worse face on sprint resources, measured against a working instrument. A sprint whose reckon-resource-state island is removed entirely passes audit-doc with OK — while roadmap and read_plan fail for every session in the project, because that island is the resource's state and the markup around it is presentation. One was authored by hand from a sibling sprint on 2026-09-22, the island omitted, the validator run, its OK believed, and the surface broken for every reader until a peer session traced it.

The instrument is sound, which is what makes this a finding rather than an impression. The same validator fails a plan carrying a relative image source (img-relative-src) from the same fixture set, and it implements roughly fifty checks including meta-missing, header-duplicate and section-unclosed. So it is not that nothing is checked; it is that the two defects it misses are both about the authoritative state rather than the prose around it — a duplicated scalar and an absent island. A validator that reads presentation and skips the state is checking the part a human would notice anyway.

Measure. audit-doc errors on any plan-* scalar appearing twice and on a typed resource whose state island is absent or unparseable, each falsified by the fixture that currently passes; the reader refuses or dedupes deterministically rather than silently taking one; and impl, version and modified are written as one contiguous block so a three-way merge treats them as a single hunk rather than three independently unionisable lines. The structural fix is the one that prevents recurrence; the guard is what makes the existing four visible.

Landed — the guard half. The three misses this section names now each report a named error from audit-doc: duplicate-plan-scalar for any plan-* scalar appearing twice, resource-island-missing and resource-island-malformed for a typed resource whose state island is absent or does not parse, and tr-unclosed for the imbalance this section's peer found in the third <tr> case. The row check extends the raw-tag scanner that already emits section-unclosed, which is the tag-list cost the peer predicted rather than a new mechanism; the other two read the parsed document, because the authoritative state is what the reader consumes and the prose is not.

Falsified against the fixtures that passed. Three documents that reported OK before the change now each exit non-zero with its code — a plan carrying plan-impl twice, a sprint resource with its island removed, and a table with one </tr> among four rows — while a well-formed document of each kind still reports OK. The negative control is applied and recorded: with the duplicate check removed from the committed tree, the duplicated-scalar fixture passes the audit again. The population over the whole corpus is measured rather than asserted: 14 duplicate-plan-scalar findings across 4 documents, which confirms this section's count of four evidence records independently, and 1 tr-unclosed, with no island finding over 21 typed-resource pages that all carry a parseable island. Those four documents each repeat an entire header block — two plan-title, two plan-summary, two plan-recorded-at — which is the union-merged landing record this section describes.

Not landed, and still open. The structural half is untouched: impl, version and modified are not yet written as one contiguous block, and the reader still resolves a duplicate last-wins instead of refusing or deduplicating deterministically. So this section's guard exists and its recurrence is still possible. The five documents the new checks report — the four duplicated-header evidence records and the one unclosed-row table — are also unrepaired: they sit outside the node that added the checks, and repairing a document an audit has only just learned to see belongs to the node that owns those paths.

Evidence. The landed record, the quoted reproduction and the population figure live at the attached evidence record.

§8 — A reused session carries one worker's context into the next

A third death shape, reported by the nova S19 coordinator on 2026-09-23, different from both the mid-thinking kills and §7's oversized prompt. Run r-20260923T031542572571-cca-exact-bank-callback-capacity-from-the-mesh on member s19n-imp-1 (clive, deepseek-v4.1-flash, effort high, session reuse) was accepted at dispatch and then refused by the endpoint in 131 ms. Its stream holds one assistant record, then result with is_error: true, Prompt is too long and terminal_reason: blocking_limit. Its prompt file was 16,105 bytes. The member had already run two earlier nodes in the same session, and the result record's modelUsage.inputTokens read 8,839,975 against a contextWindow of 200,000.

This is a cost defect before it is a death. Session reuse means a new node resumes the conversation a previous, unrelated node built, so every turn of the new node re-sends the old node's gathered context. That slows the worker's turnaround, and on a metered lane it spends the subscription on context no one asked for. The death is the point at which that history finally exceeds the window. §7's context fit cannot see it, because it estimates the node's own prompt and never counts the session it resumes.

Measure the figure first. Establish what inputTokens is on each dialect: a per-turn sum or a single prompt's size. Take the first turn of every node run on a resumed session and record the history that turn carried in, attributed to the prior node that built it. That establishes whether dispatch sends one worker's gathered context into another. The answer is either a population of runs with nonzero inherited context and its cost in tokens and wall time, or a demonstrated zero; a guess counts as neither. Take it on both the local and codex lanes, since the codex subscription is where the waste is paid for.

Then refuse to inherit. A node's first turn carries its own prompt and nothing from an earlier node. Where roster continuity wants a remembered session (a repair returning to the worker that wrote the code), the resume is deliberate and its carried size is checked against the lane's effective boundary from §7. Any other dispatch starts a fresh session. Falsify it with a fixture member whose recorded session carries more than the boundary: a fresh node dispatched to it starts a new session and inherits nothing, and a named repair resume still resumes. Then restore unconditional resume and require the fresh node to inherit again.

The census is taken: the inheritance is real where it is measurable, and unmeasurable on codex

Recorded 2026-09-23. The census walks the run directories, reads each run's first assistant turn for its own charged input, and prices the history that turn carried in as the first turn less the node's own prompt and the dialect's harness preamble — the preamble being the median residual over the runs that opened a session, which carried nothing in. Session identity and dialect come from the stream's own opening record (system.session_id, or thread.started.thread_id on codex); a run resumed a session when an earlier run directory, by dispatch order, shares that id, and that earlier run is recorded as the one that built the history. The per-run and per-backend census is docs/research/data/session-carryover.json, produced by reckon/crew/carryover_census.py.

The answer is a population, not a demonstration (snapshot of 5,473 run directories taken 2026-09-23; the census regenerates from the live run directory, so its exact per-lane counts move as the fleet runs — docs/research/data/session-carryover.json holds the figures this text reports). On the local lane 2,120 runs carried 318 that resumed an earlier session, and 284 of those carried in more history than their own prompt — roughly nine in ten — with a median of about 82,000 tokens and a maximum of about 494,000. The opus-class lane is worse in proportion: 12 resumed runs, all 12 above their own prompt, a median of about 205,000 and a maximum of about 457,000.

The codex lane is where the waste is paid and it is not answerable from the stream. 1,358 of the 3,268 codex-lane runs resumed a session, but the codex grammar publishes no per-assistant usage at all: its only usage-bearing record is one turn.completed per run, whose usage.input_tokens is the charged input summed over every model request the turn made, one per tool round-trip, each re-sending the whole context. That figure is a function of run length, not of what the first request opened with — one run in the snapshot reports 526,640,050 while its first turn is nowhere recorded. The census reports no codex first-turn figure rather than that contaminated sum: codex inheritance is unmeasurable from the stream, and the codex lane is the lane emitting the largest cumulative figures in the fleet.

The two candidate figures are not interchangeable, and the difference is measurable. Example run r-20260905T162101540255-cank-bounding-box-shape-rows reports modelUsage.inputTokens = 90,775,155 against a contextWindow of 1,016,576 while its first turn carried 53,458. The field is the run's total un-cached input — the cached shares live in cacheReadInputTokens and cacheCreationInputTokens — so it is a run-length aggregate whose magnitude tracks cache behaviour rather than turn count: a long opus run, r-20260905T172708183399-cank-wall-null-flux-tangent, reports 118 against 6,510,365 cache-read tokens because almost all of its input was cached. Either reading is the sum over the run's requests, which is why the census reads assistant.message.usage of the first turn instead.

The motivating death, re-read. r-20260923T031542572571-cca-exact-bank-callback-capacity-from-the-mesh is in the census as a resumed run whose prior session-owner was r-20260922T213154046276-cpu-fold-duplicate-probe-exit-tests, with an own prompt of 4,026 tokens. Its single assistant turn was refused by the endpoint, so its charged first turn is zero and the census reports zero inherited there — while the same run's result record reads 8,839,975 against a 200,000 window. The first-turn instrument under-reports a refusal by construction; that is recorded as a limitation below rather than papered over.

Controls. A run that opened a session reports exactly zero inherited tokens by construction, not by a threshold: more than 1,800 claude-dialect runs report zero, e.g. r-20260812T084415142910-flight-check-b (first turn 70,899, inherited 0). The negative control substitutes the result record's cumulative total for the first turn and is observed to misreport a fresh run — the fixture fresh node reports having inherited 118,500 tokens it never carried in — recorded at negative-control.log in the node's run directory.

What this does not claim. The census measures this node's own change; the merged result is a separately dispatched test node's job, and failures outside this node's declared write paths are carried under follow_ons. Two limitations are stated rather than fixed: a refused run reads as zero inherited because its turn was never charged; and the codex dialect admits no first-turn figure at all. The other half of this section — a fresh node starting a new session unless a rationale-limited repair deliberately resumes — is not implemented here and remains open.

§9 — A local worker compacts and continues instead of dying

Every death shape in this plan ends the same way: the worker stops at a limit and the work is recovered, if at all, by a coordinator resuming it by hand. The harness the local lane runs under is expected to compact a conversation that approaches its window and carry on. Nothing in this repository has shown that it does, on this lane, at this window.

Measure. Dispatch a local worker whose task is designed to exceed the lane's effective boundary partway through. Examples: reading a named corpus larger than the window in stages, or a long sequence of file reads each followed by a small written result. Require that the stream records a compaction, that the worker writes results both before and after it, and that the run ends with a complete manifest rather than a death. Record the turn at which compaction fired and the context size either side of it. A worker that dies at the boundary instead is the negative result, recorded with its last stream record's type, and the RCA below proceeds from it.

Prevention follows the measurement. With a terminal record for every death (worker-death-is-recorded), the census of §2 and this demonstration, each remaining death shape gets a harness-level prevention. Candidates are a fresh session instead of an inherited one (§8), a compaction threshold set below the effective boundary, and bounded turns so a review cannot run thirty minutes on one prompt. Each prevention is measured by the death rate of its cell before and after.
