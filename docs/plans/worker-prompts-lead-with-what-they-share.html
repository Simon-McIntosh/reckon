The cause is placement, not content. The prompt opens with 152 bytes of fixed preamble, then emits NODE <id> and GOAL <text> — both variable length — and only then the contract, the fences, the manifest template and the write scope, all of which are near-identical across every worker. §2 — Why this costs the served lane

Prefix caching matches blocks from position 0 while bytes are identical. The serve owner demonstrated the engine's behaviour directly: sending one novel prompt and then the identical prompt again returned 27.4% then 90.8% hit rate. The mechanism works perfectly the moment a prefix repeats.

The fleet average sits at 28.2% instead, measured at 369,774,336 hits against 1,310,811,845 queries. That is not an engine defect and no pool size changes it — the engine cannot hit on a prefix nobody has sent before. Reckon supplies 88% repeated content arranged so that almost none of it repeats as a prefix, and the lane spends 18,863 of 22,557 tok/s of prefill recomputing prefixes it has already built.

What was excluded. A larger KV pool was proposed and dropped: occupancy runs 0.29–0.50 with preemptions 0 and waiting 0, and the memory fraction that would enlarge it re-arms a lazily-allocated fused-MoE workspace that killed the serve at 256 concurrent. Shedding workers was also excluded — draining the fleet from 36 to 20 concurrent requests moved aggregate throughput 198 to 190 tok/s, and three long-running nodes measured 13.9 tool calls per ten-minute bucket at ~35 live workers against 16.5 at ~19–23, a 19% rise where a concurrency explanation predicts roughly double.

§ Decisions

Where does the invariant boundary sit — after the full contract, or after the contract plus the fences?

Everything ahead of the boundary must be byte-identical across every worker in every project. A longer invariant block caches more, and each additional section is another place a varying value can hide and silently destroy the prefix.

Locked on the measurement the section asked for rather than ahead of it. Contract-only is worth a measured 1315 bytes of shared prefix against 152, confirmed from dispatched files by a second party. Extending the boundary to include the fences would buy more bytes, and the case for spending them is unproven in the only direction that matters: the engine-side fleet effect is not measured, is expected to be much smaller than the byte ratio because most live traffic is long conversations where history dominates the prefix, and is now harder to measure at all because the restart reset the cache counters. Against that unquantified gain sits a quantified cost - every additional section ahead of the boundary is surface where a timestamp, an absolute path or an iteration order can vary, destroy the prefix it was meant to create, and still review as correct. Taking the increment that is measured and declining the one that is not is the conservative reading, and it is cheap to revisit: if an engine-side figure later tracks the byte ratio closely, moving the boundary is a second increment against a known payoff rather than a hope.

WHAT THIS FIX IS WORTH, QUANTIFIED, because the 8.65 figure will be quoted and needs a denominator beside it. The serve owner put the arithmetic that the plan's prose only gestured at: 1,315 bytes against a mid-conversation context of roughly 400,000 characters is 0.3 percent of the text, where 152 bytes was 0.04 percent. On that denominator the change is negligible. THE DISTINCTION THAT KEEPS BOTH FIGURES HONEST is which moment each describes. A first-turn prompt measured 11,884 to 14,425 bytes across the four sampled runs, so 1,315 bytes is about 11 percent of it and the reorder is a real change to what the engine can reuse across workers at turn one. By turn N the context has grown to hundreds of thousands of characters, the initial prompt is a vanishing fraction of it, and the reuse that matters is within a session - turn N+1 extending turn N - which a shared-prefix change does not touch at all. So the honest statement is not that the fix is negligible, and not that it is worth 8.65x: it is worth roughly a ninth of a first prompt, and approximately nothing thereafter. On the fleet measured today, thirteen of fifteen live runs over two hours old, almost all traffic is in the second regime. CONTEXT FOR ANYONE READING THE SURROUNDING MEASUREMENTS.
