§1 — Why: the backlog grows whether or not the fleet improves

In the four August weeks reckon closed about nine in ten of the plans it opened. In the fortnight to 26 September it opened 60 and closed 9, and 62 reckon plans are pending, most of them about the crew itself. reckon took 48% of the fleet's 2,303 promotions in the same fortnight; its plan, evidence and research HTML grew almost twice as fast as its source (crew pattern review §4, §9). Each measured defect becomes a new plan; nothing retires one. nova shows the same shape with eight open sprints and 32 pending plans. A cap on open work is the only lever that makes closing cheaper than opening.

The repository's own instructions hold the plan-creation diet pending the lead's design review (plan-lifecycle-hygiene), so the enforcing section of this plan waits on that decision; the measuring and triage sections do not.

§2 — Triage the pending reckon plans

Deliverable. A read-only triage, as an anchor of this plan's evidence record, of every pending reckon plan: its measured progress against the code (a plan-currency reading, not its stored impl), and a proposed disposition — keep, merge into a named plan, or close with a landed summary — with the owning coordinator session where one is live. Nothing is closed by this section; the proposals go to the lead and to the owning sessions.

Done when every pending plan has a row, the proposals leave at most 25 plans active, and each merge names its target.

§3 — A work-in-progress limit per project

Deliverable. A per-project limit on active plans in project configuration, enforced at plan creation in the mode the lead locks below, with the refusal or warning naming the plans closest to closing.

Done when creating a plan beyond the limit behaves as locked, in a test, and the velocity view's weekly opened-and-closed figures show reckon closing at least as many plans as it opens for two consecutive weeks.
