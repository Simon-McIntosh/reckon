§1 — Why: plans reach 100 % and stay open, some of them reading blocked

On 2026-10-21 the lead observed that several sessions struggle to close plans: plans reach 100 % and are then held as blocked. A census of every mounted project measured it. The declarations across all plans are 1,862 done, 376 implementable and 106 deferred. The roadmap reports 21 open plans with progress at 100 % across imas-ambix, imas-codex, iter_uda, nova and reckon, in three groups.

The sprint level met the same problem and solved it in a-sprint-closes-on-completable-work, shipped 2026-10-04: a sprint is closable when nothing completable remains, and held work is carried forward rather than pinning the sprint open. This plan applies that rule one level down. The census script, the roadmap reads and their output are in docs/evidence/archive/a-finished-plan-closes-landed.html.

§2 — A plan with nothing left to build reads closable

Readiness gains the value closable. A plan reads closable when it has no implementable section, so every section is done, descoped or deferred. A closable plan leaves ready_now, the ready counts and the critical path. The roadmap lists it under closable with what still stands between it and close: deferred sections, open followups and open gates. Each item carries the op that clears it, and the list ends with the close call itself. Its effective_status never reads blocked on account of work that is finished.

Done when: a fixture plan with every section done reads closable, is absent from ready_now, and lists the close op. A fixture with one implementable section still reads ready; that is the control. On the base commit the first fixture reads ready, and the test fails there. The census in §6 counts the closable plans per mount.

§3 — A gate on finished work stops holding the plan

A closed gate never blocks, whatever its verdict: failed is an outcome to record, not work to wait for. An open gate whose gated sections are all done or descoped no longer contributes to the plan's blocking. It raises a stale-gate finding that names the gate, its sections and the op that closes it with a verdict. An open gate on an implementable section blocks exactly as it does today. The derivation stays in the one blocker function that derived-gate-state landed; this changes which gates it counts, not where it is computed.

Done when: a fixture plan with an open gate on a done section is not blocked and raises stale-gate. A fixture with a closed gate with verdict failed on a deferred section is not blocked. A fixture with an open gate on an implementable section is still blocked; that is the control. On the base commit the first two fixtures read blocked, and the test fails there.

§4 — Deferred work leaves before the plan closes

close still refuses a plan with a deferred section, because closing over open work would hide it. The refusal now names every deferred section and the two ways to clear each one. The first is to descope it with a reason, the declaration a-project-reads-as-one-picture §9 adds. The second is to descope it with moved_to naming the successor plan section that now owns the work. The skill rule that work discovered against a finished plan belongs in a new plan says the same thing; the refusal is where a coordinator reads it. A moved_to ref must resolve, and the successor section shows the move as an incoming link.

Done when: close on a fixture with two deferred sections is refused, and the refusal names both sections and both remedies. After one is descoped with a reason and the other with a resolving moved_to, close succeeds and the successor section lists the incoming move. A moved_to that does not resolve is refused. On the base commit the refusal names neither remedy, and the test fails there.

§5 — The node that finishes a plan offers its close

When crew complete or crew land moves a plan's last implementable section to done, its response carries closable: true and the plan's closing list from §2. The coordinator's obligations gain a plan-closable row. That row clears when the plan is closed, or when the coordinator records a reason to keep it open, so the session that finished the work is the one asked to close it.

Done when: a fixture landing that ticks a plan's last implementable section returns closable: true and raises plan-closable for that session, and a landing that leaves an implementable section returns neither; that is the control. The row clears on close and on a recorded keep-open reason.

§6 — Every finished plan is closed or kept open for a stated reason

The census in §1 is re-run with the §2 readiness. Each closable plan goes to the coordinator that owns its project, with its closing list. reckon does not close another project's plans for it: the owner closes each one, or records why it stays open. The before and after counts go to the landed record.

Done when: the census across every mount reports no plan at 100 % that is neither closed nor carrying a recorded keep-open reason, and no plan reading blocked on a gate over finished work. The first census run, at the base commit, is the control and reports the 21 plans of §1.
