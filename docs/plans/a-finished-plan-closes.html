§1 — Why: plans reach 100 % and stay open, some of them reading blocked

On 2026-10-21 the lead observed that several sessions struggle to close plans: plans reach 100 % and are then held as blocked. A census of every mounted project measured it. The declarations across all plans are 1,862 done, 376 implementable and 106 deferred. The roadmap reports 21 open plans with progress at 100 % across imas-ambix, imas-codex, iter_uda, nova and reckon, in three groups.

The sprint level met the same problem and solved it in a-sprint-closes-on-completable-work, shipped 2026-10-04: a sprint is closable when nothing completable remains, and held work is carried forward rather than pinning the sprint open. This plan applies that rule one level down. The census script, the roadmap reads and their output are in docs/evidence/archive/a-finished-plan-closes-landed.html.

§2 — A plan with nothing left to build reads closable

Readiness gains the value closable, decided by the declared-remainder reader that already exists rather than a new predicate. plan_executable_remainder in reckon/_schema.py returns a plan's implementable remainder, and None for a plan that declares no sections; open_sections in reckon/_plan_html.py is the predicate under it. A plan reads closable when that remainder is empty, so every declared section is done, descoped or deferred. A None keeps today's readiness, which is the legacy population §1 sets aside. The closable test runs before the readiness ladder falls through to blocked or deferred (reckon/roadmap.py, near line 3269).

What still stands between a closable plan and close is reported by the one enumeration that already exists, closure_blockers in reckon/roadmap.py, which is on every roadmap row. It is widened from plan-terminal gates to every obstacle (deferred sections, open followups, open gates and plan-terminal gates), and each entry carries the op that clears it. A finished plan that should stay open says why the existing way, with a plan-terminal gate whose measure states the reason, so no new keep-open field is added.

This retires a surface rather than adding one. Closable plans leave ready_now, the ready counts and the critical path, which today carry 13 finished plans as ready work. The new vocabulary is one readiness value.

Done when: a fixture plan with every section done reads closable, is absent from ready_now, and its closure_blockers ends with the close op. A fixture with one implementable section still reads ready, and a fixture with no section declarations keeps its current readiness; those are the controls. A fixture with every section done, an open execution gate and no open followup reads closable, with the gate and its pass or fail op in closure_blockers, where the base reads it blocked, because split_unpassed_gates returns the gate as plan-holding once no implementable section remains. On the base commit the first fixture reads ready, and the test fails there.

§3 — A gate on finished work stops holding the plan

The change is one edit to split_unpassed_gates in reckon/roadmap.py (near line 805). It is the function that sees both the unpassed gates and the plan's implementable sections, and every reader of gate blocking reaches it: the roadmap ladder (near line 3200), serve.py (near line 1924), and the MCP readers in reckon/mcp_views.py near line 2490 and _section_gate_blockers near line 2504. Today it returns every unpassed gate as plan-holding once no implementable section is left outside the gates' scope, which is how a finished plan reads blocked. unpassed_gate_blockers in reckon/lifecycle.py takes only gate rows and cannot see declarations, so it stays as it is.

After the edit a closed gate never holds the plan, whatever its verdict: failed is an outcome to record, not work to wait for. An open gate whose gated sections are all done, descoped or deferred is neither plan-holding nor section-holding. Deferred work is held by its deferral, and the gate travels with the section until it is undeferred. An open gate on an implementable section, or one with no gated sections on a plan that still has implementable work, holds exactly as today. No new finding kind is added: a gate over finished work is reported where §2 lists what stands between a plan and close.

Done when: a fixture plan with an open gate on a done section is not blocked. A fixture with a closed gate with verdict failed on a deferred section is not blocked. A fixture with an open gate on a deferred section is not blocked; that is the shape of two of §1's three blocked plans. A fixture with an open gate on an implementable section is still blocked; that is the control. On the base commit the first three fixtures read blocked, and the test fails there. roadmap, the /plan route in serve.py and read_plan agree on every fixture, because all three read the one function.

§4 — Deferred work leaves before the plan closes

close still refuses a plan with a deferred section, because closing over open work would hide it. The refusal is the existing family, _require_every_section_ticked in reckon/_store.py, which already reports every failing guard through validate_landing_patch(report_all=True); it is extended, not joined by a new refusal. It now names every deferred section and the two ways to clear each one. The first is to descope it with a reason, using the declaration a-project-reads-as-one-picture §9 adds. The second is to descope it with moved_to naming the successor plan section that now owns the work. The skill rule that work discovered against a finished plan belongs in a new plan says the same thing; the refusal is where a coordinator reads it. A moved_to ref must resolve, and the successor section shows the move as an incoming link.

Done when: close on a fixture with two deferred sections is refused, and the refusal names both sections and both remedies. After one is descoped with a reason and the other with a resolving moved_to, close succeeds and the successor section lists the incoming move. A moved_to that does not resolve is refused. On the base commit the refusal names neither remedy, and the test fails there.

§5 — The node that finishes a plan offers its close

Neither crew complete nor crew land writes section declarations. A section moves to done through the edit_plan landing beat (set section_declarations.<id>), so that write is where a finish is seen. Its response carries the plan's readiness after the write, read from §2's ladder, together with the plan's closure_blockers. A coordinator ticking the last section therefore sees closable and the close op in the same answer.

No obligation row is added. obligations() in reckon/crew/obligations.py derives every duty from run pointers and stored reviews and reads no plan state. The fact is already carried by the plan's own readiness, by the brief (a-project-reads-as-one-picture §7) and by §6's census.

Done when: a fixture edit_plan write that ticks a plan's last implementable section returns readiness: closable with the plan's closure_blockers. A write that leaves an implementable section returns readiness: ready; that is the control. On the base commit the write returns no readiness, and the test fails there.

§6 — Every finished plan is closed or kept open for a stated reason

The census in §1 is re-run with the §2 readiness. Each closable plan goes to the coordinator that owns its project, with its closure_blockers. reckon does not close another project's plans for it. The owner closes each one, or records why it stays open with a plan-terminal gate whose measure states the reason. The before and after counts go to the landed record.

Done when: the census across every mount reports no plan at 100 % that is neither closed nor holding a plan-terminal gate that states why, and no plan reading blocked on a gate over finished work. The first census run, at the base commit, is the control and reports the 21 plans of §1.
