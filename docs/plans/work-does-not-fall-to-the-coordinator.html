§1 — What a coordinator had to do by hand, and why none of it needed a coordinator

The imas-efit S13 coordinator ran 27 landings on the local lane between 2026-09-16 20:30Z and 2026-09-17 morning, and kept a record of everything it did by hand that the crew surface could have done. Evidence and timestamps are under ~/.config/reckon/crew/reports/ship-s13/.

The list is not a wish for features. Every item is work the surface already holds the facts for and declines to perform, so a human-facing coordinator performs it instead — and each hand-performance is an opportunity to do it slightly differently from the last coordinator.

Two of the ten reported items are repaired or in flight elsewhere and are not restated here. Two more belong to plan-writes-are-not-fenced, which owns the concurrent-writer problem. This plan owns the remaining five.

The remedy is a better-designed toolset, not a better-instructed coordinator. That distinction is the plan's whole shape and it is easy to get backwards. Nothing here restricts what a coordinator may do: reading the code, editing it, and refactoring it with the overview only a coordinator has are legitimate and valuable, and a rule telling coordinators to do less of any of that would make the system worse. What this plan targets is the busy work — carrying a value from one surface to another, copying a file so it survives, registering twenty-seven members one at a time — which a coordinator performs only because the work fell through a gap and someone had to catch it.

The test for every item is therefore the same: would a well-designed surface have left this for a human at all? Where the answer is no, the repair belongs in the tool. Adding a rule instructing coordinators not to compensate would remove the compensation and leave the gap, which is strictly worse than the state this plan starts from — the work would still fall through, and nobody would be catching it.

§2 — A roster write that rides someone else's commit

crew member add rewrites docs/state/<project>/crew.json and does not commit it. Verified: there is no commit anywhere in the add path. The roster diff therefore rides whatever commit comes next — for the reporting coordinator that was a plan write (39bc64bc); for this session it would have been a promotion.

The consequence is a commit whose message describes one change and whose content carries two, which is the condition that makes a later reader unable to tell what a commit did. The coordinator registered 27 members by hand (clive-46 through clive-72) because dispatch refuses an unknown member, so the dirty write happened 27 times.

Two repairs, and they are not equivalent: member add commits its own write, or dispatch registers a missing member itself and commits that. The first is smaller. The second removes the reason a coordinator was registering members one at a time, and is the one that matches this plan's thesis.

Until either lands, the reporting coordinator's interim discipline is correct and should be kept: roster changes go in their own chore(crew) commit.

§3 — A cited gate log that will not be there later

crew complete --gate-log-path records where a log lies. It does not copy it. Verified: promotion.py contains zero copy operations.

Workers write gate logs to /tmp or to a worktree's gitignored output/, and both vanish — /tmp to reaping, the worktree to cleanup. The ledger row then cites a path that resolves to nothing, which is the same defect as a fabricated identifier one layer along: every check a reader runs on the row passes, and the evidence is gone.

The reporting coordinator copied 27 gate logs into reports/ship-s13/gates/ by hand. This session did the same twice today, for the same reason, before citing them. Two coordinators independently inventing the same compensation is the signal that it belongs in the surface.

complete should copy the cited log into the run directory and record the copy's path, so the row cites something durable rather than something that was true when written.

§4 — An evidence record that has to be authored before a plan can ship

Shipping a plan requires a cumulative evidence record. When a plan's landings are investigate nodes the deliverable is a report rather than a diff, no evidence record exists, and the coordinator authors one by hand — as the reporting session did for docs/evidence/archive/boundary-method-critical-path-closure-landed.html.

The run already carries what such a record needs: its report path, its commits, its gate, its measured outcome. A complete or edit_plan path that composes the record from the run's own report would remove the hand-authoring step entirely, and would remove the more serious hazard behind it — that a coordinator writing an evidence record by hand is writing the evidence for work it is also judging.

§5 — Per-node wall time and width-at-start, already in the ledger

The ledger row carries dispatched_at and completed_at. Per-node wall time is their difference, and width-at-start is derivable from the live pointers at the dispatch stamp. Neither is emitted, so the reporting coordinator recorded both by hand in post_repair_record.md, and another session asking for the same figures had to ask a human for them.

This is the smallest item and the clearest instance of the plan's shape: the values are present, the derivation is arithmetic, and the surface emits neither. It is a read, not a mechanism.

§6 — A gate that measures the node rather than the fleet

The reporting coordinator ran an SDCC build plus a fast unit and validation delta against a fresh baseline worktree by hand, and found a build break that a worker's test-only gate had missed. That is the single highest-value hand-check in the list, because it caught something no per-node gate could: a node's gate measures its own diff, and an integration break is a property of the merged head.

crew verify-gate exists and the coordinator could not find a way to make it run the fleet suite rather than the node's own gate. This section owns establishing whether that is a missing option, a missing surface, or a misunderstanding of an existing one — and it is scoped as an investigation first, because the reporting coordinator stated plainly that they did not find it rather than that it is absent, and this plan should not convert an unsuccessful search into an asserted gap.

§7 — What this plan does not own

Concurrent writes to shared plan and evidence HTML — the reporting session's merge conflicts and the landing write that discarded a concurrent edit. Those belong to plan-writes-are-not-fenced, because they are one defect and this plan would otherwise carry half of it.

Manifests reading unreadable while the worker works, and the dry run that returns before the launcher it predicts. Both are repaired or in flight: liveness is now evaluated once above the classification chain, and a node is rewriting the dry-run claim.

Worker uv sync repointing the shared editable install. The remedy is already written in the shared guidance and the gap is enforcement rather than knowledge. It wants a dispatch-contract check, which is a different subject from this plan's, and the reporting coordinator hit it twice.

The unpromoted-run grace and the follower lifetime. Both refusals are correct by their own rule every time they fire; whether the values are right is a tuning decision recorded below for the lead rather than a defect this plan repairs.

§8 — The measure that separates a fix from a workaround

Each section above is satisfied only when the hand step disappears, not when it becomes easier. A helper script that copies gate logs, a documented convention for roster commits, or a template for hand-authored evidence records would all reduce the effort and leave the coordinator performing the step — which is the condition this plan exists to remove.

So every node's measure names the hand step and asserts a run completing without it: a promotion whose cited gate log resolves inside the run directory after the source is deleted; a member add leaving a clean tree; a plan shipping on investigate-node landings with no hand-authored evidence file; a ledger read returning wall time and width without arithmetic by the reader.

§ Decisions

Dispatch refuses while any run is unpromoted beyond a 5-minute grace. Measured across 31 promotions: three refusal episodes, about 10.5 minutes of stalled lane refill, every refusal correct by its rule — and each one fired on a run whose landing the coordinator was already verifying, where careful verification ran 8 to 20 minutes. The grace is shorter than the task it exists to accommodate. Raise it, or keep it and accept the stall?

Does member add commit its own write, or does dispatch register a missing member itself? The first is the smaller change; the second removes the reason a coordinator registers members one at a time.

/reckon-ship work-does-not-fall-to-the-coordinator §3

The gap is now specified rather than suspected, and it is half a capability rather than a missing one.

The reporting coordinator corrected their own claim before I asked: they never ran verify-gate and had it fail. They read crew --help, saw "re-run one run's gate at the integrated revision", and judged from the description. An unexecuted search, stated as such.

Checked here. verify-gate reads the run's stored gate command from its committed ledger row and re-runs that command at --revision against --checkout-path, patching the report back onto the row. Its options are project, run, checkout-path, revision, timeout. There is no suite label and no baseline revision.

So it already owns the harder half — re-running at the merged head and recording the verdict against the run rather than only printing it. What it cannot do is run something wider than the node's own gate, or compare against a baseline. Those are exactly the two things the hand-check needed.

What the hand-check was, five times, each as one srun job on a debug partition against the main checkout at the merged head: an SDCC build; a fast preset configured, built and run under the UNIT label; a VALIDATION ctest run; and the sorted failing-rail names compared by comm -13 against a baseline built the same way in a fresh detached worktree at the pre-merge head. It caught a compile error no per-node gate could see — a symbol declared only under a test macro — because a node's gate measures its own diff and an integration break is a property of the merged head. Logs under reports/ship-s13/gates/.

The coordinator's own summary is the section's requirement: if verify-gate can take a suite label and a baseline revision rather than the run's own gate command, that is the whole hand-check.

The transcript settles which of the two repairs removes the work, and it is not the smaller one.

Dispatch refused each unknown member with identical text: project 'imas-efit' has no crew member 'clive-47'; register it with reckon crew member add before dispatching to it. The coordinator registered in batches by shell loop — five to nine members at a time, then several singly — always ahead of the dispatch that needed them once the refusal had taught them to. So the shape of the work was: one refusal per new node the first time, then a pre-registration loop per wave, then one chore(crew) commit per batch.

Their conclusion, which the transcript supports: the repair that removes all of it is dispatch registering an unknown member itself, with the harness and role it was already given. A range form on member add would only shorten the loop — it would make the busy work faster rather than absent, which is the distinction §1 is built on.

Recorded as evidence for the member-registration-owner decision rather than as its resolution; the choice remains the lead's.

Measured on the record actually authored: about 80% composition, one paragraph of judgement the run did not hold.

Transcribed from the run's own report — the measured ratios, the plan targets, the ctest command and its result, a cache caveat and an axis-offset observation. Supplied by the coordinator and not present in the run — one paragraph attributing why the failures closed, naming three intervening landings rather than the plan's own two options, and the decision that the plan ships on its measure rather than being marked superseded.

That makes the repair precise, and it is not full automation. The coordinator's own phrasing is the design: a composed record with an empty mechanism slot the coordinator fills. Compose the 80% from the run, and leave a named slot for the attribution and the shipping decision, which are judgement and belong to a coordinator.

It also disposes of the hazard §4 raised. A record composed from the run is not a coordinator writing evidence for work it is judging; a coordinator adding an attribution paragraph into a named slot, beside composed measurements it did not author, is a different and defensible act.
