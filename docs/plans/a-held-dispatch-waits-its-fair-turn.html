A held dispatch waits its fair turn

When the lane has no room, a dispatch today returns to its coordinator, which retries on its own schedule. A freed slot goes to whichever coordinator happens to retry next, so a session that dispatches a lot keeps winning, and nothing records who is waiting. This plan gives held dispatches a queue that admits them in fair-share order: the sessions using least of the lane go first.

§1 — Context

The local lane's router already publishes a fair share for each coordinator session: admission.sessions[<session>].worker_slots in the lane document, beside new_session_worker_slots and the lane-wide worker_slots. _lane_worker_allowance in reckon/crew/dispatch.py reads that share, most specific first. When the share is zero or less, dispatch raises LaneHeld, a kind of LanePaused: nothing is created, the node stays ready, and the caller is expected to retry.

So the fairness exists at the moment of admission, but not across the wait. Each held coordinator polls on its own. When a slot frees, the next coordinator to retry takes it, whatever the shares say, and a busy session that retries often keeps taking freed slots. Held work is also invisible: the follower's q column counts the router gate's waiting requests, not reckon's held dispatches, and no view lists them.

§2 — A held dispatch is queued

When a dispatch is held for want of a slot, reckon crew dispatch records the complete request as one entry in a queue on shared storage: the project, plan, section, node, session, every dispatch option, the hold's reason, and when it joined. It answers with the entry's identity and its position, and exits as held rather than refused. Queueing the same node again from the same session updates its entry rather than adding a second. --no-queue keeps today's behaviour: hold and return. A paused fleet gate holds the queue as a whole, and no entry is admitted while it is paused.

Done when:

  • A held dispatch, under a lane document granting the session no slot, writes one queue entry carrying everything a later admission needs, and prints its identity and position.
  • Queueing the same node twice from one session leaves one entry. --no-queue writes none.
  • No test reads or writes the real crew state: the queue directory and the lane document are temporary.

§3 — Freed slots go to the sessions using least of the lane

The queue's order is one function. Entries are ranked by their session's use of the lane, so a session with fewer runs in flight comes before a busier one, and entries within one session keep the order they joined. Waiting raises an entry's priority, so a busy session's work is delayed but never starved. The measure of use is the subject of the priority-measure decision.

Done when:

  • With one session holding many runs and another holding none, the next admissions alternate in the lighter session's favour, asserted on the order the function returns.
  • Within one session, entries are admitted in the order they joined.
  • An entry that has waited past a stated bound is admitted ahead of a lighter session's newer entry, so no entry waits without limit.

§4 — One drainer launches queued work

One drainer reads the lane document and the queue. Whenever the router grants room to a session that has queued work, the drainer takes the highest-ranked admissible entry and dispatches it through the same dispatch function a coordinator calls, with that entry's recorded session and options. It then records the run on the entry and removes the entry from the queue. Exactly one drainer runs per lane, held by a host lease; where it runs is the subject of the drainer decision. A dispatch that fails at launch leaves its entry in the queue with the failure recorded, rather than dropping it.

Done when:

  • A test with a lane document that opens one slot finds the highest-ranked entry dispatched, its run id on the entry, and the entry out of the queue.
  • Two drainers started against one queue admit each entry once, shown by two drainers racing over the same entries.
  • A paused fleet gate admits nothing, and a launch failure keeps its entry with the failure recorded.
  • The coordinator's follower sees the run appear as dispatched, as it does for a run it dispatched itself.

§5 — Queued work is visible and can be withdrawn

crew(view="queue") and reckon crew queue list the entries in admission order, each with its session, node, position, wait and rank. reckon crew queue withdraw removes one entry, and a withdrawn entry is never launched. The follower's q column counts the session's own queued dispatches beside the router's waiting requests. The obligations view reports a queued node as waiting, not as missing.

Done when: a test lists entries in the order §3 admits them; a withdrawn entry is never dispatched by the drainer; and the queue view and the CLI listing report the same entries, so the two surfaces cannot disagree.

§ Decisions

Where does the drainer run?

Recommended: a fleet service. The lane is shared across projects, so a per-project producer would need cross-project coordination to stay fair, while a declared service already runs once under its lease and survives a move.

What measures a session's use of the lane?

Recommended: runs in flight. It matches the router's per-session share and needs no new accounting; a recent-usage window is fairer to bursty sessions but has to be measured and stored.

Which lanes queue a held dispatch?

Recommended: the local lane first. It is the lane with published fair shares, and a metered lane's hold is a budget window rather than contention.
