§1 — Why: every node is verified and nothing is designed

The lead's report on 4 October: workers produce duplicate, shallow implementations with wide interfaces; plan-section dispatches are far too narrow; the result is busy work and half-done projects; the orchestrators are not orchestrating. The 26 September review of the crew pattern reached the same verdict from measurement — better-verified, not better-designed; the pattern verifies nodes and nothing in it owns the codebase (crew pattern review §6) — and its eight remedy plans have since landed most of their mechanics. The readings below, taken on 4 October, say why the symptom survived them.

nova, 15 September to 4 October. 3,670 commits, of which 1,020 are plan or record edits, 749 are promotions (296 of them reviews) and 182 are features. 777 ledger rows: 390 implement runs and 342 review runs over 25 active plans, of which 16 sit between impl 0.80 and 0.9995 and none has shipped since. The implement runs' done-whens run to a median 178 words — they are not under-specified, they are slivers: one measured deliverable from a section, 33 minutes median wall, eighty live worktrees. The record load is the busy work: docs/figures holds 1.2 GB and gained 141 probe and census scripts in the window, and one promoted node landed 4,876,123 lines across 49 files. The pattern review's nova finding stands: a public surface that barely moved while the plan and figure surface exploded.

reckon, the same window. The crew package is 67,197 lines in 58 modules; dispatch.py is 12,432 lines, recovery.py 11,129 and promotion.py 8,628. The reckon crew surface has 36 subcommands and dispatch takes 40 options. 103,571 lines were added to the package in three weeks against 18,600 deleted. 150 plans, 45 active, eight of them about this problem. The coordinator skill set is 37,619 words across 218 KB. Every lesson became a flag, a refusal, a view or a paragraph, and the system that is meant to stop wide shallow interfaces is the widest, shallowest interface on the workstation.

The design review was built and has never run. reckon/crew/plan_review.py holds a store, a fingerprint join, an answer loop and a recurrence count; two rubric prompts exist, one of them the prior-art-and-depth review (reuse_search, deep_module, thin_wrapper, duplicate_owner); dispatch carries a gate that reads the store. Across five projects the store holds zero plan reviews. The rubric prompts are loaded by nothing but their own tests. No command composes the review, nothing parses a reviewer's output into the store, and the gate ships report-only because — its own comment says — the node that writes a review is not built. Meanwhile the per-node code review, which has run 458 times in nova at a median score of 89, is told to stay inside the diff, not re-derive the implementation, not review code the node did not touch, so a second copy of an existing mechanism is out of its scope by construction.

The coordinator cannot say what it knows. A dispatch is one GOAL line and a plan pointer in front of about 150 lines of fences and manifest keys. --brief is mutually exclusive with --plan, and the skill says do not restate plan context, decisions, research, or done-when criteria in the worker prompt. So the one thing a coordinator learns between nodes — which module to extend, which interface not to widen, what a sibling node just found — has no channel to the next worker except editing the plan and committing it, a beat that costs the coordinator the same bookkeeping it spends 21.6 responses per promotion on. The skill also forbids it to inspect implementation details beyond what scoping or review requires, which is the reading a design judgement needs.

The unit of dispatch is the measure, not the deliverable. The eight-property contract and the single-goal lint push every node to one measurable, time-boxed, exclusively scoped outcome. A plan section — the thing a reader would call a deliverable — becomes three to ten nodes, each locally correct, each reviewed on its own diff, with the section's design held by no one. Narrow dispatch is not a wording problem; it is the contract working as written.

This plan changes four things and names what it leaves alone. A coordinator can dispatch a section with a brief (§2). The design review composes, runs on the local lane and lands a record the coordinator reads and answers, without the coordinator remembering to run it (§3). The code review judges reuse (§4). The build skill becomes a short loop whose middle step is a decision (§5). Consolidating nova and capping open work belong to the plans that already own them (§6).
