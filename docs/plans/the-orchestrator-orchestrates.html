§1 — Why: every node is verified and nothing is designed

The lead's report on 4 October: workers produce duplicate, shallow implementations with wide interfaces; plan-section dispatches are far too narrow; the result is busy work and half-done projects; the orchestrators are not orchestrating. The 26 September review of the crew pattern reached the same verdict from measurement — better-verified, not better-designed; the pattern verifies nodes and nothing in it owns the codebase (crew pattern review §6) — and its eight remedy plans have since landed most of their mechanics. The readings below, taken on 4 October, say why the symptom survived them.

nova, 15 September to 4 October. 3,670 commits, of which 1,020 are plan or record edits, 749 are promotions (296 of them reviews) and 182 are features. 777 ledger rows: 390 implement runs and 342 review runs over 25 active plans, of which 16 sit between impl 0.80 and 0.9995 and none has shipped since. The implement runs' done-whens run to a median 178 words — they are not under-specified, they are slivers: one measured deliverable from a section, 33 minutes median wall, eighty live worktrees. The record load is the busy work: docs/figures holds 1.2 GB and gained 141 probe and census scripts in the window, and one promoted node landed 4,876,123 lines across 49 files. The pattern review's nova finding stands: a public surface that barely moved while the plan and figure surface exploded.

reckon, the same window. The crew package is 67,197 lines in 58 modules; dispatch.py is 12,432 lines, recovery.py 11,129 and promotion.py 8,628. The reckon crew surface has 36 subcommands and dispatch takes 40 options. 103,571 lines were added to the package in three weeks against 18,600 deleted. 150 plans, 45 active, eight of them about this problem. The coordinator skill set is 37,619 words across 218 KB. Every lesson became a flag, a refusal, a view or a paragraph, and the system that is meant to stop wide shallow interfaces is the widest, shallowest interface on the workstation.

The design review was built and has never run. reckon/crew/plan_review.py holds a store, a fingerprint join, an answer loop and a recurrence count; two rubric prompts exist, one of them the prior-art-and-depth review (reuse_search, deep_module, thin_wrapper, duplicate_owner); dispatch carries a gate that reads the store. Across five projects the store holds zero plan reviews. The rubric prompts are loaded by nothing but their own tests. No command composes the review, nothing parses a reviewer's output into the store, and the gate ships report-only because — its own comment says — the node that writes a review is not built. Meanwhile the per-node code review, which has run 458 times in nova at a median score of 89, is told to stay inside the diff, not re-derive the implementation, not review code the node did not touch, so a second copy of an existing mechanism is out of its scope by construction.

The coordinator cannot say what it knows. A dispatch is one GOAL line and a plan pointer in front of about 150 lines of fences and manifest keys. --brief is mutually exclusive with --plan, and the skill says do not restate plan context, decisions, research, or done-when criteria in the worker prompt. So the one thing a coordinator learns between nodes — which module to extend, which interface not to widen, what a sibling node just found — has no channel to the next worker except editing the plan and committing it, a beat that costs the coordinator the same bookkeeping it spends 21.6 responses per promotion on. The skill also forbids it to inspect implementation details beyond what scoping or review requires, which is the reading a design judgement needs.

The unit of dispatch is the measure, not the deliverable. The eight-property contract and the single-goal lint push every node to one measurable, time-boxed, exclusively scoped outcome. A plan section — the thing a reader would call a deliverable — becomes three to ten nodes, each locally correct, each reviewed on its own diff, with the section's design held by no one. Narrow dispatch is not a wording problem; it is the contract working as written.

This plan changes four things and names what it leaves alone. A coordinator can dispatch a section with a brief (§2). The design review composes, runs on the local lane and lands a record the coordinator reads and answers, without the coordinator remembering to run it (§3). The code review judges reuse (§4). The build skill becomes a short loop whose middle step is a decision (§5). Consolidating nova and capping open work belong to the plans that already own them (§6).

§2 — A section dispatches with the coordinator's brief

Deliverable. reckon crew dispatch --plan <slug> --section <id> --brief <file> is accepted. The plan section stays the semantic authority and every gate that joins a node to its committed section — base-blob visibility, the plan-review gate, the done-when overlap check — runs exactly as it does today. The brief is the coordinator's voice beside it: the reuse map's verdicts for this section, the module to extend and the one not to fork, the interface the change must not widen, what sibling nodes found, which open question the worker may not settle. The prompt carries both, in this order: PLAN project:slug §N, then BRIEF with the file's bytes verbatim. The brief's sha256 and stored copy are recorded on the run and the ledger row as they are for a brief-only run. Landing stays plan-carried: the record goes to the section and impl is expected to move. A brief without a plan keeps its present meaning and its promotion owner requirement.

Why not a plan edit instead. A plan is for what should outlive the session; a brief is for what the next worker needs now. Forcing the second through the first is why coordinators stopped saying it. The brief's bytes are stored on the run, so nothing a worker read is lost; a coordinator that finds a brief worth keeping promotes it into the plan at the landing beat.

Done when validate_node accepts a node naming a plan, a section and a brief; plan_dispatch on that node reaches the committed-section gate and the plan-review gate (asserted with the same spy the exclusivity test used, now expecting the call); the composed prompt carries the PLAN line and the brief's text, and a brief-only prompt is byte-identical to today's; reckon crew dispatch --dry-run with all three succeeds; and tests/test_brief_beside_a_plan_section.py passes with its negative control — the pair check restored in validate_node — turning the acceptance case red.

§3 — The design review runs before the first build node, and lands where the gate reads

Deliverable. One command, reckon crew review-plan --project P --plan <slug> --session S [--rubric design|content] [--local], composes and dispatches the plan review the store was built for. It is a brief dispatch under the review role: the brief is the rubric prompt from reckon/crew/prompts/ with the plan's path, the codebase roots to search and the report path filled in; the node id is plan-review-of-<slug>, which the gate already exempts; the write path is one report directory under the crew reports root. Beside the report the dispatch writes a sidecar naming the plan slug, version, blob sha and content fingerprint it composed for, so the record is keyed to what the reviewer read and not to what the plan says later.

The reviewer emits the rubric's RUBRIC and FINDING lines into the report. plan_review.parse_review_report turns them into the store's record shape — one finding per line with a stable id, type, text, and the would-change verdict and reason — and plan_review.store_delivered_report joins report and sidecar and writes through store_plan_review. The gate stores at read. When require_plan_reviewed finds no stored review for the plan's fingerprint, it looks for a delivered report whose sidecar carries that fingerprint, stores it, and judges the result. The coordinator never runs a store step and never has to remember one; it dispatches the review, and the next build dispatch finds it.

The coordinator reads the review through crew(project, view="plan-review", plan=<slug>): the stored record, its unanswered findings, and any delivered report not yet stored. It answers through reckon crew review-plan --project P --plan <slug> --answer <finding-id> --acted|--declined "<reason>", which is record_response behind one flag pair. The design rubric's reuse_search finding is the reuse map the build skill has asked for in prose since hard rule 17: it names the mechanism and the file that owns it, and the coordinator's brief for each section cites it.

Done when reckon crew review-plan --dry-run against a committed plan resolves a brief dispatch whose node id is plan-review-of-<slug> and whose brief contains the design rubric's four items; a fixture report with two findings parses to two findings with ids, types and verdicts, and a report missing a RUBRIC line reports that item absent; require_plan_reviewed in enforce mode, with no stored review and a delivered report plus sidecar for the plan's fingerprint, stores the record and then refuses by the unanswered finding's name, and admits the build once both findings are answered; the MCP view returns the stored record and the unanswered ids; and tests/test_plan_review_dispatch.py passes with its negative control — the store-at-read branch removed — turning the enforce-mode case red.

Enforcement. The gate's mode stays report in the shipped defaults until the first five plan reviews have landed through this path; the decision below records when it flips.

§4 — The code review judges reuse

Deliverable. The landed-node review prompt gains a reuse clause under its fit dimension and a matching reading instruction: for each function, class or module the diff adds, the reviewer searches the repository for an existing mechanism with the same capability and, when one exists, emits a FINDING naming the owning file and whether the addition extends, wraps or duplicates it. The reading bound is kept — the reviewer opens the owner it names and nothing else — so the review stays ten minutes. A duplicate of an owned mechanism scores fit below the light tier's floor, which is what makes the finding reach the coordinator rather than the archive.

Done when reckon/crew/prompts/review.md states the reuse clause in the fit dimension and the search instruction in the reading section, the parser still reads a review emitted under the new prompt (the existing prompt-contract tests pass unchanged), and tests/test_review_prompt_contract.py asserts the clause with a control that deletes it.

§5 — The coordinator's loop

Deliverable. skills/reckon-build/SKILL.md rewritten as the coordinator's loop in at most 4,000 words, which is the-crew-contract-fits-one-read §3 brought forward at the lead's direction of 4 October. The present text moves whole to references/coordinator-reference.md so no enforced rule is lost while the core is read end to end. The loop has six steps and the fourth is the one the present skill lacks:

  1. Read the plan, the code it names and the prior evidence. Reading code for design is the coordinator's job; writing product code is not.
  2. Review the design with reckon crew review-plan before the first build node, and answer every finding. The reuse map is its output.
  3. Shape the wave. The unit of dispatch is the section's deliverable. A section is one node unless two of its write scopes must run in parallel. Each node carries a brief citing the reuse map: the module to extend, the interface not to widen, the question the worker may not settle.
  4. Decide between nodes. At each landing read the diff for design, not only for scope: a new helper beside an owner, a wrapper over a mechanism that should have been extended, a widened surface. Then choose one of three: accept and record; re-brief the next node; or re-plan the section and say so in the plan. Nothing is dispatched on an unverified or undecided landing.
  5. Integrate the reviewed head, run the integrated gate, promote, write the plan in the same beat.
  6. Close the section when its deliverable is in the tree, not when its last sliver promoted; drain followups; report what changed, what remains and why.

The core keeps the sentences the skill-contract tests pin and the four fences the tool enforces; it drops every paragraph that restates a refusal the tool will raise. The references stay for a coordinator composing a delegation by hand.

Done when skills/reckon-build/SKILL.md is at most 4,000 words and states the six steps; tests/test_skill_contracts.py and tests/test_skill_contract_parity.py pass; the former text is reachable at references/coordinator-reference.md; and the total of the skill and its references is recorded in this plan's landing against the 37,619 words measured here.
