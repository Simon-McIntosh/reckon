The Crew surface · a meter, a rate, and the one number that combines them

Where You Are, And How Fast

A quota meter says where a lane stands. A burn rate says how fast it is moving. Neither predicts exhaustion, and shown as two independent tiles they reproduce the exact blindness that spent a lane on 2026-09-03: it read clear at thirteen percent used while burning at seven and a half times sustainable, and the position figure only became alarming once it was too late to act on. This plan renders the two on one mark, with the projected exhaustion beside the reset, so the dangerous combination is legible instead of inferable.

§1 — Why this exists

Measured by a peer coordinator against the codex account surface and this repository's run corpus, and handed over rather than assumed. Four facts constrain the design, and each of them rules out a surface someone would otherwise build.

MeasurementWhat it rules out
The codex account exposes exactly one metered window: primary with windowDurationMins: 10080, and secondary: null. A five-hour meter for this backend. That period is another provider's concept, so a surface that renders one is displaying a window the account does not report. The window's duration is read from the payload and named on the surface; a backend reporting no window renders no meter rather than a default one.
The meter charges total context read, cached tokens included. Cache hit rate is 96.0% median over 1,970 runs; 23.0B of 23.6B input was cached re-reads, charged anyway. A cache-hit tile beside a burn tile. Placed together they imply a lever that does not exist — improving the hit rate does not reduce what the meter charges.
Rolling burn over four windows from the same corpus: 10.34× at one hour, 7.51× at five, 1.57× at twenty-four, 1.71× at one hundred and sixty-eight. A single burn figure. Waves are bursty — several coordinators dispatching at once spike the short window and it decays to nothing between waves — so one number alone cries wolf. At least two windows are always shown together.
The meter moved from 12% to 20% during one session, none of it that session's work, while the run corpus grew from 2,555 to 2,567 rows mid-read. A cached figure. Every value carries its observation time and is re-derived on read, because a stale burn number is worse than none: it looks current.

One caveat travels with the rates and belongs on the surface, not only in this plan. Run-level input tokens are an upper bound, because a resumed session records its whole accumulated context on every turn. The account surface is the authority for position; the derived rate is a trend indicator. A surface that presents them with equal confidence misrepresents the weaker one.

§2 — One mark, not two tiles

Position and rate are orthogonal, and the state that costs a session is low position with high burn — which two adjacent tiles render as reassuring, since the eye reads the small number and stops. The synthesis is a projection: at the observed rate, when is the ceiling reached, and is that before or after the window resets?

So the meter is one bar carrying three marks: the position filled from usedPercent, the reset at its right edge labelled with the window duration the payload reported, and the projected exhaustion plotted on the same axis from position and rate together. When the projection falls left of the reset the lane will run out before it refills, and the bar says so; when it falls right, the lane is sustainable at the current rate however high the instantaneous multiple looks. That comparison is the actionable fact and no tile in the fleet currently expresses it.

Gate for this plan, recorded when the measure runs: against this workstation's recorded backends the Crew surface renders one meter per backend that reports a window, labelled with that window's own duration, carrying the position, the reset and the projected exhaustion, beside at least two rate windows and every figure's observation time. Its evidence is a rendered capture of the unsustainable and the sustainable case with their figures recorded beside them, plus the surface rendering no meter at all for a backend whose payload reports no window.

§4 — Out of scope

The derivation itself, which belongs to dispatch-cost-per-durable-node and is this plan's declared prerequisite: the rate computation lives in Python so a CLI and MCP caller reach the same figures, and this plan only renders what that read returns. Building a second computation in the surface would be the defect the repository's invariant exists to prevent. The burn fence that refuses a dispatch is also that plan's, and is a different act from showing a reader where they stand. No cache-hit figure, for the measured reason above. And no alerting: a surface that shows the projection is not the same as a mechanism that interrupts someone, and the second is a decision about attention that this plan does not make.

Two neighbouring surfaces are deliberately not built here, and the reason is sequencing rather than scope. “Is this run costing more than it should” needs a calibrated expectation per configuration, and “which backend returns most work per token” needs a ranking that charges for rework. Both are crew(view="routing") in the prerequisite plan's own section, which does not exist yet, and its coordinator column lands before that ranking deliberately — without it a ranking scores work transferred from a worker to its coordinator as a saving. So this surface consumes that view when it arrives rather than deriving either figure now: a ranking built in JSX from sparse fields would be wrong twice over, once from the coverage above and once from the missing column.

§ Decisions

How are position and rate presented?

Two tiles are what the fleet effectively had on 2026-09-03: the lane read thirteen percent used, the reader relaxed, and the burn that settled it was seven and a half times sustainable. The rate alone cries wolf, because the same corpus reads 10.3 at one hour and 1.7 at a week. Only the projection against the reset turns two figures into a decision.

Where does the window period on the meter come from?

The codex account reports one window of 10080 minutes and a null secondary, so a five-hour meter would display a period that account never reported. A surface that assumes a period is inventing state, which is the failure this repository has spent the day removing.

How many rate windows are shown?

One number is unreadable without its neighbour: 10.3 and 1.7 come from the same corpus at different horizons, and either alone misleads in a different direction. A reader-chosen single window guarantees somebody reads the alarming one in isolation.

Are the derived rates presented with the same confidence as the meter?

A resumed session records its whole accumulated context on every turn, so run-level input tokens over-count. Presenting a trend indicator as a measurement is how a reader comes to distrust every figure on the surface once one of them proves soft.
