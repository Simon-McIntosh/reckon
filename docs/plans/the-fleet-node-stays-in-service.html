reckon · fleet node operation

The fleet node stays in service

The fleet allocation, job 1281970, has run on 98dci4-clu-2002 since 8 October. Slurm drained that node at 08:11:39 CEST the same morning, six hours after the move onto it, with the reason Kill task failed. The node the fleet left, clu-2018, was drained the same way on 3 October, and the GPU serving node gpu-0003 was drained the same way on 17 October. Each time, the step or job that failed to die had been cancelled by this account. The previous plan moved the fleet and fixed what loaded the node; it left the mechanism that drains a node untouched. This plan removes that mechanism, repairs the migrate command so it can carry a second move, bounds each worker step's memory, measures what the watch producers cost, and then moves the fleet on a schedule of our choosing.

§1 — What drained three nodes, and what loads this one

A drain does not end a running job. It stops the scheduler placing new jobs on the node, and an administrator must resume the node after the last job leaves. The fleet job has no wall clock, so the drain itself costs nothing today. Two things make it matter. First, if the job ends or is requeued, it cannot return to this node. Second, an administrator servicing the node may end the job. The move is therefore not urgent. But it should not be needed every week, and at present it is.

The configuration explains the timing. KillWait is 30 s and UnkillableStepTimeout is 60 s. When a step is cancelled, slurmstepd signals every process in the step's cgroup, then SIGKILLs them. If any process survives the timeout, slurmd drains the node. The only process SIGKILL cannot end is one in uninterruptible sleep, which on this cluster almost always means a GPFS call or a GPU driver teardown. The job records 482 steps CANCELLED by 39486 against 5,606 completed. Each cancellation is one draw against that timeout. Every step that ends by cancellation rather than by its task exiting carries a chance of draining the node. The empty cgroups step_251, step_1080 and step_5531 are still present under the job.

The stop path in reckon/crew/dispatch_launch.py sends SIGTERM to the worker pid and, after the stop grace or the terminal-manifest grace, sends SIGKILL. Two cases are possible. If that pid is the srun client, the SIGKILL ends the client and the step is cancelled from outside. If it is the worker inside the step, the SIGKILL leaves the step's other processes to slurmstepd's kill. Either way, ending the step is the scheduler's kill rather than the task's own exit, and that is the event that drains. Which of the two it is, and which run step 251 belonged to, is §2's question.

§3 — A stopped worker ends inside its step

The step must end by its own task exiting, never by the scheduler's kill. The task that a worker step runs gains a reaper as its first process. On SIGTERM the reaper signals the worker's process group, then waits for every process in the step's cgroup to exit, and only then exits itself, carrying the worker's status. The wait has no deadline shorter than a GPFS stall: a process in uninterruptible sleep is waited for, not escalated, because escalation is exactly what drains the node. The stop path signals the reaper and never sends SIGKILL to the srun client while the step is live. If a worker still runs past a long ceiling (named in the brief, at least ten minutes), the reaper records the processes and their wait channels to the run directory and keeps waiting. It does not escalate.

§2's evidence sets where the reaper sits and which pid the stop path signals, so the gate below holds this section until §2 lands. Done when two things hold. A unit test proves that the stop path signals the reaper's pid and records no SIGKILL to an srun client. And a probe on the fleet allocation stops a worker step whose child ignores SIGTERM for 40 s: sacct then reports the step COMPLETED, the reaper's record names the child, and the node's state is unchanged. The negative control runs the same probe through the old path, by direct scancel of the step, and shows CANCELLED.

§4 — The migrate command starts a second move

reckon fleet-node migrate reads the newest move-*/ledger.json, and once that ledger's next step is done it prints migration complete forever. Its census step is reachable only when no ledger exists, so the command can carry exactly one move. Measured 18 October: --dry-run printed migration complete while the fleet ran on a drained node. The fix: when the newest ledger is done, and the fleet record names a job other than the one that ledger's stand-up promoted or a later node, the next invocation begins a new move at census with a new move-<stamp> ledger, and --dry-run says so. The earlier ledger is kept untouched. In the same file pair, reckon fleet-node status replaces its warning that "the allocation ends when the drain completes". The replacement states that the scheduler places no new job on the node, that the allocation continues until it ends, and that the node returns only when an administrator resumes it.

Done when tests in tests/ cover a completed ledger followed by a fresh census and the dry-run text in both states, and the status wording. The negative control is a ledger mid-move, at cutover, which must still resume that move rather than start a new one.

§6 — A worker step carries a memory bound

The four processes killed on 17 October were not runaways. They belonged to two nova solver runs, r-20261017T073925596435-axis-facing-owner (steps 5614 and 5621) and r-20261017T102932384946-owner-cell-current (step 5700). Each step's stderr records Slurm's oom_kill event, and both runs were doing map solves at the 2,098-point size, which legitimately need 52–59 GB. The defect is placement, not leakage. The fleet allocation is 96 GB shared by every session and worker, so one such solve takes more than half of it. A second one, or the sessions' own growth, takes the whole job to its limit, and the OOM killer then picks the largest process anywhere in the job.

Every worker step is therefore placed with an explicit --mem, so undeclared work stays inside a share that leaves room for the sessions. A node whose work needs more declares it at dispatch, and the declaration is recorded on the run. A declaration above the fleet's per-node ceiling is placed in its own job on a *_debug partition rather than inside the fleet allocation. The default and the ceiling are the open decision worker-step-memory below. Done when a test shows the placed srun argv carrying the default and a declared bound, and a test shows a declaration above the ceiling routed to its own job. A probe step on the fleet allocation must also show its cgroup memory.max equal to the bound. The negative control is a probe allocating past its bound: its OOM event lands in the step's memory.events and not the job's.

§8 — Move the fleet off the drained node

Once §3 and §5 have landed, move the fleet with reckon fleet-node migrate, from census to retire, in the same tabs, with coordinators resumed. The move is live in the sense the previous plan built: sessions keep their conversations and tabs, and in-flight workers finish or are resumed by run on the new node. A process cannot cross nodes, so a short quiesce window is unavoidable, and it is taken as late as possible. The retire step cancels job 1281970, which is irreversible and needs the lead's --confirm. Moving before §3 lands would carry the drain mechanism to the new node, which is what happened on 8 October. Done when the move ledger records every census session cut over and verified, and the new node's state is ALLOCATED without DRAIN after the first wave of worker stops.
