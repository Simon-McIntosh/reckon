§1 — Why: four coordinators rebuilt power_convertor's real shape by hand

On 2026-10-21 the lead asked whether reckon can tell an agent where a project stands: its direction, its critical path, the work queued behind it and which task matters most. The test project was power_convertor, which has 17 plans, two open sprints and four coordinator sessions, and whose active sprint S3 promises a presentation in the week of 19 October. Its roadmap answered as follows.

What the roadmap reportedWhat the plans' own metadata and the coordinators said
8 of the 9 open plans are ready, 0 are blocked, and every plan's unlocks is empty.The plans declare 30 open sections and 16 cross-plan section edges in plan-section-depends-on. 11 of the 30 open sections wait on another open section.
The critical path is one plan, coil-supply-report-rebuild, at 35 h, and the five open paths are five single plans.From the stored edges, the effort-weighted section chain is iter-switched-terminal-transients#s6 (8 h) → coil-supply-report-rebuild#s4 (10 h) → #s8 (5 h). Every coordinator named a link one step further back, a solver instability in shared-coil-supply-model#s4 that no plan declares as a wait.

Nothing about work in flight.The node building iter-switched-terminal-transients#s6, the head of the chain, has been blocked since 2026-10-17. One run reads interrupted in the roadmap and running in crew live.
view=detail: refused at 141,346 characters against an 80,000 ceiling.The lossless CLI report is 214,910 bytes for 17 plans.

All four coordinators were asked how they chose their current node. Each said it was their own judgement, informed by peer messages, and that no tool ranked anything. They reconstructed blocking chains, file holds (who is writing switched_circuit.py now and who is next), the cross-plan sections that consume their output, and duplicated scope (shared-coil-supply-model#s6 and iter-switched-terminal-transients#s6 describe the same move of the integrated run). They did it by reading plan HTML, crew pointer files and each other's messages. One coordinator put it as "much of my time is relay messaging between sessions, because holds and cross-plan waits aren't in any shared view".

The information is mostly stored already. Sections carry data-effort-hours, declarations and cross-plan dependencies, and live runs carry their plan and section. The analyser builds its graph between plans. It adds plan-level depends_on refs and gate edges to that graph, but plan-section-depends-on entries only decide each plan's own section readiness and never reach it. Every cross-plan edge in power_convertor is of that third kind, so none becomes an unlocks, and a plan with any ready section reads ready. The measurement, the prototype that derived the section chain, and the code references are in docs/evidence/archive/a-project-reads-as-one-picture-landed.html. This plan makes the section the unit of the graph and derives the plan view from it. The answer contract the MCP serves it through is the-mcp-answers-the-question-asked.

§2 — The critical path runs through sections

The section edges are already resolved; they are never lifted into the plan graph. _section_scoped_edges in reckon/roadmap.py (near line 1079) parses every plan-section-depends-on entry through parse_plan_ref, resolves project:-qualified refs against the named mount, judges satisfaction through _section_satisfied and declared_section_identities, refuses malformed refs through section_dependency_refusals, and emits dangling-hard-dependency. _plan_section_deps (near line 409), _gate_section_edges (near line 302) and _after_edges (near line 987) are the other edge sources. The new work is only lifting the resolved, unsatisfied cross-plan section edges into the graph the roadmap already closes over (local_graph and dependents), so the existing unlocks (near line 3324) and the longest_path walk (near line 3434) see them. The path is weighted by each open section's effort_hours. No second resolver is written.

Surface: critical_path and open_paths gain a sections list beside their plans, and unlocks is the existing field, now non-empty where sections wait. Nothing else is added.

Done when: a synthetic three-plan fixture whose only cross-plan wiring is plan-section-depends-on, A#s2 → B#s3 → C#s1, reports a critical path naming those three sections in that order with their summed effort, and reports C in B's unlocks. The negative control is the same fixture against the base commit, where the path is a single plan and unlocks is empty, so the test fails there. On power_convertor the critical path spans at least two plans and names iter-switched-terminal-transients#s6, recorded in the gate log with the head it ran at.

§3 — The ranking reads signals that differ between rows

The ranking is the existing priority key in reckon/roadmap.py (near line 3516). It orders ready work by critical-path membership, sprint position, ROI and unlocks, and it is extended rather than rewritten. With §2's edges, critical-path membership and unlocks become section-aware. A stuck signal is added after unlocks: two or more attempts on the section (from section_attempts), or a run on it blocked for more than a day, which raises the row so that it is seen rather than skipped. ROI and effort stay as late tie-breakers.

The placing signal goes in the existing reason field of immediate_roadmap rows (near line 3551), one vocabulary for plan rows and section rows alike; no second field is added. The flat-priority-signal finding first planned here is dropped. The brief (§7) states the share of open plans carrying one ROI value in its text, which needs no new finding kind.

Done when: on a fixture where every plan is ROI high and effort M, the ranked order puts the section with the largest downstream reach first, and every row's reason names a signal other than ROI. On the base commit the same fixture returns plans in sprint and slug order, so the test fails there.

§4 — Work in flight sits on its section

The run classifier already has one owner. partition_live_runs in reckon/mcp_views.py (near line 1860) delegates to classify_pointer in reckon/crew/recovery.py, and its docstring already holds that the roadmap, the sprint view and recover cannot disagree at plan level. The extension is from plan to plan#section: _run_row and _run_target_plan (near lines 1471 and 1486) carry the run's section, so a section row lists its runs with that one classification. The row also lists the write paths they hold, from the dispatch scope claims the scopes view already reads. The interrupted-against-running pair measured on power_convertor is investigated first. It is either a second classification path, which this section removes, or a stale read, and the gate log says which.

A section's blocking is named by kind, using the existing blocker fields: section dependency blockers (section_readiness), gate_blockers and decision_blockers. Two kinds are new: run-blocked, where a manifest reports blocked, and held-path. The external-data kind first planned is dropped, because a gate already names data not yet in the repository.

Done when: a fixture live pointer with a plan and section appears on that section's roadmap row with the classification crew live gives it, and a fixture whose manifest reports blocked makes its section run-blocked with the blocker text. Two fixture runs claiming one path report the holder and the queued run, in that order. On the base commit section rows carry no runs, so the test fails there. The gate log records the explanation of the power_convertor pair.

§5 — A wait nobody declared is a finding

Two wiring findings make the edges coordinators carry in messages visible. undeclared-wait fires when a section's blocked run, gate measure or open followup names another section ref (slug#sN or project:slug#sN) that the section's dependencies do not include. It names both refs and the text that mentioned them, so the repair is one section_depends_on.<sid> write. overlapping-scope fires when two open sections in different plans declare intersecting write paths in their briefs or dispatch history. That is the shape of shared-coil-supply-model#s6 and iter-switched-terminal-transients#s6, the same integrated run planned twice.

Both are emitted through the existing _finding helper in reckon/roadmap.py (near line 1213) into wiring_findings, severity warn, like every other wiring finding. They add two codes and no surface. Neither invents an edge: the analyser reports the suspected wait and leaves the decision to whoever owns the plan.

Done when: a fixture whose blocked manifest names B#s4 from a section with no edge to it emits undeclared-wait with both refs, and the same fixture with the edge declared emits nothing. Two fixture sections claiming one path emit overlapping-scope, and two claiming disjoint paths do not. On power_convertor the findings are listed in the gate log, and each is checked by hand as a true or false positive, with the count of each recorded.

§7 — One brief an agent can act on

project_brief(project, since=None), one public function in reckon/roadmap.py beside the payload it reads, composes from §2 to §5 the page a coordinator needs before choosing work. It runs to at most 6,000 characters of plain text plus the typed rows behind each line, and contains:

  • the active sprint's goal and its time left;
  • both completion percentages, and the share of open plans carrying one ROI value when that share is 80 % or more;
  • the critical path as sections, with hours, the runs on it and what holds its head;
  • the ten highest-ranked ready sections with their reason;
  • true blockers grouped by kind;
  • at-risk work: sections with repeated attempts, runs blocked more than a day, interrupted runs;
  • wiring findings, counted, with the top three shown;
  • when since is given, what changed after that time.

The brief is a composition, not a second analyser: every figure in it is read from the payload §2 to §5 produce. Surface: one public function and one option on the existing CLI command, reckon roadmap --brief. No new MCP view is added; the MCP serves the brief as roadmap's default answer, under the contract the-mcp-answers-the-question-asked sets.

Done when: a fixture is built so that its critical-path head, its top-ranked ready section, its oldest blocked run and one undeclared wait are known in advance, and the brief names all four within 6,000 characters. The base commit has no brief and its plan-level analyser names a different head, so the test fails there. A second arm changes one fixture edge and requires the brief's critical-path line to change with it. On power_convertor the brief names the critical-path head, the run blocked since 2026-10-17 and the three ready sections with the largest reach. A parity test that every number in the brief equals its roadmap field stays as a guard against drift, not as the control.

§8 — Measured on power_convertor and two other mounts

The coordinators' questions are asked again through the new reads on power_convertor, nova and reckon itself. They are: what is critical, what is blocking a named section, who holds a named file, and what should run next. Each answer is compared with what the owning coordinator says is true, and the result goes to the cumulative landed record.

Done when: each question is answered by one call on each of the three mounts within its read deadline. Every disagreement with a coordinator is listed with its cause, as either a wiring fault in the plans or a defect in the derivation, and each derivation defect becomes a section of this plan or a new plan. Two controls make the measure able to fail. The same four questions put to the base commit on the same power_convertor snapshot must disagree with the coordinators at least on the critical path and the first-ranked item, as they do today. And a copy of the snapshot with one declared cross-plan edge removed must move the critical path, and must raise undeclared-wait when a blocker still names the removed edge.

§9 — A section can be descoped

A new declaration, descoped, with a required reason, counts as closed for close and for the critical path but not as done for impl, and is shown in history. PLAN-FORMAT.md, the published schema and the reckon-edit skill state the declaration in the same commit as the parser change.

Done when: a plan whose only open section is descoped closes, its impl excludes that section, and a descoped declaration with no reason is refused. On the base commit the declaration is refused as unknown, and the test fails there.

§10 — A refused set names the paths that work

Two coordinators concluded that edit_plan cannot write a section's waits or its effort, and edited plan HTML by hand. The base refusal for a bare set section_depends_on already names the keyed form (reckon/_store_section_ops.py, near line 438), so that refusal is not the cause; the 36 stale_server_code results in the same sessions point to old MCP images. What a refusal does not do is list what can be written instead. set after is refused as unsupported plan set path 'after', and the refusal names no settable path and none of the keyed forms (section_depends_on.<sid>, sections.<sid>.<field>, gates.<id>.<field>). An unsupported-path refusal now carries the settable top-level paths and the keyed forms, and names the nearest match when there is one.

Done when: the refusal for an unsupported plan set path lists the settable paths and the keyed forms, and for a near miss (effort-hours) names effort_hours. On the base commit the refusal for set after lists no settable path, so the test fails there.

§11 — Every relationship can be written, after included

after cannot be changed through edit_plan in either mode. A state set refuses it as an unsupported path, because it is missing from the store's settable plan fields, and text mode refuses any change to the parsed plan state, which includes after. A review that finds a stale soft edge can therefore only remove it by a direct HTML edit. after joins depends_on, blocks and informs as a whole-list set, validated with the same ref grammar.

Done when: a fixture plan's after is replaced, emptied and given a qualified ref through set, each read back through read_plan, and an unparseable ref is refused. On the base commit the first set is refused as an unsupported path, and the test fails there. The reckon-review reference drops its bypass note in the same commit.

§12 — A link that orders nothing is named

The roadmap reports a dependency on an abandoned or superseded plan (inactive-hard-dependency), but nothing names a link that is merely spent. Two informational findings name the removal candidates a review should judge. settled-link fires for a depends_on, after or section wait whose target is closed and satisfied, where no open section of the plan still waits on it. It reads each plan's declared relationship lists and section waits directly, including after and closed targets, because §2's graph holds only open sections and the edges that still order work. redundant-link fires for a declared edge implied by a path of other declared edges, read from §2's graph together with the declared plan-level edges. Each finding names the ref and the set that removes it; the set after it names for an after link is the op §11 adds. They are candidates, not removals: the review decides.

Both findings are emitted in the roadmap's wiring_findings with severity info. reckon-review reads them there as the removal candidates its link verdicts start from, and §7's brief counts them with the other wiring findings.

Done when: a fixture with a satisfied after edge and a satisfied depends_on edge to a closed plan, and no open waiting section, raises settled-link on both in wiring_findings. The same edges with an open section still waiting raise nothing; that is the control. A fixture with A→B, B→C and A→C raises redundant-link on A→C only. On the base commit neither finding exists, and the test fails there. The counts per mount go to the landed record.

§13 — A review judges every link and names its MCP calls

The lead asked that plan and sprint reviews keep links correct in both directions, removing stale or irrelevant links as well as adding missing ones, and that the review skill name the MCP tools it should use. reckon-review now inventories every link in scope in plan and portfolio mode and gives each a verdict: keep, remove, narrow, convert or re-point. It adds a link only on evidence that something waits on or consumes the target, and reports one LINK line per change plus a tally, so a pass that only adds reads as incomplete. The verdict table and the exact ops are in skills/reckon-review/references/links.md, which keeps the body under its 1,500-word bound. A Tools table maps each step to its MCP call and asks the report to name any CLI fallback, and plan mode closes a plan with no implementable section or records why it stays open.

Done when: the skill and its reference are on main and tests/test_skill_contracts.py passes. Both hold: landed at merge abd5a87bd (commit cff57f15f), with 70 of 70 skill contract tests passing on the worktree before the merge.

§14 — A closure's completion is computed once

The code review of reckon/roadmap.py on 2026-10-21 (run r-20261021T085311999670-review-code-cae6c6fec611, finding 0) found one closure's completion computed by two owners. resolve_graph_target emits shipped_of_total from its own shipped and total counts over member_keys (near lines 2176 and 2264). _dependency_endpoints emits shipped_fraction from a second count over member_slugs (near lines 2433 and 2459).
