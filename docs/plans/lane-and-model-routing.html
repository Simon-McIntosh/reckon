Why this plan exists

The lead directed on 2026-10-07 that a caller should be able to name a lane and a model within it, that Jev should choose both where applicable, and that claude-opus should not exist as its own lane because it duplicates claude. Today every model is its own backend name. The host flight declares eight backend blocks: codex, codex-luna, codex-astra, clive, claude, claude-opus, claude-haiku and native. The three codex models are joined only by budget_group: codex-sub in the model catalogue.

The design run lane-and-model-design (r-20261007T043246695894, plan one-typesafe-model-picker §6) measured the change at revision 65d0bf6e7. Its report and call-site census are at ~/.config/reckon/crew/reports/reckon/lane-and-model-design/report.md. Three findings set the scope:

  • The lane already exists in code. The dialect name (claude, codex, clive, in reckon/_backends.py _DIALECTS) is the lane, and the picker already derives it as Candidate.family. This plan promotes that derived label to a declared one rather than adding a new axis.
  • The model is already a launch parameter, not an identity. Codex receives -m <model> and claude receives --model <model>. The claude and claude-haiku blocks are byte-identical after comments, and claude-opus differs from claude in one line, a 40-minute time budget against 25.
  • The package never names the duplicate backends. claude-opus, claude-haiku, codex-astra and codex-luna have 0 references in reckon/ and skills/. The names live in configuration (three files), tests (fixtures) and history: 3,123 ledger rows, 2,471 run records and 4,404 review files. That history is never rewritten; readers resolve old names through an alias table.

The first seven sections below are independently landable in the report's migration order; §8–§16 are follow-ons from their reviews. §17–§19 follow from the lead's ruling of 2026-10-13 that lane and model are two dimensions, §20 from the picker fallbacks measured that day, §22 from §20's reviews, §23 from §21's review, §24 and §25 from the pick timings measured for §22, §26 from the hold records found untracked in every checkout, and §21 from the §18 worker's finding that a picker route never applies its model. Each leaves the tree working, and the measure for each configuration step is that the resolved flight of every project in mounts.json, counted when the step runs, is unchanged.

What the new surface retires. The plan adds a lanes: key (§1), a --model option (§4) and one resolver (§7), and each replaces more than it adds. lanes: replaces the per-model backend blocks as the declaration: three claude blocks and three codex blocks become two lane blocks (§3, §6). --model replaces naming a model through a separate backend name such as --backend claude-opus, which survives only as an alias. The resolver replaces scattered name comparisons on stored rows, reviews and review_excluded_backends. The derived entries count, which reckon flight reports, is the measure that the old surface is actually retiring.

§ Decisions

Does a bare --model imply its lane when exactly one lane declares it, or must the lane always be named?

A bare --model resolves to the one lane that declares it; a model two lanes declare is refused as ambiguous, so the shorthand never guesses.

When do derived entries such as claude-opus leave the resolved config?

A derived entry leaves the resolved config once nothing declares it, and its old name keeps resolving as an alias, so retirement breaks no caller and needs no ordering of section 7 before section 3.

Does Jev pick a lane and then a model, or a lane-and-model pair in one decision?

The lead directed that Jev choose both lane and model where applicable. The design measured that refusal, context fit, rates and outcomes attach to a model, while burn, pace and windows attach to the shared account. Asking twice would make the model choice depend on a lane answer given without model-level fit, so pairs keep one typed decision with every fact in it.

Are lanes named by their dialect (claude, codex, clive), or can a lane be named independently, for example two subscriptions both running claude?

One lane per dialect (claude, codex, clive), as the design proposes; claude-opus stops being a separate lane and becomes a model inside the claude lane.

Which wins when a lane model's declared effort or time budget meets the role overlay?

Every implement and review dispatch applies roles.<role>.by_spec_level.<level>.effort = high and the role's time budget over the backend's own keys, so astra and luna declared at xhigh launch at high and Sonnet declared at medium launches at high, while Jev's candidate rows show the declared values.

The per-model values are the operator's more specific and more recent choice, set for this routing; the role overlay stays the pin for any model that declares nothing, and catalogue defaults stay below it so clive keeps launching at high. Explicit dispatch flags still win over everything.

How should a metered worker lane be stopped before its subscription is spent?

On 2026-10-20 the lead rejected the 92% dispatch ceiling (100% less the resume and coordinator reserves) and asked that Jev be shown each lane's burn rate and what it has left, so it routes by priority, difficulty and availability. The credit line's author agreed to advisory pace for codex provided the exhausted-window and credit-cap stops stay.

The lead wants the whole subscription used with Jev deciding from burn and availability; codex-sub carries no orchestrators, so its reserves and pace hold protect nothing, while claude-sub keeps both. The credit line's author agreed, keeping the exhausted-window and credit-cap stops.

How should a newly released model be noticed, and confirmed on a subscription?

OpenRouter's public model list answers with no key and lists every declared lane model; the codex CLI keeps its subscription's served list in ~/.codex/models_cache.json; Claude keeps no local list. Nothing in reckon reads any of them.

Both sources cost nothing to read. A probe spends a sliver of subscription quota and no money, and only after a model is flagged. Switching models stays the operator's decision.

How does a lane model key's outcome history carry to its next model generation?

The picker keys every outcome tally by backend and exact model id, so a key re-pointed to a new id starts with no history although its earlier ids carry hundreds of runs.

A new generation should not be judged blind, nor hidden behind the old one's record; the prior is shown only until the id has enough runs of its own.

Does a coordinator's move off Jev's pick, made with a stated reason, count as evidence for the lane it moved to, or keep counting against Jev's pick as §4 of jev-learns-from-its-routes rules?

nova's lead said on 2026-10-18 that forcing a lane for a specific reason is legitimate and that Jev should treat a named lane as authoritative. A lane named at dispatch already counts in that lane's own outcomes. A move made after Jev's pick still counts against the pick under §4, so nova's three moves of clive picks to codex that day, each with a reason, sit in moved_in rather than in codex's own outcomes.

Ruling of nova's lead on 2026-10-18, relayed by the nova coordinator session: split by attempt. Each attempt of a moved run, and the review of the revision that attempt produced, counts for the lane that ran it. A run's clive attempts before a move, with their reviews, stay clive's, and the codex attempts after it are codex's. The move itself is recorded against Jev's original pick as an override carrying its stated reason, not as a failure of either lane. This replaces §4 of jev-learns-from-its-routes, which kept a moved run's whole outcome out of the receiving lane's counts and charged it to the pick. The ruling's first cases were three moves that day, each recorded with a reason: lobe-share-partition r-20261018T063028156714 (clive reviewed 68, then codex), seed-census-departure r-20261018T065749620386 (clive reviewed 77 then 80, then codex) and efit-axis-adjudication r-20261018T091852734602 (clive reviewed 66, then codex).
