Artifact feeds and the reader · the longest chain in the sprint

Four Artifact Tabs, One Reader

Research and evidence exist in the landed SPA only as a rail inside a plan's reader, so a document a worker just wrote can be found only by knowing which plan it hangs off, and figures cannot be found at all. The canvas makes the four artifact types sibling tabs that share one index layout and one reader, sorts each as a feed by created or edited, and lets new documents arrive while you are looking at the page without moving what you are reading.

§1 — Why this exists

From the handoff triage (design/reckon-spa-handoff.md, items A6, B3, B3a, B3b and B7) and the canvas (design/reckon-spa-handoff.dc.html), checked against main on 2026-09-03:

  • The plan list is a persistent rail with a reader beside it; research and evidence are attachments in that reader's head bars. There is no index of either, and no route reaches one directly.
  • Figures under docs/figures/ are served as static files and appear nowhere in the surface.
  • The reader's dependency strip is always on and mixes research and evidence edges with prerequisites, which is what made a plan with four evidence documents look more entangled than one with four real prerequisites.
  • Reading mode fixes the reader pane, not the shell; prev and next step through a queue the shell derives, and the position readout is derived a second time.
  • A live change stream already exists: docs/ui/state-loader.js opens /_changes/<project> and reloads state on a change event. The triage's claim that new content requires a refresh is out of date; what is missing is the arrival discipline, because a reload re-sorts and re-scrolls the list under the reader.
  • The palette searches plans only.

This plan depends on two others. served-surface-data-contracts supplies figure rows and the edited stamp every feed sorts by. spa-shell-scope-and-visibility supplies the topbar whose artifact tab group reads its labels from the route module, so §2 adds tabs by extending a list rather than editing the bar.

§2 — Routes and tabs

Four artifact views, each with an index and a reader route: #plans, #research, #evidence, #figures for the index and #plan/<slug>, #research/<slug>, #evidence/<slug>, #figure/<slug> for the reader, where a figure slug is its path under docs/figures/. The artifact tab group published by the route module becomes Plans · Research · Evidence · Figures; a tab click returns its section to the index and drops full-screen reading, so clicking the tab you are on is never a dead click. The shell's view switch mounts one artifact surface component for all four kinds, parameterised by kind; the plan-specific rail and reader columns are retired in favour of the full-width index that drills into a full-width reader, which is how the sprint and graph surfaces already behave. Hide-done is one control with one setting that every index header and the sprint and graph headers share.

§3 — Index lists as feeds

One index component for four kinds. Header: the kind's title, N in <project>, inline mono figures (left and held for plans; today for the other three), an Edited/Created segmented toggle that changes the sort key, and hide-done. Status chips (All · active · blocked · pending · shipped with counts) render for plans only. Rows: a dot coloured by status (plans), verdict (research: supports, refutes), gate (evidence: passed, failed, running) or neutral (figures); title and slug; for plans a completion bar and Nh · <sprint>; for figures a 50 by 34 thumbnail and dimensions; for every kind the stamps created <when> · edited <when>; a status chip. Newest first by edited is the default. Hide-done hides plans at 100% unless the shipped chip is active, and hides evidence whose gate passed. The empty state names the kind and project, or says everything is done when hide-done emptied it. The palette searches all four kinds and its placeholder names them.

§4 — Reader chrome: source trail, full screen, prev and next

All four kinds share one reader. Source trail. The path chip and title become one breadcrumb: a figure or evidence document shows its sprint, its plan and itself; research shows the sprint and plan of the first entry in informs; a plan shows its sprint and itself. Every segment but the last navigates. Full screen. The f key and a chip drape the reader over the entire shell, a fixed inset-0 pane above the topbar, because every topbar control navigates away from the document; esc or the chip exits; it is a style swap on the existing reader, not a second copy of the markup, and it cannot be armed from an index. The measure grows from 760 to 820px in full screen. Prev and next. Buttons reading ‹ n / N ›, the arrow keys, and the readout all read the one ordered list the index rendered, including its status filter and sort, published by the index at render time; deriving the list a second time inside the step handler is how it silently breaks. Keys are suppressed while an input has focus. The metadata row under the trail carries the kind's fields (status, impl, effort, wall, sprint, repo for plans; verdict or gate, created, edited, repo otherwise; dimensions for figures). A figure reader shows the image with its docs/figures/… path beneath. A copy control copies /reckon-ship <slug> for a plan and the slug for anything else. The provenance list at the foot of a plan lists the research that informs it, the evidence for it and its figures; the foot of a research or evidence document lists the plans it attaches to.

§5 — The dependency cone is opt-in, and plan edges only

The always-on dependency strip above a plan's body becomes a toggle in the reader header labelled with its counts, 2 ↑ 1 ↓, or standalone and dimmed when the plan is isolated. It opens a three-column cone inline above the body: prerequisites, this plan, dependents, each card with status, hours and completion. Only depends_on and its reverse appear; research and evidence are provenance and stay in the list at the foot of the reader. The Graph tab's closure uses the same edge rule, so the two cannot disagree.

§6 — Arrival is non-destructive

On a change event from the existing stream the loader fetches discovery and diffs the new inventory against the rendered snapshot by resource id and version. Rows that are new are held as pending, not inserted: the topbar receipt reads N new instead of live, the affected tab carries a count badge, and the affected index shows a banner at its top, N new <kind> since you opened this list · show. Clicking the banner or the receipt inserts the held rows in sort position with a one-shot three-second tint and clears the count. The list is never re-sorted or re-scrolled under the reader, and a document that changed in place updates its row without moving it. A change with nothing new leaves the receipt at live.

§9 — The palette crashes the application, and nothing opened it

Found 2026-09-04 by the containment guard's diagnostic, which could not measure the command palette because opening it destroys the page. docs/ui/shell-palette.jsx calls paletteKindLabel(p.kind) as a bare identifier at line 42, inside the map over result rows. That function is defined module-locally in docs/ui/shell-plans.jsx at line 182 and published only as window.ReckonShell.plans.paletteKindLabel at line 373. Each JSX file is compiled as its own module, so the bare reference resolves to nothing: opening the palette with any matching result raises ReferenceError: paletteKindLabel is not defined and unmounts .r-app, taking the whole shell with it.

It survived because the reference sits inside the results map, so a palette opened against an empty result set never reaches it, and no test opens the palette with a populated one. The fix is to reach the function the way every other cross-module reference reaches its neighbour, through the published namespace. The measure has to open the palette with results, because that is the only state in which the defect exists.

§8 — Out of scope

Whether the filtered list ghosts in on hover during full-screen reading is an open design question in the handoff; the canvas keeps it hidden until esc and so does this plan. No server change: figure rows and edited stamps come from served-surface-data-contracts, and the change stream is used as it exists. Comment authoring in the reader is unchanged.

§ Decisions

Do the artifact tabs keep the rail-plus-reader layout or follow Sprints and Graph?

The canvas states it: the artifact tabs now follow Sprints and Graph, so one layout serves seven surfaces and the reader gets the width its measure needs.

What does full-screen reading cover?

Every topbar control navigates away from the document and the list is what you are already stepping through; nothing in the bar helps a reader.

Which edges does the reader's dependency cone draw?

Research and evidence say why a plan exists and whether it worked, not what must land first; mixing them in made the old cone unreadable, and the Graph tab uses the same rule so the two agree.

What happens to the list when a new document arrives?

A list that re-sorts under the reader loses the reader's place; the canvas holds arrivals and shows the count, and the existing stream makes the reload path the measured defect.
