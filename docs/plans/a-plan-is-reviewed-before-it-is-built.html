§1 — Why every plan needs a second reader

The 2026-09-24 re-plan measured what nobody had re-read. imas-codex's sn-catalog-audit-instrument had 13 reviewed and merged nodes and read as nearly done. Measured against real call sites it stood at 0.56: 11 of 25 features were shallow, and one computed its findings on every review for no template to read. imas-ambix closed a plan as shipped whose criterion had never been met. Reckon's own consumers turned out to hardcode 1,537 lines of ~/.config/reckon paths. In every case the author was competent and the plan read well, but nothing checked the plan against the code or against a rubric before work was built on it. Code gets an independent review before promotion; plans get none.

§2 — A rubber-duck review of every plan create and edit

Every plan create, and every edit that changes prose, done-whens, relationships or sections, queues one review of the settled version. Edits are debounced, so a burst of edits gets one review, and a pure metadata change such as an impl bump is skipped. Hand edits are caught at commit as well as through the MCP write path. The review is read-only, runs on the local lane, and checks a fixed rubric:

  • the plan is wired: its depends-on, informs and gates are present and correct;
  • each done-when is measurable, with a declared negative control;
  • each node has a single goal;
  • evidence and gate logs sit on durable paths, never in a session scratchpad;
  • claims are cited, and every file, section or commit an anchor names resolves;
  • naming carries no plan labels or ticket numbers into code.

It is advisory, a rubber duck, not a gate. The authoring agent acts on each finding or declines it with a one-line reason, and both are recorded. The loop is visible in the reckon web surface next to the plan, and reaches the author as one follower line. Nothing blocks.

Done when a plan create and a prose edit each produce one review record within the debounce window. A burst of five edits produces one review, and an impl-only edit produces none. The web surface shows the review's state (pending, ready, acted on, declined) next to the plan version it reviewed. The author's action or declining reason is stored with each finding.

§3 — A prior-art and depth review before a plan is built

Before a plan's first implementation node is released, a second review searches the codebases for existing machinery the plan could reuse. That extends the build skill's reuse-map rule, which a coordinator currently remembers to apply, into a review that runs without being remembered. The review then judges the design for depth. The preference, stated as the rubric, is deep modules behind small interfaces over a spread of shallow wrappers. A new module must hide substantial behaviour behind an interface smaller than what it hides. A plan that adds a thin layer over existing machinery must say why that machinery cannot be extended. A duplicate of shared infrastructure, such as placement, path resolution, scheduler queries or state readers, is a finding naming the owner to extend. Today's evidence for why this matters: imas-ambix carried three duplicated placement paths, and the thin-interface audit found consumers parsing reckon's CLI prose in 151 places.

The review also reads the interface budget. The velocity view publishes weekly counts of reckon's interface in four families, from reckon/interface_counts.py: public_definitions, cli_options, mcp_views and refusal_families. The review cites the current week's four figures, and holds every section that adds to any of them to one rule: the section names, in its own text, what it retires or merges, or states why nothing can be. A section that adds surface without either is a finding.

Done when a plan whose design duplicates an existing mechanism receives a finding naming that mechanism and its file. A plan that extends a deep module passes clean. The finding reaches the author through the same loop as §2.

Also done when the review of a plan names the current week's four interface counts. A section that adds a public definition, CLI option, MCP view or refusal family without naming in its text what it retires or merges, or why nothing can be, receives a finding. A reviewer can check that from the section's text alone.

§16 — A section reviewed as authored stays reviewed after it lands

§14 made a landing collapse earn no review, and it holds when the collapsed section was already a landed card at review time. It does not hold for the common case: a section reviewed as authored text and collapsed at its landing. Measured 2026-10-05 on a-worker-reuses-before-it-writes: the stored review of version 96 carries fingerprint e23d15f6…; collapsing §7 and §8 at their landings produced version 98 with fingerprint 69197f8a… and neither current form matched, so the gate refused the dispatch of §9, a section whose authored text had not changed since the review, and a fifteenth design review was composed to re-read content the fourteenth had already passed. Every landing beat therefore costs one review cycle before the next dispatch, and a plan with several sections landing in sequence pays it each time.

The fingerprint is whole-document, and a whole-document digest cannot be stable across a change it is designed to see. The repair makes the unit of review the authored section. reckon/crew/plan_review.py gains one module-private function, _section_digests(plan), returning a mapping from section identity to digest with one further key for the document unit, everything authored outside any section (decisions, followups, authored comments and the normalised parsed state the present digest folds in through its state component); plan_fingerprint is recomposed as the digest of that sorted mapping, the composer in recovery.py, which today writes only plan_fingerprint into the sidecar, writes the mapping beside it under the record key section_digests, and the coverage predicate reads that key; and _AuthoredProseParser keeps its single streaming pass and its depth counters, because they do what a span join cannot: keep a landed card's heading in the prose while dropping the card's interior. What it must not do is decide section identity by a rule of its own. The rule that maps a heading and its enclosing element to a section identity is not a new symbol and is not this section's to land: §17 hoists section_record_id, the canonical derivation, into the core layer and extends it to accept the enclosing section element's attributes beside the heading's own, because the wrapper-id case keeps the identity on the enclosing element and a derivation over the heading alone cannot see it; this section consumes that one function; the prose parser today keeps only a stack of protection booleans and emits one string, so it is extended in the same change: it records the enclosing section element's attributes as it opens one, which is bookkeeping about its own position in the stream and not a second identity rule, since the span parser keeps the same bookkeeping about offsets, and both hand what they recorded to the one hoisted derivation; it calls that derivation at every heading with the heading's attributes and the enclosing element's, and buckets the chunks it emits under the identity in force, with chunks outside any section under the document unit, so _authored_prose returns prose keyed by identity rather than one string, and _section_digests is the digest of each bucket; the whole-document prose the present digest hashes is the concatenation of those buckets, which keeps the legacy form computable. section_record_id and section_id_candidates live today in reckon/crew/routing.py and normalise dotted and hyphenated spellings; §17 hoists them into reckon/_plan_html.py so the core layer owns the derivation and routing imports it, and they are public today and stay public, so the hoist moves two names and adds none. § Decisions

When an author declines a finding, is that final, or does a finding declined across several plans surface to the lead?

A finding type declined in three or more plans surfaces to the lead. A repeated decline means either the rubric or a practice is wrong, and both are the lead's to rule on.

Where do plan reviews live?

Plan reviews go in the crew review store, keyed by plan and version, with a one-line pointer on the plan. That makes them queryable across plans and projects and reuses the code-review machinery. The 2026-09-25 review-utility census found its measures blocked by unjoinable records, so plan reviews must be joinable from the start.
