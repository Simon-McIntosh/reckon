§1 — Two clocks are published and nothing reads them

The metered provider publishes its own pace on the stream of every served run that carries a rate-limit event: unifiedWindows.five_hour and unifiedWindows.seven_day, each with a utilization and a resetsAt. Reckon reads neither. Its budget fence consults a single ceiling, so a wave may legitimately spend most of a five-hour window in one hour and the fence stays silent until the window is nearly gone.

Measured 2026-09-21. The newest reading available on this workstation, 17 minutes old at the time of writing: five_hour utilisation 0.13 resetting at the top of the hour, seven_day utilisation 0.31 resetting 2026-09-27T06:00Z. Across an audit wave earlier today the five-hour figure ran 0.64 to 0.93 and reset at 10:40Z. These are real provider windows: a 429 carrying a reset clause follows when the five-hour figure reaches 1.0.

What the pace is for. The five-hour window is not self-pacing and is not meant to be the policy. The policy is: keep the weekly burn a little above linear, end the week with the subscription drained, and never drain it on Monday. The free lane absorbs whatever the window will not admit. Stated that way, the window is not a fence at all — it is the threshold that decides which lane a node goes to.

§2 — The pace function, on two clocks

The five-hour allowance is derived from the week, not configured. A fixed five-hour cap cannot react to load: it is either too tight on a quiet Tuesday or too loose on a busy Monday, and it never learns. Deriving it needs no gain term and no tuning constant, because the arithmetic is self-correcting.

Let D be the drain deadline — the point the subscription should be spent, a configured lead time before the weekly reset (12 h, so the week ends drained rather than exhausted mid-Sunday-night with work still queued). Then for a declared budget group:

remaining_budget  = 1 - utilisation
remaining_windows = (D - elapsed) / 5h

allowance_5h      = remaining_budget / remaining_windows

That single line is the whole governor. Spend heavily now and remaining_budget falls while remaining_windows falls more slowly, so the next window's allowance shrinks. Spend nothing and it grows. It converges on exhausting the budget exactly at D from any starting position, and it needs no memory of how the week has gone — the two figures already carry it.

The best five-hour estimate to start the week with is the fixed point of that formula, which is simply the nominal share: 5 / D. At D = 156 h that is 3.205% of the weekly budget per five-hour window, over 31.2 windows. A group exactly on pace returns 1.00× nominal, which is the consistency check the implementation should assert rather than a number to hardcode.

The provider's own five-hour ceiling still applies and is a separate number. The effective limit is the lesser of the derived allowance and the provider's hard ceiling: the first paces the week, the second stops a burst. Collapsing them loses the week.

Done when the allowance is computed per declared group from its freshest owned reading, asserted at four states — ahead, on pace, behind, and exhausted — with the on-pace case asserted to return exactly the nominal share, since that equality is what proves the formula self-consistent rather than merely plausible; the effective limit is asserted to be the lesser of the derived allowance and the provider ceiling, with a case where each wins; and the deadline lead time is a flight key whose change moves the allowance, asserted, so the twelve hours is data rather than a constant in the code.

§3 — A rising bar, not a switch

Lane choice is not metered-or-local by category. Each node gets an open-endedness score from what dispatch already knows, and the metered lane admits it only when the score clears a bar that rises as the windows fill.

The third input is the one with evidence behind it: prescription completeness is what the local pilots showed makes a node viable on the free lane, and reckon/crew/prescription.py already computes it. The bar is therefore a function of window state, and everything below it routes local as a prescribed node — from zero when the account is behind pace and the window is empty, to admitting only verification bookends and resumes when the five-hour ceiling is reached.

Done when the bar is one pure function of window state, unit-tested at the extremes and at the transitions, and a node's lane is asserted to follow score-against-bar rather than any category; and a node routed local by the bar is asserted to be prescribed, because routing unprescribed work to the free lane is the failure the fitness evidence already records.

§4 — Reserve the bookends, or the repairs starve

A local repair is verified by a short metered review. Those verifications are small, and they are exactly what an audit wave starves: today a pilot's bookend had to wait for a window reset because the wave ahead of it had eaten the window.

So a fraction of each five-hour window is reserved for review and verify roles and is not available to implementation work at any bar. A reserve that only applies when the window is nearly full is not a reserve; it holds from the start of the window.

Done when an implementation dispatch is refused against the reserved fraction while a review dispatch in the same window state succeeds, asserted as a pair, since asserting either alone would pass on a reserve that does nothing.

§5 — Reading the windows without inventing them

Most runs do not carry the windows, and this is the correction that shapes the reader. Measured over the 400 newest streams on this workstation: 54 carry unifiedWindows and 346 do not. The local lane carries them on none of its 28, which is correct — it is unmetered. So "read the newest event" must mean the newest event that carries a window, and a reading is normally minutes to hours old rather than current.

Three rules follow, and each exists because its opposite has already been observed:

  • Read from a served run's stream, not from an account surface. The account read fails on one side with the binary absent from the server's PATH and on the other with a 403.
  • Carry the age with the figure. A window utilisation with no observation time cannot be distinguished from a current one, and the five-hour clock rolls over.
  • Never read unknown as either full or empty. Absence of a signal is not exhaustion — that rule already holds in the budget fence and must not be weakened here — and it is equally not headroom.

Done when the reader returns a figure with its observation age or an explicit unknown, asserted for a fleet whose newest streams carry no window at all, and asserted not to fall back to zero or to one in that case.

The first four share one reset to the second — one subscription. codex-spark is a separate subscription with its own pair of windows. Every lane reports budget_group: null, so a pre-flight can clear codex-terra at "86% remaining" when that is the same 86% codex is spending. Four lanes each believe they hold a full allowance of one wallet.

The mechanism for this already exists and is simply unconfigured. budget_group is a declared slot in reckon/schema/flight.yaml, the lanes view reads it and emits a budget_groups map, and a guard prevents the discarded quota_pool spelling from ever grouping again — all landed under burn-is-visible-before-it-bites. What is missing is a declaration: no config layer names a group. So this section is a configuration change plus the pacing that consumes it, not a build.

Pacing reads the group, never the lane. A target, a bar and a reserve are properties of a wallet. Computing them per lane would divide one allowance four ways and report each quarter as a whole.

A stale figure must be re-read, not reported. Today the view hands back a 2.4-hour-old receipt and a 6.7-day-old one as though they were positions. A reading older than its own shelf life is not a figure: the call re-queries the lane's probe, and only when that fails does it report the last reading with its age and a serving state of unknown. serving_state: stale already exists in the payload and nothing acts on it, which is the same shape as the reviewer that had no caller.

Done when the four sol-family lanes and spark resolve to two declared groups, asserted from resolved flight config rather than from lane names, with an undeclared backend left ungrouped; a pace target, a bar and a reserve are each computed once per group and asserted not to be computable per lane; a reading older than its configured shelf life triggers a re-query, asserted by driving the reader with a stale receipt and a live probe and requiring the probe's figure to be the one returned; a re-query that fails returns the old figure with its age and a serving state of unknown rather than either a fresh-looking number or nothing, asserted; and a lane whose figure came from a shared account probe renders as unobserved rather than carrying the probed account's number — the measure already written into burn-is-visible-before-it-bites §5 against spark specifically, which this section consumes rather than restates.

The module's only position callable takes a declared group identifier: group_position("codex", …) refuses a lane, so a per-lane position cannot be computed through it. A group with no ageable member reading is unobserved with no member and no age, never a zero that would read as a figure taken this instant.

§7 — A block must be real, and most of today's were not

A governor that holds on a figure it cannot vouch for is worse than none, because the hold looks identical either way and the coordinator routes around it. Every hold therefore carries its evidence, and a hold that cannot produce fresh, owned evidence does not hold.

The assertion is mechanical, not a habit. A hold names its group, its figure, that figure's observation age and source, and the ceiling it breached. A hold whose figure is past its shelf life re-queries first and holds only on what comes back; if the re-query fails the state is unknown, and unknown does not hold. That rule already exists in the budget fence and must not be weakened here, because the two failure directions are not symmetric: a missed hold costs some quota, a false hold costs the lane.

§8 — The pattern that is not blocked

The dispatch pattern that never meets a hold falls out of the two sections above, and it is the shape this workstation drifted into by accident today:

  • The metered lane carries bookends only, and they are reserved. Reviews and verifications are small, bounded, and never contend, because §4's reserve is theirs from the start of the window rather than whatever is left at the end. A pattern whose metered demand never exceeds its reserve cannot be blocked by construction.
  • Implementation runs local as prescribed nodes. One artifact, file and line named, a numeric gate with its before value, a literal control mutation, a thirty-minute fence. The lane ceiling is 64 and the binding constraint has been bookkeeping, not capacity.
  • Metered implementation opens only when the week is behind. That is §3's bar driven by §2's allowance: behind and late, the bar drops and open-ended work flows metered; ahead and early, it rises and everything prescribable goes local.
  • Width comes from more nodes, never larger ones. Node width is a capacity property — an over-large node cannot run beside its siblings and consumes the pool that would have carried them.

The steady state is local wide, metered narrow and reserved. It is unblocked because it never asks the metered lane for more than the reserve guarantees, and the governor only ever widens that ask when the evidence says the week is underspent.

Done when a week's committed rows show metered demand staying inside the reserve except where the allowance explicitly opened the bar, and the local-versus-metered split by class of work is reproducible from those rows alone — the same cut the fitness table needs, so one record serves both.

§9 — The log that lets someone else tune this

Every dispatch records the group, the utilisation it was judged against with that figure's age and source, the five-hour position, elapsed and remaining windows, the derived allowance, the node's open-endedness score, the bar, the lane chosen, and whether a hold fired with its evidence. That set is chosen so the decision is reproducible from the row alone — a row that needs the streams re-read to be interpreted has not been recorded.

It serves two readers who must not be conflated. Self-tuning is the governor comparing its realised drain curve against target and correcting its own estimate. Independent tuning is a person or a later session replaying the week from the log, asking whether a different lead time, floor or reserve would have drained better, without re-running anything and without trusting the governor's account of itself. The second is what catches a governor tuned to look good on its own metric, which is why both are named rather than one.

Done when one week of rows reproduces the allowance curve and every hold decision without reading a stream, asserted by recomputing both from the rows and comparing against what was recorded at the time; and a deliberately mistuned lead time is shown to be detectable from the log alone, since a log that cannot expose a bad setting cannot tune one.

§6 — The pace is visible before a wave opens

A governor nobody can see is a governor nobody trusts. crew preflight prints both clocks with their ages, the current bar, and which of the ready nodes the bar would admit, so a coordinator reads the pace before committing to a wave rather than discovering it in a refusal.

Every dispatch records the window utilisations as observed, the node's score, the bar it was judged against, and the lane chosen. That record is what makes the policy falsifiable afterwards: the weekly report shows pace against target and the local-versus-metered split by class of work, which is the same cut the fitness table in background-agent-fitness-and-dispatch-scope needs.

Done when a pre-flight names both clocks, their ages, the bar and the admitted subset of a stated ready set; and one week's committed rows reproduce the pace curve and the lane split without reading any stream, since a record that needs the streams to be interpreted has not been recorded.
