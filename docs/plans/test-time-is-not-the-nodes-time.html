§1 — Measure what a node's time budget controls, and how much of it the gates spend

Why. On 2026-10-18 nova's lead directed that "test work should not count as timing towards the node's work". nova reported the same day that most blocking review findings, on both lanes, came from gates squeezed to fit the 60-minute fence. Workers ran suites in-process under timeout 480 or timeout 560, cut them off at 75 of 104 tests, skipped baseline arms, or let jobs reach their limit. That report names no run or review a reader can open yet, so this section's measurement records the run id of every truncated gate it finds.

What the budget controls, as read from the code on 2026-10-18:

  • At dispatch, validate_node refuses a declared budget above the flight's fences.time_budget (reckon/crew/node.py, around line 1293; the fence is read by resolved_time_ceiling, reckon/crew/routing.py:473).
  • The worker's prompt states a deadline, its launch instant plus the budget (time_fence_statement, reckon/crew/prompts.py:382). It tells the worker to stop as blocked when "the time budget is spent with the measure still unmet" (line 701).
  • Classification reports budget_overrun and its cause after the fact (reckon/crew/recovery_stream.py).
  • One path can stop a worker: _apply_budget_watchdog (reckon/crew/recovery_stream.py:320, called from reckon/crew/dispatch_sessions.py:337) signals a live CLI worker past its budget times fences.budget_grace_multiple. It acts only when fences.enforce_budget_watchdog is true, and reckon/schema/flight-defaults.yaml sets it false. A coordinator ruling of 2026-10-03, recorded on crew-observability-truth, kept that opt-in ceiling and found no flight on this host enabling it.

If no resolved flight enables the watchdog, the budget is a soft limit. Workers then cut their own gates to meet a deadline they are told to keep, and the remedy is to change the deadline they are given.

Deliverable. A read-only investigation that writes docs/research/how-much-of-a-node-is-its-gate.html and the script that computes it, scripts/gate_share_of_run_time.py. It answers two questions:

  • Does anything stop, cut short or penalise a run when it passes its deadline? The places to check are the supervisor, the watch and sweep, the review and repair reflexes, the picker's outcome and run-time readers, and the review rubric. The answer names each such path with its file and line, and for the watchdog it reports fences.enforce_budget_watchdog as resolved for every mounted project.
  • How much of a run's wall time is its gates? For implement runs in every mounted project in the 14 days to the run, a run's gates are found in two places and joined: the distinct log paths the run cites (test_logs, baseline_suite.log_path, after_suite.log_path, often naming the same files, each read once), and the test-command tool calls in its stream (pytest, ctest, a declared gate script). A tool call that wrote a cited log is the same gate as that log. Each gate's duration is the runner's own reported duration in its log, such as pytest's "in N s" summary. Only where a gate has no log reporting a duration, fall back to the wall time of the foreground tool call that ran it. A run's wall time, the denominator of every share, is the span between its ledger row's own dispatched_at and completed_at stamps, as read_records (reckon/ledger.py) reports it under wall_seconds. The stored field of that name, which runs() passes through, is not read: on nearly half the ledger it is a different quantity. The document states whether that figure covered every attempt of the runs it read, checked against their per-attempt exit records. A gate run as a background job returns from its tool call long before it finishes, so tool-call time alone would miss exactly the long gates this plan is about. A run with a gate that has neither a logged duration nor a foreground tool call, such as a backgrounded gate that cites no log, has no measurable gate share. It is counted as unmeasurable and listed by id, and it enters neither median. Report the gates' share of the run's wall time per lane twice: once over the runs whose every gate duration came from a log, and once over every run with a measurable share. Beside each median, give the share of runs that used the fallback and the count of unmeasurable runs. Also list, by run id, the runs whose gate was visibly truncated: a timeout prefix on the test command, -x or --maxfail, a partial test count, or a skipped baseline arm. List separately the runs whose gate ran in the background.

It changes no product code.

Reuse. The script composes owners that already do each step and adds only the join and the shares. Gate durations come from _recorded_gate_seconds and _GATE_LOG_DURATION (reckon/crew/promotion_gate.py:508, :44). Stream tool calls come from stream_tool_calls (reckon/shadow_reads.py:66) and transcript_tool_blocks (reckon/velocity.py:1718). Per-lane run wall time comes from run_time_profile (reckon/crew/run_time_profile.py:339). Where one of them cannot carry what this section needs, the document says why beside the figure it affects.

Write paths. docs/research/how-much-of-a-node-is-its-gate.html and scripts/gate_share_of_run_time.py, both new, and figures under docs/figures/how-much-of-a-node-is-its-gate.

  • It answers the second with a table per lane: runs; median gate share of wall time over the runs whose every gate duration came from a log; the same median over every run with a measurable share, with the share of runs that used the tool-call fallback; the runs with no measurable share, each listed by id; runs with a truncated gate, each listed by id; and runs whose gate ran in the background, each listed by id. §2 — Time spent running a declared gate does not count against the node's budget

Why. This carries out the lead's directive once §1 has said what the budget actually controls. It is deferred until §1 lands and the decision gate-time-shape is locked, because the two candidate shapes differ in what they build.

  • A stated gate allowance. Dispatch takes a gate allowance beside the work budget. The fence applies to the work budget only. The worker's stated deadline becomes launch plus work budget plus gate allowance, and the prompt says the allowance is for running the declared gate and its base arm in full. Nothing pauses at run time, and where the watchdog is enabled it measures against the same extended deadline.
  • A gate verb. The worker runs its gate through a reckon verb, for example reckon crew gate run -- <command>. The verb records each gate's wall time on the run. The supervisor and the watchdog exclude that time from the budget. The worker's stated deadline and its stop instruction exclude it too: the prompt says that time inside the verb does not count, and time_fence_statement states the deadline as launch plus budget plus the gate time recorded so far.

Under either shape the gate's wall time is still recorded as a cost the picker can read, so Jev keeps seeing what a node costs. Until §2 lands, nova's briefs size --time-budget as the work plus the gate's wall time, and split nodes with more than three done-when clauses.

Whichever shape is chosen names what it adds to the command surface and what it retires or merges. The gate verb in particular sits beside the gate-log reader in reckon/crew/promotion_gate.py and the budget timing in reckon/crew/recovery_stream.py.

The done-when, write paths and negative control are written once §1 has landed and the decision is locked, and the section is then reviewed before it is built.

At its deadline a worker checkpoints, then finishes only a bounded step it named

Why. On 2026-10-19 the power_convertor session reported that the worker prompt is the only thing that stops a worker at its deadline. fences.enforce_budget_watchdog is false in reckon/schema/flight-defaults.yaml:201, and the prompt says "Exceeding it means stop and report, never push on" (reckon/crew/prompts.py:635). Its stop list repeats this: "the time budget is spent with the measure still unmet" (reckon/crew/prompts.py:701). Workers obey. A numerical node whose remaining step is one solver batch under timeout 570 stops anyway, writes a partial manifest and waits for a resume. In power_convertor's committed ledger at 11b0f9ec (docs/state/power_convertor/runs/*.json), 163 of the 338 implement, test and investigate rows have attempt_kind: resume. Repair rounds are among those resumes, and how many of them were deadline stops is not measured. A hung worker is caught separately, by the 900-second stall window, so the deadline does not need to double as a hang detector. The rule should still stop a worker that has lost its way. It should let one whose next step is bounded, and already written down, finish that step.

Deliverable. One deadline rule, stated the same way at every place a worker is told it:

  • At the deadline, write the checkpoint. The manifest's status, checkpoint and remaining work are current on disk before anything else runs.
  • Continue only through a bounded step the manifest named before the deadline. That step is one command under its own timeout, or a stated number of them: a declared gate, its base arm, or a named solver batch. Finish it by the hard stop, then update the manifest and end the turn. Never start new work, a fresh fix or an unnamed step past the deadline.
  • The hard stop is the launch instant plus the budget times fences.budget_grace_multiple. This is the same instant the opt-in watchdog enforces (reckon/crew/recovery_stream.py:333), and both read the multiple through one owner: a resolved_grace_multiple beside resolved_time_ceiling (reckon/crew/routing.py:473), which the watchdog then calls in place of its own read. The reader returns the configured number, 1.0 when the key is absent, and nothing when the value is not numeric. The watchdog does nothing on nothing, so it keeps both of today's behaviours: an absent multiple acts at the deadline, and an invalid one leaves it inert. time_fence_statement (reckon/crew/prompts.py:382) states it beside the deadline, for a first attempt and a resumed one alike (reckon/crew/dispatch_sessions.py:1468). When the multiple is missing, invalid or at most 1.0, the hard stop is the deadline and the rule reads as it does today. The one surface this adds is a keyword for the multiple on time_fence_statement and compose_prompt, carried the way compose_prompt already carries needs_help_after_failures from the same fences block. Nothing it supersedes remains to retire.
  • Otherwise stop and report. The stop list at line 701 names the hard stop, and a deadline passed with remaining work that is not a named bounded step, in place of "the time budget is spent".

The two restatements in skills/reckon-build/references/worker-protocol.md, the fence table row at line 66 and the stop list at line 346, carry the same rule. Change nothing else. The dispatch ceiling (fences.time_budget), the watchdog and the stall window stay as they are; the ceiling is §4.

Telling it which clock to read did not stop that.

Deliverable. The deadline statement gives the worker a command that answers how much time is left, so it never compares two clock readings.

  • The statement tells the worker to judge the deadline only by that command, and never by a file time, a log stamp or a date reading, which may print in the host's local zone.
  • A resumed attempt's fence is built by the same function, called from _restate_time_fence in reckon/crew/dispatch_sessions.py, so it carries the command measured from that attempt's own launch.

No code in prompts.py parses the raw value: it keeps only the rule that a multiple at or below 1.0, or none, allows no continuation.

§ Decisions

How does a node's gate stop counting against its time budget?

Decide after §1 reports. The choice turns on whether any mounted project's resolved flight enables fences.enforce_budget_watchdog , and on whether that is likely to change. While none does, the budget is a soft limit, and a stated gate allowance changes only what dispatch accepts and what the worker is told. A gate verb adds a new command, supervisor accounting and a deadline that moves as gates run, which matters most where the watchdog is enforced.

What shape should the shipped time ceiling take?

Decide after §1 reports and gate-time-shape is locked. The shipped fences.time_budget is one 60-minute figure for every role, from a 4-minute review default to a numerical node whose base and head passes alone can take over half an hour. A project can raise it in its own flight, as power_convertor did at 180 minutes on 2026-10-19, so this decides only what a project gets without one.
