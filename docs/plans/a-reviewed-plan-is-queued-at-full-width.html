§4 — One read says, section by section, whether a plan's work may be queued

Three callers need to know whether a section may be queued: the dispatch a coordinator runs, the admit service (§7) and the wait that queues its upstream (§8). Today the pieces of that answer live apart. A dispatch naming one section checks the plan's review, and an unanswered finding anchored on a section holds only that section. The dispatch naming a whole plan does not check the review at all, so it queues the sections of an unreviewed plan; building-a-plan-queues-all-of-it §3 changes that to queueing them held. The roadmap separately computes which open decisions hold a plan, and the whole-plan dispatch separately skips a plan another session coordinates.

Deliverable. One read answers, for each implementable section of a plan, whether it may be queued now and, if not, one reason from a fixed list: a review of it is owed or has an unanswered finding, an open decision holds the plan, another live session coordinates the plan, or a coordinator's hold covers it. The read takes each reason from the place that already computes it. The whole-plan dispatch and the single-section dispatch give the same answer the read gives for every section, in the same words.

Done when. tests/test_one_read_says_what_may_be_queued.py passes, covering: in a plan whose review has one unanswered finding anchored on one section, the read answers "review owed" for that section and "may be queued" for the others; a plan with an open decision answers that reason for every section; a plan another live session coordinates answers that reason naming the session; and for each of those plans the whole-plan dispatch and a single-section dispatch report, per section, the reason text the read gives. The read over a fixture of 300 plans completes in under two seconds. Each arm's log sits under the run directory and carries its revision, its literal command and a closing EXIT= line.

Negative control. On main after b5444d24ba the first case must fail with the whole-plan dispatch queueing the section whose review is owed.

§6 — The queue view counts waiting requests by what they wait for

The pane showed 14 requests waiting, the oldest five days old, beside a lane with no running work. That one figure adds together requests waiting for a lane slot, requests whose upstream has a run on its way, and requests nothing is building toward. The admit pass stamps a class on a request that waits on an upstream; a request waiting only for a slot carries none. The queue view is the one place reckon counts its own requests; the lane document is the router's and is not changed here.

Deliverable. The queue view gives its waiting count in four parts with the age of the oldest in each: waiting for a slot, waiting on an upstream that is advancing, waiting on an upstream nothing is building toward, and waiting on an upstream that can never land. It takes the last three from the class already stamped on each request and never recomputes it. The pane renders the four parts, and draws a fault naming the upstream plans when no work is running and the third part is not zero.

Done when. tests/test_the_queue_view_counts_waits_by_cause.py passes, covering: two requests with no upstream wait, three stamped advancing and two stamped idle give the three counts with their oldest ages in the queue view, and the pane row rendered from it shows them; a lane with no running work and two idle waits renders the fault and names their two upstream plans; a queue with only slot waits and advancing waits renders no fault; and changing one request's stamp between two reads moves it from one count to the other with no other input. Each arm's log sits under the run directory and carries its revision, its literal command and a closing EXIT= line.

Negative control. On main after b5444d24ba the first case must fail with a single waiting count given.

§7 — The admit service queues every section that may be queued

The lead decided on 21 November 2026 that the queue must fill and drain unattended: reckon itself queues reviewed plans, and a coordinator audits and lands the work without having to start it.

Deliverable. On each pass the admit service files, through the existing whole-plan dispatch, a request for every implementable section that §4's read says may be queued and that has none. A request filed this way is recorded as queued by the service, under the plan's coordinator when the existing resolution names a live one and under the service's own identity otherwise. A project switches the service's queueing off with one flight setting. A coordinator keeps a plan out of it with the existing queue hold command, which this section extends to accept a plan that has no request yet and to cover requests filed for it later; that hold is the fourth reason in §4's list. This section adds one flight setting and one form of an existing command, and no new command.

Done when. tests/test_the_service_queues_what_may_be_queued.py passes, covering: a reviewed plan with three implementable sections and no request has three requests after one pass, each recorded as queued by the service, and no more after a second; a section §4 answers "may not" for gets none while its siblings do; a project with the setting off gets none; a plan held by the queue hold command before any request exists gets none, and gets them after the hold is released; a plan with a live coordinator has its requests filed under that session, and one with none has them filed under the service identity. On the fleet node at the node's head revision, the median duration of ten consecutive passes with the setting on is within two seconds of the median of ten with it off, both read from the service's own pass readings. Each arm's log sits under the run directory and carries its revision, its literal command and a closing EXIT= line.

Negative control. On main after b5444d24ba the first case must fail with no request filed.

§8 — A wait on a section nobody is building queues that section

At 20:40Z on 21 November 2026 seven stored requests, 3 to 25 days old, waited on sections that were declared implementable and had no request. reckon already raises a duty on the owner of such a request, telling them to queue the upstream or drop the request; those seven show the duty going unanswered. The lead decided the same day that such a wait is resolved by queueing its upstream.

Deliverable. When the admit pass stamps a wait as one nothing is building toward, and §4's read says the upstream section may be queued, the pass files that section's request through the call §7 uses, and the rule then applies to what that section waits on. This holds whether or not the project has switched §7's queueing off, because the waiter is work a coordinator already chose to queue. The existing idle-wait duty is then raised only where the upstream may not be queued, and its text gives §4's reason in place of the instruction to queue the upstream.

Done when. tests/test_an_idle_wait_queues_its_upstream.py passes, covering: a request waiting on an unqueued implementable section has that section queued after one pass, raises no idle-wait duty, and is released once the section lands; a chain of three such sections is queued to its root in one pass; the same holds in a project whose service queueing is off; an upstream whose review is owed is not queued and the waiter's duty gives that reason in the words §4's read gives; and a wait shaped as the two imas-codex requests of 15 November (phase blocked on an upstream nobody is building, in a live plan) has its upstream queued. Each arm's log sits under the run directory and carries its revision, its literal command and a closing EXIT= line.

§9 — A session takes on an unowned plan only inside a sprint it works in

The lead ruled on 21 November 2026 that unowned plans are taken on, but not as a free-for-all: a session takes one only within the sprints in which it already coordinates a plan, and the other live sessions in the repository are told. A plan's coordinator is already derived from who dispatched it, so taking a plan on is dispatching it; no second ownership record is added.

Deliverable. A whole-plan dispatch by a session of a plan that the existing resolution gives no live coordinator is accepted only when the plan's sprint is one in which that session already coordinates a plan. Otherwise each section is skipped with one more reason in the list the whole-plan dispatch already skips by, beside "coordinated by another session": the plan's sprint is not one the session works in. When the dispatch is accepted, every other live session in the project is shown one row naming the plan and the session that took it. This section adds one skip reason to an existing list and one row; it adds no refusal family, command or record, and retires nothing because nothing existing states this rule.

Done when. tests/test_a_plan_is_taken_on_within_a_shared_sprint.py passes, covering: a session coordinating a plan in a sprint dispatches an unowned plan of that sprint, its requests are queued, the resolution then names that session as the plan's coordinator, and a second live session in the project receives the row; the same session's dispatch of an unowned plan in a sprint it coordinates nothing in skips every section naming that sprint and queues nothing; and a dispatch by the service identity (§7) is not subject to the rule. Each arm's log sits under the run directory and carries its revision, its literal command and a closing EXIT= line.

Negative control. On main after b5444d24ba the second case must fail with the plan's sections queued.

§11 — A plan-level dispatch reads a section's negative control from the section

A section the queue cannot read this from is a section no unattended pass can queue, which is what §7 needs.

§14 — A wait probe is evaluated by one reader at a time

A waiting run names a probe, and every reader that polls the wait runs it. On 22 November 2026 run r-20261122T072450492220-work-flows-to-the-lane-s67 waited on a probe that copied admission records out of the run store. Between 07:33Z and 08:41Z nine copies ran at once, a new one every few seconds, each holding the 1.16 GB store open, and every promotion on the node failed with "database is locked … past the wait bound (120 s)": at least nine promotions in three sessions, and queued requests stopped launching. It ended when the probe was replaced by hand with a clock check.

Deliverable. A wait's probe runs at most once at a time and no more often than once every 30 seconds, however many readers poll the wait, and each reader is given the last result. A probe still running when its interval comes round is not started again, and a probe that runs longer than 60 seconds is stopped and the wait is raised to its coordinator naming it.

Done when. tests/test_a_wait_probe_runs_once_at_a_time.py passes, covering: ten readers polling one wait within a second start the probe once and all read its result; a probe that takes longer than 30 seconds is not started a second time; and a probe exceeding 60 seconds is stopped and raises one obligation naming the run and the probe. Each arm's log sits under the run directory and carries its revision, its literal command and a closing EXIT= line.

Negative control. At the base revision, the first case must fail with the probe started once per reader.
