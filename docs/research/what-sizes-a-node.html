Which dispatch-time size separates outcomes within a requirement

Across 5,193 completed runs from 6 projects in the 14 days to 2026-11-15, 4 of the five candidate size signals clear the fixed bar in at least one cell: write-path count in 7 cells, estimated context in 4 cells, done-when clauses in 2 cells and negative control declared in 2 cells. 10 of 112 cells rank any signal at all. The control clears the bar in 1 of those cells, and nothing is named there.

Population, matching and bar

The measurement reads the committed run records of every project in the mount registry: one JSON document per promoted run under <docs>/state/<project>/runs/. A row enters the population when its own completed_at, or dispatched_at where that is absent, falls inside the 14 days to 2026-11-15T22:03:26+00:00 — the cutoff _candidates_scan applies at line 258 of reckon’s crew/picker/snapshot.py.

Rows are matched as _candidates_scan matches them, by calling the picker's own predicates rather than a copy of them: role and spec level are compared exactly, a row's recorded requirement is read through _recorded_requirement (the reasoning and risk under node_definition.capability.requirements), and a row whose lineage moved it away from the backend its pick chose is dropped through _picked_backend_away. A row that recorded no requirement forms the unrecorded group rather than being matched by default.

A cell is one project, role, spec level and recorded requirement — a population one pick could read, because a pick reads only its own project's ledger. Nothing is pooled across projects: a pooled table could not be any pick's population, and it decides no verdict.

The bucket edges were fixed before any outcome was read: done-when clauses 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+; write paths 1 / 2–3 / 4+; negative control declared or not; estimated hours under 1 / 1–2 / over 2 / absent; rendered estimated_context under 1 token and a rendered state that carries none both read absent, else under 20,000 / 20,000–60,000 / over 60,000; and the control is the parity of the run id's timestamp seconds. The absent bucket is reported and never compared. For an ordered signal the compared pair is its lowest and highest occupied bucket; a two-valued signal compares its two values.

The bar. A compared pair clears the bar when each bucket holds at least 5 rows and their pass rates differ by at least 20 percentage points. The control is judged on those two conditions alone. Where the control clears the bar in a cell, that cell's sample cannot rank signals and no separating signal is named there. A candidate signal separates a cell only when its own pair also clears the bar and its difference exceeds the control's difference in that cell.

What this does not establish

  • The buckets are small: no verdict rests on fewer than 10 rows across its two buckets, and a 20-point difference over a 5-row bucket is one row, so a single verdict here is a lead rather than a measurement of a lane.
  • estimated hours is absent on 4,689 of 5,193 rows and has no comparable pair in 90 of 112 cells; estimated context is absent on 3,216 of 5,193 rows and has no comparable pair in 67 of 112 cells; done-when clauses is absent on 2 of 5,193 rows and has no comparable pair in 58 of 112 cells. An absent bucket is reported and never compared, so a signal recorded on few rows is a signal most cells cannot judge.
  • A signal that separates no cell means that the cells which could compare it do not separate it; it does not mean size carries no information. Only 10 of 112 cells rank any signal at all.
  • Rows were taken as committed records. A run promoted after the read instant is not in any figure, and a row's recorded requirement is what its dispatch recorded, so a row dispatched before requirements were recorded lands in the unrecorded group.
