§1 — Verdict

The ratio is not at most one. In the week after the small-node rule reached the worker prompt, the fleet's small nodes landed 7,093 record lines against 1,574 product lines, a ratio of 4.51. Measured by the node's net tree diff instead of by its own commits the same week reads 6,096 against 1,535, a ratio of 3.97. Neither reading is near one.

The aggregate hides the structure, and the structure is the finding. A small node that changes product lands about a quarter of a record line for each product line it lands — 395 record lines against 1,574 product lines across 73 nodes, a ratio of 0.25. Almost all of the record comes from the other 105 small nodes, which change no product at all. Fifty-four of those are admitted to the population only by the rule's second arm, the arm that calls a node small when it changes no source or test and stays under 300 lines.

So the plan's done-when, taken as written over every node the rule calls small, fails by a factor of four and a half. Taken over the nodes that actually trade record for product, the same week passes with room to spare. Which of the two the section means is a decision the census can inform but not make.

The unit is the node's own commits. Each run record names the commits it made, and each of those is measured against its own parent. That is the per-commit reading the velocity view already takes of a landed range, and it is the only reading that stays the node's own when a run is dispatched from a revision another run's commit landed after. Fifty-three of the 890 nodes are dispatched that way, and base_sha..promoted_revision then carries the foreign commit into the node's diff.

The classes are the ones the velocity view uses. Every changed path is classed by reckon.path_classes.file_class: source and tests are product; plan_evidence_research_html (plan, evidence and research documents) and docs_state are record. The figures directory is excluded from both, as in the 5.35-line baseline this measure is written against. Lines are added plus deleted.

The threshold is the rule's own. A node is small when its diff has at most 50 changed lines, or when it changes no source and no test and has at most 300.

What this does not count. The landing comment promotion writes onto the plan section is not in the node's commit, so record is, if anything, understated here. Review runs commit nothing in this window and contribute nothing to either column.

§4 — Where the record comes from

Of the 178 small nodes, 105 land no product line at all, and they carry 6,698 of the 7,093 record lines. The remaining 73 land all 1,574 product lines and 395 record lines between them. The population therefore splits cleanly into two shapes that the single ratio averages into a meaningless number:

Within the second shape, 54 nodes are admitted only by the 300-line arm — they would not be small under the 50-line arm — and they alone carry 5,567 record lines. Replacing a paragraph of prose with a 250-line evidence fragment keeps a node under 300 lines and so keeps it inside the population the measure is about.

Fifty-two small nodes land no record line at all. The rule is not uniformly ignored; it is obeyed by some nodes and dominated by the ones that write a fragment for every node regardless.

§5 — The instrument, and its controls

Three checks were run before the figure was believed.

The classifier sees what is there. Spot-checked on named nodes: a node landing imas_ambix/agent/live_panel.py (5 added, 2 removed), tests/test_live_panel.py (32 added) and a 4-line evidence fragment reads as 39 product lines and 4 record lines, which is the commit's contents exactly. A node landing a 245-line evidence fragment and a one-line SVG reads as 373 record lines, 1 figure line and 0 product lines.

The diff is the node's own. The primary reading sums each named commit against its parent, which reproduces the run record's own changed_lines exactly on 601 of 890 nodes. The 289 that differ differ by the amount a node's repeat edits to one file add when counted commit by commit rather than net — median 36 lines, ninetieth percentile 270. The largest gaps are nodes that re-generate a large artifact across commits and so count it twice; one such node's two readings are 752 lines and 62,358. A merge commit in a node's list is skipped for the same reason: measured against its first parent it carries everything the merged branch brought in, which is another run's work. One node's diff was 109,059 lines until its merge was excluded, against the 371 its record reports.

The verdict survives the choice of unit. The two readings classify 43 nodes differently around the 50-line threshold, and change the population from 178 to 145. Both give an aggregate between 3.9 and 4.6 and a product-carrying ratio between 0.25 and 0.26. No reading of the same data puts the aggregate near one.

What the instrument does not see. A run whose named commits are not reachable from any project checkout is dropped and counted: one node landed with 890 readable and 1 unreadable. Record written by a coordinator's promotion rather than by the node is not attributed to the node.

§6 — What this says about the section

The rule is landing where it was aimed. The nodes that write product write about a quarter of a line of record per product line, well inside the section's target and a long way from the 5.35-line fleet figure the section quotes as its baseline. The target fails only when the population is widened to every node the rule calls small, and the widening is done by the rule's own second arm.

Two readings of the done-when are available, and they give opposite answers on the same week:

  • Over every small node, as the sentence reads, the ratio is 4.5 and the target is not met. The number is dominated by nodes whose denominator is zero.
  • Over small nodes that change product, the ratio is 0.25 and the target is met with a factor of four in hand.

A third option is available to the rule itself: the 300-line arm can be tightened to exclude a node that lands no product line, which removes the denominator-of-zero population rather than averaging over it. That is a change to the rule, not to the measure, and belongs to whoever owns the section.

§7 — Reproducing it

The population is read from the run store: runs rows completed inside the window, taking base_sha, promoted_revision and the named commits from each payload. Line counts come from git diff --numstat in each project's checkout, and classes from reckon.path_classes.file_class. The two scripts that produce the figures above are smallnode_census.py (per commit) and net_reading.py (net tree diff); both write their JSON beside themselves, under the run directory ~/.config/reckon/crew/runs/r-20261103T081947319381-measure-the-smallnode-record-ratio-after-landingline/census/, which also holds the per-node rows the tables are cut from.
