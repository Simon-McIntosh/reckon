Backlog and throughput

The request snapshot holds 79 requested, two running and two parked. The oldest requested run has waited 56.45 minutes. In the preceding hour, at least 100 retained requests arrived, while the drain recorded 11 promotions and nine refusals. These are differently retained populations: they establish current overload, not a stationary queueing-rate estimate. At 11 successful promotions per hour, ten more successful promotions consume about 55 minutes before accounting for the existing queue, reviews and failures; this is not a claim that every promotion lands source.

The retained drained log spans 27.60 hours, from 2026-11-15 18:40:43 UTC to 2026-11-16 22:16:41 UTC. It contains 1,055 outcomes: 244 promoted, 264 refused, 511 deferred and 36 failed. This is 38.23 terminal outcomes/hour but 8.84 successful promotions/hour. Calling all terminal outcomes “landings” would measure churn as useful work. The log trims to 500 lines after 256 kB, so a seven-day service/arrival rate cannot be recovered from it.

For 460 retained requests with both stamps, requested-to-start median / p90 / worst is 0.62 / 19.39 / 2,437.25 minutes. For 328 completed service intervals, start-to-finish is 0.24 / 3.06 / 11.16 minutes. These conditional samples overrepresent retained failures. Two fleet-wide slot leases bound promotion concurrency, and each slot remains held through a callback that can include locks, gate work and bookkeeping. Queue order puts linked reviews before subjects and otherwise uses run ids; it is not strictly requested-at order. Increasing width without measuring store/index contention could reduce useful throughput.

The admit service runs PromotionService beside its ordinary loop. A refill thread wakes when a slot frees, and reload waits for in-flight promotion workers before re-exec. The lander submits heavy merged-head measures to a partition and finishes pending merges. Thus neither “one promotion globally” nor “a restart on every landing” describes this revision. Store-lock refusals are present, but per-stage service spans do not apportion every minute between lock, suite, remote publication and reload.

A manual landing needs the duty, manifest, independent review, gate evidence and diff read, then reckon crew complete --run … --gate passed --land; a refusal adds an answer/retry and a conflict adds repair. An already eligible automatic delivery needs zero coordinator commands. The sample's two successful responses total 121,113 bytes; its retained refusal adds 1,825. A bounded batch receipt can reduce commands and duplicate reading, but cannot make ten critical sections or ten gates free. It should name each action, head, review and gate log, with wide detail fetched only when needed.

The prototype and its control

The parked requests are r-20261116T211234648844-review-of-vofd-design-report-127aab134a7f and r-20261116T211733528569-review-of-vofd-design-report-127aab134a7f-figure. Both were requested at 21:19:56 UTC and last checked at 21:20:35–36 UTC. Their latest complete-manifest mtimes are 2026-11-16 22:02:37 UTC and 2026-11-16 22:02:35 UTC. The dispatch brief's earlier 21:36 repair is not independently dated by those later rewrites. Their head-keyed review records predate the census cutoff. At replay, the unchanged review_self_state returned ready for both, while their stored wait reason still said the manifest did not read complete. The recorded condition had cleared; that is not a promise that all publication checks would pass.

Reproduction. A temporary crew home held copies of the two requests, pointers, manifests, exit receipts, declared review records and one earlier real slot-taking request. The current PromotionService.tick ran with no slot available. Its actual readiness function rechecked 0 of 2 parked reviews: the earlier ready request reached the slot check, failed to obtain one, and broke the loop before reaching either wait. Membership of WAITING in QUEUED therefore does not ensure timely reconsideration under congestion.

Prototype. A bounded readiness-only pass before slot admission called the same guard and rechecked 2 of 2 in 0.042942 seconds, changing only copied requests to requested and acquiring no slot. Changing one copied manifest to in-progress still returned the review run's manifest does not read complete, so its verdict has not been written. The unmodified control left both copied requests parked and their pointers delivered. An audit hook refused writes outside the temporary home; SHA-256 checks proved all 24 sampled original files unchanged afterwards.

Limit. This is a readiness scheduling prototype. It executed zero landings. It does not prove a successful promotion, merged-head gate, primary push, a new drain rate, or that this two-request defect is the largest causal contributor over seven days. The first replay passed its behavioural assertions but its own cleanup audit misresolved a dir_fd path; the corrected replay completed with EXIT=0. Both logs are retained. No live run, request, review, hold, service or session was acted on. End-to-end publication and attribution of the dominant bucket remain unmet measures.
