Are review nodes worth their time?

A measured review-cost and defect-catch record for choosing whether a node needs a review, and how deep that review should be.

§1 — Corpus and method

This record was generated on 2026-09-25 from the run ledger's durable review records under /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/reviews/ and the run directories under /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/runs/. The filter accepted JSON objects carrying all five review score dimensions and a reviewed_run_id: 820 review records, across imas-ambix, imas-codex, imas-efit, nova and reckon. The raw census is reproducible with review_metrics.py; its row-level output is review-rows.jsonl, its grouped output is review-summary.tsv, and the complete summary is review-summary.json in the report directory named in the manifest.

The join is deliberately conservative. An explicit reviewer_run_id resolves the review stream; if it is absent, the review still contributes to finding, repair and score counts but contributes no invented wall time, turn count or model. That leaves 222 reviewer-run joins, 197 reviews with a timestamped review wall, and 169 latency joins. The missing 377 model labels and the missing timing are not filled from a profile, scheduler or filename. This distinction matters: a coverage number is evidence about the instrument, not evidence that the unjoined reviews cost zero.

Review wall is the first-to-last event timestamp in the reviewer stream.jsonl plus any resume-*.jsonl. Review turns and implement turns are counts of assistant stream events; review tool steps count assistant tool_use items and structured item.started tool calls. Implement completion is the final event in the reviewed run's stream set. Node class comes from changed_paths: all docs/reports/artifacts is docs-or-evidence-only, all tests/ is test-only, and the remainder is source-change. A missing manifest is unknown.

§3 — Findings, repairs and later defects

692 of 820 reviews (84.4%) carried at least one finding. A repair is credited only where the run record shows a later finding-bearing review for the same implement run, or the reviewed run's manifest carries a commit after the reviewed head; abbreviated and full commit IDs are matched by prefix. On that definition, 171 of 692 finding-bearing reviews (24.7%) led to an observable repair. The complement is not “no repair”: it is either promoted unchanged or lacks a durable later receipt, so the latter must remain visible rather than being folded into success.

The corpus has zero clean-review-then-finding sequences for the strict missed-defect proxy: a review with no finding followed later by a finding-bearing review of the same implement run. This is not a claim that no defect surfaced after promotion; verify-gate failures, added suite failures and re-cuts are not represented as a complete cross-run relation in the review JSON. The observable missed-defect measure is therefore unmeasured beyond that zero, and any decision should treat it as an instrumentation gap rather than a zero miss rate.

Low score predicts that a review will carry a finding, and weakly predicts that the finding will lead to an observable repair: 36.6% for low scores versus 20.1% for high scores among finding-bearing reviews. It is not a sufficiently sharp repair rule on its own. Model, node class and the missing receipt fields explain enough uncertainty that the score should prioritize a second look, not automatically demand one.

§5 — One positive control traced end to end

The paid-headroom document is the hand-traced control. Its first review record stored score 75 and four findings: inert --help handling, an implicit global read, an untested gatherer and an unwired production path. The review session returned those findings to the implement node; the landing record names repair commits db50a1e3, 559e9fe7 and d1ae179c, and the cumulative record reports the merged head a953e4d7. The re-review scored 87 after the repair round. This is the positive control for the repair relation: finding → resume/repair commits → re-review → promoted head, rather than an inference from a green suite.

The current evidence supports a cost-aware choice, not a universal “review everything” or “skip small nodes” rule. The next measurement should preserve explicit model identity, add a test-only cohort, and link verify-gate failures and re-cuts to the reviewed head. Until then, the reported repair fractions are lower-bound observable outcomes, the Luna comparison is unavailable, and the missed-defect rate is not established.
