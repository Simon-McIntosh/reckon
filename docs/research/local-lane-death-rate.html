The Local Lane Death Rate

Three in ten review attempts on the locally served lane end with nothing they were asked to produce, and the runs' own streams say so. Over the 30 days to 2026-10-02, 393 of 1342 review attempts at xhigh — the effort this lane declares for the review reflex — ended mid-turn on the clive lane: the process was gone and its stream carried no result record. Counting the review role at every declared effort gives 416 of 1855 (22.4%), and the same lane killed implement attempts at every effort at 191 of 2759 (6.9%). That gap is the reason sessions moved their review reflex onto the orchestrator lane, and it is the number this record exists to state.

Every death that carries a supervisor exit record ended by a signal, and that record covers a third of the review deaths. A second, independent read of the same corpus — §3 below — finds 131 of the 393 review deaths carrying the record the supervisor wrote when it collected the process, and all 131 are signalled: 92 SIGTERM and 39 SIGKILL. Not one recorded review death shows the process exiting cleanly. The remaining 262 review deaths carry no record at all, so the distinction §1 of the plan needs — a signal the worker did not choose against a clean exit — is now drawn for a third of the population and still unavailable for the rest.

Why it is a record rather than a footnote. The plan that fences background work off the orchestrator lane gates its own default on this figure: closing the escape hatch while the locally served lane still dies nine times a day converts a degraded review loop into no review loop. Until the review-reliability repair lands and a post-repair after-rate exists, that plan's fence warns and records rather than the plan refuses. This page states the before-rate, the after window the effort move has since produced, and says plainly that the post-repair after-rate does not yet exist.

§1 — What was measured, and where the answer comes from

The population is the runs' own attempt streams. Every run directory under ~/.config/reckon/crew/runs/ holds one stream per attempt: stream.jsonl for the first, resume-N.jsonl for each later one. 7733 run directories were enumerated, 3909 were recorded on the local lane (backend: clive in the ledger), and the 3907 of those carrying streams hold 5034 attempts.

A death is a stream that stops without a result. The harness writes a top-level {"type":"result"} record when a turn reaches its end. A stream whose last record is anything else, for a run whose process is no longer alive, is an attempt that died mid-turn. No summary, rollup or ledger field was read for this classification — the streams were read directly, and the ledger supplied only the run's recorded backend, role and effort.

The stream says a death happened and cannot say what ended it. That is a second fact about the same attempt, recorded separately: the supervisor that waited on the worker writes an exit record beside the stream, carrying the signal or the exit code it collected. §3 reports that read for every cell, and it covers only the attempts a supervisor wrote a record for, so it is stated with its own coverage rather than as a figure over the whole population.

Two populations, named wherever they appear. The headline death count is the review reflex's own configuration — the review role at the effort this lane declares for reviews. Review attempts dispatched at any other effort are counted in the role-wide figure beside it, and never pooled into the headline: a count that mixes populations reads as the effort cell it sits beside while counting something else, which is the defect this page was corrected for.

Two views, and they are not the same number. The attempt view counts every stream as one observation, which is the event the plan is about: an attempt that died is an attempt whose work was lost, whether or not it was later resumed. The terminal view reads only a run's last attempt, so a run that died and was successfully resumed reads as completed. The figures here are the attempt view; the terminal view sits in the same data file and is the convention reckon.crew.death_census documents.

The script and its output both persist, and the script is versioned. The scan is produced by docs/research/scripts/scan_local_lane_deaths.py, committed beside this page, invoked as timeout 1200 <venv>/bin/python docs/research/scripts/scan_local_lane_deaths.py --out docs/research/data/local-lane-deaths.json. The corpus parses at roughly 55 MB/s, so a full pass takes minutes and the bound is what stops a runaway scan holding the node. The measurement it writes is the committed file docs/research/data/local-lane-deaths.json — which names the script that produced it, and the moment of the scan — and the assertions over that file live in tests/test_the_local_lane_death_rate_is_stated.py. The chart below is drawn from that same file by docs/research/scripts/plot_local_lane_deaths.py, also committed, so it is a view of the measurement rather than a second one. It was drawn from the 2026-09-28 scan and says so; the table beside it carries the current scan, and re-running the plot script against the current file redraws the chart.

What this figure is for. It is the before block: a fence that refuses background work on the orchestrator lane is only defensible once the alternative it sends that work to is measured, and this is that measurement at the effort the review reflex uses. It is not a claim about any other lane, and it is not a claim about why the deaths happen — that belongs to the review-reliability work.

The after block — the review reflex at effort high, since the move. Local-lane reviews moved from xhigh to high at dotfiles f3f653a, about 18:36Z on 2026-09-30. Over the window that opens there — the scan of 2026-10-02T23:17Z, 52.7 hours — 22 of 467 local-lane review attempts at high died mid-turn: 4.7%, with a 95% Wilson interval of 3.1% to 7.0%, against the 29.4% before-rate the plan states for reviews at xhigh (392 deaths in 1333 attempts). The window is past the 60-attempt floor the plan sets for stating a rate, so the rate is stated rather than the window deferred. Seventeen of the 22 deaths carry a supervisor exit record, and sixteen of those name a signal, all sixteen SIGKILL.

Two readings of one population, and the difference between them is the corpus, not the rate. The table above carries this scan: 393 in 1342, 29.3%. The plan states the before-rate as 392 in 1333, 29.4%. The difference is the corpus, not the rate: the xhigh cell still grows slowly through attempts of pre-move runs resumed after the move — one of which has died since the plan's figure was written — so the rerun does not reproduce the published figure, and it is not meant to. No reading of the cell changes the comparison: whatever the boundary, roughly three in ten xhigh reviews died, and roughly one in twenty of the moved reviews has.

The filter's two axes are checkable from the same scan. 466 of the 467 in-window attempts carry their own stream timestamps; one is dated by its file's mtime, so one attempt's place in the window rests on a file clock rather than on a record — and none was unreadable. Of the 513 review attempts at high in the whole history, the 46 that started before the window are counted just outside it, and zero review attempts at any other effort, and zero at xhigh, started inside it: the window separates the moved review reflex from both the pre-move population and any review still declared at the old effort.

This compares two configurations of the review reflex, not an isolated effort effect. The lane's endpoint ignores thinking budgets, so high and xhigh are dispatch declarations, and the move was accompanied by whatever else changed on the lane at the same time. Read it as a natural before-and-after: the review reflex's configuration since the move dies far less often — 4.7% against 29.4% — and that after window is now the baseline §9's repair would be measured against.

Note on the effort axis. Effort is recorded, not effective, on this lane: the endpoint ignores thinking budgets, so xhigh and high are dispatch declarations rather than two experimental conditions. The review reflex and the implement role are therefore close to collinear in this data — reviews run at the declared maximum, implements mostly at high — and the 29%-against-8% gap should be read as the review reflex's configuration dies far more often, not as an isolated effect of effort. The one cell that separates them points the other way: implement at xhigh died at 3.8%, so the role, not the effort alone, carries the gap's larger share.

§3 — What ended the review deaths that carry a record

The rate says how many died and cannot say what ended them, and nothing did. The classifier reads the stream's shape, which is all a stream carries. A supervisor that waited on the worker writes a second record beside the stream — attempt-N-exit.json in the run directory, carrying the signal or the exit code it collected — and this section is that record read across the whole corpus. It is a separate fact about the same attempt rather than a second classification, so where it is absent the attempt is unread and is not assigned an ending.

Every recorded review death ended by a signal. Of the 393 review deaths at xhigh, 131 carry an exit record — 33.3% of them — and all 131 name a signal: 92 SIGTERM and 39 SIGKILL. Sixty-nine of the 131 name it in the record's own signal field; the other 62 carry it in an exit code at or above 128, by the shell's 128+N convention — 143 is SIGTERM and 137 is SIGKILL — which the block reads as that signal and labels with the reading it used, so a death recorded that way is not mistaken for a clean exit. Not one recorded death shows the process exiting on its own terms.

The 100% is a reading and not a rule, and the same reader reports clean exits where they exist. Across the lane, 1905 attempts carry a record: 1726 of them completed and 179 died. The recorded exit codes are mostly clean — 1720 zeros and 14 ones — beside 72 137s and 23 143s (the shell's 128+N spelling of SIGKILL and SIGTERM) and 76 records that name a signal without carrying a code, so the same block that reports the signals also reports clean exits where they exist. Two recorded completions carry a signal — the shape of a run killed after its result was written — and not every recorded death is signalled: 169 of the 179 lane-wide carry one, so a death whose record named no ending is left out of the signalled count rather than counted as clean. The block's own construction carries the distinction rather than deriving it: a recorded death whose exit code was 0 would be counted in deaths_with_exit_record and left out of deaths_signalled, and for the xhigh review deaths the two figures are equal because no such record exists.

The gap is the more consequential half. 262 of the 393 review deaths — 66.7% — carry no exit record at all, so for two thirds of the population the question the plan's §1 exists to answer is still unanswered: the record distinguishes a signal the worker did not choose from a clean exit where it was written, and the supervisor wrote one for a third of the review deaths. A lane-wide figure of 35.6% holds for the review role at every effort — 148 of 416 — and the corpus-wide coverage is 179 of 636 (28.1%).

What the record names, and what it does not. It names the signal and the process it ended; it does not name who sent it, so an external kill and the harness's own stop of a run look identical in this block. A deliberate stop is recorded on the run's live pointer, which is deleted when a run is promoted, so it is not available for the historical corpus. The signal breakdown is therefore a divided account of how these runs ended and not of why.

§4 — The parser that read every run as dead

The first measurement of this rate was wrong in the direction that would have made the plan look right. The first classifier matched each line against the harness's record shape with an anchored pattern, {"type":". The harness writes the result record with duration_api_ms as its first key, so the one record that separates a completion from a death never matched: 3335 of 3336 attempts and 1057 of 1058 review attempts classified as dead. Every attempt in the corpus read as a corpse, and a limb of the plan would have been gated on a lane that appeared to kill 99.8% of everything it was given.

The check that was supposed to catch it could not fail. The check ran the same regex over a stream whose last line was a result record and reported that it did not find one — which was true of the regex and false of the stream. It confirmed the parser against its own reading rather than against an independent fact, and it passed. What caught the error was asking the question the wrong answer makes uncomfortable: whether any attempt in the corpus ever completes at all.

The fix is the record's own field, and the control rides with it. Every line is now parsed whole and the record's own top-level type is read. Parsing the corpus that way costs about four minutes, which is why the scan is bounded. Because a classifier that reads a completion as a death and one that reads a death as a completion fail differently — the second inflates confidence in the lane, the first destroys it — the measurement carries both directions on one run. The dialect trap is one layer out. The corpus carries streams from more than one harness, and the harnesses do not agree on how a turn ends: a stream on the codex backends ends with turn.completed. A reader assuming a single schema reports those runs as dead — measured here at 48 of 250 runs in one day on 2026-09-23. The census above is therefore restricted to the clive lane, where one dialect applies throughout, and the classification is checked against a run known to have died rather than against the counts the same parser produced.

§5 — The post-repair after-rate does not exist yet

No post-repair after-rate is stated, because the repair it would measure has not landed. The work that is to fix this is a-review-dies-where-nobody-is-looking §9, which takes one reading of a review dying mid-turn and makes the worker compact and continue instead. That section is declared implementable and its driving followup is open. There is no post-repair window in the corpus to measure. The after block §2 carries is the window the effort move created — the review reflex's configuration since 2026-09-30 — and is not evidence about the repair.

What the fence should do until then, stated as the plan states it. The orchestrator-lane fence warns and records rather than refuses. It still records the dispatch and the lane, which removes the silent case without stopping the reviews — and the reviews still need somewhere to go, because a lane that kills three in ten review attempts is not a lane to send them back to until the rate moves.

What makes the post-repair rate measurable. Once the repair lands, the same scanner given an after-window start produces the block: it reads whatever attempts exist when it is run, and it groups them by the effort each run declared. The comparison needs a window that begins after the repair's first successful review, and enough attempts in it to distinguish a drop from noise — the moved review reflex ran 467 attempts in 52.7 hours, so a post-repair window of comparable length carries a rate worth gating on.

§6 — Method limits a reader should carry

  • Attempt, not run. A run that died and was resumed contributes two observations. This is deliberate — the plan counts lost work, and a resumed attempt is not evidence that the first one finished — but it means the rate cannot be compared against a figure computed the terminal way.
  • Timestamps fall back to file mtime. Streams that carry no timestamp field are dated by the file's mtime, so a stream whose file was touched after its last record lands on the wrong day. The daily series is therefore good for shape and not for a single day's exact count.
  • Signals are corroboration, not classification. Only some attempts carry a supervisor exit record — 1905 of 5034 lane-wide, and 131 of the 393 review deaths. The classification never depends on one, as a stream may die for reasons the exit record does not capture, and the classifier reads the stream shape; §3 reads the record separately and states its coverage beside every figure it quotes. The record names the signal and not the sender, so a signalled death is not evidence of a fault on its own.
  • The window is the lane's whole recorded life. The start is the first local-lane attempt the corpus holds, not a chosen boundary; a rate computed over a sub-window is not directly comparable to it without recomputing.
  • Role comes from the dispatch prompt. The run's own prompt.txt declares its role; the ledger row is the fallback where the prompt is absent, and disagreements between the two are recorded in the data file rather than silently resolved. On this corpus the two sources did not disagree: all 3907 local-lane runs carrying streams declared a role in the prompt, and none contradicted its ledger row — the file's role_attribution block carries those counts so a reader can check the claim rather than take it.
