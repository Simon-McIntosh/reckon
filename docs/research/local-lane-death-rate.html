The Local Lane Death Rate

Three in ten review attempts on the locally served lane end with nothing they were asked to produce, and the runs' own streams say so. Over the 26 days to 2026-09-28, 299 of 1018 review attempts at xhigh — the effort this lane declares for the review reflex — ended mid-turn on the clive lane: the process was gone and its stream carried no result record. Counting the review role at every declared effort gives 300 of 1066 (28.1%), and the same lane killed implement attempts at every effort at 161 of 1969 (8.2%). That gap is the reason sessions moved their review reflex onto the orchestrator lane, and it is the number this record exists to state.

Why it is a record rather than a footnote. The plan that fences background work off the orchestrator lane gates its own default on this figure: closing the escape hatch while the locally served lane still dies nine times a day converts a degraded review loop into no review loop. Until the review-reliability repair lands and an after-rate exists, that plan's fence warns and records rather than the plan refuses. This page states the before-rate and says plainly that the after-rate does not yet exist.

§1 — What was measured, and where the answer comes from

The population is the runs' own attempt streams. Every run directory under ~/.config/reckon/crew/runs/ holds one stream per attempt: stream.jsonl for the first, resume-N.jsonl for each later one. 6290 run directories were enumerated, 2486 were recorded on the local lane (backend: clive in the ledger), and the 2484 of those carrying streams hold 3354 attempts.

A death is a stream that stops without a result. The harness writes a top-level {"type":"result"} record when a turn reaches its end. A stream whose last record is anything else, for a run whose process is no longer alive, is an attempt that died mid-turn. No summary, rollup or ledger field was read for this classification — the streams were read directly, and the ledger supplied only the run's recorded backend, role and effort.

Two populations, named wherever they appear. The headline death count is the review reflex's own configuration — the review role at the effort this lane declares for reviews. Review attempts dispatched at any other effort are counted in the role-wide figure beside it, and never pooled into the headline: a count that mixes populations reads as the effort cell it sits beside while counting something else, which is the defect this page was corrected for.

Two views, and they are not the same number. The attempt view counts every stream as one observation, which is the event the plan is about: an attempt that died is an attempt whose work was lost, whether or not it was later resumed. The terminal view reads only a run's last attempt, so a run that died and was successfully resumed reads as completed. The figures here are the attempt view; the terminal view sits in the same data file and is the convention reckon.crew.death_census documents.

The script and its output both persist, and the script is versioned. The scan is produced by docs/research/scripts/scan_local_lane_deaths.py, committed beside this page, invoked as timeout 1200 <venv>/bin/python docs/research/scripts/scan_local_lane_deaths.py --out docs/research/data/local-lane-deaths.json. The corpus parses at roughly 55 MB/s, so a full pass takes minutes and the bound is what stops a runaway scan holding the node. The measurement it writes is the committed file docs/research/data/local-lane-deaths.json — which names the script that produced it, and the moment of the scan — and the assertions over that file live in tests/test_the_local_lane_death_rate_is_stated.py. The figure below is drawn from that same file by docs/research/scripts/plot_local_lane_deaths.py, also committed, so the chart and the number a fence reads cannot drift apart.

§2 — The before-rate

Window and population. 2026-09-02T13:32Z to 2026-09-28T08:05Z, the whole life of the local lane's recorded history, closed at the moment of the scan. Population: 2486 local-lane runs carrying 3354 attempts, of which 1066 are review-role attempts and 1018 are review attempts at xhigh.

Recent windows are worse, not better. Trailing seven days (2026-09-22 to 2026-09-28): 228 review deaths in 715 attempts, 31.9%. Trailing day: 6 in 17, 35%. The rate is not decaying on its own, and the two heaviest days on the chart — 2026-09-25 with 80 deaths (72 of them review) and 2026-09-18 with 65 — are the recent ones. The lane was not improving while sessions were migrating their reviews off it.

What this figure is for. It is the before block: a fence that refuses background work on the orchestrator lane is only defensible once the alternative it sends that work to is measured, and this is that measurement at the effort the review reflex uses. It is not a claim about any other lane, and it is not a claim about why the deaths happen — that belongs to the review-reliability work.

Note on the effort axis. Effort is recorded, not effective, on this lane: the endpoint ignores thinking budgets, so xhigh and high are dispatch declarations rather than two experimental conditions. The review reflex and the implement role are therefore close to collinear in this data — reviews run at the declared maximum, implements mostly at high — and the 29%-against-8% gap should be read as the review reflex's configuration dies far more often, not as an isolated effect of effort. The one cell that separates them points the other way: implement at xhigh died at 3.8%, so the role, not the effort alone, carries the gap's larger share.

§3 — The parser that read every run as dead

The first measurement of this rate was wrong in the direction that would have made the plan look right. The first classifier matched each line against the harness's record shape with an anchored pattern, {"type":". The harness writes the result record with duration_api_ms as its first key, so the one record that separates a completion from a death never matched: 3335 of 3336 attempts and 1057 of 1058 review attempts classified as dead. Every attempt in the corpus read as a corpse, and a limb of the plan would have been gated on a lane that appeared to kill 99.8% of everything it was given.

The check that was supposed to catch it could not fail. The check ran the same regex over a stream whose last line was a result record and reported that it did not find one — which was true of the regex and false of the stream. It confirmed the parser against its own reading rather than against an independent fact, and it passed. What caught the error was asking the question the wrong answer makes uncomfortable: whether any attempt in the corpus ever completes at all.

The fix is the record's own field, and the control rides with it. Every line is now parsed whole and the record's own top-level type is read. Parsing the corpus that way costs about 120 seconds, which is why the scan is bounded. Because a classifier that reads a completion as a death and one that reads a death as a completion fail differently — the second inflates confidence in the lane, the first destroys it — the measurement carries both directions on one run. The committed file names a run that died and a later attempt of that same run that completed, and a test requires both:

Record counts in the table are read at scan time and move if the run is resumed again — the fixed facts are the two classifications.

The dialect trap is one layer out. The corpus carries streams from more than one harness, and the harnesses do not agree on how a turn ends: a stream on the codex backends ends with turn.completed. A reader assuming a single schema reports those runs as dead — measured here at 48 of 250 runs in one day on 2026-09-23. The census above is therefore restricted to the clive lane, where one dialect applies throughout, and the classification is checked against a run known to have died rather than against the counts the same parser produced.

§4 — The after-rate does not exist yet

Only a before block is reported, because the repair it would measure has not landed. The work that is to fix this is a-review-dies-where-nobody-is-looking §9, which takes one reading of a review dying mid-turn and makes the worker compact and continue instead. That section is declared implementable and its driving followup is open. There is no post-repair window in the corpus to measure, so no after block is stated: a second number with no repair behind it would be a fiction with the same shape as a measurement.

What the fence should do until then, stated as the plan states it. The orchestrator-lane fence warns and records rather than refuses. It still records the dispatch and the lane, which removes the silent case without stopping the reviews — and the reviews still need somewhere to go, because a lane that kills three in ten review attempts is not a lane to send them back to until the rate moves.

What makes the after-rate measurable. Once the repair lands, the same scanner run again produces the after block with no code change: it reads whatever attempts exist when it is run, and it groups them by the effort each run declared. The comparison needs a window that begins after the repair's first successful review, and enough attempts in it to distinguish a drop from noise — at roughly 100 review attempts a day on this lane (715 in the trailing seven days, 228 of them deaths), the trailing-seven-day figure is the smallest window that carries a rate worth gating on.

§5 — Method limits a reader should carry

  • Attempt, not run. A run that died and was resumed contributes two observations. This is deliberate — the plan counts lost work, and a resumed attempt is not evidence that the first one finished — but it means the rate cannot be compared against a figure computed the terminal way.
  • Timestamps fall back to file mtime. Streams that carry no timestamp field are dated by the file's mtime, so a stream whose file was touched after its last record lands on the wrong day. The daily series is therefore good for shape and not for a single day's exact count.
  • Signals are corroboration, not classification. Only some attempts carry a supervisor exit record naming the signal. The classification never depends on one as a stream may die for reasons the exit record does not capture, and the classifier reads the stream shape.
  • The window is the lane's whole recorded life. The start is the first local-lane attempt the corpus holds, not a chosen boundary; a rate computed over a sub-window is not directly comparable to it without recomputing.
  • Role comes from the dispatch prompt. The run's own prompt.txt declares its role; the ledger row is the fallback where the prompt is absent, and disagreements between the two are recorded in the data file rather than silently resolved. On this corpus the two sources did not disagree: all 2484 local-lane runs carrying streams declared a role in the prompt, and none contradicted its ledger row — the file's role_attribution block carries those counts so a reader can check the claim rather than take it.
