§1 — What this is, and the discipline that makes it a menu rather than decoration

This page is the endpoint menu a future session leader can dispatch to. The reasoning that motivates it is on the crew-effort-routing plan: every crew dispatch resolves its worker model and effort from flight config, more than one backend now exists to route to, and multiple backends also raise net throughput when implementing and reviewing. Three fleets have all been feeding measurements into messages; this page is the single place those measurements land, one row per configured backend, so a routing decision reads the matrix instead of a chat history.

The matrix is built under one binding rule, and the rule is the whole point of the page: every cell is either a dated snapshot with its derivation, or explicitly marked UNMEASURED. No figure is inferred for a lane nobody has run, no number is carried from one model to its sibling, and no cell is left blank — a blank reads as zero or as unknown depending on the reader, and the difference matters. Today most cells are unmeasured and the matrix says so plainly: a menu that looks complete when it is mostly guesses is worse than an empty one.

Three kinds of content appear in cells, and they are labelled rather than mixed:

  • M — a dated measurement snapshot, with the ledger filter that reproduces it stated in §8 and a link to the ledger-derived evidence beside it.
  • CONFIG — a declared configuration value (what flight config says the lane runs), not a measurement; it reports the setting, never the outcome.
  • UNMEASURED — no data exists; a reader must not treat the cell as a fact in either direction.

A lane ceiling belongs to its deployment and is discovered, never assumed. The local lane's concurrency admission limit changed by a large factor within a single day and the serving pool behind it moved twice; any ceiling written here is a dated reading of one deployment, not a property of reckon or of the model. Where a number is known to move, the page says so rather than freezing it.

Measurement mechanics used throughout — an unmetered lane's 429 is backpressure rather than exhaustion, stderr is not a status surface on the local lane, a session must never be resumed across models, and the structured retry record is the discriminator when text is ambiguous — follow skills/reckon-ship/references/worker-backends.md, which carries the local lane facts, and the routing policy in skills/reckon-ship/references/effort-routing.md. This page agrees with those references rather than restating them differently.

§5 — Concurrency ceilings, and what binds each lane

A fleet cannot open more work than the lanes admit, and the binding constraint is not the same on every lane. It is one of three shapes today:

  • A router allowance (fleet-level, all lanes). The coordinator-side ceiling on in-flight runs. Measured: a 16-live-run allowance was never reached because graph width — how many independent nodes exist that do not contend for the same files — bound the fleet first; client ceilings in live-run counts convert at roughly 0.5–0.7 requests per live run, so a "16 runs" ceiling throttles to about half that many simultaneous requests. This is a property of the routing layer, not of any lane.
  • Member serialisation (all CLI lanes). A roster member holds one live session at a time; a second dispatch for that member is refused (member-in-flight, exit 9) before worktree creation. Sessions also must not cross models — each model needs its own member, because a resumed session inherits the prior model's whole history.
  • A server admission limit (local lanes) or a weekly/rolling account window (metered lanes). These are the lane-level constraints, per row below.

The local lane's ceiling belongs to its deployment and is discovered, never assumed. It is a number on the serving deployment, and reckon carries none of its own. It moved within a single day of measurement and the serving pool behind it moved twice, once as a side effect of an unrelated configuration change. Units mislead: a reckon-side ceiling counts live runs while the server's admission limit counts simultaneous requests, and the ratio is variable — measured, four concurrent workers generated two simultaneous requests, because an agentic worker spends most of its wall clock between turns. So the dated reading below is a working bound, not a queue model.

§6 — Failure modes and recoveries per lane

The common shape is the 429: the same status and retry records appear on the wire whatever the lane's meteredness, and the declared meteredness is the only discriminator for what it means. An unmetered lane's retry stream is the serving queue telling the worker to stop asking for a while — no reset time, clears when a slot frees, and a shed run classifies as blocked and resumable rather than abandoned. A metered lane's is a budget refusal, may carry a reset moment that correctly holds a wave, and can be exhausted. Key on the structured retry record, never on the refusal sentence — the sentence is a strict and unreliable subset of the record.

§7 — Best-fit node classes and the routing reading

The matrix row's last column is the routing recommendation, and it is read against the crew-effort-routing policy: the coordinator declares a specification level, flight config resolves the backend and effort underneath, and an explicit --set override always wins. The honest reading today, given how much is measured on only one or two lanes:

  • Exact, mechanical, small (≤ 0.5 worker-hour), named check: the local lane (--local → clive) is qualified for it — 39/45 exact on the reckon ledger at 2026-09-06 and 42/44 in the dated cross-project snapshot — and codex-spark covers the same shape when the meter is being saved. Never let an open or under-specified node land on either.
  • Guided with the measurement handed over: local lane is permitted only with the measurement handed over as established fact; otherwise codex (high) or claude-opus by --set.
  • Open design or decide-when-measurement-is-done: codex (high) with claude-opus as the override for heavy judgment nodes; never the local lane.
  • Review: measured on codex (8/12) and a single claude-opus pass; unproven on clive (0/3). Review routing should stay on a frontier lane; the local lane has no authority argument yet.

Two quantities the routing reading must not confuse. Cost ordering: the local lane looks free and its imputed ledger cost ranks it dearest, while the claude family carries real per-run USD and high cache rates — the charged-fresh column, not total input, is the budget for the metered reading. Volume ceiling: the sustainable ceiling on the incumbent lane is roughly 298 nodes/week against a measured 564 dispatched (M 2026-09-04) — node count, not node size and not effort, is the quantity that decides whether a lane survives its week, so routing half the volume across the alternates is how multiple backends raise net throughput rather than just re-pricing the same queue.
