The local lane at width

Over a 55-minute window on 2026-09-25 coordinators ramped the local model lane to a peak of 36 concurrent requests. The earlier capacity limit on this lane is a prefill effect: cold prompts, whose prefixes miss the cache, are recomputed as prefill that displaces decode and collapses per-stream output. That collapse did not reproduce at this width, but the lane did degrade: from about 30 running, per-stream output fell by more than half and mean TTFT tripled.

Method

A sampler read the lane document every 30 s for the whole window and wrote one JSON row per tick, recording running, the derived ceiling, headroom, waiting, KV occupancy, the prefix hit rate, the router gate in-flight count and width, and the count of process-alive local crew runs. It appended every serving-telemetry row the router published, and every fifth tick scanned each live crew pointer for the declared stall signature: a stream silent for more than 900 s while its process is alive. 110 ticks were recorded, from 08:05Z to 08:59Z. The samples are the 110-row JSONL beside the analysis driver in the run's reports directory; the reduced series and the figure below are reproduced by docs/figures/local-lane-at-width/make_figure.py.

The prefill collapse did not reproduce, but the lane degraded from about 30 running. Running reached 36 at 08:41Z, and 25 ticks sat at or above 30, so the window did cross the width at which the earlier collapse was recorded. The cold-prefix signature itself did not appear: the prefix hit rate held a mean 0.9697 over the window with no sustained fall, KV occupancy peaked at 0.63 against 0.88 at the earlier collapse, and no live worker's stream stalled for the declared signature. What did appear is throughput degradation at the top of the ramp. Across the 25 ticks at or above 30 running, per-stream output fell to a mean 9.64 tok/s against 25.31 tok/s at the 15-running positive control, mean TTFT rose to 2.94 s against 0.94 s, and the router gate queued up to 2 requests. At the peak tick itself per-stream output was 9.47 tok/s and aggregate output 182.9 tok/s against a window mean of 366.6 tok/s. KV occupancy stayed low throughout, so this is not prefix eviction; the lane sits at its throughput band and past it per-stream latency absorbs the extra concurrency. The prefill-rate limit as a cold-prompt effect therefore does not bind at the observed width; the width itself does, consistent with the operating band of 29 to 31 recorded on the decode logs for this lane.

Caveats

Peak running is not fixed by the lane: it rises and falls with what coordinator sessions happen to hold in flight, and this window samples one ramp, not the ceiling of the lane. The aggregate output rate is computed from completion events whose requests complete inside each tick's trailing 35 s window, so a tick at which many long requests are still in flight undercounts it; the per-tick series in the figure shows that spread and the mean is stated over the window rather than read from any single tick. The prefix hit rate is the lane document's own derived field; on the ticks where it reads exactly 0.0 its neighbours 30 s either side read above 0.1579, which is what a staleness artefact looks like, but three of those readings fall in a row across the 08:41 peak, where a genuine cold-prefix moment would also appear. Without an independent counter this record cannot separate the two, and it reports the two readings side by side rather than picking one. The serving telemetry never populated cached_prompt_tokens in this window, so the hit rate could not be cross-checked against an independent counter, and the raw completion-token output of a stream was not available: per-stream output is the request-average completion-to-duration rate.
