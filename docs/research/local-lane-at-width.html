The local lane at width

Over a WINDOWMIN-minute window on 2026-09-25 coordinators ramped the local model lane to a peak of PEAK concurrent requests. The earlier capacity limit on this lane is a prefill effect: cold prompts, whose prefixes miss the cache, are recomputed as prefill that displaces decode and collapses per-stream output. At this width the effect did not appear.

Method

A sampler read the lane document every 30 s for the whole window and wrote one JSON row per tick, recording running, the derived ceiling, headroom, waiting, KV occupancy, the prefix hit rate, the router gate in-flight count and width, and the count of process-alive local crew runs. It appended every serving-telemetry row the router published, and every fifth tick scanned each live crew pointer for the declared stall signature: a stream silent for more than 900 s while its process is alive. SAMPLES ticks were recorded, from FIRSTTS to LASTTS. The samples are the SAMPLES-row JSONL beside the analysis driver in the run's reports directory; the reduced series and the figure below are reproduced by docs/figures/local-lane-at-width/make_figure.py.

The prefill limit did not bind at the observed width. Running reached PEAK with no cold-prefix signature: the prefix hit rate held near HITMEAN, KV occupancy stayed below KVMAX of the full-token pool against 0.88 at the earlier collapse, mean TTFT stayed near TTFTMEAN s against 3.0 s, the router gate never queued a request, and no live worker's stream stalled for the declared signature. Aggregate output over the window was AGGMEAN tok/s. The earlier collapse is a cold-prompt prefill effect, and this ramp produced neither of its inputs: not enough width to force prefix eviction, and not the prefix misses that separate collapsed intervals from healthy ones. Part of that may be the relaunch now live on the lane: the lane document reports the router gate at width 36 with an effective width of 36, consistent with the auto-width path that the prefix-fix relaunch activated, so the bound is drawn beside prefix-ordered scheduling rather than against the older first-come-first-served engine. The peak is also below every width at which the earlier collapse was recorded (33 and 36 running), so this window bounds the limit's onset to above PEAK and does not by itself rule it out at a higher width or a colder cache.

Caveats

Peak running is not fixed by the lane: it rises and falls with what coordinator sessions happen to hold in flight, and this window samples one ramp, not the ceiling of the lane. The aggregate output rate is computed from completion events whose requests complete inside each tick's trailing 35 s window, so a tick at which many long requests are still in flight undercounts it; the per-tick series in the figure shows that spread and the mean is stated over the window rather than read from any single tick. The prefix hit rate is the lane document's own derived field; on the isolated ticks where it reads exactly 0.0 its neighbouring ticks 30 s either side read above HITMIN, so it is recorded as a publication artefact of the document, not as a cold-prefix episode. The serving telemetry never populated cached_prompt_tokens in this window, so the hit rate could not be cross-checked against an independent counter, and the raw completion-token output of a stream was not available: per-stream output is the request-average completion-to-duration rate.
