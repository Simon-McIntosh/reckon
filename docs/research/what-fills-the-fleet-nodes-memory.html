Research input · fleet operations

What fills the fleet node's memory

The old 96 GiB allocation reached its limit repeatedly. Health and process samples identify large contributors, but do not time-stamp the 125 OOM kills. Some proposed protections already exist; none of the measurements supports treating every resident-set peak as memory that a change will return.

Method and evidence limits

Read the full incident account at /home/ITER/mcintos/Code/imas-ambix/docs/research/a-gate-log-overwrote-the-shared-interpreter.html, then analyzed its saved health and process tables alongside the current fleet job. All live crew-home, fleet-state, and /dev/shm reads were read-only. Service profiles ran once in separate Rigel jobs; none signaled or restarted a fleet service. The job and process plots use SI labels and are embedded below.

The historical health file contains 3,806 one-minute samples from 2026-11-13 12:43:42 UTC through 2026-11-16 05:04:59 UTC. Its positive control is job_mem_mb, present and positive in all 3,806 rows. It does not contain OOM event times or /tmp usage. The 116 saved process tables support per-process RSS and age observations, but cannot turn a process's RSS into exact cgroup memory saved by moving or restarting it.

1. Bound one declared service

Measured cause

The 2 GiB rehearsal allocation exposed no delegated memory-control directory to the service UID: the parent cgroup reported memory controllers, but creating a child failed with PermissionError: [Errno 13] Permission denied. The available job cgroup was root-owned. A per-process RLIMIT_AS of 768 MiB caused both allocation attempts to fail with MemoryError; the probe wrapper started a replacement child while its peer PID remained alive. This exercised restart-shaped behavior, not the production fleet supervisor. In the unbounded control, both stand-ins were killed with signal 9 and memory.events increased oom_kill by 2. This proves a process address-space limit and independent job OOM outcomes, not an aggregate service-tree memory limit.

A step-level srun --mem probe did not launch because Slurm rejected CPU binding outside the allocation. The systemd-run --user --scope probe stopped at an incompatible --pipe option. Neither result establishes whether those mechanisms can isolate a service on this cluster. Current cgroup delegation is not feasible for the service UID without an allocation or site-side change.

Existing-plan verdict

Partly covered. the-fleet-node-stays-in-service bounds a worker step at 24 GB, not each declared service. the-fleet-supervisor-runs-declared-services already restarts children with backoff but does not set a service memory limit. The remaining question is whether the site can delegate a child cgroup or provide a supported step boundary.

Prototype and control

Before: with no service bound, two allocation-heavy siblings both died at the job's 2 GiB limit; the peer was not preserved. After: with 768 MiB RLIMIT_AS on each stand-in, the bounded child failed its allocation on both attempts and the peer PID stayed alive. This is a successful process-level control and an unsuccessful aggregate-service prototype: the probe wrapper, not the production supervisor, restarted the child, and one child can still have descendants outside its address-space limit.

Proposed done-when and effort

On the declared Rigel rehearsal node, place one service and one peer in the chosen supported boundary; record the service cgroup's memory.current/memory.max or step limit and memory.events; force the service above the bound and show that it restarts while the peer PID remains unchanged, then run the same pair without a bound and show both hit the job OOM; assert the service is capped below 2 GiB and the peer survives in tests/test_fleet_supervisor_service_memory_bound.py. Site-side delegation must first be available; estimated effort is 12 quarter-hours (3 hours) after that dependency.

2. Dispatch, admission, and station memory

Measured cause

Across 116 process tables, the largest observed single-process RSS values were 47.42 GB for dispatch, 35.34 GB for admission, and 15.75 GB for station. The station process with the longest tracked life grew from 3.73 GB at age 15.00 h to 10.17 GB at 17.10 h, or 3.06 GB/h over that interval. The longest tracked admission process grew from 0.07 GB at 0.005 h to 6.13 GB at 1.054 h, or 5.78 GB/h. These are observed process histories, not proof that the same growth continues indefinitely.

One read-only crew admit --once profile reached its lease write and exited with EROFS, so it did not complete its pass. Its peak RSS was 7,224,820 KiB (7.22 GiB); tracemalloc peaked at 2,901,912,123 bytes and retained 97,789,190 bytes at exit. The largest traced sites at the peak snapshot were codecs (30.7 MB), importlib (28.6 MB), and BeautifulSoup elements (3.56 MB). Those top sites do not explain the multi-gigabyte RSS peak; most traced peak memory had been released by exit or was outside tracemalloc accounting. The live run store was 797,155,328 bytes with 11,947 run rows, 11,947 details, and 12,879 retired rows. The pending dispatch profile will test whether batch preparation decodes this store repeatedly.

Existing-plan verdict

Partly covered. the-monitor-reads-each-fact-once has a station retained-memory census and a follow-up to try malloc_trim; its largest retained site was json.decoder.raw_decode. It does not establish dispatch or admission peak allocation sites, nor a before-and-after RSS reduction. the-crew-home-has-a-bound limits stored state size, while crew-state-stays-bounded retires old records; neither bounds the memory of decoding a store. the-monitor-tells-the-truth-under-load measures stage durations rather than allocation.

Prototype and control

The admission profile is a partial read-only prototype, not a successful before-and-after comparison: it reached a forbidden lease write after the read path, and there is no completed post-change result. The 8-entry dispatch comparison is still running on Rigel. It compares one 8-entry batch with eight isolated one-entry batches against equivalent temporary homes and a copied read-only store; admission gates are paused, so it cannot queue live work. Until its log completes, no dispatch top-site or reduction claim is made. The process-age plot is the positive control that the saved process tables can show service identity and observed growth; the missing allocation profile is not an empty allocation result.

Proposed done-when and effort

For one named 8-entry dry run, report peak RSS, tracemalloc peak and top allocation sites, store bytes and decoded row counts, then apply the cheapest measured change and rerun the identical input; show the after value is lower while the one-entry runs provide the negative control for per-process versus cumulative batch cost, using tests/test_dispatch_batch_memory_profile.py. For station and admission, the same gate must complete the read-only pass without writing live state and report its exit receipt; estimated effort is 16 quarter-hours (4 hours), including a safe profiling seam for their lease and station-reading paths.

3. Admit work against memory pressure

Measured cause

The old job's memory fraction reached 1.0, with 102 exact-limit samples. It spent 37.79 h at or above 80%, 31.45 h at or above 85%, 23.23 h at or above 90%, 13.56 h at or above 95%, and 4.31 h at or above 99%. The 125 kills are recorded by the incident account, but their event timestamps are not present in the health series; therefore no fraction can be claimed as the kill cluster. The process-independent signal already exists in reckon/crew/summary.py: the admission path reads cgroup memory.current, memory.max, and memory.events, and computes whether current use plus a declared worker share fits.

In a same-minute check on job 1319168, the health row showed 89,917 MiB of 147,456 MiB (61.0%); the cgroup read showed 95,524,626,432 of 154,618,822,656 bytes (61.8%), with oom_kill=0. The difference is consistent with sampling within the minute, not a calibrated simultaneous pair. For a 24 GB declared worker share, the reserve rule puts the threshold at 72 GiB (75%) under a 96 GiB limit and 120 GiB (83.3%) under the current 144 GiB limit.

Existing-plan verdict

Partly covered. the-monitor-reads-each-fact-once includes a one-read cgroup memory probe and dispatch memory bound. The bound reserves declared worker memory but is not a fixed percentage threshold, and it does not isolate resident services, tests, or session scratch. the-monitor-tells-the-truth-under-load samples job memory but has no kill timestamps to correlate with the 125 events.

Prototype and control

The read prototype works: the same-minute health and cgroup samples both report about 61% of the current limit, and the cgroup reports no OOM kill. For the historical positive control, the old health series contains 102 readings at its known 96 GiB limit. Its negative control is the current job, which has no samples at or above 80% and whose cgroup reports zero OOM kills. The history supports a reserve sized to the declared next worker, but not a retrospectively fitted kill threshold.

Proposed done-when and effort

At every admission pass, read the job cgroup once and record current bytes, limit bytes, and oom_kill; refuse a launch when current plus the configured 24 GB worker reservation would exceed the limit, and show a synthetic over-bound request refused while a below-bound request launches; compare the recorded fields with the one-minute health sample and verify the reader reports zero for an idle cgroup in tests/test_admission_respects_job_memory.py. Preserve the 75% old-job and 83.3% current-job reserve calculations as capacity-specific references, not as a universal percentage; estimated effort is 8 quarter-hours (2 hours).

4. Keep tests off the fleet node

Measured cause

In the 2026-11-16 00:27:40 UTC process table, an executable-based positive-control matcher found 12 pytest invocations in 13 process-tree members, all worker gates, totaling 12.39 GB RSS. A separate sample's peak was 22 test processes and 13.06 GB RSS, also classified as worker gates. The incident account's broader 00:27 cohort was 163 processes and 23.1 GB; the narrower executable matcher does not reconcile that cohort. It is not evidence that the remaining processes were absent or belonged to other origins. The verified worker gates show that heavy test commands can execute in the worker's fleet allocation when their gate command does not submit elsewhere.

Existing-plan verdict

Partly covered. test-time-is-not-the-nodes-time gives tests a time budget and existing measurement-partition and sbatch paths can place declared gates on compute. The policy is not a universal command-level placement check, so a worker gate that runs directly can still consume fleet memory. The worker's own gate is the only origin positively classified in the reliable sample; the larger incident cohort remains unclassified here.

Prototype and control

The process-table classifier was checked against visible pytest executable commands and found the expected worker-gate roots. An initial text matcher falsely counted pytest text inside long sandbox arguments; the repaired executable matcher is the accepted result. No gate was moved during this read-only census, so a before/after memory reduction is not measured. Negative control required for the follow-up: include a known non-test service process and require it not to enter the test count; the current saved sample does not provide a separately verified non-test matcher receipt.

Proposed done-when and effort

For the same focused test command, capture its cgroup memory and process-tree RSS when attempted in a fleet allocation and when submitted to the project's declared measurement partition; the fleet attempt must be refused before pytest starts and the partition attempt must complete, with a known non-test service excluded by the sampler; count test-root processes and bytes once per sample and cap fleet test roots at zero in tests/test_tests_are_submitted_to_the_measurement_partition.py. Reconcile the verified cohort against the incident's 163-process table before claiming full origin totals; estimated effort is 12 quarter-hours (3 hours).

5. Move session scratch off memory-backed storage

Measured cause

The supervisor's prepare_session_tmp replaces the inherited value with /dev/shm/<uid>-fleet-<job> when available; the fleet-node launcher initially sets TMPDIR=/tmp, then the service supervisor selects session scratch. In current job 1319168, an explicit listing of the 32 session directories under /dev/shm/39486-fleet-1319168/claude-39486 preceded size measurements. They held 1,601,015,808 allocated bytes (1.60 GB); the largest session held 1,590,841,344 bytes (1.59 GB), nearly all of it in one session's icm-merge directory. At the same observation, node-local /tmp had 14 GB of 23 GB used and 8.8 GB free.

Existing-plan verdict

Partly covered. a-crew-process-ends-with-its-owner and crew-state-stays-bounded put run scratch under /tmp and bound parts of run state. They do not relocate harness session scratch from /dev/shm or cap a session's allocation. the-fleet-supervisor-runs-declared-services currently chooses the memory-backed session path without a per-session bound.

Prototype and control

The census measures the current memory-backed footprint; no relocation was performed. A 256 MiB/session ceiling would be far below the largest current directory and 32 sessions would reserve 8 GiB, leaving only 0.8 GB of the observed 8.8 GB free before other temporary work. That is a candidate budget, not a safe configured limit. No before/after savings are claimed. A relocation rehearsal must compare the same session's listed bytes on /dev/shm and /tmp, with a zero-scratch session as the negative control.

Proposed done-when and effort

Redirect new session scratch to a quota-enforced node-local /tmp allocation; report per-session allocated and peak bytes for all 32 sessions, demonstrate refusal above a 256 MiB candidate quota, and show a zero-scratch session remains at zero while its peer is capped; assert its byte count is excluded from /dev/shm and present on /tmp in tests/test_session_scratch_uses_bounded_node_tmp.py. Verify headroom on the actual mount before adopting 32 such quotas; estimated effort is 8 quarter-hours (2 hours).

6. Sample node-local /tmp

Measured cause

Neither historical nor current health TSV includes /tmp bytes or inode counts. The missing-field check was validated against job_mem_mb, which is present in all 3,806 historical rows and all 1,080 current rows; this is a schema gap, not a zero reading. The old allocation's health series remains available on GPFS after job death, but the sampler itself runs in the allocation, so it cannot add new samples after that allocation exits. The current one-shot filesystem reading was 14 GB used of 23 GB total (about 61%), with 8.8 GB available.

Existing-plan verdict

Partly covered. the-monitor-tells-the-truth-under-load records fleet health and the saved series survived the old job. It lacks node-local /tmp space and inode fields and an external writer that continues after the fleet allocation is gone. No reader currently acts on a missing /tmp signal because the field is not sampled.

Prototype and control

A direct df read provides the current 14/23 GB observation, while the health file's known-present memory field confirms the file and parser were readable. The health schema has no before/after sample because no sampler change was made. The external-writer requirement is not satisfied by the historical files alone: they prove data durability, not continued sampling after job death. The proposed negative control is a second mount with a separately known capacity, so a sampler that repeats one mount's values fails.

Proposed done-when and effort

Write one GPFS health row per minute containing node, job id, /tmp total/used/available bytes and free inodes from both the in-job and external sampler; compare the row with an independent df --block-size=1 reading for /tmp, then stop the fleet job and show the external writer adds another timestamp while a separately sized test mount is reported with its own values; have admission and the monitor reader flag less than 8 GiB free in tests/test_fleet_health_records_node_tmp.py. The 8 GiB trigger is a proposed operational reserve matched to the 32-session candidate budget and needs validation against non-session temporary work; estimated effort is 8 quarter-hours (2 hours).
